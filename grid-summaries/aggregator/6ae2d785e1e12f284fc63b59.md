## Universal RSV Protection for Newborns Could Sharply Cut EU Infant Hospital Stays

A new report from the ECDC concludes that offering RSV immunization to all newborns could substantially lower the number of infants hospitalized across the EU. The analysis compares two preventive approaches, each aimed at protecting babies during their most vulnerable early weeks.

### What the models show

According to the report's models, giving newborns a monoclonal antibody immunization at birth could prevent 61% of RSV-related hospitalizations during the first two months of life. Vaccinating mothers during pregnancy could prevent 52% of such hospitalizations in the same period. Both options point to a considerable reduction in early infant admissions.

### Why the timing matters

Europe is heading into fall and winter, the season when RSV, flu, and other infections put pressure on hospitals and raise risks for infants. Health systems are looking for prevention strategies that can reduce pediatric admissions before seasonal peaks arrive, and these findings add to that discussion.
