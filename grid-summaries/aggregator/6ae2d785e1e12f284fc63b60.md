## Atomic-Scale Metal Layer Amplifies Spin Signals for Low-Power Electronics

Researchers at Kyushu University report that a metal film just 0.3 nm thick can strengthen key spin-related signals in layered spintronic devices. The finding points to a new way of tuning materials for energy-saving technology.

### What the researchers found

The team added an ultrathin layer of iridium manganese to YIG/Pt spintronic structures. This tiny layer boosted both the spin Seebeck effect and spin Hall magnetoresistance signals. The results suggest that controlling atomic-scale interface layers can improve how spin is transferred, which could support ultralow-power electronics and devices that turn heat into electricity.

### Why it matters now

Demand for energy keeps rising as AI, computing, and connected devices spread. That makes materials enabling lower-power or battery-free electronics increasingly valuable. Spintronics is attracting attention as a path toward sustainable computing and as a way to harvest heat that would otherwise be wasted.
