## Atomic Magnetism Explains Why Iron Catalysts Harness Sunlight for Ammonia and Chromium Ones Don't

Researchers have worked out why two almost identical metal–organic framework catalysts perform so differently when light is involved. Iron-based versions can use light to help turn nitrogen into ammonia, while chromium-based versions cannot.

### The Magnetic Difference

The deciding factor is atomic magnetism. Each metal's spin interactions shape how nitrogen binds to the catalyst's nodes and how it is later released. In the iron-based material, those interactions allow light to assist the conversion. In the chromium-based material, they do not, even though the structures are so similar.

### Why It Matters

Ammonia is essential for making fertilizer, yet producing it still demands a great deal of energy. That makes low-energy, sunlight-driven alternatives an important goal for climate and food-security efforts. The finding provides a new "magnetic fingerprint" that could guide the design of better catalysts.
