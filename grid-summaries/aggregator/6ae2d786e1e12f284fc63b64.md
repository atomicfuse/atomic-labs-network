## Achilles Injections Fall Short of Sham Treatment in Major Trial

A randomized trial published in the BMJ has found that high-volume injections for midportion Achilles tendinopathy offer little real advantage over a sham procedure.

### What the trial found

Researchers tested high-volume injections given either with or without a corticosteroid. At 12 months, neither approach produced a meaningful improvement in pain or disability when compared with sham treatment. The results raise questions about the routine use of these injections for the condition. The authors point instead toward exercise-based care as the foundation of management, along with clearer, more honest counseling so patients understand what to expect from treatment.

### Why it matters

Achilles tendinopathy is common and stubborn, and it can be especially frustrating for active adults. That frustration fuels demand for quick-fix procedures. This trial adds timely evidence against overusing injections when their long-term benefits appear weak or uncertain.
