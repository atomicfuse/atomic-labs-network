## Canadian Measles Outbreak Shows How Immunity Gaps Can Undo Elimination Status

A study in the BMJ examines the Ontario-centered measles outbreak that helped lead Canada to lose its measles elimination status. The research points to specific patterns in how the disease moved and what that means for other countries.

### How the outbreak spread

According to the study, the outbreak traveled mainly through communities that have historically had low vaccination rates and strong social ties. Those connections carried the virus across borders. The authors argue that even countries with robust vaccine programs stay vulnerable if persistent immunity gaps and low trust in vaccination are left unaddressed.

### Why it matters now

Canada's loss of status serves as a warning for other high-income countries dealing with vaccine hesitancy, public health fatigue, and the movement of disease across borders. Because measles is so contagious, small pockets of undervaccination can quickly grow into national and international outbreaks.
