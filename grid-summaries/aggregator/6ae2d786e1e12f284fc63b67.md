## Molecular Shape Explains Why Wild Tomatoes Fend Off Leafminers

Wild tomatoes withstand the tomato leafminer better than cultivated ones, and new research points to the three-dimensional structure of a single defense molecule as the reason. The finding suggests a path toward tomatoes that can protect themselves more effectively.

### A Defense Molecule With a Toxic Twist

The key compound is filotomatine, which wild tomatoes use to defend against the leafminer, their main pest. Its 3D structure makes it more toxic to the insect than the version found in cultivated plants. Researchers discovered that altering just four amino acids in one enzyme can change the molecule's configuration, shifting it toward the more potent form.

### Why Gene Editing and Chirality Matter

This precise genetic route could allow gene-edited tomatoes with stronger natural resistance, supporting efforts to cut pesticide use and protect crops from major pests. The work also highlights chirality, the "handedness" of molecules, as an underexplored tool in crop protection and biotechnology.
