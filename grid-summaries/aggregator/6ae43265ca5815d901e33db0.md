## Political anxiety emerges as its own distinct form of distress

New research suggests that the stress people feel about politics is not simply general anxiety under another name. Elections and polarized political climates, the findings indicate, can produce a form of distress all their own.

### What the research found

Kevin Smith and Aaron Weinschenk ran a survey in 2025 that replicated much of their 2020 research on election-related anxiety. Their findings point to political anxiety as separate from general anxiety, shaped by the political environment people live in rather than by broader worries alone.

### Why it matters now

Voters are dealing with information overload, deep polarization, and constant pressure from the election cycle. As a result, political engagement is increasingly linked to both mental and physiological health. The research arrives as creators, audiences, and institutions try to understand how politics affects everyday well-being.
