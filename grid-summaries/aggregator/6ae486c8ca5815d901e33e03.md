## Autism and intellectual disability tied to higher rates of Parkinson's-related disorders

A large study of nearly 57 million Americans has found that adults with autism, intellectual disabilities, or both are more likely than the general population to have Parkinson's-related movement disorders.

### What the study found

Researchers reported higher rates of these movement disorders among adults with autism and/or intellectual disabilities. Notably, the connection held even when people taking antipsychotic medications were left out of the analysis. That suggests the increased risk cannot be explained only by side effects from those drugs.

### Why it matters now

As autistic adults grow older, clinicians and caregivers are giving more thought to long-term health risks that may previously have gone unnoticed. The findings point to a need for better screening and for establishing motor function baselines earlier in life. They also underline the importance of telling apart lifelong differences in movement from new signs of neurological decline.
