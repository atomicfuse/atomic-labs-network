## Study Links Young-Adult Obesity to Higher Cancer Risk

New research suggests that carrying excess weight in your 20s and 30s may raise the chance of developing cancer before midlife. The study followed 6.3 million South Korean adults aged 20 to 39.

### What the data showed

Cancer risk climbed step by step with body weight, from 1.84% at moderate weight to 2.62% at severe obesity. Abdominal obesity, meaning a high waist circumference, carried a similarly elevated risk even among people whose overall weight was moderate. The strongest associations appeared for thyroid, endometrial, ovarian, colorectal, kidney, liver and pancreatic cancers.

### Why it matters now

Obesity and early-onset cancer are both becoming more common among younger adults worldwide, which gives the findings added urgency for Gen Z and millennials. The study recasts weight management as more than a cosmetic or short-term health matter, presenting it as a long-term strategy for preventing cancer in people in their 20s and 30s.
