## Decades-Old Compression Therapy Shows Promise for Early Alzheimer's

Researchers have found that external counterpulsation, a compression therapy first used in the 1970s, may improve thinking skills in people with early Alzheimer's disease. The approach is thought to work by increasing blood flow to the brain.

### What the Study Found

A 12-month study of 190 participants reported cognitive gains in several areas, including memory, attention, language, and reasoning. The benefits were also reported to persist after treatment had ended, suggesting the effects were not limited to the period of therapy itself.

### Why It Matters Now

Alzheimer's disease affects millions of people around the world, and new treatment options remain limited. That makes any promising non-invasive therapy worth attention. The renewed interest in this older technique offers hope for early intervention that does not rely on drugs or surgery.
