## Rubin Observatory Spots Possible Giant Dwarf Planet Past Pluto

Astronomers using the Vera C. Rubin Observatory have identified a new dwarf planet candidate in the distant outer solar system. Called 2026 ED138, the object lies beyond Pluto and may rank among the biggest of its kind ever found.

### What was found

The candidate, 2026 ED138, is estimated to measure between 370 and 930 miles across. That wide range reflects an uncertain size, but even the upper end suggests the object could be one of the largest dwarf planets located in the outer solar system. For now, it remains a candidate rather than a confirmed dwarf planet.

### Why the timing matters

The observatory only started its 10-year survey in June 2026, and the find arrives early in that effort. Its advanced detection capabilities allow it to pick out objects in crowded regions of the sky that earlier telescopes missed. The discovery signals the start of a new era of outer solar system exploration.
