## Ceres May Be Hiding Far More Salt Beneath Its Surface Ice

Caltech researchers studying data from NASA's Dawn spacecraft report that water ice on the dwarf planet Ceres could be concealing much more salt than scientists had thought, possibly 30 to 50 percent more.

### How the Salt Builds Up

The study finds that salt becomes concentrated through sublimation. Deposits that begin as a mix of salt and ice lose their water content over time, leaving the salt behind at the surface. This challenges earlier assumptions that Ceres' bright spots were salt-rich from the start.

### Why Ceres Matters

Ceres is the largest object in the asteroid belt and makes up roughly 25 to 30 percent of the belt's total mass. That makes its geology useful for understanding how the solar system formed and evolved. The new findings also resolve a decade-long paradox from the Dawn mission about how salt deposits form and persist on icy surfaces.
