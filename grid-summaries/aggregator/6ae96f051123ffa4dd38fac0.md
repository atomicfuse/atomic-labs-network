## Distant Climate Patterns Steer Where UK Storm Surges Strike

Cambridge researchers have found that storm surges along UK coasts are shaped by climate patterns located thousands of kilometres away. Their analysis drew on 70 years of sea level records.

### What the research found

Using machine learning, the team connected specific regional surge patterns to faraway conditions, especially atmospheric pressure configurations across the North Atlantic and Arctic. Arctic weather, Atlantic pressure systems and storms that begin off Canada and Greenland all help determine which parts of the UK coast face the greatest flooding risk.

### Why it matters now

Arctic warming is reshaping the jet stream and wider Northern Hemisphere climate patterns. As a result, the locations and intensity of storm surges are likely to shift. That makes the findings important for coastal communities that are already vulnerable to flooding, and for forecasters who will need to adapt their warning systems to a changing climate.
