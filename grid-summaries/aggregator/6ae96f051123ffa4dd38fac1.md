## Neptune's Elusive Trojan Asteroids Finally Spotted by Belfast Team

Researchers at Queen's University Belfast have found Neptune's long-sought Trojan asteroids, using data from the newly operational Vera C. Rubin Observatory. These icy bodies lead and trail the planet along its orbit.

### What the team found

The researchers identified two confirmed asteroids and one candidate belonging to Neptune's trailing Trojan population. The discovery addresses a puzzle that has troubled astronomers for more than a century, since similar groups of Trojans have been found around other giant planets.

### Why the timing matters

Trojan populations around Jupiter, Saturn and Uranus have been known since 1906, leaving Neptune's as a conspicuous gap in solar system science. The Rubin Observatory, fully operational as of June 2025, offers unprecedented sensitivity and sky-survey speed, which finally made detection possible. The result signals a new era of discoveries about the small bodies of the outer solar system.
