## Body's own gas shows promise against wound infections in moth trial

Researchers at the University of Reading have found that nitric oxide, a gas the human body makes naturally, may help tackle bacterial infections of the skin and wounds when it is delivered through tiny nanoparticles.

### What the moth study found

The team tested the approach on wax moth larvae infected with bacteria. Among the infected insects that received nanoparticles releasing nitric oxide, 90% survived. In comparison, 60% of the larvae that went untreated survived the infection. The results point to the gas, carried in nanoparticle form, as a possible way to help combat bacterial infection.

### Why it matters now

Antibiotic resistance is a critical global health threat, and new ways of treating infections are urgently needed. This research offers a potential alternative mechanism, one that bacteria cannot easily develop resistance to. That could help address a pressing gap in the options currently available for treating infections.
