## Toxicology Reveals Which Drugs Accompanied Inca Capacocha Sacrifices

Archaeologists have analysed eight Inca mummies to learn which psychoactive substances were consumed during capacocha, the empire's most sacred ritual, which involved child sacrifice. The results point to a practice that differed from place to place.

### What the analysis found

Toxicological testing showed that coca leaves were consumed in every case studied. Alcohol turned up in some of the mummies but not all. At least one mummy also contained harmine, a compound linked to ayahuasca. This mix of substances suggests that the ritual was not carried out in an identical way at every Inca sacred site.

### Why it matters now

Newer toxicology techniques can detect very low concentrations of ancient drugs in preserved hair and nails, something that was not possible until recently. This opens a fresh view into Inca religious life. The findings challenge the idea that capacocha followed a single standard form, and instead suggest a complex religious system adapted to local circumstances.
