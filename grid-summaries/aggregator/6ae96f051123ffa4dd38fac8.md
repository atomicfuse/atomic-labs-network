## Deep-Sleeping Stem Cells May Be the Body's Hidden Reserve for Blood

German researchers have found a previously overlooked group of blood stem cells that slip into an unusually deep dormant state. The cells appear to stay functional for a whole lifetime, which could help explain how the body keeps producing blood and immune cells as the years pass.

### What the researchers found

The so-called deep sleeper cells are marked by a protein called EPCR. They remain inactive far longer than conventional blood stem cells do. They also show a preference for generating immune cells, setting them apart from their more active counterparts.

### Why it matters

Populations are aging, and blood disorders such as leukemia are common, so understanding how stem cells last and function is an urgent medical priority. The discovery could change how stem cell transplant therapies are approached and may help prevent age-related decline in the blood and immune systems.
