## Liverwort Evolution Shaped by a Reproductive Trade-Off

Scientists have worked out how the female reproductive structures of liverworts evolved to meet two conflicting demands. Fertilization requires water, while spreading spores works best in dry conditions.

### A stepwise path to a solution

The study shows that these structures developed gradually rather than all at once. Researchers identified a water-transport system that had not been known before. They also found that the specialized structures used for fertilization appeared only after the elevated stalk that helps disperse spores. In other words, the lifted stalk came first, and the fertilization features followed.

### Why it matters

The findings help explain a major evolutionary transition, namely how plants first established themselves on land while reconciling the need for water with the need for dryness. The research also points toward possible biomimetic uses, with the newly found system offering ideas for passive liquid-transport technology in engineering.
