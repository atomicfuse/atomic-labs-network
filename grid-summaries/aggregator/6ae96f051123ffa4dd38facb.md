## Plant Compound Shows Promise Against Cancer Drug Resistance

Researchers at Johns Hopkins have found that triptolide, a natural compound from the thunder god vine, may help cancer cells stay vulnerable to a class of drugs known as DNMT inhibitors. The work was published in Nature Communications in August 2026.

### How the compound works

DNMT inhibitors are designed to reactivate tumor-suppressing genes. Cancer cells can resist them, however, using an enzyme called DCTPP1. The team found that triptolide blocks this enzyme. In lab and animal models, pairing triptolide with the drug decitabine significantly boosted the drug's ability to kill cancer cells.

### Why it matters

Drug resistance is a major barrier that has limited DNMT inhibitors to blood cancers. The finding opens a potential pathway toward extending these therapies to solid tumors, which addresses a critical unmet need in oncology. The study has drawn attention in the precision medicine and cancer research communities.
