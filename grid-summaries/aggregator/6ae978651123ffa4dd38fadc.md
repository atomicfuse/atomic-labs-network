## Congressional Control May Stay Undecided Well Past Election Night

Learning which party will run Congress after Tuesday's election could take much longer than a single evening, even though the stakes are plain. The reasons come down to how and when votes are tallied.

### Why counting takes time

Places count ballots on different timelines, and procedures add further delays. Mail-in ballot processing can slow results, and ranked choice voting requires extra steps before a winner can be named. Together, these factors mean some races may stay unresolved long after polls close.

### Why the wait matters

Margins are razor-thin. Republicans need only 218 House seats to hold a majority, while Democrats need 51 Senate seats, since the vice president's tiebreaking vote would settle a split. With control hinging on so little, the uncertainty over when winners will be called has real implications for political momentum and for public confidence in the results.
