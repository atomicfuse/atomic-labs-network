## Old Quaker Cemetery Ties Childhood Rickets to Climate Shocks

A study of teeth and burial records from an 18th–19th century Quaker cemetery in northeast England connects childhood vitamin D deficiency and rickets during the Industrial Revolution to climate disruption. The findings point to volcanic eruptions and low solar activity during the Little Ice Age.

### What the study found

Researchers argue that disease patterns in this period were not shaped only by pollution, poverty, and urbanization. Reduced UV exposure, linked to environmental instability, also played a part. By examining teeth alongside burial records, the study traces how these shocks left a mark on children's health.

### Why it matters now

Climate instability is again reshaping public health, and the research shows that environmental disruption has long worked together with social conditions to worsen disease. It also recasts the Industrial Revolution as a case study in how climate, labor, housing, and inequality combine to affect human bodies.
