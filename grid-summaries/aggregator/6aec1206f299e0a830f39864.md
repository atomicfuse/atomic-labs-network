## Volcanoes and Weak Sunlight Added to Victorian Children's Rickets Burden

A bioarchaeology study of teeth from a 19th-century Quaker cemetery in northeast England points to a new factor behind childhood rickets in the Victorian era. Researchers suggest reduced ultraviolet exposure played a part, alongside the better-known influences of industrial pollution and poverty.

### What the Study Suggests

The teeth analysed in the research came from the Quaker burial ground. According to the findings, volcanic eruptions and low solar activity during the Little Ice Age cut the amount of UV light reaching people. That reduction appears to have made rickets worse among children, rather than the disease stemming only from Industrial Revolution conditions.

### Why It Matters Today

The work shows that environmental change has long interacted with inequality, labour conditions and disease, a relevant point as climate instability again affects health. It also recasts Victorian public health as a story of overlapping causes, rather than one industrial culprit.
