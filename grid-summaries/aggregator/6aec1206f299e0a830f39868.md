## Orbiter spots possible signs of volcanic stirring on Mars

A European orbiter has picked up new hints that Mars may not be as quiet as it looks. Near the Alba Mons volcano, it found short-lived bursts of hydrogen chloride and fresh fissures in the surface.

### What the orbiter found

The hydrogen chloride spikes were localized, and the fissures appeared in the same area around Alba Mons. Together, they suggest gases may be venting from hot magma lying beneath the surface. The article argues that Mars may be dormant rather than geologically dead, and that active volcanic systems could still exist underground.

### Why it matters now

The result adds to a growing body of evidence that Mars still holds internal heat, produces marsquakes, and may have active volcanic plumbing. It also sharpens the hunt for life. Scientists are being pointed toward places where heat, chemistry, and possible groundwater could combine to create habitable niches.
