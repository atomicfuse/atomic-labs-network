## Hydrogen Spotted Hiding Between Metal Atoms for the First Time

University of Sydney researchers have directly observed hydrogen dissolved in the tiny interstitial spaces between metal atoms, reaching a milestone that metallurgists have sought for a long time.

### What the observations show

The findings reveal how atomic arrangement and chemistry influence where hydrogen settles inside a metal. That insight offers clues to a persistent problem: why metals crack when exposed to hydrogen-rich environments. Until now, scientists lacked direct views of hydrogen at this scale.

### Why it matters for clean energy

Hydrogen is becoming central to clean energy storage and transport, so infrastructure safety depends on metals that can resist hydrogen embrittlement. The discovery gives scientists atomic-scale evidence they need to design stronger pipelines, tanks, and high-strength steels. With a clearer picture of where hydrogen resides, researchers can better understand how these materials fail and work toward more durable alternatives.
