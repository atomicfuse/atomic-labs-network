## New Research Argues Britain's Iron Age Ruler Verica Was a Woman

Archaeologists have put forward a fresh claim about Verica, the Iron Age ruler whose exile helped set off Rome's invasion of Britain in A.D. 43. According to the new research, Verica was female rather than male, as long assumed.

### The evidence behind the claim

The argument draws on three strands: the imagery found on ancient coins, the name "Verica" itself, and a rethinking of assumptions about gender and power. Researchers suggest that both Roman writers and Victorian-era scholars read the past through their own expectations, which may have shaped how the ruler was understood.

### Why it matters

If the claim holds, one of the most consequential turning points in British history would be seen through the actions of an overlooked female political leader. The finding also arrives at a time of growing public interest in archaeology that questions patriarchal readings of the ancient world.
