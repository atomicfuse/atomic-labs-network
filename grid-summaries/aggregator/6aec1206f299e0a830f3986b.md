## Iron Age Ruler Behind Rome's Invasion Of Britain May Have Been a Woman

Verica, the Atrebates ruler whose exile helped set off Rome's 43 CE invasion of Britain, was probably a woman and not a man, according to a new argument. Historians have long described this figure as a king.

### The case for a female ruler

Researchers point to three kinds of evidence: Verica's name, Roman accounts of Claudius, and the imagery found on coins. They argue that these clues, read together, suggest that generations of historians misgendered a pivotal political figure.

### Why the finding matters now

The claim recasts one of the most consequential turning points in ancient British history by highlighting gender bias in how the past has been interpreted. It also arrives as public interest grows in overlooked female rulers, in archaeology, and in the ways modern assumptions shape our view of earlier eras.
