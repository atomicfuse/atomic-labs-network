## Resting brain activity may flag Alzheimer's risk early

Researchers at Stanford Medicine report that patterns of brain activity recorded at rest could indicate a person's risk of Alzheimer's disease years before memory loss, plaques, or tangles show up.

### What the study found

The team introduced a measure called the ARC score. It is designed to tell apart brain patterns resembling the resilience seen in so-called super agers from those resembling the vulnerability seen in Alzheimer's. According to the findings, the score may do a better job than existing biomarkers at signaling who is at risk.

### Why it matters now

Alzheimer's research is increasingly focused on catching the disease early, before damage becomes harder to slow or treat. Public interest in brain scans, biomarkers, and longevity science is also growing. This study offers a hopeful angle: risk may be visible earlier than expected, and possibly modifiable.
