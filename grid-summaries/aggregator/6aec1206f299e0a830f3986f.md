## Genetic map points toward smarter tuberculosis drug combinations

Scientists at Rockefeller have built the most comprehensive genetic interaction map so far for *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*, the bacterium behind TB. It reveals nearly 6,500 relationships among the microbe's essential genes.

### What the map can do

The study demonstrates several uses for the resource. Researchers can use it to uncover the functions of genes that were previously unknown, and to identify new targets for drugs. It can also help predict which antibiotic combinations might work better against TB than those currently available.

### Why it matters now

Tuberculosis still kills more than a million people every year. Treatment takes a long time, which gives the bacteria more opportunities to develop resistance. Drug-resistant TB remains a global health threat, so systematic tools for designing smarter antibiotic regimens are urgently needed.
