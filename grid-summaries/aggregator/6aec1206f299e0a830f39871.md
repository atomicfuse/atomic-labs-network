## Shockwave therapy may ease short-term frozen shoulder pain

A new review in The BMJ, covering 189 randomized trials, examined treatments for frozen shoulder. It suggests some options help in the short term, though much remains unclear.

### What the review found

Extracorporeal shockwave therapy probably reduces frozen shoulder pain in the short term. Manipulation under anesthesia may improve short-term arm flexion. For most other outcomes, including longer-term benefits, how safe the treatments are compared with one another, and the effects of other approaches, the evidence remains uncertain.

### Why it matters

Frozen shoulder affects many middle-aged adults, especially women. Treatment choices range from physical therapy to injections and surgery, which can make decisions difficult. The review offers patients and clinicians a clearer snapshot of short-term evidence, while also showing how much uncertainty still surrounds these treatments.
