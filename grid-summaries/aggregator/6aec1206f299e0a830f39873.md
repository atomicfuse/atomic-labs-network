## UCLA Team Moves CRISPR Sickle Cell Repair Toward Human Trials

A UCLA-led team has taken a CRISPR-Cas9 therapy for sickle cell disease from a laboratory concept to manufacturing that is ready for a clinical trial. The approach is designed to fix the underlying genetic fault directly.

### What the researchers did

The therapy uses CRISPR-Cas9 to correct the disease-causing mutation within a patient's own blood-forming stem cells. In preclinical work, the strategy showed promising safety and durability, along with improvements in hemoglobin. The team also advanced the process to the point where it can support clinical-trial manufacturing.

### Why it matters now

Gene therapy for sickle cell disease is entering a new phase following recent FDA approvals. This study points to a potentially more direct strategy that repairs the mutation itself. The work is especially relevant because the disease disproportionately affects Black communities, and scalable access to curative therapies remains a major issue.
