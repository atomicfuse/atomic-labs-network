## Missouri Democrats End GOP Supermajority in Both Chambers

Democrats have broken the Republican supermajority in both chambers of the Missouri legislature for the first time since 2012. Republicans still hold control of the House and the Senate, so the change does not hand Democrats power over the agenda.

### Limited Policy Effect, Bigger Political Signal

The immediate impact on policy may be modest, since Republicans retain majorities in both chambers. The analysis argues the real significance lies in the politics. The result could influence whether Democrats are able to rebuild competitiveness in statewide races by 2028, a goal that has eluded the party in recent years.

### A Shifting Landscape Ahead of 2028

The change arrives during a broader Democratic surge in the midterms and after several ballot defeats for conservative priorities in Missouri, including measures on abortion restrictions and redistricting. Major statewide offices and a U.S. Senate seat will be on the 2028 ballot, and both parties are now recalibrating their strategies for that next big election cycle.
