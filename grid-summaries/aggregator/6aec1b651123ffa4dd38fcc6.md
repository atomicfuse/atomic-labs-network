## Southwest Ohio Voters Take On Data Center Projects

Residents in three Southwest Ohio communities used the ballot box to challenge large data center plans, and the results suggest a growing local backlash against the politicians who backed them.

### What voters decided

In Piqua, Urbana, and Wilmington, ballot measures banned or limited data center development, or reversed zoning connected to proposed projects. The votes targeted not only the developments themselves but also the officials who fast-tracked or supported them, showing resistance to tech infrastructure building at the local level.

### Why the issue is heating up

Data centers are spreading quickly to serve AI, cloud computing, and the broader growth of big tech. Many communities worry about how the facilities affect land use, power demand, water supply, and local control over decisions. The Southwest Ohio results signal that residents may be turning data centers into a major local political issue.
