## Alaska waits on ballots as Senate and governor's races may head to ranked tabulation

Alaska's U.S. Senate and governor's races remain undecided, and both could be settled by ranked-choice tabulation if no candidate collects more than 50% of first-choice votes. Legal disputes, allegations of candidate manipulation, and delays in counting ballots have added to the uncertainty.

### Why the count is taking time

Because no candidate may clear the 50% threshold on first choices, officials could need to run ranked tabulations to name winners. Absentee ballot timelines and slow counting mean the outcomes could be pushed back until those tabulations are completed, leaving voters and observers waiting.

### What is at stake

The state's election system is drawing national scrutiny, including partisan attacks on ranked-choice voting. A ballot measure could repeal Alaska's open-primary and ranked-choice system. The results may shape narratives about Senate control, decide who governs Alaska, and influence the future of election reform in the state.
