## Why High-IQ Women Called "Too Much" as Girls May Still Shrink Themselves

An article examines eight habits that often follow high-IQ women into adulthood after they were labeled "too much" as children. It argues that these patterns are less personal flaws than long-standing coping strategies.

### The habits and their roots

The piece lists behaviors ranging from over-explaining to hiding enthusiasm and feeling unseen. Its central claim is that each one began as a survival adaptation. Growing up in environments that punished intensity, intelligence, and emotional depth, these girls learned to adjust themselves to stay accepted, and the adjustments stuck.

### Why the topic resonates

Discussions of giftedness, neurodivergence, burnout, and women "shrinking" themselves are drawing strong interest in self-development and mental health circles. The article taps into a wider cultural push to recast traits once criticized in girls as strengths that society suppressed rather than as problems to fix.
