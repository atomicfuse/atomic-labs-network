## National Hero Titles May Honour Victims While Leaving Justice Undone

A new piece argues that Indonesia's National Hero titles can recast victims of state violence as patriotic symbols, even as the crimes committed against them remain unresolved. The case of Marsinah is used to illustrate the pattern.

### Recognition without accountability

According to the argument, official recognition tends to arrive only once accountability has become politically or legally unreachable. Marsinah's case highlights this dynamic. The honour elevates the victim's memory, but it can also close a chapter without answering who was responsible, allowing symbolism to stand in for resolution.

### Why the debate continues

Indonesia is still debating historical memory, labour rights and state violence, and the piece warns that symbolic honours risk substituting for justice. Marsinah's image remains potent in contemporary protests. Demonstrators invoke it in campaigns over gender equality, workers' protections and impunity, showing that the issues tied to her case are still live.
