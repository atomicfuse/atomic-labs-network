## Smoky bacon crisps become flashpoint in UK-EU food row

A proposed UK-EU food standards deal could require Britain to follow EU rules limiting meat-related words on products that contain no meat. Smoky bacon crisps have turned into a symbol of a larger argument.

### What the deal could change

Under the proposal, Britain would adopt EU restrictions on terms such as "bacon" or "chicken" when they appear on items without meat. That has made everyday snacks a talking point in the long-running Brexit debate over sovereignty and regulation, with critics warning that Britain is becoming a "rule taker."

### Why the argument is heating up

The UK government wants closer alignment with the EU on food to reduce trade costs. Opponents are using familiar products like crisps to make the issue politically charged. The rules would not apply until 2029, but negotiations are happening now, so the row offers a preview of future fights over post-Brexit compromise.
