## Op-ed Says Political Polarization Is Largely a Misperception

An opinion piece titled "VADNOS: America's political polarization is a misperception" argues that the divide between Americans is smaller than most people believe. It also lays out a practical approach for talking across ideological lines.

### The Misperception Problem

The article contends that many people assume those on the other side are more hostile and more anti-democratic than they actually are. That inflated picture, it suggests, makes the country look more divided than it is. To help people talk with those they disagree with, the piece offers a framework called "RUN," built on three elements: receptivity, common ground and narratives.

### Why It Matters Now

In a tense political climate, particularly on campuses and in discussions tied to elections, many people avoid disagreement because they expect conflict. The piece reframes polarization as a social problem that can be solved, not only a national crisis, and presents better conversations as a workable starting point.
