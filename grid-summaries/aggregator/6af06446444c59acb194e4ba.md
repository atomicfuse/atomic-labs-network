## A Column Calls for Shielding Long-Term Policy From Election Swings

A new opinion column contends that America's election-driven political system produces constant policy reversals, which hold back progress on slow-moving problems. The author points to climate, healthcare, immigration and trust in institutions as areas where lasting results are hard to reach.

### The Proposal

The column suggests that bipartisan solutions built by experts should be protected from partisan rollback for a period of 10 to 30 years. That window, the author argues, would give such policies enough time to be properly tested and judged on their results rather than discarded after the next change in power.

### Why It Resonates Now

The piece arrives amid polarization, gridlock and administrative reversals that shape nearly every major policy debate. It speaks to frustration that short-term politics is failing to solve generational problems. Climate, immigration, healthcare costs and faith in democracy all remain urgent, yet they are structurally difficult to address within the span of a single election cycle.
