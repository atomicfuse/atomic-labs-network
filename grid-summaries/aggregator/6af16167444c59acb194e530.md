## Inquiry calls for sweeping reform to lift white working-class pupils

An independent inquiry has concluded that "once-in-a-generation" reform is required to improve educational outcomes for white working-class pupils in England. The panel says society as a whole must act, and it is now preparing the next stage of its work.

### Where the inquiry goes next

The following phase will concentrate on three areas: the early years, the move from primary to secondary school, and the routes available to young people aged 14 to 19. Those routes cover progression into further study, training and employment.

### Why the timing matters

The issue is being linked to broader worries about school readiness, youth unemployment and growing numbers of young people not in education, employment or training. Government figures are backing new approaches, including Education Secretary Lucy Powell and Prime Minister Andy Burnham, who support fresh thinking on technical education and community-led support.
