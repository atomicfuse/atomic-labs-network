## Study finds cannabis may affect men and women differently

A UCLA Health study suggests that how marijuana feels can depend on a user's sex and how often they use it. Researchers compared people with similar habits and found that the differences showed up mainly among those who use cannabis only occasionally.

### What the researchers found

Occasional male users appeared to feel more intoxicated, sedated, impaired and anxious than women with comparable use habits. This was the case even though both groups had similar THC levels in their blood. Among frequent users, the gap was not as strong, which points to a person's history of cannabis use as an important factor in how the drug affects them.

### Why it matters now

Cannabis use is rising, and many products now sold contain far more THC than the amounts tested in the study. The findings challenge common assumptions that cannabis affects everyone in the same way, or that something described as "natural" must carry little risk.
