## Why Fame Often Survives Serious Allegations

An article looks at why famous men accused or convicted of serious abuse can keep their fans, or even attract more. It uses Russell Brand, Chris Brown, Diddy, and Conor McGregor as examples of the pattern.

### How Fandom Turns Controversy Into Profit

The piece argues that loyal fandoms, the incentives built into online platforms, and normalized misogyny can combine to make allegations work as fuel. Attention, whether positive or negative, can be turned into income, so controversy does not always damage a public figure's standing.

### Why the Debate Is Back

Russell Brand's sentencing has renewed questions about accountability and justice for survivors. It has also raised the issue of whether public consequences really follow legal ones. In an attention economy, controversy can still bring followers, ticket sales, views, and monetization, which keeps the question urgent.
