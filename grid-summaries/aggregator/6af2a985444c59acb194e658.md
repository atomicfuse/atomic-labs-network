## Study Links Wildlife Imports to Higher Odds of Zoonotic Outbreaks

A global analysis spanning 39 years and 214 countries has found that nations importing more wildlife face greater odds of zoonotic emerging infectious disease outbreaks, meaning illnesses that jump from animals to people.

### What the research found

The study points to import activity as the stronger signal for spillover risk from animals to humans. It did not find the same pattern for exports, and the link does not appear to be explained by general reporting bias. In other words, countries that bring in larger volumes of wildlife show a clearer association with outbreaks than the other factors examined.

### Why it matters now

Preventing future pandemics remains a major public health priority, and wildlife trade is drawing growing scrutiny as a possible route for novel pathogens. The findings offer policymakers a clearer focus: tighter controls at wildlife import points.
