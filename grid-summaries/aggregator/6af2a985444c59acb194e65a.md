## Light-Pulse Pairs Capture Femtosecond Collapse of a Xenon Core Hole

Researchers have directly observed how quickly an inner-shell vacancy in a xenon atom decays, using paired extreme ultraviolet light pulses that are phase-coherent with each other.

### How the decay was seen

The team applied photoelectron wave-packet interferometry to xenon's 4d core hole. As the delay between the two pulses changed, the contrast of the resulting interference fringes faded, tracing the decay of the hole on a femtosecond timescale. Spin-orbit beating was also visible, adding a second signature of the ultrafast inner-shell dynamics. Both effects were read out with ordinary electron spectroscopy.

### Why it matters

Ultrafast atomic and molecular imaging is moving beyond specialized attosecond streaking setups. This work offers a synchrotron-based alternative for tracking electron dynamics. It points toward more accessible ways to study core-hole behavior in molecules, materials, and condensed matter systems.
