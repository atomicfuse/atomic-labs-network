## Maple Leaves Reveal Cold War Nuclear Echo in Gatineau Park

A 65-year record of sugar maple leaves from Gatineau Park has traced how Cold War nuclear weapons testing left a radiocarbon "bomb pulse" in the Ottawa-area biosphere, one that lingered until very recently.

### A Two-Year Lag in the Leaves

The study found that new leaves carry carbon stored from previous years. This creates a lag of about two years in their radiocarbon signal, meaning the foliage does not reflect the atmosphere of its own growing season alone.

### Why the Timing Matters

Atmospheric radiocarbon has only recently dropped below pre-bomb levels, which makes this a timely look at how mid-20th-century nuclear activity still echoes in local ecosystems. The findings also matter for climate and environmental science, because fossil fuel emissions are now diluting radiocarbon signals in the atmosphere.
