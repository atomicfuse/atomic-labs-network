## Invasive Heart Procedures Show No Long-Term Survival Edge in Stable Disease

A long-term follow-up of the ISCHEMIA trial has found that early invasive treatment for stable heart disease does not help patients live longer than starting with medication and lifestyle changes.

### What the follow-up found

Patients with stable heart disease who received early procedures such as stents or bypass surgery did not survive longer than those treated first with drugs and lifestyle changes. This held true over nearly a decade. The procedures may still ease chest pain and improve quality of life, but survival was not improved.

### Why it matters now

The results challenge decades of routine intervention for stable coronary disease. They arrive as cardiology moves toward more personalized, evidence-based decision-making. For patients, the findings offer timely context when weighing the benefits of symptom relief against the risks that come with procedures.
