## Accidental Finding Lets Molybdenum Recrystallize at Room Temperature

Researchers have stumbled on a way to reshape the crystal structure of molybdenum without heating it. The surprise result could change how engineers handle a metal valued for demanding applications.

### What the researchers found

By pressing molybdenum precisely with a diamond-tipped nanoindenter, the team found its crystal structure can recrystallize at room temperature. The discovery was unplanned. It could make targeted repair of defects possible, and it may open new uses for molybdenum in advanced chips, aerospace, nuclear systems, and other high-performance materials.

### Why the timing matters

Demand from AI is pushing for smaller, faster, and more efficient microchips, and semiconductor makers are looking beyond copper for next-generation interconnects. Molybdenum shows promise at nanoscale dimensions. However, controlling its brittleness and its microstructure has been a major barrier, which is the problem this finding may help address.
