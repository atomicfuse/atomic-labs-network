## Single amino acid swap changes how Andes virus protein behaves in human cells

Researchers at Mount Sinai have identified a tiny difference between Andes virus isolates that appears to matter inside human cells. The finding centers on one amino acid that separates versions taken from rodents from those taken from humans.

### What the researchers found

The single amino acid difference changes how a key viral glycoprotein travels through human cells. It also affects how quickly virus-like particles are released from those cells. The work shows that even a minimal change in a viral protein can alter how the virus behaves once it is in a human host.

### Why it matters now

Andes virus stands out among hantaviruses because it can spread from person to person. Recent outbreaks have renewed attention on how small viral changes might shape infection, transmission, and disease behavior. The new result adds a specific example of such a change.
