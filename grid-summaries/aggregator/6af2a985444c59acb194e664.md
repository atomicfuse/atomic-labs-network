## Most couples with unexplained infertility have a baby within two years, trial finds

A New Zealand trial of 737 couples with unexplained infertility has found that nearly three in four had a baby within two years of beginning treatment. The result held whether couples started with intrauterine insemination (IUI) or moved straight to in vitro fertilisation (IVF).

### How the two treatments compared

IVF produced pregnancies faster and performed better than researchers had expected. Even so, the long-term picture was much the same for both approaches, with similar live birth outcomes by the end of the two-year period. Starting with IUI did not appear to lower a couple's overall chances of having a baby.

### Why timing and access matter

Demand for infertility care is climbing, yet public funding and access have not kept pace. That gap leads to long waits, which can reduce the odds of success as patients get older. The findings give couples and policymakers new evidence for weighing treatment choices and timing, and they strengthen the case for earlier access to care.
