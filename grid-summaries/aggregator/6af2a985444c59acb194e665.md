## Methane-Eating Microbes Grow Electric Nanowires on Electrodes

Researchers have shown that ANME archaea, microbes that consume methane, can grow on electrodes and form electrically conductive nanowires. These tiny structures carry electrons as an electric current.

### What the researchers achieved

The team also produced a culture that is 95% pure ANME. That level of purity makes it easier for scientists to study how these microbes turn methane into electricity. Growing them directly on electrodes shows that the organisms can pass electrons along their nanowires and into a circuit.

### Why it matters now

Pressure is rising to find low-emission, energy-efficient ways to use methane and generate power, and microbial electricity production is becoming an appealing long-term area of research. The finding hints at a possible alternative to conventional electrolysis. Practical applications, however, may still be decades away.
