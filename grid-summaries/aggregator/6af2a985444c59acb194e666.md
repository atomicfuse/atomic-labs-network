## Roman Frog Graveyard Mystery Solved by Scientists

Researchers have worked out why thousands of frog bones ended up in an ancient ditch near the Fen edge. The likely culprits were birds of prey rather than people.

### Who ate the frogs?

About 8,000 frog bones were found in a Roman-era ditch. Scientists studied the bones, used radiocarbon dating, and examined environmental evidence. Their conclusion is that short-eared owls or hen harriers probably ate the frogs. Changing wetlands and deforestation seem to have produced a seasonal frog feast for these predators.

### Why small bones matter

The study shows how tiny animal remains can reveal larger stories about ancient landscapes, climate, wildlife behavior, and human land use. It also arrives as public interest grows in archaeology that reconstructs everyday life, looking beyond kings, battles, and monuments.
