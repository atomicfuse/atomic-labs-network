## Cleaner Air Could Spare Over 9 Million Lives a Year, Study Finds

Researchers at Cambridge have estimated the human toll of air pollution across 136 low- and middle-income countries, and the numbers are striking. Their modeling suggests that meeting World Health Organization guidelines could transform public health in these nations.

### What the Modeling Found

The team modeled reductions in two pollutants, PM₂.₅ and NO₂, and concluded that hitting WHO guideline levels could prevent or postpone more than 9 million deaths every year. The study also points to substantial avoided illness, savings in healthcare costs, and gains in productivity that would come with cleaner air.

### Why the Timing Matters

Urbanization, vehicle use, and reliance on fossil fuels are all growing in developing economies, making air quality a central public health and economic concern. Governments are currently weighing decisions on transport, energy, and city planning. Those choices could lock in either cleaner growth or worsening pollution.
