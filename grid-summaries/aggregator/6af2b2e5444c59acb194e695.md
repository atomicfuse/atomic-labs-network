## Democratic Gains Revive Push to Change How the US Elects Presidents

New Democratic control in several state governments is bringing fresh attention to a long-running plan to shift the presidency toward the national popular vote winner.

### How the compact would work

The National Popular Vote Interstate Compact would take effect once participating states together hold 270 electoral votes. At that point, those states would effectively award the presidency to whoever wins the national popular vote. Recent Democratic wins in key states such as Pennsylvania, Wisconsin, Arizona, Michigan, and Nevada could bring that threshold within reach.

### Obstacles and timing

The path is not clear. Some Democrats remain hesitant, Republicans oppose the idea, and constitutional challenges are likely. Still, the 2028 election is approaching, and frustration with the Electoral College, tied to the outcomes of 2000 and 2016, is keeping the debate on how America picks its presidents newly urgent.
