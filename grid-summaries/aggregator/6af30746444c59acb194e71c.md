## Oxford Museum Hands Back 39 Naga Ancestral Remains to India

Oxford's Pitt Rivers Museum has returned the ancestral remains of 39 Naga people, most of them skulls, to India. The remains were taken during British colonial rule, and the handover is being presented as a significant moment for decolonisation and repatriation.

### What is happening to the remains

The returned remains are slated for burial under a joint memorial in Nagaland. The plan marks a move from museum custody to interment in the region the Naga people come from, according to the account of the handover.

### Why the return matters now

Museums around the world face growing pressure to give back human remains and cultural objects acquired through colonial violence or coercion. This case also points to the widening role played by India and by indigenous communities in reclaiming ancestral heritage held by Western institutions.
