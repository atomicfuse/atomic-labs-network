## Language Gaps Linked to Unequal Headache Care After Childbirth

New research suggests that new mothers with limited English proficiency are less likely to receive standard treatment for headaches that can follow an epidural. The findings point to language barriers as a possible factor in uneven postpartum care.

### What the research found

The article highlights evidence that mothers who have limited English skills are treated differently when they develop post-epidural headaches. Specifically, they are less likely to be given the standard treatment for the condition. Researchers identify language barriers as a potential driver of this gap, suggesting that communication difficulties may play a role in whether patients receive the care typically offered.

### Why it matters now

Maternal health equity is drawing growing scrutiny, and hospitals are working to reduce preventable complications and disparities in postpartum outcomes. Language access stands out as a practical gap that can be fixed, and it can directly affect patient safety. Closing it is one concrete way for hospitals to address unequal care for new mothers.
