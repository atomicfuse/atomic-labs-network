## Seventh Straight Change Election Points to Anti-Incumbent Mood

An article argues that the latest Democratic wave fits a run of "change elections," in which voters keep handing power to the opposition or asking for a new direction.

### What the Article Argues

Its central claim is that control of Congress reflects a broader anti-incumbent mood, not simply enthusiasm for one party. In this view, the Democratic gains are less a lasting endorsement and more the newest example of voters rejecting whoever currently holds power. The piece counts this as the seventh consecutive election of that kind.

### Why It Matters Now

Repeated change elections suggest deep voter dissatisfaction and instability in American politics. That framing helps explain why electoral mandates feel short-lived and why backlash politics keeps dominating the national conversation. For creators, it offers a timely lens for explaining those patterns to audiences who want to understand why power keeps shifting.
