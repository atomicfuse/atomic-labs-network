## Microplastics Detected in Blood of Remote Brazilian Wildlife

A study published in Nature reports that microplastics have turned up in the blood of wild lowland tapirs and giant anteaters in Brazil. Some of the animals lived more than 40 miles from human infrastructure.

### What the Researchers Found

Microplastics were present in the blood of both species, even in animals far from roads, buildings and other developed areas. The research ties higher microplastic burdens to greater human disturbance in the animals' surroundings and to closeness to waste facilities. Distance from people did not guarantee a plastic-free bloodstream.

### Why It Matters

Microplastics are increasingly appearing well beyond cities, which raises concerns for wildlife health and conservation in regions that are developing quickly. The study offers rare real-world evidence that land use and waste management directly shape how much pollution land-dwelling mammals are exposed to.
