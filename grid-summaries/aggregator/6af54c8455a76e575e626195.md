## Fungus That Preys on Powdery Mildew Is More Common Than Thought

A new study from NC State shows that Ampelomyces, a fungus that parasitizes powdery mildew, turns up more widely than scientists realized. It also flags problems with how the fungus is recorded in genetic databases.

### A Wider Reach, and Mislabeled Data

Researchers found Ampelomyces in about 7% of the herbarium specimens they analyzed, suggesting it is far more widespread than previously known. The work also revealed that many public DNA database entries labeled as Ampelomyces are misidentified. That raises concerns about errors cascading through scientific datasets that draw on those records.

### Why the Findings Matter Now

Agriculture is seeking more precise and sustainable ways to manage plant diseases, and hyperparasites like Ampelomyces could become valuable biological control tools. At the same time, researchers increasingly rely on massive open genetic databases, which makes the study's emphasis on better data quality especially timely.
