## New Test Listens to Neurons to Spot Water Risks Others Miss

Researchers have described a testing method called iFNT that detects neurotoxic effects in water which conventional monitoring can overlook. Instead of examining cell structure, it tracks the electrical activity of neurons grown in the lab.

### How the method performed

The in vitro functional neurotoxicity approach "listens" to neuronal signals to pick up harmful effects that structural assays and standard water-quality indicators fail to register. In comparisons, researchers found it was far more sensitive than neurite outgrowth testing. That advantage held for both laboratory chemicals and real wastewater samples.

### Why it matters now

Water systems increasingly contend with complex mixtures of pharmaceuticals, pesticides, industrial chemicals, and unidentified byproducts. Traditional monitoring may not capture subtle biological risks from such combinations. The study points toward effect-based testing, which measures how contaminants actually affect living systems rather than only what is present in the water.
