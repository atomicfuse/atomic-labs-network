## Engineered "living medicine" cuts biofilms on ventilator tubes in pigs

A genetically engineered "living medicine" delivered through a nebulizer reduced drug-resistant Pseudomonas aeruginosa biofilms on ventilator tubes in pigs by about 40%. The result points to a possible new tactic against ventilator-associated pneumonia.

### How the approach works

The treatment uses reprogrammed lung bacteria, inhaled as a mist, to attack biofilms growing on breathing tubes. Biofilms are a major obstacle because antibiotics often struggle to penetrate them. The study suggests that engineered bacteria could offer a way around that barrier, tackling infections that standard drugs have difficulty reaching.

### Why the timing matters

Antibiotic resistance is escalating in hospitals, and patients on ventilators are especially vulnerable to stubborn infections tied to their devices. The pig results move the approach closer to human safety trials, since pig lungs are more comparable to human lungs than the mouse models often used in earlier research.
