## Skipping Morning Blood Pressure Pills Cuts Low-Pressure Episodes in Older Surgical Patients

A trial published in the BMJ has found that older adults who held back their usual blood pressure medication before surgery were less likely to experience a dangerous drop in pressure on the operating table.

### What the trial found

Participants were older adults having noncardiac surgery who normally take an ACE inhibitor or an ARB. Those who skipped the morning dose on the day of their operation had fewer episodes of dangerously low blood pressure during the procedure. The results support a simple strategy: leave out the morning dose when low pressure during surgery is a concern.

### Why it matters now

More operations now involve adults over 60, and clinicians want clearer direction on handling common medications safely before anesthesia. Guidelines in this area have been uncertain, and the trial adds stronger evidence to help settle the question.
