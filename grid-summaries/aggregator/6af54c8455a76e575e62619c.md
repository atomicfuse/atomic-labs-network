## Experts Warn Drug Firms' Anti-Stigma Obesity Campaigns May Serve Commercial Goals

Experts writing in The BMJ say pharmaceutical campaigns aimed at reducing the stigma around obesity can be scientifically sound while also serving a company's commercial strategy. They point to Eli Lilly's U.K. "Obesity Is a Disease" campaign as a key example.

### The Core Concern

The authors' main worry is that messaging built on compassion could steer obesity policy toward clinical treatment centered on drugs. In their view, that shift risks drawing attention away from the broader social and environmental causes of obesity, which would then receive less focus in public debate and policymaking.

### Why It Matters Now

New obesity drugs such as tirzepatide are changing public conversation, medical practice and market incentives around weight loss. As drug companies move into stigma-reduction messaging, regulators and public health leaders are under pressure to decide where education ends and indirect promotion begins.
