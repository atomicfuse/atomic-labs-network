## Electoral College workaround nears threshold as an early champion wavers

Democrats' growing control of state governments could push the National Popular Vote Interstate Compact past the 270-electoral-vote mark. Crossing that line would effectively swap the Electoral College for a national popular vote.

### A plan moves from theory toward reality

Newly Democratic-controlled governments in key swing states have made the compact's final push look plausible before 2028. The timing matters because partisan distrust of elections runs deep, and the current system has twice recently delivered the presidency to candidates who lost the popular vote. Those outcomes have kept scrutiny of the Electoral College high.

### An architect has second thoughts

Legal scholar Akhil Reed Amar was an early intellectual force behind the idea. He now fears the compact could create serious problems with counting votes and with the legitimacy of election results. His doubts add a notable twist as the plan edges closer to success.
