## Candidates Who Beat Back White Grievance Politics

An article argues that several Democratic candidates, many of them Black and running in Southern or swing-state races, weakened Republican attacks built on white grievance and racial fearmongering. It points to wins and near-wins by Abdul El-Sayed, Angie Nixon, Keisha Lance Bottoms, Jamie Davis, and Scott Colom.

### Class-Focused Campaigns

The piece presents these results as evidence that campaigns centered on class can cross racial and partisan lines. According to the article, the candidates stressed everyday economic concerns rather than answering culture-war messaging on its own terms, and voters responded in ways that undercut the appeal of racial fear as a campaign tool.

### Why It Matters Now

The article says Trump-aligned politics is leaning harder into claims of white victimhood. Against that backdrop, it suggests cost of living, corruption, health care, and local economic issues may be outweighing culture-war messaging. The argument arrives amid renewed fights over voting rights, federal layoffs, D.E.I., and efforts to mobilize Black voters.
