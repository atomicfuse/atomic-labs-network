## Germany expands spy agencies' powers to counter foreign hybrid threats

Germany's parliament has approved a major overhaul of its intelligence services, giving the BND and the BfV new tools to confront espionage, terrorism, sabotage and disinformation from abroad.

### What the law changes

The reform grants both agencies new powers for covert action, cyber operations, surveillance and protective interventions. Supporters describe the changes as essential for building resilience against foreign "hybrid threats." Critics, however, warn that the law erodes the limits placed on secret-service power after the Second World War.

### Why the timing matters

Germany says it faces intensified threats from Russia, China and Iran. These include suspected sabotage linked to the war in Ukraine, as well as cyber and disinformation campaigns. The law arrives amid a wider European debate over how far democracies should expand their security powers without weakening civil liberties.
