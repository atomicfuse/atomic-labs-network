## Alaska Voters Keep Open Primaries and Ranked Choice Voting

Alaska voters have turned down a ballot measure that sought to scrap the state's open primary system and its ranked choice voting for general elections. The outcome leaves the current election model in place.

### What Voters Decided

The measure would have repealed two linked features of Alaska's election setup: the open primary and the use of ranked choice voting in general elections. By rejecting it, voters chose to preserve the reform model the state already uses, rather than return to the arrangement that existed before it.

### Why the Result Matters

Ranked choice voting and open primaries are drawing national attention as states debate election access, polarization, and voter choice. Alaska's result offers a timely signal for both reform advocates and opponents. Many are watching to see whether these systems can withstand backlash, and this vote shows that, at least in Alaska, they have survived a direct challenge at the ballot box.
