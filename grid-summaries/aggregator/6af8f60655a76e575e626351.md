## Measles Status Loss Puts Health Secretary Under Fire

The United States has reportedly lost its measles-elimination status after 26 years, prompting sharp criticism of Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. The development has quickly become a flashpoint in debates over public health leadership.

### Who is being blamed

Lawmakers and health professionals have pointed to vaccine misinformation, declining trust in public-health institutions, and the Trump administration's leadership as factors behind the disease's resurgence. Kennedy has drawn much of the scrutiny, with critics tying his role to the reported setback and questioning the direction of national disease prevention.

### Why it matters now

Measles cases and deaths are increasingly described as a preventable failure of public health, arriving at a time when confidence in vaccines has become politically polarized. The reported loss of elimination status has turned what was largely a medical concern into a broader fight over accountability, with Kennedy's responsibilities and the future of disease prevention in the United States at the center of the argument.
