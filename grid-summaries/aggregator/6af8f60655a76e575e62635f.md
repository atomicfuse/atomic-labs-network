## Ex-Joint Staff Chief Says Japan Must Hold U.S. Close While Building Its Own Strength

Yoshihide Yoshida, a former chief of Japan's Joint Staff, has given an interview on how Japan should shape its security strategy. He speaks against a backdrop of great-power coercion, wars in Europe and the Middle East, and an uncertain political climate in the United States, which he describes as a "variable."

### Keeping Washington engaged

Yoshida argues that Japan needs to make sure the United States stays involved in the Indo-Pacific. At the same time, he says Tokyo should reinforce its own defenses and deepen its alliances. He frames this approach as "values-based realism."

### Why the timing matters

Japan is getting ready to revise key security documents, and concern is rising over China, Russia, North Korea and a more transactional United States. The interview speaks to a central regional question: how can Japan deter conflict without giving up its defensive principles?
