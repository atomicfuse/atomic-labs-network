## Hormone Therapy and Brain Health: Timing and Genes May Matter

A new study in Neurology suggests that women with longer lifetime estrogen exposure, including those who reach menopause later, and those who used menopausal hormone therapy (MHT) show healthier brain markers in older age. The findings add detail to an ongoing debate, though MHT has not been proven to prevent dementia.

### What the Study Found

Researchers linked longer estrogen exposure and MHT use with fewer signs of brain small-vessel disease and better white matter integrity in older women. The article stresses that the benefits may depend on when therapy is used, on APOE-ε4 genetics, and on each woman's individual health factors, so the results should not be read as a guarantee of protection.

### Why It Matters Now

Women account for about two-thirds of Alzheimer's cases worldwide, and menopause is increasingly viewed as a brain-health transition as well as a reproductive one. With interest in personalized hormone therapy growing, the study offers nuance about when MHT may help, while showing where the evidence still falls short.
