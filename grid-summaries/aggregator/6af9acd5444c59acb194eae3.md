## A New Obesity Definition Looks Beyond BMI

A large international study says body mass index alone can misclassify obesity. Its authors back a broader approach that pairs BMI with other measures to gauge who is truly at risk.

### What the study proposes

The research argues that adding waist measurements and an assessment of organ function identifies health risk more accurately than BMI by itself. It supports a new framework that splits obesity into two categories. Clinical obesity describes an active illness, while preclinical obesity describes a state of elevated risk of developing problems in the future.

### Why it matters now

Obesity drugs, surgery, and prevention strategies are becoming more widespread, which increases pressure to decide who really needs intensive treatment. The framework could move the conversation away from simple weight labels and toward individualized health risk, giving clinicians a way to distinguish between people who are already ill and those who may become so.
