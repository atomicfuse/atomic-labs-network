## Indian start-ups turn AI toward everyday civic problems

An article on India's AI boom argues that its energy lies less in building frontier models and more in practical tools aimed at local frustrations. Firms such as Landeed and Fractal Analytics, along with pothole-detection apps, are applying AI to land records, healthcare, roads and public services.

### Built for messy data and many languages

The piece says these downstream applications are designed around fragmented data, multilingual users and low-cost markets. Rather than competing on the largest models, the start-ups focus on hyperlocal problems where information is scattered and customers cannot pay much. Landeed's work on land records and the road-focused apps show how that approach looks in practice.

### Funding is rising, but obstacles remain

The story is timely because AI funding in India is surging, even as start-ups face GPU shortages, weak paths to commercialization and government pilots that are hard to scale. India is trying to turn AI into public infrastructure while competing with better-funded ecosystems in the US and China.
