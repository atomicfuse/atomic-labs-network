## Planets and Stars Become Dark Matter Traps in New Search

Researchers have examined whether slow-moving dark matter particles might be captured by large celestial bodies, including Earth, the sun, Jupiter and white dwarfs. The study found no direct evidence of such capture, yet it still yielded useful results.

### How the Search Worked

The team relied on two main tools: searches for neutrinos and measurements of heat. Both were used to look for hints that captured dark matter might be leaving a signature inside these bodies. Because nothing conclusive turned up, the findings were used to rule out certain possible properties that dark matter particles might have, narrowing the field of candidates.

### Why It Matters

Dark matter remains one of the biggest unsolved mysteries in physics, and traditional detectors continue to come up empty. This approach broadens the hunt by treating planets and stars as natural laboratories for dark matter. Instead of depending only on instruments built for the purpose, scientists can use these huge objects as additional places to test ideas about what dark matter is.
