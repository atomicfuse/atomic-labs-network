## Study says GP-led adult ADHD care needs training and funding

University of Queensland researchers argue that letting GPs diagnose and treat adults with ADHD will only work if it comes with mandatory ADHD-specific training and stronger Medicare funding.

### What the research highlights

The study looks at Queensland's GP-led model and cautions that adult ADHD assessments are complex and take a lot of time. Without proper support, the researchers warn, the approach could become inequitable, with some patients better placed than others to get care.

### Why the timing matters

Demand for ADHD care is rising, and Australian states are actively reshaping who can diagnose and treat adults. Queensland stands out because GP training in this area remains voluntary. That raises timely concerns about the quality of assessments, how easily people can access them, and whether patients can afford them.
