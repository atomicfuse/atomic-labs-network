## Flipped Atomic Layers Could Steer Heat in 3D Chips

Researchers in Japan have proposed a technique called "flipping engineering" to manage heat in stacked computer chips. It involves arranging Janus atomic layers either upside down or right side up.

### What the researchers did

The team stacked Janus layers in different orientations to control how heat travels through the material. Using machine learning, they identified sequences of MoSSe layers that conduct heat strongly along the chip while blocking its movement between layers. In effect, one structure can behave like a radiator in one direction and an insulator in another.

### Why it matters now

Chipmakers are moving toward stacked 3D circuits to keep computing performance rising, and heat buildup is becoming a major bottleneck. Materials that can both spread and block heat could prove crucial for AI hardware, memory stacks, and next-generation semiconductors.
