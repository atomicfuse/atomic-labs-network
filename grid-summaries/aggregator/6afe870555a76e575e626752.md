## Cochrane review casts doubt on turmeric for knee arthritis pain

A new Cochrane review concludes that turmeric supplements may provide little or no clinically meaningful pain relief for people with knee osteoarthritis. The finding challenges earlier studies that pointed to a benefit.

### What the review found

Reviewers examined the evidence on turmeric for knee osteoarthritis and found that any pain relief may be too small to matter in practice. They also stressed that the quality and trustworthiness of the underlying trials may have distorted conclusions drawn in the past. In other words, the earlier positive signals may not be as reliable as they appeared, which complicates the case for using the supplement to ease knee symptoms.

### Why it matters now

Turmeric remains a popular natural remedy for joint pain, and shoppers are increasingly seeking supplement advice grounded in evidence. The review lands amid wider scrutiny of wellness claims and of how reliable clinical research really is. For consumers weighing whether to spend money on turmeric, it adds a cautionary note about what the current evidence can support.
