## Stool Test Cutoff May Need Rethinking for Younger Adults

A University of Nottingham-led review has examined how well faecal immunochemical tests (FIT) detect colorectal cancer in symptomatic patients under 50. It pooled 12 studies covering more than 69,000 people.

### What the review found

The analysis concluded that FIT stool tests do work for detecting colorectal cancer in this younger group. However, the standard cutoff used to decide who gets referred may not suit younger adults. The authors argue for age-aware thresholds or personalised risk models, which they say could help prioritise colonoscopy referrals more effectively.

### Why it matters now

Early-onset colorectal cancer is rising globally. That increases pressure to improve diagnosis without subjecting patients to unnecessary invasive tests or overloading health systems. As screening and symptom-testing policies continue to evolve, the research challenges the idea that a single cutoff can serve every age group.
