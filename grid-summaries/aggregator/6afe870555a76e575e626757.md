## Synchronized Ions Produce a New Style of Frequency Comb

Researchers at Caltech and Stanford report that ytterbium ions in a crystal, when excited by a laser, can fall into step with one another and emit regular pulses of light. The effect, called superradiance, yields a new kind of frequency comb.

### How the comb forms

The team found that the excited ions synchronize through superradiance, releasing rhythmic pulses. These pulses create a frequency comb in both microwave and optical light. The finding points to a potentially more stable, programmable light source for precision measurement and quantum networking.

### Why the timing matters

Frequency combs underpin atomic clocks, sensing, spectroscopy, and ultrafast science. Quantum computing, meanwhile, urgently needs better ways to link microwave-based superconducting devices with long-distance optical communication. Because this comb spans both microwave and optical light, the discovery offers a timely bridge between ultra-stable photonics and scalable quantum networks.
