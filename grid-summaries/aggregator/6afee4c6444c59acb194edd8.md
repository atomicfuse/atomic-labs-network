## Digital Infrastructure Giants Raise a Question of Democratic Accountability

An article argues that technology companies supplying critical infrastructure, such as cloud services, AI, satellite internet and data systems, can gain real political power once governments come to rely on them. Democracies, it suggests, may need stronger ways to hold such firms to account.

### Lessons From Ukraine and Postwar Reforms

To make its case, the piece points to Starlink's role in Ukraine as an example of a private firm embedded in public authority. It also looks back at corporate reforms carried out in Germany and Japan after the war, using them to suggest that checks on private power have precedent and could inform current thinking.

### Why the Timing Matters

Governments are racing to secure advantages in AI, cloud computing and defense technology, and many are outsourcing core state functions to a small group of private companies. That shifts dependence on these firms beyond a market concern. The article frames it as a matter of democratic governance and national security.
