## A Roman General's Boast Becomes a Warning on Power and Law

A column from Patrick's Politics takes aim at Marco Rubio's comparison of American defiance of the International Criminal Court to Pompey's famous line about "men with swords." The author argues the comparison exposes a troubling "might makes right" outlook.

### The Lesson of Pompey

The piece looks at Pompey's rise and eventual downfall to make its case. In the author's telling, his story shows that power untethered from legal restraint is unstable and ultimately anti-democratic. Relying on force over rules, the column suggests, carries long-term risks for the very leaders who embrace it.

### Why It Matters Now

The article links the argument to current U.S. hostility toward the ICC, including sanctions and ongoing debates over accountability for war crimes. It presents these moves as part of a wider rejection of international law. The column sees this as timely because American foreign policy rhetoric increasingly casts military strength as superior to legal norms.
