## Why Revolutionary Victories on the Streets Rarely Bring Lasting Power

A new article examines a persistent pattern in political upheaval: revolutions frequently topple governments, yet the broad coalitions that made those victories possible seldom end up in charge.

### Lessons from Five Revolutions

Drawing on France, Russia, Iran, Egypt, and Tunisia, the piece shows that the moment a regime falls is not the moment the question of power is settled. What counts afterward is who has organization, institutions, military strength, and social networks. Those assets, more than the size or passion of the crowds, determine who governs once the streets have cleared.

### Why It Matters Today

Protest movements and anti-establishment politics continue to shape events around the world, and the article offers a way to read them. Removing a leader, it argues, is only the first stage of political change. Revolutionary energy is often overtaken by disciplined parties, militaries, or entrenched state institutions, which helps explain why many uprisings end in outcomes their original supporters never intended.
