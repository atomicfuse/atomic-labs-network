## South Korea's Constitutional Reform Push Stalls on Political Distrust

South Korea's effort to amend its constitution has hit a wall. The opposition is refusing to back any changes while President Lee Jae Myung is still in office, leaving the reform bid stuck.

### The Standoff

At the center of the impasse is a lack of trust between political camps. Opposition lawmakers will not support amendments during Lee's time as president, which effectively freezes the process. The dispute is less about the details of any proposed changes and more about whether the parties can work together at all. Without cross-party backing, the reform drive cannot move forward.

### Why It Matters Now

Constitutional reform is a high-stakes matter in South Korea's polarized political climate, and major change depends on a baseline of trust between parties. The current standoff shows how personal and partisan conflict can derail efforts to improve governance. Until that distrust eases, institutional change looks difficult to achieve, and the question of reform remains tied to the broader rivalry shaping national politics.
