## Essay Asks Why Outrage Over the Cornell Lawsuit Seems to Need Exaggeration

An essay on public reaction to the Cornell sexual assault lawsuit questions why some people feel the story must be made worse than it is. It argues that anger over the case should not hinge on embellished details or on presenting the survivor as flawless.

### A Consent Standard That Holds Up

The piece calls for a framework of consent that stays firm even when a woman's choices, desires, or circumstances are complicated. In its view, a survivor's credibility and the seriousness of an alleged assault should not rise or fall based on whether she fits an idealized image. Nuance, the essay suggests, need not weaken the principle itself.

### Why the Debate Matters Now

The Cornell case is turning into a proxy battle over rape culture, fraternity accountability, media narratives, and feminist credibility. As accounts travel across campuses and social platforms, the essay asks whether advocacy can embrace nuance without handing ground to victim-blaming.
