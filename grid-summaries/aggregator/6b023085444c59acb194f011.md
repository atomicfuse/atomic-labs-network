## Boston's Past Offers a Playbook for Today's City Politics

Boston's current battles over housing, development, affordability, and inequality echo long-running urban debates about whether growth can advance social justice. An article revisiting those debates finds guidance in the city's own history.

### Equity and flexibility

The article points to Ray Flynn's time as mayor and to Michelle Wu's adjustments to her housing policy as examples. Together, they suggest that effective city politics depends on two things at once: a firm commitment to equity and the practical willingness to adapt when circumstances demand it.

### Pressure on cities now

Rents, home prices, construction costs, and voter frustration are all climbing, and cities face growing pressure to show that growth benefits residents rather than only developers and investors. The piece ties Boston's housing debate to a wider national shift toward urban politics centered on affordability, making the city's experience relevant well beyond its borders.
