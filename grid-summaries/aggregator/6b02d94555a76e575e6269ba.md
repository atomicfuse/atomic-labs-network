## Evangelical loyalty could be a double-edged sword for the GOP

An article argues that white evangelical Christians remain the Republican Party's most dependable voting bloc, yet leaning too heavily on them may carry strategic risks. With 2028 on the horizon, the question is how the party builds a winning coalition.

### The risk of overreliance

According to the article, the GOP's dependence on white evangelicals could become a liability rather than an asset. Relying so much on one loyal group may make it harder for Republicans to rebuild the broader coalition they would need to win a presidential contest.

### Why the timing matters

Following the latest election, both parties are reassessing which voter groups could decide the next presidential map. The Republican path forward may depend on whether the party can keep evangelical turnout high while avoiding the alienation of swing voters in key states.
