## Structured Lifestyle Program Outperforms Self-Guided Plan in Brain Health Trial

The U.S. POINTER trial followed older adults at risk of cognitive decline and compared two approaches to healthier living. Its findings suggest that a structured program combining physical, mental, and social habits did more for heart and metabolic health than a self-guided plan.

### What the trial found

Participants in the structured program improved their cardiometabolic health more than those who set their own course. The gains covered body mass index, blood pressure, cholesterol, blood sugar, and resting heart rate. Improvements in these measures were linked to better cognition, tying the physical changes to mental performance.

### Why it matters now

Dementia risk is a growing public health concern, and this study adds stronger clinical-trial evidence that brain aging may be shaped by everyday, modifiable habits. It also reinforces the increasingly important connection between heart health and brain health.
