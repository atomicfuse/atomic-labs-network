## Carrots and shrimp become the latest route to a "natural" tan

Social media users are loading up on carotenoid-rich foods, and sometimes supplements, in pursuit of an orange-bronze glow. The trend goes by names such as carrotmaxxing, tomatomaxxing and shrimpmaxxing.

### What dermatologists say

Skin specialists note that the color change can be real and is usually harmless. However, the warm tint offers no protection against UV damage, so it is not a substitute for sunscreen or other sun safety. Supplements may also carry risks of their own, which makes them a less straightforward choice than simply eating the foods.

### Why it's catching on now

The trend is spreading across TikTok and wellness culture as people look for "natural" beauty hacks and winter glow-ups that avoid tanning beds and sun exposure. It also feeds into a wider conversation about supplement safety, health claims made by influencers and the spread of skincare misinformation.
