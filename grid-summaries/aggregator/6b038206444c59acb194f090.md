## Column Says Political Identity Is Often Performance

A new opinion column contends that political identity is frequently shaped by self-interest and image management rather than conviction. It points to Elizabeth Holmes, conservative cultural shifts, and "blue wave" social media reactions as illustrations of the trend.

### The Argument

The writer's central claim is that both public figures and ordinary people perform politics more than they practice real civic engagement. The examples span a high-profile individual, changes within conservative culture, and everyday online behavior. Together they are used to suggest that the display of a political stance can matter more than the work of acting on it.

### Why It Resonates Now

Political wins and losses increasingly play out as social media moments, and the column taps into frustration with performative activism and rigid party loyalty. Its timing coincides with renewed "blue wave" optimism, as well as wider skepticism about whether either party can deliver meaningful change.
