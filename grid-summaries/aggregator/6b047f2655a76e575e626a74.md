## Evers' Late-Term Commutations Mark a First Since 1995

Gov. Tony Evers has commuted 14 prison sentences as his time in office winds down, the first such commutations in Wisconsin since 1995. The article presents the decision as politically strategic, noting that it came after the governor's election.

### Rules, Parole and Backlash

The piece details who is eligible for commutation and what the changes mean for parole. It also covers the criticism from Republicans, who have pushed back on the decision. Criminal justice reform, parole and victim rights are quickly becoming flashpoints in state politics as a result.

### What Comes Next

Incoming Gov. David Crowley is set to inherit the commutation system as Evers leaves office, so the future of the process rests with him. Attention may sharpen around a Dec. 11 hearing, which includes Brendan Dassey's appeal and could bring more public scrutiny to the issue.
