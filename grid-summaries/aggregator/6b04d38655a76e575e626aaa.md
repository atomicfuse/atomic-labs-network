## Voters Tend to Blame the President's Party When Divided Government Stalls

A newsletter from the Politics Desk looks at how voters have typically reacted to divided government. It also previews the 2027 Southern governor's races, where Democrats hope to build on recent regional gains.

### Who gets the blame for gridlock

The analysis argues that recent elections show voters usually punish the president's party for dysfunction, rather than the party that won Congress in the midterms. That pattern suggests the White House, not the new congressional majority, often carries the political cost when Washington stalls.

### Why the timing matters

Democrats are poised to control Congress while Trump remains in the White House, so the next two years could test whether voters reward legislative results or blame the president for gridlock. The piece is timely because affordability, governance and positioning for 2028 are already shaping the post-election narrative.
