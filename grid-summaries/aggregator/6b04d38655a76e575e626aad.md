## Democrats' 2028 Race Opens With a Fight Over Dark Money

A new Democratic National Committee rule is already stirring up the party's next presidential contest. White House hopefuls would have to denounce dark-money support to qualify for primary debates, and the requirement is being treated as the first ideological clash of the 2028 cycle.

### Two camps, two worries

Progressives see the rule as a way to curb the influence of billionaires on politics and to signal that the party takes the issue seriously. Moderates, by contrast, fear the party could be undercutting its own candidates by limiting how they can compete. The disagreement reflects a familiar divide over how far Democrats should go in policing their own campaign tactics.

### Why it matters now

The primary is still taking shape, and most candidates have not yet declared, yet this rule has created the first visible fault line. It also arrives amid continuing frustration over super PACs and donor secrecy, and it raises the question of whether a party can succeed while rejecting the modern campaign finance system.
