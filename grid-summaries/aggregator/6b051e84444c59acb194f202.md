## Diranersen slows decline in early Alzheimer's, interim trial data suggest

Interim two-year results from the CELIA phase 2 trial indicate that diranersen, a gene-silencing therapy that targets tau and is given twice a year, slowed clinical decline in people with early Alzheimer's disease.

### What the trial found

The strongest signal came from the 60 mg dose, which slowed decline by 38% on the CDR-SB measure. It also produced larger cognitive benefits on some other measures. The results come from an interim analysis at two years, and the study is still at phase 2.

### Why it matters

The results were presented at CTAD 2026 and add momentum to a shift in Alzheimer's drug development away from amyloid and toward tau and other disease mechanisms. If phase 3 confirms the findings, a treatment needed only twice a year could change expectations around convenience, adherence and long-term management of the disease.
