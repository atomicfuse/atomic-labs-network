## Finch Genomes Reveal a Mosaic Behind Darwin's Diverse Beaks

A new study in Nature Communications reports that some Galápagos tree finches are genetic mosaics. The work sheds light on how the birds came to differ so much in form and diet.

### What the Study Found

Western large tree finches genetically resemble small tree finches almost everywhere in their genomes. The exception is a key supergene linked to larger beaks and bodies. According to the research, hybridization and natural selection can work together to produce distinct forms, different diets, and possible boundaries between species.

### Why It Matters Now

Genomics is reshaping how scientists define species, and Darwin's finches remain a live case study in evolution rather than only a textbook example. The findings add timely evidence that interbreeding can fuel adaptation instead of simply blurring the lines between species.
