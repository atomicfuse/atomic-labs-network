## A Small Subsystem Can Vouch for a Whole Quantum Device

Researchers have introduced a protocol that certifies global quantum properties by relying only on local measurements taken on a small subsystem. The properties covered include fidelity, entanglement, circuit complexity, and "magic."

### The key finding

The central result is that these properties can be verified with a constant number of experimental runs. That stands in contrast to approaches whose costs grow rapidly as the number of qubits increases. The protocol does not require full state tomography, which makes it a lighter-weight way to check what a quantum system is actually doing.

### Why it matters now

Quantum computers are scaling toward hundreds or even thousands of qubits, and confirming that they work as intended is turning into a major bottleneck. The new method points to a more practical route for testing noisy, real-world quantum devices as they grow in size.
