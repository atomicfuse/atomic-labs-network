## Fruit and fish in childhood tied to lower Crohn's risk

A large Scandinavian study has examined how diets in childhood and the teen years relate to the chance of developing inflammatory bowel disease later in life. The findings point to some foods that may matter, though they do not prove cause and effect.

### What the study found

Researchers followed a large group from Scandinavia and compared early eating habits with later IBD outcomes. Diets rich in fruit and fish were associated with a lower risk of Crohn's disease. In contrast, eating salty snacks during adolescence was associated with a higher risk of IBD overall. The article stresses that these are associations only.

### Why it matters now

IBD rates are climbing around the world as diets become more Westernized, which has made early-life nutrition a growing public health focus. Parents, teenagers and health creators are looking for practical ways to support gut health before disease risk appears, and this research adds to that conversation.
