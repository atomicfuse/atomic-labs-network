## Solar system's tail reshaped by the Sun's 11-year cycle, IBEX data show

A Southwest Research Institute-led study has used 14 years of data from NASA's IBEX spacecraft to track how the heliotail, the comet-like wake trailing the solar system, changes over time.

### What the data show

The researchers mapped four lobes within the heliotail. They found that solar wind activity, which rises and falls with the Sun's 11-year cycle, reshapes the emissions from these lobes and shifts their positions in a predictable way. The result gives a long-term view of how the solar system's outer boundary responds to changing solar behavior.

### Why it matters now

The study provides a baseline just as NASA's IMAP mission starts building on IBEX's work to map the heliosphere in finer detail. It also helps settle competing models of the solar system's boundary shape, as solar activity ramps up and fades.
