## Moderates Hold Back Most, Duke-Led Study of Students Finds

A study led by Duke researchers reports that politically moderate students are the group most likely to self-censor on campus. The result adds a new angle to ongoing discussion about who feels free to speak and who does not.

### What the study found

According to the findings, moderates are more inclined to keep their opinions to themselves than students elsewhere on the political spectrum. The main reasons identified are lower confidence in their own views and a sense that political issues matter less to them personally. In other words, the hesitation appears tied to uncertainty and distance from the topics, rather than to fear alone.

### Why it matters now

Campus speech and political polarization continue to be major cultural flashpoints. This finding complicates the common assumption that self-censorship is driven mainly by ideological minorities. It suggests that the students holding back may include those in the middle, whose voices are less often part of the debate.
