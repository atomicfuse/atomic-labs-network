## Community Groups Seen as Backbone of Democratic Life

A Nonprofit Quarterly article examines what political education really means and argues that community-based organizations play a central role in sustaining democracy. It looks at how these groups teach people about civic life, both deliberately and as a byproduct of their everyday work.

### Democratic Infrastructure at the Local Level

The piece contends that community-based organizations function as essential democratic infrastructure. They build trust among residents, meet material needs, and open pathways for collective action. Political education, it explains, can be an explicit practice or an implicit outcome of that work, a point made especially relevant by rising authoritarianism.

### Why the Moment Is Urgent

With voting rights, immigrant rights, and basic civil liberties under growing threat, the article says communities need more spaces where people can understand power, history, and their own role in shaping democracy. Community organizations are well placed to turn service provision into civic agency, at a time when democratic participation feels both fragile and urgent.
