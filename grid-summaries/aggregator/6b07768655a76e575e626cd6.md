## Alaska Pulls Out of Voter Data Deal With Justice Department

Alaska has terminated an agreement that let the Justice Department examine its voter rolls for possible noncitizens who might be removed. The move follows a recent election and comes amid wider debate over federal access to state voter information.

### What the state and critics say

State officials say the arrangement led to no voters being removed and that the Justice Department never raised any concerns. Critics, however, argue the decision may be connected to ongoing litigation and to the arrival of an incoming Democratic administration. The facts provided do not settle which explanation is correct, and the two accounts remain at odds.

### Why the timing matters

The decision arrives after Alaska certified a major election in which Democrats flipped both the governor's office and a U.S. Senate seat. It also comes as scrutiny grows over federal efforts to obtain state voter data. Courts across the country have largely rejected similar Justice Department demands, placing Alaska's exit within a broader pattern of resistance and legal pushback.
