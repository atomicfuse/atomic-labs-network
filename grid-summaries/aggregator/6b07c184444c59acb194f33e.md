## Is an Australian astronaut worth A$100 million?

An article examines the proposal for an ESA-backed mission to send Katherine Bennell-Pegg to space. It asks whether a likely cost of A$100 million or more can be justified, and how anyone should judge that.

### Weighing the benefits

Supporters point to symbolic, industrial and inspirational gains, including the chance to encourage interest in STEM. The article notes, though, that the returns remain unclear. It also says the mission aligns weakly with existing policy and would compete with other priorities in the space sector.

### Why the debate is live

The #FlyKatherine campaign has brought the question into public view at a time of cost-of-living pressure and limited funding for the national space agency. People are also asking again what an Australian space program should deliver. The outcome could set a precedent for how the country pays for prestige science, STEM inspiration and space industry development.
