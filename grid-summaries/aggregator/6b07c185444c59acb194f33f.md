## UMaine Lidar Sensors Sharpen Maine's View of Its Forests

A new aerial sensor system at the University of Maine is changing how forests across the state are mapped and monitored. Equipped with lidar, it produces detailed, geo-referenced data that supports both timber work and conservation.

### Sharper data for everyday tasks

The article argues that higher-resolution information makes jobs faster and more precise. Examples include spotting illegal dumping, measuring biomass, and planning timber harvests. Because the data is tied to exact locations, users can act on findings with greater confidence than they could with older approaches.

### Why it matters now

Forest managers face pressure to balance timber production, conservation, carbon accounting, and landscape change driven by climate. Airborne mapping offers universities, landowners, and conservation groups a practical way to monitor huge areas more efficiently than older imagery or satellites alone can manage.
