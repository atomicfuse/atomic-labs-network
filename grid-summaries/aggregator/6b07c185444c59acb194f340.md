## U.S. Stroke Death Rate Fell Since 2000, but Gains Are Uneven

CDC research finds that the age-adjusted stroke death rate among U.S. adults declined between 2000 and 2024, falling from 81.4 to 51.9 deaths per 100,000. The overall drop is notable, though the picture varies across groups and places.

### Where Progress Has Slipped

Recent increases appeared among younger adults, women, and some older adults. Rising rates were also seen among residents in parts of the Midwest and South, as well as in several other jurisdictions. These trends show that improvement has not been shared evenly across the population.

### Why the Findings Matter

Stroke prevention remains a major public health issue, and the recent stalls or reversals suggest gains are not reaching everyone equally. The findings arrive as health systems and creators focus on disparities, prevention, and post-pandemic shifts in chronic disease outcomes.
