## Saw-Like Slicing May Have Powered Theropod Feeding

New experiments suggest that meat-eating theropod dinosaurs may not have simply bitten straight down on their prey. Instead, they likely used curved, serrated teeth to drag backward through flesh, working much like a saw blade.

### What the experiments found

Researchers built a machine called DinoSAW to test how theropod teeth cut through meat. It showed that a diagonal pull-cutting motion needed about one-third less force than vertical puncturing. That finding supports the idea that the teeth were suited to slicing and tearing, so these animals may have fed in a way that resembles modern birds of prey and Komodo dragons.

### Why it matters now

The study offers a fresh view of how well-known predators such as Allosaurus and Velociraptor may have eaten. It also highlights how engineering tools can be used to test long-held assumptions about extinct animals, turning questions about dinosaur behavior into measurable experiments.
