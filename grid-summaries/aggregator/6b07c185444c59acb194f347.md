## Giant Deer Bones Hint Humans Reached Ireland Far Earlier Than Thought

New analysis of giant deer bones from Ballynamintra Cave suggests people may have lived in southern Ireland about 13,500 years ago. That would be roughly 1,000 years earlier than the current confirmed evidence.

### What the bones show

Researchers examining the remains found marks that may come from butchery and from splitting bones to reach the marrow. They also found that Ice Age layers inside the cave are still intact, and these deposits could contain further proof of early human activity. The bones therefore point to a possible human presence well before the accepted timeline.

### Why it matters now

If confirmed, the discovery could rewrite the story of Ireland's earliest people and link them more closely to hunter-gatherer movements across Britain and northwest Europe after the Ice Age. It also shows that museum collections once overlooked, along with Victorian cave sites thought to be emptied, may still yield major archaeological finds.
