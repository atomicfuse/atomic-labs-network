## Ancient Danish Pottery May Point to Ritual Use of Fermented Milk

New research in experimental archaeology suggests that fermented dairy products could have held symbolic weight for Neolithic communities in Denmark, going beyond their role as everyday food.

### What the Experiments Suggest

Corroded Neolithic pottery found in Danish passage tombs was likely used to ferment dairy, particularly fresh, unpasteurized milk. Based on this, the findings argue that fermented milk products may have carried symbolic or ritual power in funerary practices, linking a familiar food to ideas about death.

### Why It Matters Now

The study adds fresh evidence that early European farming communities used fermentation for more than preservation and digestion. It points to a role in making meaning around death and ritual. The work also taps into current fascination with ancient diets, gut health, fermentation, and the social lives of everyday objects.
