## ASU Team Builds Tiny DNA Cubes That Assemble Themselves Into 3D Structures

Researchers at Arizona State University have created a programmable system of DNA "cubes" that come together on their own to form complex three-dimensional nanostructures, much like blocks in a sandbox game.

### How the cube system works

The team used computational design to cut down on assembly errors and to reduce how many unique building blocks are required. The work shows that large DNA structures can be made this way, and that the resulting shapes can be reconfigured. The researchers point to possible foundations for nanorobots and three-dimensional nanoelectronics.

### Why the timing matters

Nanotechnology is moving toward programmable materials, molecular robotics, and extremely small electronics, and reliable self-assembly remains a major bottleneck. This advance suggests that complex nanoscale objects could be designed in a way that resembles writing software, then produced through chemistry.
