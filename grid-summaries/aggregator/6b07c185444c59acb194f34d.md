## Peatland drainage leaves a lasting mark on global warming

New research led by MIT estimates that draining peatlands around the world has driven about 2.7% of the warming attributed to human activity today. The finding highlights how land-use choices made long ago can continue shaping the climate.

### A burden that lasts for centuries

The article explains that once peatlands are drained, the exposed peat can release carbon dioxide for hundreds of years. That means the climate impact of drainage does not end when the work is done. Instead, it builds into a long-lasting legacy of warming that persists well beyond the original land conversion.

### Why the findings matter now

Peatlands cover only a small share of the planet's land, yet they hold more carbon than all forests combined. Even so, their role is often underappreciated. As restoration and land-use policy move toward the centre of climate strategy, the study strengthens the argument for stopping further drainage and rewetting peatlands that have already been degraded.
