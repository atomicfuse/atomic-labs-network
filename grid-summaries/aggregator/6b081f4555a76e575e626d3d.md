## Uganda and Zambia Take the Lead on Cheap HIV Prevention Shots

Uganda and Zambia are at the front of Africa's rollout of low-cost generic lenacapavir, an HIV prevention injection given twice a year. The drug is described as nearly 100% effective.

### A breakthrough in access

The article presents lenacapavir as a major step forward for countries carrying a heavy HIV burden, particularly in sub-Saharan Africa. Price is central to that claim. A medicine that once cost about $28,000 a year in the United States may now reach African patients for roughly $40 annually, putting it within reach of health systems that could not previously consider it.

### Why the timing matters

The rollout comes as global health groups race to widen HIV prevention before the UN's 2030 Aids target. At the same time, US foreign aid cuts threaten existing HIV programs. Against that backdrop, a cheap, long-acting preventive option takes on added weight for the countries leading its introduction.
