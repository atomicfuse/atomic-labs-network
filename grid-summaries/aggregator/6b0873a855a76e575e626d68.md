## Belgium Gains Power to Pull Hate-Filled Personalized License Plates

Belgium has adopted a royal decree that lets authorities cancel personalized license plates already issued if they carry antisemitic or other extremist hate messages. The step ends a three-year push by Jewish lawmaker Michael Freilich.

### What prompted the change

Freilich began his campaign after plates referring to "Heil Hitler" and to the October 7 massacre were seen in Brussels. Until now, such plates, once issued, could not be revoked, so the messages stayed on public roads. The new decree closes that gap and gives officials a way to act on plates that were previously approved.

### Why the timing matters

The decision arrives amid heightened worry about antisemitism in Europe since October 7, along with closer attention to coded extremist symbols in public spaces. It also shows how bureaucratic loopholes can let hate messaging persist even while governments publicly condemn it.
