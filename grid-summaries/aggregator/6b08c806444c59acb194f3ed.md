## French presidential hopefuls court mayors in race for ballot access

Candidates hoping to run for the French presidency are turning to mayors and local officials to gather the 500 endorsements needed to stand. That has made the annual mayoral congress an important campaign battleground, with about 40 potential contenders in play and the vote five months away.

### Endorsements decide who runs

The endorsement hunt could determine who even reaches the ballot, so local officials hold unusual influence over a crowded and fragmented field. Candidates are pitching themselves on immigration, public spending, Europe and France's role in the world, while trying to win over the people whose signatures they need.

### A local pitch against centralised power

David Lisnard stands out with a localist, anti-bureaucracy message. His approach fits a wider mood after years of criticism that power has been too centralised under Macron. Candidates are leaning on local legitimacy and rural outreach to distinguish themselves from rivals and show they understand communities beyond the capital.
