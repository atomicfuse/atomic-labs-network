## US Farmers Feel the Pinch as Foreign Food Aid Shrinks

Cuts to American foreign food aid are doing more than affecting people overseas. According to the article, they are also hurting US farmers by removing a significant outlet for surplus crops.

### A Shrinking Market for Surplus Crops

The article argues that foreign food aid has served as a key market for American farmers, giving them a place to send crops that might otherwise go unsold. As that aid is reduced, demand falls, and the effects reach farms across the country. The piece links decisions about global aid policy directly to struggles already facing the farm sector at home, including drought and high costs for fuel and fertilizer.

### Why It Matters Now

Farmers are already under strain from extreme weather, inflation, and unstable commodity markets, so any lost demand is felt more sharply. The story also reframes aid cuts as a domestic economic issue, rather than only an international humanitarian one.
