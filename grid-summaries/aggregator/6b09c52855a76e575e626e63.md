## Alleged attack on Helen Haines sparks call to curb political hate speech

An opinion article argues that the alleged attack on Helen Haines should push Australia's political leaders, media outlets and technology platforms to face up to the real-world consequences of hateful political rhetoric. It says symbolic expressions of concern are not enough.

### What the article calls for

The piece urges concrete action in three areas: abuse within parliament, threats made online, and polarisation driven by media coverage. It contends that those with influence over public debate share responsibility for the tone it takes, and that this responsibility should translate into changes in behaviour rather than statements of regret.

### Why the debate is intensifying

The incident has fuelled discussion about whether Australian public life is becoming dangerously Americanised, with political hostility increasingly treated as normal both online and offline. It also arrives amid growing warnings about threats against MPs, particularly women, and renewed calls for politicians and commentators to turn the temperature down.
