In a world where kids can feel overwhelmed by school stress and social pressures, parents are often left grasping for the right ways to connect with them. If you’ve found yourself asking, “How was your day?” only to be met with a shrug or a one-word answer, you’re not alone. Behavior analyst and mother Jenna Mazzillo shares five gentle parenting strategies that can help kids open up naturally and talk about their school experiences without feeling pressured.

## 1. Greet Them with Love, Not a Question
Instead of diving straight into questions about their day, try greeting your child with warmth and affection. This could be a hug, a smile, or a simple: “I’m so glad to see you!” Creating a welcoming atmosphere sets a positive tone and lets them know that they’re safe and valued in your presence.

## 2. Join Whatever They Are Doing for 10 Minutes
Children often communicate through play and activities they enjoy. Take a moment to engage in whatever your child is doing, whether it’s building blocks, coloring, or playing a game. By spending just ten minutes participating in their world, you’ll not only strengthen your bond but also open the door for them to share their thoughts and feelings more freely.

## 3. Share Your Experiences First – Then Play Rose Bud Thorn
One effective way to entice your child into a conversation is to share your own experiences first. After telling them about your day, introduce the game ‘Rose Bud Thorn,’ where you each share a highlight (the rose), something you’re looking forward to (the bud), and a challenge you faced (the thorn). This method normalizes sharing and makes it easier for your child to respond with theirs.

## 4. Ask Questions That Cannot Be Answered in One Word
When you engage your child with open-ended questions, you encourage deeper conversation. Instead of asking, “Did you have a good day?” try asking, “What was the most fun part of your day?” This prompts them to recall more details and share their thoughts, enriching your understanding of their experiences.

## 5. Respect Their “No” Every Single Time
A key aspect of building trust is respecting your child’s boundaries. If they don’t want to talk about something or simply say “no,” accept that without pushing further. Consistently respecting their wishes reassures them that they are in control of their conversations and promotes a safe space for communication.

### Why These Techniques Matter
With the increasing impact of screen distractions and the stresses of school life, finding emotionally intelligent ways to communicate with your children is more vital than ever. These gentle parenting strategies are not just helpful; they are also practical everyday tools that you can integrate into your daily routine, transforming mealtime or after-school time into moments of connection.

By incorporating these tactics into your parenting style, you can foster a richer, more open dialogue with your kids, making them feel respected and heard without the pressure of standard inquiries. The next time you wonder what to say after school, try one of these tips and watch them open up in ways you never expected!
