Forbes recently reported that around La Fortuna, Costa Rica, local ingredients and traditional Costa Rican cooking are becoming the kind of dining luxury travelers increasingly prefer. The details of specific restaurants and menus aren't something we can vouch for here, so we'll stick to the idea at the heart of the story: great meals start with where the food comes from.

That idea happens to be very Paleo. You don't need a plane ticket to use it. By the end of this guide, you'll have a simple method for building place-based, whole-food meals at home, plus swaps that keep traditional-style dishes Paleo-friendly.

## The Takeaway From La Fortuna: Start With the Ingredient

Per Forbes, the appeal is local ingredients and traditional cooking, not complicated technique. That's good news for a home cook. A meal built around one excellent ingredient needs less fuss, fewer additions, and almost no processed extras.

Try this approach each week:

1. Pick one ingredient that is at its best right now where you live (a fruit, a root vegetable, a leafy green).
2. Buy your protein from the nearest source you can find, such as a farmers market or a local butcher.
3. Build the plate around those two things and keep the seasoning simple.

## Build a Plate in Three Parts

A reliable template makes weeknight cooking easier. Use this one for any cuisine-inspired Paleo meal:

### 1. A protein
Chicken thighs, fish, pork, or eggs. Season with salt, pepper, garlic, lime juice, and fresh herbs such as cilantro.

### 2. A starchy side that isn't grain
Sweet potato, plantain, cassava, or squash. Roast, pan-fry in avocado oil, or mash. These give the comfort that rice and tortillas usually provide.

### 3. Something fresh and acidic
A quick salad of chopped tomato, red onion, cilantro, and lime. Or diced mango and cucumber. Acid brightens everything and replaces the need for heavy sauces.

## Smart Swaps for Traditional-Style Dishes

Many classic home-style plates pair meat or vegetables with grains, beans, and dairy. Here is how to keep the spirit while staying Paleo:

- **Rice:** Use cauliflower rice, sautéed in a hot pan for 5 to 6 minutes so it browns rather than steams.
- **Corn tortillas or wheat wraps:** Use large lettuce leaves or collard leaves, or make a simple cassava-flour flatbread.
- **Cheese or sour cream topping:** Mashed avocado with lime and salt gives creaminess without dairy.
- **Sugary sauces:** Blend fruit, such as pineapple or mango, with lime and chili for a bright topping with no refined sugar.
- **Beans as a side:** Swap in sautéed greens, diced roasted squash, or extra plantain.

## One-Pan Dinner: Lime Chicken With Plantain and Peppers

This is a simple, original idea inspired by the local-ingredient approach, not a recipe from the Forbes piece.

**You'll need:** 4 bone-in chicken thighs, 2 ripe plantains (sliced thick), 1 bell pepper, 1 red onion, 2 tablespoons avocado oil, 2 limes, 3 cloves garlic, salt, pepper, and a handful of cilantro.

1. Heat the oven to 425°F (220°C).
2. Toss the chicken with half the oil, the zest and juice of one lime, garlic, salt, and pepper.
3. On a sheet pan, toss plantains, pepper, and onion with the remaining oil and a pinch of salt.
4. Nestle the chicken skin-side up among the vegetables.
5. Roast 35 to 40 minutes, until the chicken reaches 165°F (74°C) and the plantains are browned at the edges.
6. Finish with cilantro and a squeeze of the second lime.

## Make It a Meal-Prep Plan

This dinner scales well. Double the batch on Sunday and portion it into containers with a fresh slaw added the day you eat it. The chicken and plantains reheat well for about three to four days in the fridge. Keep lime juice and herbs separate until serving so the flavors stay lively.

## A Sweet Finish, Fruit First

For dessert, skip the baking and let fruit carry it. Grill pineapple slices for 2 to 3 minutes per side until caramelized, then top with toasted unsweetened coconut flakes and a little lime zest. It takes under ten minutes and needs no added sugar.

## Eat Like You're Shopping Near the Source

The lesson from La Fortuna's dining scene, as Forbes tells it, is that local and traditional can feel luxurious. Your version is simple: this week, choose one local ingredient, build one plate around it, and keep everything else out of its way.
