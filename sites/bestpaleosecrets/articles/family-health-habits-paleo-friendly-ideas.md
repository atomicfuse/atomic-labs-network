October is National Children's Health Month, and it overlaps with National Parenting Week. Auburn University's Torie Ennis shared six everyday tips for healthier family routines, as reported by Blackbeltnewsnetwork. The timing matters: Halloween candy, parties and a treat-heavy holiday season are all arriving at once.

Here is how to use each of her six tips in a Paleo-inspired kitchen. The aim is not perfection. Ennis's core message, per the report, is that small, simple habits around food, hydration, movement and modeling can support children's long-term well-being. Pick one or two to start this week.

## 1. Build a Colorful Plate

The easiest way to do this with Paleo ingredients is to treat color as the checklist. Aim for three colors at dinner.

- **Protein:** roasted chicken thighs, ground turkey, baked salmon or eggs.
- **Two or more vegetables or fruits:** orange sweet potato cubes, green broccoli, red peppers, purple cabbage.
- **Something fun:** a small pile of berries or apple slices on the side.

A one-pan dinner makes this painless. Toss chicken thighs, sweet potato chunks and broccoli with olive oil and salt on a sheet pan, and roast at 400°F for about 25 to 30 minutes. One pan, three colors.

## 2. Make Healthy Snacks Easy

Snacks go to whatever is easiest to grab. So make the better option the easy one.

### Set up a snack zone

Keep a low shelf in the fridge and a bowl on the counter that kids can reach on their own. Fill them with:

- Washed grapes, clementines and apple slices (a splash of lemon juice keeps them from browning)
- Carrot and cucumber sticks with guacamole
- Hard-boiled eggs, peeled
- Small bags of nuts or seeds, if your family has no allergies

### Try a no-bake bite

Mash a few pitted dates with almond butter and a pinch of cinnamon, roll into balls, and chill. They satisfy a sweet craving with fruit as the only sweetener.

## 3. Have Water as the Go-to Beverage

Make water the default and the rest occasional. A few ways to make that feel less like a rule:

- Give each child a favorite bottle and let them decorate it.
- Add frozen berries, cucumber slices or orange wedges to a pitcher for natural flavor.
- Freeze fruit in ice cube trays so plain water looks like a treat.

Around Halloween, this is also a handy counterweight when party tables are full of sugary drinks. Send your child with a filled bottle so water is already in hand.

## 4. Make Movement a Part of Each Day

Movement does not have to mean a workout. Colder weather pushes families indoors, so keep a few ideas ready.

- **Kitchen dance break** while dinner roasts.
- **Living room obstacle course** with couch cushions and a timer.
- **After-dinner walk**, even ten minutes, with a flashlight once it gets dark early.
- **Cook together**, since washing, peeling and stirring keep little hands busy.

Tie movement to something you already do daily. It sticks better than a new scheduled activity.

## 5. Team Up for School Meals

Ennis's tip is about partnering on school meals, and in practice that means involving your child. Let them help choose and pack lunch, and talk with them about what the school offers so you can fill in the gaps at home.

### A packable Paleo lunch formula

Let your child pick one item from each group:

1. **Protein:** turkey roll-ups, leftover chicken drumsticks, hard-boiled eggs
2. **Crunch:** cucumber rounds, snap peas, bell pepper strips
3. **Fruit:** grapes, orange segments, berries
4. **Extra:** guacamole cup, olives, or a date-nut bite

Prep the components on Sunday, such as boiling eggs and cutting vegetables, and mornings become assembly only. Kids who choose their own items tend to be more invested in eating them, though every child is different.

## 6. Be a Healthy Role Model

Children watch what you do. So the most effective step may be the one with no recipe: eat the colorful plate yourself, reach for water, and move where they can see you.

Keep it low pressure:

- Skip labels like "good" and "bad" foods.
- Let them see you try something new and react honestly.
- Invite rather than instruct: "Want to take a walk with me?"

## A Halloween-Week Plan

If six changes feels like too many, try this for the week of Halloween:

1. Serve a real dinner before trick-or-treating, built from the colorful plate idea.
2. Fill a water bottle for each child to carry along.
3. Set out a snack tray of fruit and veggies at home.
4. Take a family walk while you trick-or-treat.

None of that takes the fun out of the holiday. It simply gives the candy some company.

## Start With One

Pick the tip that fits your week best and do only that. A pitcher of fruit water or a Sunday lunch-prep session is enough. Small habits repeated are what this advice is built on.
