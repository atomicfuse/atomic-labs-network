Batch cooking works best when one pot of food can become several different dinners. A chuck roast does that well, and a birria-style seasoning shortcut makes it easy. Here's how to cook one roast on a weekend and turn it into meals that don't feel like leftovers, with the Paleo swaps that keep it on plan.

## Where the idea comes from

A recent BuzzFeed shopping roundup of kitchen helpers featured EZ Bombs birria cooking bombs. Per BuzzFeed, they're meant for quick, flavorful tacos, quesadillas, ramen, nachos, and more. The appeal is simple: you skip buying a shelf of spices and the long prep that restaurant-style birria usually needs.

I can only speak to what BuzzFeed reported, so I'm not going to tell you how these taste or what's in them. That leads to the first practical step.

## Step 1: Check the label before you cook

Seasoning blends vary a lot. Before you commit a roast to any pre-mixed product, read the ingredient list for:

- **Added sugar** (cane sugar, dextrose, maltodextrin)
- **Wheat or flour fillers** used as anti-caking agents or thickeners
- **Soy-based ingredients** such as soy sauce powder
- **Seed oils or "natural flavors"** you'd rather avoid

If the label has anything you avoid, you have two options: skip it, or build your own blend. A homemade version is easy and I've included one below.

## Step 2: A Paleo-friendly birria-style blend

This is a general starting point, not a copy of any product. For a 3 to 4 pound chuck roast, mix:

- 1 tablespoon chili powder (check it's pure, no added sugar)
- 2 teaspoons ground cumin
- 2 teaspoons dried oregano
- 1 teaspoon smoked paprika
- 1 teaspoon garlic powder
- 1 teaspoon onion powder
- 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
- 2 teaspoons salt

Adjust the chili to your heat tolerance. If you have dried guajillo chiles, toast and soak a few, then blend them into the cooking liquid for a deeper flavor.

## Step 3: Cook the roast

Cut the roast into big chunks, rub with the blend, and sear in a hot pan with avocado oil or tallow until browned on a couple of sides. Then move it to your cooker with:

- 1 cup bone broth
- 1 diced onion
- 4 smashed garlic cloves
- A splash of apple cider vinegar

### Pressure cooker or slow cooker?

- **Pressure cooker:** about 60 to 75 minutes at high pressure, then a natural release. Best when you decide at 4 p.m. that you're cooking.
- **Slow cooker:** about 8 hours on low. Best when you start before work and want dinner waiting.

Either way, you're done when the beef shreds with a fork. Save every drop of the cooking liquid. It's the best part of the week.

## Step 4: Turn one pot into five meals

This is where the roast earns its keep. Shred the meat, keep it moistened with the juices, and portion it.

### Night one: lettuce or plantain tacos

Use butter lettuce cups or cassava tortillas if you tolerate them. Top with diced onion, cilantro, lime, and avocado. Serve a small cup of the cooking liquid for dipping.

### Night two: loaded sweet potato nachos

Slice sweet potatoes thin, roast at 425°F until crisp at the edges (about 20 to 25 minutes), then pile on the beef, guacamole, pickled onions, and jalapeño. No cheese needed, and you won't miss it much with enough guacamole.

### Night three: birria-style noodle soup

Beef-based ramen was one of the uses BuzzFeed mentioned. For a Paleo version, warm the cooking liquid with extra broth, add the shredded beef, and use zucchini noodles or sweet potato noodles instead of wheat ramen. Finish with a soft egg, scallions, and lime.

### Night four: breakfast hash

Crisp diced potatoes or cauliflower in a skillet, add the beef, and top with fried eggs. This is the easiest way to stretch a few leftover cups of meat.

### Night five: stuffed peppers

Halve bell peppers, fill with beef and cauliflower rice, and bake at 375°F for about 25 minutes.

## Smart swaps for the usual birria sides

Traditional birria is often served with corn tortillas, melted cheese, and sometimes ramen noodles. Here's how to handle each:

- **Wheat noodles:** zucchini or sweet potato noodles, or just extra vegetables in the broth.
- **Cheese:** mashed avocado or a drizzle of cashew cream if you include nuts.
- **Tortillas:** cassava or plantain-based wraps, or lettuce.
- **Sugary sauces:** fresh salsa, lime, and pickled onions carry the flavor on their own.

## Storage and reheating

Cool the meat quickly and store it submerged in its juices. This keeps it from drying out. It will keep in the fridge for about three to four days, and the freezer is the better option for anything you won't eat by then. Freeze in meal-size portions with some liquid in each container. Reheat gently on the stove.

## When a shortcut is worth it

A ready-made seasoning bomb makes sense if the label passes your check and it saves you from buying spices you'll use once. If you cook birria-style dishes often, the homemade blend costs less per batch and you control every ingredient.

Either way, the move is the same: buy one chuck roast this weekend, cook it in one pot, and plan the five meals before you start.
