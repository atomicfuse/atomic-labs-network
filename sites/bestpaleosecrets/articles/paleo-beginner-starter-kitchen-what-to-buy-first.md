BuzzFeed Shopping recently rounded up 26 products for people who want to start their "home cook era," pitching them as approachable tools to make cooking at home feel easier. The trend behind it is familiar: takeout fatigue, grocery bills, and a wish to cook more. The roundup's product-by-product details aren't something we can vouch for here, so this guide takes the idea and makes it practical for Paleo cooking. You'll leave knowing which few tools to buy first, what to skip for now, and how to run your first week.

The angle: you don't need 26 products. You need a short list that covers the way Paleo meals actually get made, which is mostly protein, vegetables, and good fat, cooked simply.

## Start With the Cooking You'll Actually Do

Paleo cooking leans on a small set of repeat moves: roasting vegetables, searing meat or fish, scrambling eggs, simmering soup. Buy for those moves. If a tool doesn't serve one of them, it can wait.

## The First-Purchase Shortlist

These are general kitchen basics, chosen by what Paleo meals need. They are my suggestions, not picks from the BuzzFeed list.

### A large skillet
One 12-inch skillet handles eggs, seared chicken thighs, ground meat, and one-pan dinners. It's the most useful pan you can own.

### A sheet pan (or two)
Sheet-pan dinners are the easiest Paleo format: chopped vegetables and a protein, olive oil, salt, one pan to wash. Two pans let you batch-cook for the week.

### A sharp chef's knife
Paleo means a lot of chopping. A decent knife makes prep faster and safer than a dull one. Pair it with a big cutting board that doesn't slide.

### A medium pot with a lid
This covers soups, braises, boiled eggs, and sweet potatoes. Soup is one of the most forgiving ways to build confidence.

### Glass storage containers
If you plan to cook ahead, containers are what make lunches portable. Get a set in a few sizes so leftovers go straight into the fridge.

### An instant-read thermometer
It takes the guesswork out of chicken, pork, and fish, which is where beginners worry most. Cook to the temperature, not the clock.

## What Can Wait

Skip single-purpose gadgets until you've cooked for a few weeks. A spiralizer, a specialty egg cooker, or a fancy blender are fun, but they're easy to leave in a cabinet. Once you notice a real friction point, such as always wanting smoothies or soups blended, buy to solve that.

## Stock a Basic Paleo Pantry

Tools only matter with food to cook. A starter pantry might include:

- Olive oil, avocado oil, or ghee for cooking
- Salt, black pepper, garlic powder, paprika, cumin
- Canned tomatoes and full-fat coconut milk for sauces and soups
- Eggs, plus a couple of frozen proteins
- Frozen vegetables as a backup for busy nights
- Sweet potatoes and onions, which keep well

## Your First Week of Cooking at Home

Keep it small. Three cooked meals beat seven ambitious ones.

### Day 1: Scrambled eggs and sautéed greens
One skillet, ten minutes. Practice heat control and seasoning.

### Day 3: Sheet-pan chicken thighs with vegetables
Toss everything in oil and salt, roast, and check the chicken with your thermometer. Make extra for lunches.

### Day 5: Simple ground-meat soup
Brown the meat in the pot, add onion, canned tomatoes, vegetables, and broth, then simmer. Freeze a portion.

Fill the other days with leftovers, eggs, fruit, and nuts. That's enough to build a rhythm.

## Smart Swaps While You Learn

You don't need to rebuild every recipe. Use coconut milk where a recipe calls for cream, cauliflower or sweet potato in place of grains on the plate, and fruit for dessert, such as baked cinnamon apples. Start with meals that are naturally Paleo before you tackle Paleo baking.

## The Takeaway

Buy a skillet, a sheet pan, a good knife, and a pot this week, then cook three simple meals. Add one new tool only when a recipe makes you wish you had it.
