## Start with the goal: easier, not perfect

The pressure points on this holiday are predictable: disrupted routines, a big meal, travel, and a crowd. That combination is exactly when kids melt down and food standoffs happen. So set a modest goal before you cook anything: everyone eats something they like, and nobody fights about it.

That goal shapes every decision below.

## Build the plate around foods kids already eat

If you cook Paleo, much of the Thanksgiving table already works. Turkey, roasted vegetables, sweet potatoes, and cranberries need little adjusting. For a picky eater, the trick is keeping things plain and separate.

- **Turkey:** Set aside plain slices before you add gravy or herb butter. Many kids will eat it dry, with dip on the side.
- **Sweet potatoes:** Cube and roast them with olive oil and salt at 425°F for about 25 minutes. They get sweet and slightly crisp, and they're easy to eat by hand.
- **A familiar fruit:** Apple slices, clementines, or grapes on the table mean there's always a safe option.
- **One "deconstructed" plate:** Put each food in its own spot instead of touching. Plenty of kids reject mixed dishes but accept the same ingredients served apart.

You don't need a kid-specific menu. You need one or two things on the table your child will reliably eat.

## Smart swaps that keep the classics

The usual Thanksgiving dishes lean on wheat flour, dairy, and refined sugar. These swaps keep the flavor and spare you from cooking two versions.

### Gravy without flour
Simmer the pan drippings with broth, then thicken with a slurry of arrowroot starch and cold water (start with 1 tablespoon starch to 2 tablespoons water per cup of liquid). Whisk it in and simmer a couple of minutes until glossy.

### Mashed potatoes without dairy
If you're skipping dairy, mash cauliflower or potatoes with olive oil or ghee if you tolerate it, plus warm broth for creaminess. Salt generously. Bland mash is the usual reason people reject the swap.

### Stuffing without bread
Use sautéed onion, celery, mushrooms, and diced apple with sage and toasted pecans. It reads as stuffing because of the herbs, and it's one of the easier dishes to make ahead.

### Dessert without refined sugar
Bake apples or pears with cinnamon, a splash of apple juice, and chopped nuts. If you want something sweeter, a spoonful of maple syrup or a few chopped dates goes a long way. Fruit-based desserts also suit kids who ignore pie.

## Scripts for food pressure from relatives

Food comments are a common holiday stressor. A short, friendly line works better than a debate. These are my suggestions:

- **"He's had plenty, thanks."** Say it lightly and move on to another topic.
- **"We're keeping it low-key today."** This works when someone questions why you aren't pushing a clean plate.
- **"That looks great, we'll save some for later."** It accepts the offer without forcing a bite.
- **"Can you help me carry this to the table?"** Redirecting a pushy relative beats arguing.

Prepare one line before you arrive. You'll use it less often than you expect, but having it ready keeps you from snapping.

## Keep everyone healthy while you gather

Illness worries were also part of the conversation, per WPRI. A few easy habits cover most of the ground:

1. **Wash hands before eating.** Make it a pre-meal ritual for the kids.
2. **Keep cold food cold and hot food hot.** Don't let dishes sit out for hours. Put leftovers away within about two hours.
3. **Cook turkey to 165°F** in the thickest part of the breast and thigh, checked with a thermometer.
4. **Give sick guests an easy out.** If someone feels unwell, a friendly "stay home, we'll send a plate" protects the rest of the table.

## Prep ahead so you're calm at 2 p.m.

Most holiday meltdowns, kids' and adults', happen when the cook is stretched thin. Do these the day before:

- Make the stuffing and cranberry sauce.
- Chop vegetables and store them in containers.
- Bake the apple dessert and reheat it.
- Set out a snack plate of fruit, nuts, and sliced turkey for the hour before dinner, so kids aren't starving when the food lands.

That last one matters most. A hungry child at a long dinner is the setup for every struggle you're trying to avoid.

## The one thing to do

Pick two dishes your kids will surely eat, make them simple, and let the rest of the day be imperfect. A calm table beats a flawless one.
