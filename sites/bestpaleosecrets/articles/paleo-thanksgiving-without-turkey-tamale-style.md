Thanksgiving doesn't have to revolve around a turkey. Per Fox News, WWE legend "Stone Cold" Steve Austin is planning a low-key holiday with his wife, and the menu may feature South Texas tamales instead of turkey. Austin also called turkey "overrated" and praised canned cranberry sauce, according to the report. His wife is vegetarian and plans the menu, per the same report.

That's a handy permission slip. Below, you'll get a Paleo-friendly way to build a tamale-inspired Thanksgiving: what to swap, what to cook, and how to keep it simple.

## Why a Non-Turkey Thanksgiving Works for Paleo

Turkey is the hardest part of the meal to do well: it's big, it dries out, and it hogs the oven. Dropping it frees up time and oven space. Paleo cooking is flexible about the centerpiece anyway. Any whole-food protein, vegetable or filling can anchor the table.

That said, I'm drawing the tamale idea from Austin's reported plans only. The Paleo menu that follows is my own take, not his.

## The Big Swap: Traditional Tamales vs. Paleo-Friendly Versions

Classic tamales rely on masa, which is corn dough, and often lard or other fats. Corn is not part of strict Paleo, so you have a few options.

### Option 1: Skip the dough entirely
Cook the filling on its own as a bowl or stuffed vegetable. Slow-cooked pork or beef seasoned with chili, cumin, garlic and onion carries the flavor that makes tamales appealing.

### Option 2: Use a vegetable "wrapper"
Spoon the filling into roasted sweet potato halves, roasted poblano peppers or large cabbage or collard leaves. Cabbage leaves work well steamed until pliable.

### Option 3: Make a sweet potato mash "dough"
Mash cooked sweet potato with a little fat and salt, and a spoonful of cassava or tapioca flour to help it hold together. Shape around filling and bake until set. This won't taste like masa, but it scratches the same comforting itch.

If you follow a looser Paleo approach and include corn occasionally, traditional masa tamales are your call. Decide based on your own rules.

## A Simple Tamale-Inspired Filling

This makes enough for about 6 servings.

1. Brown 2 pounds of pork shoulder cubes in a heavy pot with avocado oil or lard.
2. Add 1 chopped onion, 4 cloves of garlic, 2 tablespoons chili powder, 1 teaspoon cumin and 1 teaspoon salt.
3. Pour in about 1 cup of broth.
4. Cover and simmer low for 2.5 to 3 hours, until it shreds easily.
5. Shred, then stir it back through the cooking juices.

Make it a day ahead. The flavor improves overnight, and it frees Thanksgiving morning.

## Build the Rest of the Plate

### Vegetable sides that don't need much work
- **Roasted sweet potatoes** with olive oil, salt and a pinch of cinnamon.
- **Green beans** sautéed with garlic and a squeeze of lemon.
- **Roasted Brussels sprouts** at 425°F for about 20 minutes, until the edges char.
- **Cauliflower mash** made with olive oil or ghee in place of butter and cream. Ghee is a common compromise for those who tolerate it.

### Gravy without flour
Simmer the leftover cooking juices from your pork filling, then thicken with a slurry of arrowroot starch and cold water. Start with 1 tablespoon of arrowroot per cup of liquid and adjust.

## The Cranberry Question

Austin praised canned cranberry sauce, per Fox News. Most canned versions are heavily sweetened with refined sugar or corn syrup, which doesn't fit Paleo. The fix takes ten minutes:

1. Simmer 12 ounces of fresh cranberries with the juice of one orange and a few tablespoons of water.
2. Sweeten with 2 to 3 tablespoons of honey or maple syrup, to taste.
3. Cook until the berries pop and the sauce thickens, then chill.

You'll get the tart, jammy texture that canned sauce fans love, with far less sugar. It keeps for several days in the fridge.

## A Fruit-Based Dessert That Keeps It Easy

Skip the pie crust. Bake apples or pears with cinnamon, a drizzle of honey and a handful of chopped pecans until tender, about 30 minutes at 375°F. Top with coconut cream if you avoid dairy.

## A Sensible Game Plan

- **Two days before:** make the cranberry sauce and shop.
- **One day before:** cook the pork filling and chill it.
- **Thanksgiving morning:** reheat the filling, roast the vegetables, and bake the fruit dessert while you eat.

The oven needs only two temperature settings, and nothing needs a four-hour bird watch.

## The Takeaway

Pick one dish you actually want to eat, build the table around it, and let the turkey go if it isn't earning its spot. This week, cook the filling a day early and see how much calmer the holiday feels.
