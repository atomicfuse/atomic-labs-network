When life shifts under your feet, the kitchen can be the one place you still know how to operate. A personal essay in Vogue, "First, End Your Marriage. Then, Cook Rice," captures that. The writer describes her first night in a sublet apartment, separated from her husband and from a home of 24 years, and from her children. She cooked, and her sister came over. The dish was butter rice.

This piece takes one idea from that essay: a simple, familiar dish can anchor you when everything else feels unsettled. Here is how to keep that ritual going on a Paleo-inspired plan, with a one-pan comfort meal, a few smart swaps, and a short kit for cooking in a kitchen that isn't yours yet.

## What the essay shows about cooking in a hard week

Per Vogue, the essay centers on the act of making butter rice with a sister on a first night alone. Its focus isn't the conflict or the loss. It's the quiet, ordinary act of cooking and having someone show up.

That's the useful part. Comfort cooking doesn't need to be impressive. It needs to be:

- **Familiar.** You can make it without a recipe open.
- **Low-effort.** One pot, few ingredients.
- **Shareable.** It works for one person or two.

Hold those three rules in mind and you can adapt almost any comfort dish to Paleo.

## Swap 1: Cauliflower "butter rice"

Rice is a grain, so it sits outside strict Paleo. Riced cauliflower gives you a similar soft, buttery bowl in about 10 minutes.

**You need:**
- 1 medium head of cauliflower, or a 12 oz bag of pre-riced cauliflower
- 2 tablespoons ghee (or grass-fed butter if you tolerate dairy)
- Salt and pepper
- Optional: a minced garlic clove, chopped scallions

**Steps:**
1. If using a whole head, grate it on a box grater or pulse in a food processor until the pieces look like rice.
2. Melt the ghee in a wide skillet over medium heat.
3. Add the cauliflower and a good pinch of salt. Cook 5 to 7 minutes, stirring now and then, until tender but not mushy.
4. Finish with pepper, garlic if using, and scallions.

Don't cover the pan. Trapped steam makes the cauliflower soggy rather than fluffy.

## Swap 2: Make it a full one-pan dinner

Plain rice is a side. To turn it into dinner without dirtying a second pan:

1. Cook the cauliflower rice as above, then push it to one side of the skillet.
2. Add 2 or 3 eggs to the empty side and scramble them, or crack them in and cook sunny-side up.
3. Top with leftover roast chicken, canned salmon, or sliced avocado.

That gives you protein, fat, and vegetables in one pan, which is about as little cleanup as dinner gets.

## Swap 3: Dairy-free butter

If dairy is off the table, ghee is the easiest fix since most of the milk solids are removed, though people with a true dairy allergy should check with their doctor. Otherwise, use avocado oil or coconut oil and add a squeeze of lemon at the end so the dish doesn't taste flat.

## A starter kit for a borrowed or half-stocked kitchen

The essay's setting is a sublet, and anyone who has moved, rented short-term, or started over knows the problem: you have a pan or two and not much else. A minimal Paleo pantry that handles most comfort cooking:

- **Fats:** ghee or avocado oil
- **Proteins:** eggs, canned salmon or sardines, a rotisserie chicken
- **Vegetables:** frozen riced cauliflower, onions, garlic, a bag of spinach
- **Flavor:** salt, pepper, lemon, a jar of your favorite spice blend
- **Tools:** one 10 or 12-inch skillet, one wooden spoon, one sharp knife

That's enough for a week of simple dinners. Nothing here requires a matching set of cookware.

## Cook for two, even if it's just one

One detail from the essay is worth borrowing: a sister came over. Cooking is easier to start when someone else is in the room, and the meal doesn't need to be good conversation fodder. This recipe scales easily. Use a full head of cauliflower and 3 tablespoons of ghee for two bowls, and add four eggs.

If you're eating alone, make the full batch anyway. Cauliflower rice keeps in the fridge for up to four days in a sealed container and reheats in a skillet in about three minutes. That's tomorrow's lunch, handled.

## Make it a ritual, not just a recipe

The point of a comfort dish is repetition. Pick one meal that takes under 15 minutes, make it the same way a few nights running, and let it become the thing that says you're home, wherever home is this month.

Tonight, melt a spoonful of ghee in whatever pan you have, add the cauliflower, and cook it slowly. Then eat it at a table, not over the sink.
