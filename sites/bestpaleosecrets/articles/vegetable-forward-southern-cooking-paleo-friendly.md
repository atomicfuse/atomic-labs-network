Southern food has a reputation: fried, heavy, buried in gravy. Texas-based chef and restaurateur Tiffany Derry pushes back on that. As Fox News reported, she challenges common assumptions about the foods traditionally served across the South and points to wholesome ingredients, colorful vegetables, and simple preparation.

That's good news if you cook Paleo-inspired meals. A vegetable-forward, simple-ingredient approach already lines up with how you probably shop and cook. Below is how to borrow that mindset at home, with practical swaps and meal ideas. The cooking ideas are mine, not Derry's; her reported point is the framing.

## Start With the Reframe: Southern Food Is Produce-Driven

According to the Fox News report, Derry presents traditional Southern cooking as fresh, colorful, and rooted in simple, wholesome ingredients rather than heavy stereotypes. The practical takeaway: build the plate around the vegetables, not around the fryer.

A simple test when planning a Southern-style dinner: look at the plate and ask whether half of it is vegetables. If yes, you're cooking in the spirit of what Derry describes.

## Keep the Preparation Simple

Simple preparation is the other half of her message. For home cooks, that means fewer steps and fewer ingredients:

- Cook greens (collards, mustard, turnip) low and slow with onion, garlic, and a splash of vinegar.
- Roast okra, sweet potatoes, or squash with olive oil, salt, and pepper until browned at the edges.
- Slice tomatoes, season well, and serve them fresh when they're in season.
- Simmer beans or peas-style dishes with aromatics and let time do the work.

None of these need a recipe longer than a few lines. Good ingredients carry the flavor.

## Smart Swaps for Paleo-Style Southern Plates

Some Southern staples lean on wheat flour, dairy, or refined sugar. A few easy swaps keep the spirit and fit Paleo-inspired eating:

### Swap the breading
Instead of flour-and-buttermilk fried coatings, try an almond flour or cassava flour crust with seasoning, then bake on a rack or pan-sear in a little avocado oil. Okra and chicken both work well.

### Swap the dairy
For creamy sides, use full-fat coconut milk or a drizzle of olive oil and a little mashed sweet potato for body. Ghee is a common Paleo-friendly stand-in for butter, though strict versions of the diet vary on dairy, so use your own rules.

### Swap the sugar
For glazed carrots or sweet potatoes, use a small amount of maple syrup or let roasting caramelize natural sugars. For sweet treats, fruit-based desserts like baked peaches with cinnamon and chopped pecans fit the theme.

### Swap the seasoning meat
Traditional greens often get flavor from cured pork. If you eat it, choose a version with no added sugar. If not, smoked paprika and a little onion deliver depth.

## Three Easy Meal Ideas to Try This Week

### One-pan sheet dinner
Toss chicken thighs, sweet potato chunks, and sliced okra with oil, paprika, garlic powder, salt, and pepper. Roast at 425°F until the chicken is cooked through and the vegetables are browned.

### Quick skillet greens
Sauté chopped onion and garlic, add a big pile of washed greens and a splash of broth, cover, and cook until tender. Finish with a little apple cider vinegar.

### Summer-plate snack
Sliced tomatoes, cucumbers, and avocado with salt, pepper, and olive oil. Add leftover shredded chicken for a portable lunch.

## Batch Cook the Southern Way

Vegetable-forward dishes suit meal prep. A big pot of greens keeps for several days in the fridge. Roasted sweet potatoes reheat well. Cook a tray of seasoned chicken on Sunday, then rotate sides through the week so lunches don't repeat.

## Stock a Simple Pantry

You don't need much to cook this style:

- Olive oil or avocado oil
- Apple cider vinegar
- Smoked paprika, garlic powder, black pepper, cayenne
- Onions and garlic
- Almond or cassava flour for occasional coatings
- Canned or boxed broth with no added sugar
- Seasonal vegetables, whatever looks best

## The Takeaway

Next time you plan a Southern-inspired dinner, start at the produce section. Pick two colorful vegetables, season them simply, and build the rest of the plate around them. That's the idea Derry is pushing, and it happens to fit a Paleo kitchen well.
