Veranda has published its list of 12 kitchen trends that designers say will be everywhere in 2027, and the through-line is easy to spot: calmer, warmer, more integrated rooms. Per the outlet, the big shift is toward kitchens that hide clutter, lean on richer natural materials, and work as polished living spaces instead of purely utilitarian ones.

The timing makes sense. As open-concept living continues, the kitchen is increasingly visible from the sofa, the dining table, and the front door. Veranda frames the trends as a move away from sterile minimalism and toward warmth, craftsmanship, and what it calls practical luxury.

My take: the real story isn't twelve separate trends. It's one idea in twelve outfits. **The kitchen of 2027 is a room that's allowed to be seen, so it had better hide the dishes and flatter the guests.** Here's how to read the list that way.

## The Hide-the-Mess Trio

Three of Veranda's picks are essentially about concealment.

### The Rise of the Scullery

A scullery is a secondary workspace behind the main kitchen, and Veranda lists its rise as a 2027 trend. The appeal is obvious: the cooking chaos goes backstage while the front kitchen stays presentable. If you've ever shoved a pot of soaking pans into the oven because company was coming, you understand the pitch.

I can't tell you from Veranda's list how designers scale this down, so what follows is my own spin: a pantry, a utility nook, or even one cabinet run with doors can play the same role. The principle is a designated place for the messy stuff.

### Invisible Kitchens

Also on the list: kitchens that visually disappear. Think of it as the room doing a magic trick, with the work happening somewhere you can't see it. It's the natural partner to the scullery.

### Smart Kitchens That Don't Look High-Tech

Technology stays, but the gadget-showroom look goes. Veranda's version of smart tech is the kind that doesn't announce itself. It's hard not to see this as the same instinct again: even your appliances should know when to be quiet.

## The Living-Room Pivot

### Kitchens That Feel Like Living Rooms

This one is almost a thesis statement for the whole list. If your kitchen sits in sight of your seating, designers say it should feel like it belongs there.

### Kitchen Islands as Gathering Tables

The island stops being a prep counter with stools shoved underneath and becomes a place where people actually sit and linger. Anyone who has watched a party migrate to the kitchen no matter how nice the living room is will recognize that this is a design world finally conceding a point.

### Tonal Kitchens

Tonal schemes, where related shades sit together, round out the calm-and-cohesive mood. This is my read rather than Veranda's, but it fits: fewer contrasts means fewer things competing for your attention while you're trying to find a lid.

## The Warmth Package

If the all-white kitchen has been the default for years, this is its replacement kit, per the list:

- **Richer wood tones**, for depth instead of pale and blank.
- **Natural materials and craftsmanship**, which signals that handmade and textured is back in.
- **Statement stone**, putting a dramatic slab front and center.

The theme is that surfaces should have character. Warmth, in this telling, comes from materials with some visible personality rather than from paint alone.

## The Showpiece Details

The remaining trends are where a kitchen gets to show off a little.

- **Creative millwork:** cabinetry and trim treated as design features, not just storage.
- **Decorative range hoods:** the appliance above the stove becomes a focal point instead of a stainless-steel afterthought.
- **Artistic tile backsplashes:** the wall behind the sink and stove gets to be art.

Notice the logic. If the clutter is tucked away and the palette is calm, the eye needs somewhere to land. Hoods, tile, and millwork are those landing spots.

## What to Actually Do With This

Most of us aren't adding a scullery this year, and Veranda's list is aimed at designer-level renovations. So here is my translation, clearly my own and not the outlet's:

1. **Decide what gets a hiding place.** Small appliances, dish racks, and the drawer of mystery cords are prime candidates for closed storage.
2. **Warm up one surface.** A wood cutting board display, a wood stool, or a richer-toned accent can nod toward the trend without a remodel.
3. **Treat the island like furniture.** Clear it off so it can be a table, not a landing strip for mail.
4. **Pick one showpiece.** If you're upgrading anything, tile or a hood is where designers say the personality goes.

## The Bottom Line

The sleek, show-kitchen era valued looking clean. The 2027 version, as Veranda's designers describe it, values being clean-looking and lived in, which is a harder trick. The cleverest move on the list may be the oldest one: put the mess where nobody's looking, then make everything else worth looking at.
