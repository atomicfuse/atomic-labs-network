House Beautiful has published a roundup of 20 elegant Halloween decorations, drawn from Pottery Barn and other retailers, that aim to be festive without tipping into tacky. The list runs from wall mirrors and candle holders to tableware and planters. The pitch, in the outlet's own logline, is to make your home look "ghoulishly glamorous."

With Halloween shopping in full swing, the timing makes sense. Shoppers are increasingly looking for seasonal decor that feels elevated and reusable instead of disposable, which is essentially quiet luxury, but make it haunted.

The angle here: the list works best if you treat it as a tablescape-and-shelf strategy rather than a shopping list. Here's how the pieces sort themselves.

## The Rule Behind the List: Spooky Is a Motif, Not a Costume

Scan the 20 items and a pattern emerges. Almost nothing is orange plastic. Instead you get materials (antique brass, smoked amber glass, enameled cast iron, linen, ceramic) carrying a spooky motif: a spider web, a snake, a skeleton, a ghost. The scare is in the shape, while the object itself would pass at a dinner party in March.

That's the whole trick, and it's a useful one. When the material looks expensive, the novelty reads as a wink instead of a gag.

## Build the Table First

If you're only going to do one thing, the roundup makes a strong case for the table. A huge share of the list is dinnerware and bar gear:

- Pumpkin-shaped enameled cast iron cocottes
- A skeleton serving collection with wine glasses
- A skull charcuterie board
- Ghost-shaped pinch bowls
- A gold-decaled "Hex Mix" snack bowl and a lidded snack bowl
- Scorpion-topped cocktail picks
- Autumn leaf inlay coasters
- Linen napkins with an oak vine design

It's hard not to notice that this is a complete party in eight entries. Put out the charcuterie board, pour wine into the skeleton glasses, and spear olives with the scorpion picks. Each piece does one small spooky thing, and the linen napkins and leaf coasters keep the whole spread grounded in autumn instead of horror-movie set.

### Why the cocottes are the smart buy

Enameled cast iron is the kind of cookware that lives in a kitchen for decades. A pumpkin-shaped version works for Halloween dinner, then does Thanksgiving duty without anyone blinking. In terms of cost per use, that's the list's most practical pick (my read, not the outlet's).

## The Wall and Shelf Layer

Then there's the stuff that goes up and stays up. The list includes an antique brass spider-web mirror, a serpentine wall hook, and antique brushstroke birdcages. A sculptural golden snake and a smoked amber glass candle holder round out the surfaces category.

Here the fandom crowd gets a treat. The roundup includes two Harry Potter-flavored picks: hanging taper candles described as Hogwarts-inspired, and a snake-wrapped mirror described as Nagini-inspired. Floating candles are the Great Hall's signature look, and Nagini is Voldemort's snake, so these are genuinely recognizable nods. They're also subtle enough that a non-fan sees a handsome mirror and some candles, while a fan sees the reference immediately. That's a nice trick for a home: a joke that only the right guests get.

A practical note from the taste department: the list has several snake and serpent items (the wall hook, the mirror, the golden sculpture). Pick one or two, not all three, unless you're committed to a reptile house.

## The Cozy and Entryway Pieces

The softer end of the list leans friendly. There's a Gus the Ghost throw pillow with a bouclé pumpkin, a light blue ceramic pumpkin planter, and a woodland critter doormat. A ghost incense smoker adds scent to the mix.

These are the pieces that keep the look from feeling severe. A pale blue pumpkin planter is a good example: it's a pumpkin, but not an orange one, which is the sort of small swerve that makes a porch look styled instead of stocked.

## How to Make It Look Intentional

A few styling moves follow from the list itself (my suggestions, not the outlet's):

1. **Pick a palette.** Brass, amber, cream, and a single pale blue accent covers most of these pieces and keeps things cohesive.
2. **Limit the novelty items.** One skull board, one snake, one ghost pillow. The restraint is what makes it look elegant.
3. **Lean on the neutral pieces.** The brass mirror, amber candle holder, linen napkins, and birdcages don't scream October, so they can stay out after the holiday.
4. **Thrift the shapes.** Candle holders, brass objects, and linens are classic secondhand finds, so the look can be approximated on a smaller budget even if the exact items stay at Pottery Barn prices.

## The Takeaway

The most useful thing in this roundup isn't any single item. It's the principle: choose well-made objects where the spooky part is a detail, not the whole personality. Do that and your house can be ghoulishly glamorous on October 31st and simply nicely decorated on November 1st, which, for a holiday with a short shelf life, is the best deal on the list.
