Somewhere between Halloween and the holiday party avalanche, Friendsgiving has become fashion's favorite excuse for a long, unhurried dinner. Vogue rounded up the season's standout gatherings, which ranged from candlelit suppers to art gallery takeovers to apartment hangouts. The list includes Net-a-Porter x Jessica McCormack, Thom Browne x Mr Porter, Gabriella Khalil x Olivia von Halle, Jordan Firstman, Pamela Anderson x Pandora, and Urban Jürgensen.

That is a lot of luxury name-dropping for a holiday whose core ritual is making your friends bring side dishes. So here is the angle: what can a normal host with a normal apartment steal from the fancy rooms?

A note on what follows. The details Vogue's roundup is built around are the atmosphere, guest lists, menus and seasonal touches of each party. I'm working from the roundup's framing and the names on the list, not from a seating chart, so what follows is my read on the trend rather than a report on what was served at any specific table.

## The Real Story: Friendsgiving Is a Format, Not a Menu

Per Vogue, the gatherings vary widely: brand dinners, a gallery takeover, a cozy apartment hangout. That spread is the point. The one thing they share is the shape of the evening: a long table, a reason to linger, and people you actually like.

It's hard not to notice that this is the opposite of Thanksgiving's usual pressure cooker. Nobody is negotiating with an in-law about the dressing. The stakes are low and the vibe is high, which is exactly why brands and tastemakers keep claiming it. A dinner party is a cultural moment with a very approachable dress code.

## Steal #1: Pick a Setting With a Point of View

Vogue's list swings from art gallery takeovers to apartment hangouts, and the lesson is that the room is part of the menu. Fashion hosts choose spaces that feel like somewhere.

At home, that means committing to one idea rather than decorating everything:

- **Light it low.** Candlelit suppers are on the list for a reason. Candles cost less than any centerpiece and flatter every guest and every slightly overcooked vegetable.
- **Clear one wall or surface.** A gallery feel is mostly about editing. Take things off, don't add them.
- **Let the apartment be an apartment.** Jordan Firstman's inclusion on the list is a reminder that a casual hang can sit right beside the designer dinners. Cozy counts.

## Steal #2: Give Guests a Dress Code (a Soft One)

The trend is fashion's, so clothing is part of the plot. You don't need a stylist. You need a single sentence in the group chat. Pick a lane, such as "festive tailoring" or "your nicest loungewear," and let people interpret it.

The soft dress code does two things. It makes arriving feel like an event, and it gives the shy guest an instant conversation starter. Compliments on someone's jacket are the cheapest icebreaker available.

## Steal #3: Make the Menu Do Less, Better

I can't tell you what Urban Jürgensen or Thom Browne served, and I won't pretend to. But the broad principle of a long, unhurried evening points to a menu built for lingering rather than for performing.

A practical version for the rest of us:

1. **Make the main yours.** One dish the host owns, everything else assigned.
2. **Assign sides by name.** "Someone bring something" yields five desserts. Be specific.
3. **Prep the day before.** The host who isn't sweating at the stove is the host whose party feels expensive.
4. **Batch one cocktail.** A single pitcher beats a bar, and you get to sit down.

## Steal #4: Borrow the Brand Move, Skip the Brand

Why do labels and jewelers throw Friendsgivings at all? Because dinner makes the product feel like part of someone's life rather than an ad. That's a marketing observation, mine rather than Vogue's, but it's useful for hosts too: the best parties have a signature detail people remember.

Pick one. A handwritten place card. A shared toast where everyone names what they're grateful for. A dessert everyone takes home in a container. One ritual, repeated, is what turns a dinner into "the Friendsgiving."

## The Chosen-Family Part

The pop-culture lineage here is easy to feel, even if I won't claim a specific history: friends-as-family has been a sitcom staple for decades, and Friendsgiving is the holiday that makes it official. The fashion world has simply noticed that its own people, scattered far from home, want a table too.

That's the quiet reason the trend works. Behind the candlelight and the guest lists, it's a very old idea: feed the people you've chosen.

## The Takeaway

You will not get Pamela Anderson at your table (Vogue lists her party with Pandora; your odds are your own). What you can get is the template: one good room, one soft dress code, a menu that lets you sit down, and one ritual people remember. The luxury brands have the budget. You have the better guest list, because you picked it.
