Dark chocolate has spent years as the wellness world's favorite permission slip: it's good for you, so have another square. But a new rundown from Health says the cocoa-forward treat, while high in magnesium, isn't the richest source out there. The outlet names six foods that beat it: pumpkin seeds, spinach, amaranth, Brazil nuts, chia, and soybeans.

That's not a takedown. Health's framing is that dark chocolate is a good source, just not the top one. Think of it as a supporting actor who's been getting lead billing.

## Why the magnesium chatter won't quit

Magnesium has become a wellness-aisle regular, tied in popular conversation to sleep, stress, and muscle recovery. As more people look to get nutrients from food first, a practical question follows: what's already in the kitchen that does the job? The answer, per Health, is a short list of pantry staples that are far less glamorous than a chocolate bar and far more meal-prep friendly.

This piece commits to that angle: not a supplement guide, but a **meal prep upgrade**. Here's how each of the six could slide into a week of ordinary eating. (The ideas are my suggestions; the magnesium ranking is Health's.)

## The six, and where they fit

### 1. Pumpkin seeds

The easiest win on the list. They need no cooking and no recipe, which is exactly what a Sunday prep session wants. Keep a jar on the counter and scatter them over yogurt, soup, or salads. It's hard to think of a lower-effort upgrade.

### 2. Spinach

The classic Popeye move, and Popeye was onto something here, at least by Health's reckoning. Spinach disappears into eggs, pasta, smoothies, and grain bowls, so it's a stealthy add for people who claim not to like greens.

### 3. Amaranth

The one most people have to look up. It's a grain-style staple you can cook like porridge or fold into a batch-cooked base. If quinoa has gotten a little predictable in your rotation, this is the plot twist.

### 4. Brazil nuts

A snack-friendly option that needs zero prep beyond opening the bag. Pair them with fruit for a grab-and-go container. Health lists them among the top sources, so they earn their spot in the snack drawer.

### 5. Chia

Chia is the meal prepper's best friend: stir it into overnight oats or pudding jars and let the fridge do the work. It's also a quiet way to boost a smoothie without changing the flavor much.

### 6. Soybeans

The legume entry. Cooked soybeans can go into grain bowls, salads, and stir-fries, and they batch well for the week. They're the sturdy workhorse of the group.

## A sample "upgrade" week

Here's how a lazy-but-smart prep might look, again my own layout rather than anything from Health:

- **Breakfast:** chia overnight oats topped with pumpkin seeds.
- **Lunch:** a spinach-and-soybean grain bowl built on a batch of amaranth.
- **Snack:** a few Brazil nuts, with a square of dark chocolate if you like.

That last part matters. Nothing here asks you to break up with chocolate. It's still a good source, per Health. The point is to stop treating dessert as your main magnesium plan.

## The takeaway

There's something satisfying about the reveal: the "healthy" indulgence everyone reaches for gets outranked by a handful of seeds and a bag of spinach. Wellness trends love a glamorous hero, but the real overachievers tend to live in the pantry, unbranded and unbothered. Keep the chocolate for joy, and let the pumpkin seeds do the heavy lifting.
