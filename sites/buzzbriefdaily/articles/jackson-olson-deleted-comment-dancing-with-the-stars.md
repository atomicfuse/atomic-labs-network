Dancing With the Stars fans have found their latest cause, and it comes with a delete button. Per Cosmopolitan, Jackson Olson faced backlash after he allegedly commented that a baseball player recreated Guillermo Rodriguez's choreography "better" in just two hours of rehearsal. The comment was reportedly deleted, but not before fans noticed and accused him of mocking Guillermo and his pro partner, Witney Carson.

The backlash, according to Cosmopolitan, centered on one demand: give the choreography proper credit.

## What's Actually Reported

Here is what the reporting supports, and where it stops. Cosmopolitan says Olson allegedly left a comment suggesting a baseball player did Guillermo's routine better after only two hours of rehearsal. The comment was then deleted. Fans read it as a dig at both Guillermo and Carson, and responded by defending the pair and calling for choreography credit.

What we don't have is the full wording of the comment, any statement from Olson explaining it, or any response from Guillermo or Carson. Because the comment is gone and the word "allegedly" is doing real work, treat the specifics as secondhand until someone with firsthand knowledge speaks up.

The timing matters too. Season 35 is airing now, and fan engagement around eliminations and contestant behavior is running hot, per Cosmopolitan. A stray remark in that climate does not stay stray.

## The Real Story Is the Credit

The most interesting part of this flap is not who danced better. It's the fan reaction, which turned on a point that gets lost in competition shows: choreography is somebody's work.

On DWTS, the routine a celebrity performs is built by their pro partner. In this case, fans point to Carson. So when a comment implies that someone else picked up the routine in two hours, fans heard it as a shot at the person who made it, not just the person who performed it. That's the reading, to be clear, and it's the fans' reading. We can't say what Olson meant.

It's hard not to wonder, though, why "two hours" became the sticking point. Learning a finished routine and creating one are very different jobs. Anyone who has watched a friend assemble flat-pack furniture from the instructions, then claimed they could have designed the bookshelf, knows the difference. Copying a finished thing is a skill. It just isn't the same skill as building it.

## Why Deleted Comments Never Really Disappear

A deleted comment tends to say two things at once: the person had a thought, and the person reconsidered it. Fans usually fixate on the first part. That's likely what happened here, since the deletion apparently did little to cool the reaction.

That's our take, not a finding. A deletion could mean regret, a misread tone, or just a decision not to feed an argument. Cosmopolitan's reporting doesn't say which, and neither can we.

## The Stan Machine Spins Up

DWTS fandom has a protective streak, and this episode fits the pattern: fans rallying around a pair they feel was slighted, with credit as the rallying cry. The brand of loyalty is specific. These viewers are not just voting for dancers; they are policing how the dancers are talked about.

That has real stakes in a live season. Cosmopolitan notes that social media moments involving the cast can influence viewer sentiment as eliminations loom. We can't draw a straight line from one deleted comment to a result, and nobody should pretend to. But it's a fair bet that the next time Olson is on screen, a chunk of the audience will be watching with a raised eyebrow.
