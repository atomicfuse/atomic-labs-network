Designer Ken Fulk has dreamed up a wallcovering called "Monkey Business" for Perennials' fall collection, and per Elle Decor, it is exactly as unrestrained as the name suggests. The design features spider monkeys, toucans and butterflies, all printed on sisal grass cloth. Elle Decor reports that Fulk sees it as a statement piece for dining rooms and other bold design moments.

That's the news. Here's the take: this is a wall that wants to be the main character, and the smartest way to live with it is to let it.

## What You're Actually Getting

The facts are short and vivid. Spider monkeys. Toucans. Butterflies. A sisal grass cloth base, which means the jungle isn't just printed on a surface, it's printed on a natural-fiber one. Elle Decor describes the result as whimsical wildlife and tropical fantasy brought indoors.

I can't tell you from the reporting how it's priced, how it's sold, or what colorways exist, so I won't guess. What the coverage does establish is the intent: Fulk is aiming for a dining room moment, not a hallway you pass through on the way to something calmer.

## Why the Dining Room Is the Right Target

This is my read, not Fulk's, but the dining room is the one place in a house where a wall full of animals makes narrative sense. You sit there for a while. You face the walls. Guests have nothing to do between courses but look around. A wallpaper with monkeys in it is, functionally, a conversation starter you never have to refill.

It also helps that dining rooms tend to be used in bursts. A bedroom wallpaper has to survive your 6 a.m. self. A dining room one only has to survive dinner parties, which are, conveniently, when people are most in the mood for a little theater.

## The Maximalism Moment

Elle Decor's piece lands as maximalist, nature-inspired design is surging, with consumers looking for personality and escapism at home. Luxury wallcovering collections, the framing goes, are becoming key trend-setters, and designer collaborations like this one hint at where high-end interiors are heading.

It's hard not to see the logic. After years of beige and "quiet" interiors, a toucan is practically a protest sign. Pop culture has long had a soft spot for the tropical-jungle room as a shorthand for glamour with a sense of humor, and a designer-led take on it fits that tradition neatly.

## How to Style a Statement Wall Without Losing the Room

None of the following is from Fulk; it's general styling sense, offered as my own advice for anyone tempted by a bold wall like this.

### Let the wall be the loudest thing

When the wallpaper is the star, the furniture can be the supporting cast. Solid-colored chairs and a simple table give the monkeys room to perform. Pattern on pattern is a maximalist move, but you need a deliberate plan for it, not an accident.

### Pull one color from the walls

Pick a single shade from the design, whether a leafy green or a bright accent, and repeat it in a few accessories. It makes the room feel composed rather than chaotic.

### Think texture

Since the wallcovering is printed on sisal grass cloth, natural materials like wood, rattan or linen will echo the base rather than fight it. That's an inference from the material, but a safe one.

### Mind the lighting

Warm light is kind to a dense, colorful wall. Dimmable fixtures let you swing from weeknight dinner to full jungle-gala without repainting anything.

## If a Designer Wallcovering Isn't in the Budget

Elle Decor's piece is about a luxury collection, so a shortcut isn't what Fulk is offering. But the idea travels. You can borrow the spirit with one accent wall, a smaller wallpapered space such as a powder room, or animal-themed art and textiles layered into a calmer room. The principle is the same: pick one place to be fearless.

## The Takeaway

Monkey Business works because it commits. Nobody puts spider monkeys on a dining room wall hoping to be subtle. If the maximalist wave is real, and Elle Decor says it is, the lesson isn't that everyone needs a jungle. It's that the one wall in your house you'll actually stare at over dinner deserves to have something to say.
