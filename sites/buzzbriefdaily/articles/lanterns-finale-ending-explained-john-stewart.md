Lanterns has wrapped its first season on HBO Max, and the finale did something rare for a superhero show: it kept its voice down. Per Cosmopolitan's breakdown of the ending, the episode resolves some big questions, including who the second Manhunter is, who killed Hal Jordan, and what John Stewart's job looks like now that the dust has settled.

Spoilers ahead, obviously. Turn back now if you haven't finished.

## What Cosmopolitan says happened

The finale's major reveals, as Cosmopolitan lays them out:

- **Hal Jordan's killer is Kerry.** The show does not hand Hal a heroic send-off. Kerry is the one responsible for his death.
- **The second Manhunter is Noah.** He is Kelly's son, and he is also a secret Manhunter. Cosmopolitan notes that sci-fi mechanics made his existence possible.
- **John Stewart takes a new role as Earth's protector.** He does it by blending green and yellow Lantern powers.

That's the fact base. Everything past this point is my read.

## The angle: the quiet finale is the whole point

Cosmopolitan describes the season's approach as deliberately understated, and it defies typical superhero spectacle. It's hard not to see that as a statement of intent. The usual season-ender is a sky beam, a CGI army and a cameo that makes the group chat explode. Lanterns, by that account, went the other direction and let its reveals be about people.

Consider what the reveals are. A killer who is a character we know, not a cosmic warlord. A Manhunter who is somebody's kid. A hero whose big move is a creative job description, not a punch. These are small-scale, character-first answers, and a show that spends its finale on them is telling you what it cares about.

### Kerry killing Hal is the tone-setter

Superhero stories usually treat the iconic name as protected. Hal Jordan is a headline Lantern, and the genre's habit is to bench a character like that dramatically, then bring him back with fanfare. Per Cosmopolitan, here Kerry is simply the one who did it. I'd argue that choice tells you the series isn't interested in the genre's comfort rules.

There's a flip side. Cosmopolitan's piece floats the idea that Hal could return as a Black Lantern, and that's speculation, not confirmation. If you know DC lore, you know death is more of a revolving door than a wall. So the show may have killed Hal in a way that leaves the door ajar. Whether season two walks through it is anyone's guess.

### Noah, and the "WHAT ARE YOU?!" energy

The source's tagline, "WHAT ARE YOU?!", is a fair summary of the Noah reveal's vibe. He is Kelly's son and a secret Manhunter at once, a dual identity that reframes a family relationship as a plot twist. The mechanics that make it work are sci-fi, per Cosmopolitan, and I'll leave the specifics to their full write-up rather than guess at details I can't back up.

What I can say is that a twist rooted in a parent and child lands differently than a twist rooted in a secret weapon. It's the kind of reveal that makes a rewatch feel like a different show.

### John Stewart, hybrid hero

The finale's last big swing is John Stewart's new role. Cosmopolitan reports he becomes Earth's protector in an unconventional way, combining green and yellow Lantern powers. Cosmopolitan frames this as a fresh take on DC lore that sets him apart from comic canon.

For fans who know the color-coded Lantern system, mixing the two is the sort of move that gets a long forum thread. Green and yellow are traditionally treated as different approaches to power, so a hero who uses both is a design choice, not just a costume tweak. Where it leads is something the show hasn't spelled out in anything I can cite.

## Why low-key can work

The case for the quiet finale is simple. Spectacle is cheap to repeat, and audiences have seen plenty of it. Character consequences are harder to forget. A death with a personal culprit, a child with a hidden identity and a protector who rewrites his own toolkit give season two real material without needing a bigger explosion.

There is also a practical upside. Cosmopolitan notes that potential crossovers are looming for the DCU. A show that closes on grounded stakes can plug into a larger universe without feeling like a trailer for something else. Whether that's what the creators intended, I can't say, but the structure sure allows it.

## What to watch for in season two

A few open questions, framed as questions because that's all they are:

- Does Hal actually come back, possibly as a Black Lantern, as Cosmopolitan floats?
- How does Kerry live with what the finale reveals?
- What does Noah's Manhunter status mean for Kelly?
- Can John Stewart's green-and-yellow approach hold up when the DCU's bigger players show up?

None of those require a cosmic battle to answer, which may be the finale's cleverest trick. Lanterns ended by making the audience want to know what people will do next, and in a genre built on what heroes can punch, that's the more unusual cliffhanger.
