Minimalists, according to The Spruce, have a short list of things they never let settle into the living room: messy cords, toys, mail, open shelf clutter, and too much decor. The outlet's argument is that clearing out this kind of visual noise makes a room feel more intentional and more relaxing.

There's no price tag attached to the idea, which is part of its appeal. Nobody is telling you to buy a new sofa. The advice is about what to take away, and that's a rare kind of home advice: it costs nothing and the only tool required is a laundry basket.

Here's the angle worth committing to: this is less a purge than a set of **hiding-and-homing decisions**. A calm living room isn't an empty one. It's one where every object has somewhere to be. Let's walk through the five.

## 1. Messy Cords

The Spruce lists tangled cords first, and honestly that tracks. Cords are the one clutter category that serves no decorative purpose and still manages to be the most visible thing behind the TV.

My take: this is the highest-payoff fix on the list because it's invisible work. Nobody walks into a room and says "nice cable management," but they do feel the difference. A bundle of wires snaking down a wall reads as unfinished, no matter how nice the furniture is.

## 2. Toys

Here's where the minimalist ideal collides with real life. Toys are on the list, but that doesn't have to mean banishing them to another zone of the house if you share your space with kids.

It's a reasonable guess that the point is containment rather than elimination: toys scattered across the floor read as chaos, while toys in a lidded bin or a closed cabinet read as a living room. That's my interpretation, not The Spruce's, but it fits the stated goal of keeping the room feeling intentional. The "functional without feeling sterile" balance matters most here.

## 3. Mail

Mail is a sneaky one. It arrives daily, it's rarely urgent, and it migrates to whatever flat surface is nearest. Coffee tables and console tables are the usual victims.

The pop-culture comparison writes itself: every sitcom living room has a stack of unopened envelopes that somehow never gets addressed. On screen it signals a lovably chaotic character. In real life it signals a to-do list sitting in your line of sight while you're trying to watch television.

A practical read of The Spruce's advice: give mail a home outside the living room, like an entryway tray, and sort it the day it comes in.

## 4. Open Shelf Clutter

Open shelving is lovely in photos and demanding in practice. Because everything on it is visible, everything on it counts as decor whether you meant it to or not.

The Spruce flags clutter on open shelves as something minimalists avoid. The takeaway I'd draw: if you love open shelves, treat them like a gallery wall. Leave breathing room, edit down to the things you'd actually point out to a guest, and move the rest behind a door.

## 5. Too Much Decor

The last item is the one with a built-in plot twist: even decor can be clutter. The Spruce's wording is "too much decor," and the word doing the work is *too*. Decor itself isn't banned. The quantity is the problem.

It's hard not to think of the Marie Kondo era here, when a generation of viewers learned to hold an object and ask whether it was worth keeping. The Spruce doesn't make that comparison, but the spirit is close: a room reads as calm when each remaining piece looks chosen.

## Why This Resonates Right Now

Per The Spruce's framing, minimalist design stays popular as people optimize their homes for rest, focus, and mental clarity. Small decluttering moves are appealing for anyone wanting low-cost ways to lower stress at home.

That tracks with how this advice feels in practice: none of the five fixes requires a renovation, and all of them can be tackled in an afternoon.

## A Quick Living Room Reset

If you want to try it this weekend, here's a simple order of operations based on the five items:

- **Cords:** Gather and tuck away the wires you can see.
- **Toys:** Pick a closed container and make it their home.
- **Mail:** Clear every surface and relocate the pile.
- **Shelves:** Pull everything off, then put back only what earns its spot.
- **Decor:** Remove a few pieces and live with the room for a few days.

Take a before photo. It's oddly satisfying, and it makes the difference obvious.

## The Real Lesson

The sneaky insight in The Spruce's list is that none of these five items is inherently ugly. Cords power things, toys mean someone is having fun, mail is proof you're a person with a life, and decor is why you bought the room in the first place. Minimalism, in this telling, isn't about disliking your stuff. It's about not making your stuff shout at you while you're trying to sit down.
