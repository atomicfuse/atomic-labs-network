The Spruce is out with a pitch for anyone staring at a compact sofa and a coffee table that seems to have grown overnight: a small living room doesn't have to feel cramped. In a piece titled "A Small Living Room Can Feel Surprisingly Spacious With These 7 Easy Changes," the outlet frames simple design tweaks as the key to making a smaller room feel more open, airy, and functional.

A caveat up front: the summary of the article I have to work from doesn't spell out all seven changes, so I'm not going to guess at them or pretend to rank them. What it does give us is the thesis, and the thesis is worth chewing on.

## The Promise: Feel Bigger, Skip the Renovation

The Spruce's core claim is that small rooms get their breathing room from easy, implementable changes rather than construction. That lands at a useful moment. With more people living in apartments, condos, and compact homes, small-space design stays relevant, and the audience for it wants affordable, low-effort fixes. Nobody renting a one-bedroom is knocking out a wall.

Note the verbs in the pitch: "feel" and "look." The Spruce's description talks about making a smaller living room *look* larger. That's the honest framing. Your square footage is not changing. Your perception of it is, and perception is the cheapest thing in interior design to adjust.

## The Angle: Treat It Like a Weekend Project

Here's my take, and it's mine, not The Spruce's: the smartest way to use a list like this is to stop thinking of it as decorating and start thinking of it as a weekend project with a before and after.

It's hard not to see why the format works. Anyone who has watched a home-makeover show knows the formula: a cramped, cluttered reveal at the start, a calm, bright room at the end, and a lot of satisfying commentary in between. The small-room version is the most relatable of all, because the "before" is a space real people actually live in, and the "after" doesn't require a crew or a budget with a comma in it.

So a practical way to run the experiment:

### Step One: Photograph the Room as It Is

Take a picture from the doorway before you move a single thing. Phones are brutally honest about clutter in a way your eyes, which have learned to ignore the mail pile, are not. This is my suggestion, not part of The Spruce's list, but it makes the rest of the exercise measurable.

### Step Two: Make One Change at a Time

If you're working through a set of tweaks, do them one by one and look at the room after each. Some changes will do a lot of work and some will barely register. You'll learn what your particular room responds to, which beats following a rule that was written for somebody else's floor plan.

### Step Three: Take the After Photo From the Same Spot

Same doorway, same angle, ideally similar light. If the second picture looks like it belongs to a bigger room, you've done the job. If it doesn't, you have a record of what to try next.

## Why Small-Room Content Never Goes Out of Style

The appeal is partly financial and partly emotional. The Spruce's framing leans on a reassuring idea: you don't need a bigger home, you need a better-edited one. That's a more hopeful message than "move," and a cheaper one.

There's also a quiet pop-culture lineage here. Tiny-space makeovers have long been a staple of home TV and design blogs, and the appeal is the same each time: a constraint that looks like a problem turns into a puzzle, and puzzles are fun to solve. The small living room is the genre's perennial lead character.

## The Mistakes Mindset

One of the more useful ways to approach any small-room advice is to flip it around and ask what's currently making the space feel cramped. The Spruce's piece is built around the opposite, positive version, the changes that open a room up, but the two framings are really the same coin. If you can name what's closing the room in, you already know where to start.

I can't tell you which of The Spruce's seven changes address which problem, since that detail isn't in what I have. For the full list, the original article is the place to go. But the principle travels: pick the easy, low-cost moves, try them over a weekend, and judge by the photo.

## The Takeaway

A small living room isn't a verdict. According to The Spruce, it's a design brief with a short deadline and a modest budget, and seven easy changes are enough to shift how the whole room feels. The sharpest part of that pitch is the least glamorous: the space you have is rarely the problem. How you've arranged your life inside it usually is, and that's the part you can fix by Sunday night.
