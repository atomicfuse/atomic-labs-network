Swifties have a new theory, and it comes with a Dionne Warwick soundtrack. Per Cosmopolitan, fans think Taylor Swift's next song could be called "That's What Friends Are For," after the same phrase reportedly turned up in posts from Taylor, from Cara Delevingne, and from SNL. The possible nod is to Warwick's 1985 hit of the same name.

The timing matters. Taylor just released four new songs from *The Life of a Showgirl: The Encore*, which means the fandom is in peak theory-crafting mode, the state where a stray caption can become a thesis statement by lunch.

## The Case So Far

The theory rests on one repeated phrase showing up in three places. According to the report, those are posts from Taylor, from Cara Delevingne, and from SNL, all using "that's what friends are for." The coverage doesn't detail each post here, so I won't invent specifics. The pitch is the pattern itself: three sources, one phrase, and a classic song title waiting underneath.

Then comes the numerology, which is where a Swiftie theory goes from "plausible" to "please look at my corkboard." Per Cosmopolitan, the theorist points to:

- **2,121 days**, a count that reads like a mirrored 12/21
- **12:12 timestamps** in Taylor's posts
- **12/12**, Taylor's birthdate

That is a lot of twelves. Taylor's attachment to the number is one of the best-known fan lore facts there is, which is exactly why it's so tempting to see it everywhere.

## Why It's Fun (and Why It Could Be Nothing)

Here's my read, and it's only that: the theory has real charm. A phrase popping up across unrelated accounts is a genuinely curious coincidence. Fans are also drawing on a long tradition of Taylor rewarding close readers with hidden clues, which is why this crowd takes a repeated phrase seriously.

But it's hard not to wonder how much of this is the phrase being an extremely common one. "That's what friends are for" is a standard thing people say, and a friendship-themed caption from a pop star, a fellow celebrity, and a sketch show isn't automatically a conspiracy. Nothing in the reporting suggests Taylor or her team has confirmed a song with this title, so treat it as fan speculation, not a tracklist.

The numerology deserves the same gentle skepticism. When your own birthday is 12/12, you will generate 12s with almost no effort. That doesn't make the theorist wrong. It just means the evidence is the kind that's very easy to find once you're looking for it.

## The Dionne Warwick Factor

The most interesting part, to me, is the song itself. Warwick's 1985 version is a warm, sentimental ballad about loyalty, which would be a notable swerve from a cover or interpolation standpoint, though that's pure speculation on my part. The report only says the phrase potentially references that hit. Whether Taylor would cover it, sample it, borrow its title, or ignore it entirely is anyone's guess.

What I can say is that a friendship anthem would fit the mood of an encore release, a victory-lap kind of moment. That's me connecting dots, which is, admittedly, the exact sin I'm teasing the fandom about.

## The Verdict

Believe it at your own risk, enjoy it at no risk at all. The theory is a good reminder that a Swiftie with a calculator and a timestamp can build a cathedral out of a caption. Whether the next song is called "That's What Friends Are For" or something else entirely, the hunt is half the fun, and Taylor's fans have never needed a confirmed title to commit fully to the bit.

If the song does land with that name, expect a lot of people to say they knew all along. If it doesn't, expect the theorist to find a new 12 by Tuesday.
