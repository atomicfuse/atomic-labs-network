## Instagram Stories: The New Family Court

Here's the take, and it's ours: the most interesting part isn't who's right. It's the venue.

Celebrity breakups used to play out through leaked quotes, "sources close to the couple," and the occasional tell-all sit-down. Now they unfold in 24-hour vertical slides, where a custody arrangement, a parenting role, and a new partner's place in the family can all become public narrative within a single scroll.

That format rewards speed and punchiness. Co-parenting, meanwhile, runs on the opposite: patience, boring logistics, and not saying everything you think. Those two things rarely coexist well.

## The Real Question: Who Gets to Talk About the Kids?

The flashpoint, according to the report, is a new partner speaking publicly about children who aren't hers biologically and about an arrangement she didn't negotiate. It's hard not to wonder where the line sits.

A few things are worth separating:

- **Speaking about your own life vs. speaking about the kids' lives.** A new partner can talk about her relationship, her feelings, her home. Custody terms and parenting disputes belong to the parents.
- **Defending yourself vs. relitigating.** When a partner is criticized, the urge to answer is human. Doing it publicly, though, tends to make the original argument bigger and permanent.
- **Privacy for the children.** The kids can't opt out of a post. Whatever gets said now is searchable later.

None of this is unique to the Lochte family. It's the central tension of blended-family life in the influencer era, just with an Olympian's name attached.

## Why This Lands Now

The timing matters because this is a pattern, not a one-off. Celebrity splits are increasingly narrated in real time on social platforms, with each party posting their version and fans acting as jury. The result is a kind of courtroom with no judge, no rules of evidence, and no sealed records.

For Lochte, Reid and Gillihan, the public record now includes a dispute about children that, ideally, would have been settled in a quiet text thread or a mediator's office. Of course, that's easy for outsiders to say, and we only know what's been reported.

## The Takeaway

The sharpest observation here isn't about any one person's post. It's that the platform sets the terms. When the only stage available is a slide that disappears in a day, every grievance gets staged like a performance, and "desperate for attention" becomes an accusation that can be thrown at nearly anyone who posts at all.

The kids, who are the actual subject of the custody arrangement, are the only people in this story who never got to choose the venue.
