The Dodge M4S Turbo Interceptor, the 1980s prototype pace car that became a movie star in *The Wraith*, has been reimagined as a modern hypercar. Per Autoevolution, the render comes from Andrea Piazza, an IED Turin Transportation Design graduate who goes by "apiazzadesign" on social media. By the end of this piece, you'll know what makes the original worth reviving, what Piazza's CGI keeps and changes, and whether a modern Dodge halo car along these lines makes sense.

To be clear up front: this is a digital design exercise. Nothing in the reporting suggests Dodge is building it.

## Why the M4S still grabs attention

The M4S was a real prototype pace car, not a studio fantasy, and that matters. Cars that existed physically, then became screen icons, carry a different kind of nostalgia than pure concepts. *The Wraith* is what turned the car into a cult object, and it's the reason a designer fresh out of school would pick it as a subject decades later.

The lesson for brands is simple: a recognizable silhouette outlives its era. You can recall a low, wedge-style performance car from a single glance, and that is exactly the asset Piazza builds on.

## What Piazza's redesign does

Autoevolution describes the project as a modern hypercar reinterpretation that preserves the original silhouette while pushing the design into futuristic, aero-driven territory. That's the hard balance in any retro revival. Change too little and you've made a tribute; change too much and the name is just a sticker.

The approach here, as reported, is to keep the profile that people remember and let modern aerodynamics do the updating. Autoevolution frames the result as an American grand tourer rendered in CGI, which is a useful reminder of the format's freedom: no crash regulations, no packaging compromises, no cooling budget arguments.

### What to look for in a good retro-futurist render

When you see a revival like this, a few checks separate smart design from fan art:

- **Is the original shape still readable?** If you can identify the car in silhouette alone, the designer respected it.
- **Does the new aero look purposeful?** Vents and wings should suggest airflow, not decoration.
- **Does it suit the brand today?** A revival should feel like it belongs in the manufacturer's current lineup.

## Why Dodge makes the daydream tempting

The timing of the speculation isn't random. Retro-futuristic design is having a moment, with creators and brands mining cult classics for modern EV and hypercar inspiration. Dodge is also in the middle of a performance reboot, including the new Charger and Hurricane-powered variants.

That context is why the M4S feels ripe for a comeback, at least on screen. Dodge has a current performance story to tell, and a halo car is how brands usually tell it.

## Would a Hurricane-powered M4S make sense?

This is my take, not reporting: the idea is fun, and it has a logic. The Hurricane is part of Dodge's current performance lineup, and the original M4S carried "Turbo" in its name. A turbocharged engine in a car called the Turbo Interceptor is a neat narrative echo, even if a hypercar-grade build would demand far more than a Charger's powertrain.

The harder question is business. A low-volume halo car costs real money to engineer, and nothing in the source says Dodge has considered one. Treat it as a thought experiment about what the brand's identity could look like, not a product rumor.

## What this tells you about concept culture

Independent designers now do something manufacturers rarely can: they test public appetite for an old idea at almost zero cost. A render circulates, enthusiasts react, and the response becomes a signal about which nameplates and shapes still carry emotional weight.

The M4S is a strong candidate precisely because its fame came from a film rather than sales numbers. It never had to be practical, so reviving it doesn't require being practical either.

## The takeaway

Next time a retro render goes around, judge it by whether it keeps the soul of the original while earning its modern details. Piazza's M4S passes the first test by holding onto the silhouette. Whether Dodge ever builds anything like it is a separate, and so far unanswered, question.
