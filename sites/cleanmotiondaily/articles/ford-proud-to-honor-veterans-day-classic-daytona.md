Ford Motor Company is putting a new event on the calendar at one of racing's most famous tracks. Per Fox Business, the automaker is launching the inaugural "Proud to Honor Veterans Day Classic" at Daytona on November 11, a NASCAR race paired with a concert headlined by Zac Brown Band. If you follow motorsports or watch how automakers use racing for brand storytelling, here is what the event is and why it stands out.

## What Ford is putting on at Daytona

The core of the event is a NASCAR race featuring six teams, each representing a branch of the U.S. military. A concert with Zac Brown Band as the headliner rounds out the day, so it is built as a combined race-and-music experience rather than a standard race weekend.

The date matters too. November 11 is Veterans Day, and Ford is tying the race directly to the holiday instead of just adding a tribute segment to an existing schedule.

## Who is behind it

Will Ford, Ford's racing general manager, is the face of the effort. He is also the great-great-grandson of founder Henry Ford. Per the report, Ford Racing's general manager said the Veterans Day Classic will unite motorsports and the military community at one of America's most iconic tracks.

The family connection gives the announcement a personal frame. Ford is presenting the event as an extension of the company's longstanding commitment to veterans and military support, with a Ford family member leading the racing side of it.

## Who is racing

The names attached to the event include Joey Logano, Brad Keselowski, and Frankie Muniz, who are competing for a cause. Seeing two established NASCAR names alongside Muniz, who is better known as an actor, shows the event aims to draw both hardcore race fans and casual viewers.

## Where the money goes

The event is connected to the "Proud to Honor Fund," and ticket proceeds support Blue Star Families. That is the practical payoff behind the pageantry: attending or buying a ticket feeds a fundraising effort tied to military families. The coverage I have does not spell out dollar figures or the exact share of proceeds, so check Ford's official event page for specifics before you buy.

## Why Daytona makes sense

Daytona is among the best-known tracks in American motorsports, and that name recognition does work for this kind of event. A tribute held on a stage that fans already treat as significant carries more weight than one staged at a lesser-known venue. That is my read, not a claim from Ford, but the choice of venue and the Veterans Day date clearly aim to make the event feel like a national occasion.

## The bigger picture for Ford and racing

It is also worth watching this as a branding move. Automakers keep looking for ways to make racing relevant beyond horsepower and lap times, and a cause-driven signature event does that. Ford gets to lean on its racing heritage and its history with the military community at once. Whether the Classic becomes an annual fixture is unknown. The "inaugural" label suggests Ford wants it to, but nothing in the reporting confirms future editions.

## What to do next

If you want to be there or follow along, mark November 11, look for Ford's official ticketing and broadcast details, and confirm how your purchase supports the Proud to Honor Fund and Blue Star Families. Those details will tell you more about the event's real impact than any press line.
