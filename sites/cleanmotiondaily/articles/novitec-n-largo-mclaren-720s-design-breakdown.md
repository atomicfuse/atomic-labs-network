A McLaren 720S that looks more like a track weapon than a road car has turned up, and it's one of the most aggressive Novitec builds in recent memory. Autoevolution spotlights the N-Largo-kitted supercar, and the question it poses is a fair one: do you like it? Here's a design breakdown so you can decide for yourself, plus why the build says something about where the 720S stands today.

## What this build is

The car is a McLaren 720S fitted with Novitec's N-Largo widebody package. According to Autoevolution, the build adds widebody aero, center-lock wheels and race-car styling cues. It also wears a custom wrap applied in Vietnam.

That's the whole fact set we have, so we'll stick to it: no power figures or pricing to quote here, and we won't guess at them. What we can do is look at what the visual choices add up to.

## The test: tasteful aggression or too much?

The 720S is already a dramatic car, so a widebody kit is a risky addition. Go too far and you lose the clean shape that made the car appealing. Autoevolution's argument is that this build stays on the right side of that line: extreme to look at, yet still tasteful and visually compelling.

Here's a simple way to judge any tuned supercar, this one included:

- **Does the aero follow the car's lines?** Wider bodywork should look like it grew from the original shape, not like it was bolted on.
- **Do the wheels suit the stance?** Center-lock wheels are a motorsport cue, and they only make sense when the body looks committed to the same idea.
- **Is the finish doing work?** A wrap should emphasize the shape, not fight it.
- **Is there a theme?** The best builds pick one story, in this case race car, and stick to it.

By those measures, the reported combination of widebody aero, center-locks and race-inspired details points in one consistent direction. That's likely why it reads as deliberate rather than overdone. (That's our read of the described spec, not something we've inspected in person.)

## Why center-lock wheels matter visually

Center-lock wheels are held on by a single central nut rather than five or so lug bolts. Even when you're only looking at photos, the look signals racing hardware. On a road-legal McLaren with a wide body, it supports the track-car impression Autoevolution describes without needing extra wings or stickers to do the talking.

## The wrap from Vietnam

The custom wrap was applied in Vietnam, per Autoevolution. Wraps are the reversible part of a build like this, which makes them a popular way to push a car's character hard. Here, the finish helps the aero read clearly rather than hiding it, which is part of why the overall look hangs together.

## Why a 720S still draws attention

The 720S has been replaced by the 750S, so it's now a discontinued model. You might expect that to dull the interest. Instead, high-end tuner projects like this keep the older car in the conversation. Custom widebody exotics also tend to travel well on social media, and a standout build can give a retired performance car a second life.

That's our take on the timing, but it fits the picture: the newer car gets the showroom headlines, while the previous one gets the creative builds.

## So, do you like it?

Taste decides, and plenty of enthusiasts prefer a stock 720S. But if you're going to take a supercar this far, this is a useful template for doing it with restraint: one clear theme, parts that agree with each other, and a finish that supports the shape.

Next time you see a wild widebody build, run the four-point check above before you react. It will tell you quickly whether you're looking at a cohesive design or just a lot of parts.
