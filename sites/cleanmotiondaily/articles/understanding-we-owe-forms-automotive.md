When purchasing a vehicle, the last thing you want is to deal with hidden damage and unmet promises. That's exactly what happened to a car flipper who bought a Ford Bronco, only to discover a significant scratch that the dealership promised to repair but later inadequately addressed. This incident highlights a crucial tool in the car buying process: the 'We Owe' form.

## What is a 'We Owe' Form?
A 'We Owe' form is a document that outlines promises made by the dealership during the vehicle purchase process. It serves as an acknowledgment that the dealer agrees to take certain actions, such as fixing damage, providing additional accessories, or completing any repairs before finalizing the sale. This written agreement holds the dealership accountable for their commitments.

## How to Utilize 'We Owe' Forms Effectively
### Clarity in Wording
The effectiveness of a 'We Owe' form often hinges on how precisely it is worded. Ensure that the form clearly states what the dealership is obligated to do. For example, instead of a vague promise like "we will fix the scratch," the form should specify, "we will repair the 3-inch gash on the driver's door by [specific date]." This eliminates ambiguity and gives you a stronger position should any disputes arise.

### Timing is Everything
You should never sign any purchase agreement while the car is still damaged. Not only does this hinder your negotiation ability, but it also puts you at a disadvantage. If you notice damages, insist on having them documented in a 'We Owe' form before finalizing any contracts. This means you can negotiate a lower price or demand more immediate repair actions.

### Document Everything
Maintaining thorough documentation of the condition of the vehicle at time of purchase is critical. Take photos of any damage, including the scratch on the Bronco, and keep a record of all communications with the dealer. This evidence can support your claims if the dealership fails to uphold their agreements.

## The Impact on Resale Value
Poor repairs or lack of repairs can impact your vehicle's resale value. If a dealership makes a subpar attempt at fixing a scratch, you might face difficulty when it's time to sell or trade in the vehicle. Properly documented complaints and repair promises can serve as leverage to negotiate better resale conditions down the line.

## Pressure Through Reviews and Surveys
Negative reviews and feedback in manufacturer surveys play a crucial role in holding dealerships accountable. If you encounter issues like those faced by the Bronco buyer, sharing your experience can influence the dealership's reputation and incentivize them to make things right. Manufacturer surveys especially can have more weight, as they reflect customer experiences and can lead to dealership repercussions if feedback is consistently poor.

## Red Flags to Watch For
While navigating the purchasing process, be on the lookout for red flags that indicate a deal may turn sour. These can include:
- Late appointments or delays in scheduled repairs
- Inconsistent explanations regarding vehicle condition
- Justifications related to budgets when discussing repairs or services

Being aware of these potential warning signs can save you a lot of hassle.

## Conclusion
Buying a car, especially one that may have hidden damage, requires vigilance and knowledge of your rights. A well-structured 'We Owe' form can serve as a crucial tool in ensuring that the dealership upholds their promises. By understanding its importance, documenting everything, and being aware of potential red flags, you can better protect yourself in the car-buying process. Keep these strategies in mind to ensure your experience is smooth and your investment is safeguarded.
