You can build a longevity-minded morning drink from three things you may already own: coffee, 100% cacao and raw honey. Those are the three foods a Florida functional medicine doctor says he consumes daily to support healthy aging, per a Fox News report. Below: what he claims, where the evidence ends and opinion begins, and how to make the mug without overdoing sugar or caffeine.

## What the doctor actually says

Per Fox News, the physician points to coffee, 100% cacao and raw honey as daily staples, citing antioxidants and polyphenols. The report frames them as possibly offering antioxidant, cardiovascular, immune and gut-health benefits when they're part of a sustainable diet.

Notice the hedging: "may," "support," "part of a diet." This is one doctor's personal routine and reasoning, not a clinical trial. Treat it as a reasonable starting point for a habit, not a proven recipe for a longer life.

## Why this trio works as a drink

The appeal is practical. All three are things people already like, and none requires a supplement aisle or a subscription. They also happen to combine into one cup, which makes the habit easy to repeat. A longevity routine you actually keep beats an optimal one you abandon in week two.

## A simple morning mug

This is my adaptation, not the doctor's recipe. The Fox News piece doesn't give measurements, so these are starting points you should adjust to taste and tolerance.

1. **Brew your coffee your usual way.** Drip, pour-over or Moka pot all work. A medium roast keeps the cup balanced rather than ashy next to cacao.
2. **Stir in 100% cacao.** Start with a teaspoon of unsweetened cacao powder. Whisk it into a splash of hot coffee first to make a paste, then add the rest. Dumping it on top leaves clumps.
3. **Sweeten lightly with raw honey.** Begin with half a teaspoon. Honey dissolves best when the coffee is hot but not boiling.
4. **Taste, then adjust.** Cacao is bitter, so it's tempting to compensate with honey. Resist; that's how a "healthy" mug turns into a dessert.

## Coffee: the one you already have

Coffee is the easy part of the trio, since it's likely already in your routine. The doctor's case rests on its antioxidant and polyphenol content. What's in your cup depends on your beans and brew, so it's worth buying fresh and brewing consistently rather than treating coffee as a delivery system.

The practical caution is caffeine. If your mug is on top of other coffee, an energy drink or a late-day habit, adding cacao pushes your intake higher, since cacao is its own source of stimulation. Keep an eye on sleep and jitters, and cut back where you need to.

## Cacao: the label matters

The doctor specifies 100% cacao, and that detail matters. A bar or powder labeled cacao can still be loaded with sugar, which undercuts the point. Check the ingredient list: you want cacao, and not much else.

Expect it to taste bitter and earthy. That's normal. Coffee's roasted notes and a touch of honey smooth it out, much like a mocha without the syrup.

## Raw honey: a small spoon, not a pour

Honey is the sweetener in this lineup, and it's still sugar. The report ties raw honey to immune and gut-health benefits, but a small spoonful is where the sensible version of this habit lives. If you currently take two sugars, one measured teaspoon of honey is a step in the right direction; if you take your coffee black, you don't need to start.

## What's evidence and what's opinion

Here's how I'd separate the claims:

- **The doctor's routine** is personal practice. It's a data point about what he does, not proof it works.
- **Antioxidant and polyphenol content** is the stated reasoning. That's a plausible mechanism, but a plausible mechanism isn't the same as a longer life.
- **Cardiovascular, immune and gut benefits** are described as possible in the report. Read them as "may help," not "will."
- **Overall diet quality** is the caveat the report itself emphasizes. No mug makes up for the rest of your day.

If a food-as-longevity claim sounds too tidy, that's your cue to look for the caveats. Here they're stated up front, which is a point in the piece's favor.

## Keeping it sustainable

The appeal of this trio is that it's affordable and simple, the opposite of expensive biohacking. To keep it that way:

- Set a ceiling on caffeine and stick to it.
- Measure the honey with a real spoon for the first week.
- Buy cacao with a one-item ingredient list.
- Treat the mug as one part of a normal, varied diet.

## The takeaway

Tomorrow morning, make one mug: your usual coffee, a teaspoon of 100% cacao, half a teaspoon of raw honey. Taste it, note how you feel by midday, and keep it only if you'd genuinely want it again next week.
