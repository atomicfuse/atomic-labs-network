Forbes recently rounded up 17 fall cocktails, spanning whiskey sours, hot toddies, martinis and margaritas, with seasonal flavors from apple to pumpkin. We only have a handful of those 17 named in the summary we worked from, so this isn't a recap of the full list. Instead, here's how to use the ideas Forbes highlighted to plan an easy, coffee-minded fall drink night at home.

## Start With the Cozy Classics

The named picks in Forbes' roundup are familiar ones: whiskey sours, hot toddies, martinis and margaritas. That's useful for hosting. You don't need a cabinet full of obscure bottles to make a night feel seasonal. A classic you already know can be nudged toward autumn with one ingredient.

The seasonal flavors Forbes points to are apple and pumpkin. Think of those as the upgrade layer, not the whole recipe. Keep the base drink you can make without checking a recipe, then swap or add one fall element.

## Pick One Hot Drink for Cold Nights

The hot toddy is the obvious warm option on the list. Its appeal is that it's served hot, so it suits the first properly chilly evenings, and it asks very little of the host.

If you're already set up for coffee, you have an advantage: a kettle, a way to hold water at temperature, and mugs that handle heat. A hot drink station works well because guests can build their own cup while you stay out of the kitchen.

## Where Coffee Fits In

Forbes' summary doesn't mention coffee drinks, so this part is our take. A few places coffee can earn a spot at a fall drink night:

- **Martini night:** A coffee-forward martini is a natural next step for anyone who likes the format. Brew strong and chill it fully before mixing so the drink isn't diluted.
- **Dessert pairing:** Serve a small pour of coffee alongside a spiked drink so guests who want to taper off have something satisfying.
- **The no-alcohol option:** A mocktail swap is easy if you already have good beans. Pair cold-brew concentrate or strong drip coffee with apple or warm spices for a drink that still feels like part of the event.

These are suggestions, not recipes from the Forbes list, so adjust ratios to your taste.

## Apple or Pumpkin: Choose a Lane

Apple and pumpkin are the two flavors named, and you'll get a better menu if you pick a lead instead of using both everywhere. Apple tends to read bright and crisp, which pairs naturally with a sour. Pumpkin leans richer and more dessert-like, which suits a creamier or spiced drink. That's our read on the flavors, not a claim from the roundup.

## Build a Short Menu

Seventeen drinks is a lot for one night. Three is plenty:

1. **One shaken classic**, such as a whiskey sour with an apple twist.
2. **One hot drink**, such as a hot toddy.
3. **One alcohol-free option** with a coffee or apple base.

That covers different tastes and temperatures without turning you into a full-time bartender. If you can prep a pitcher or a kettle of something ahead of time, do it. The goal is that the cozy night stays cozy for you too.

## Keep It Simple

The Forbes list works because it leans on comfort and recognizable drinks rather than complexity. Take the same approach: choose one flavor, one hot option and one no-alcohol swap, and make them well. Next chilly evening, set the kettle on and start with a hot drink you can make on autopilot.
