Aldi is putting a £19.99 Crane Pilates Board into its Specialbuys aisle this week, pitched as a budget alternative to premium reformer-style machines that can cost up to £1,999, according to The Sun. That's a gap of £1,979.01, or roughly the £1.8k saving the outlet's headline mentions once you compare against a slightly cheaper luxury model. Below, you'll see how to judge whether the cheap option fits what you actually want, and how to avoid paying twice.

The Aldi board costs about 1% of the top-end machine (19.99 ÷ 1,999 = 0.01). Even a luxury machine priced somewhat lower than £1,999 would still cost many times more than the Aldi board.

One caution: these are very different products. The Sun describes the Aldi item as a budget alternative, or dupe, to reformer machines. A £20 board and a £1,999 machine are unlikely to offer the same range of exercises or the same feel. The price comparison is real; the like-for-like comparison is not guaranteed. Check the product listing and, if you can, the box in store before assuming it replaces a reformer.

## What you're really comparing it to: classes

For most people the true alternative isn't owning a £1,999 machine. It's paying for reformer classes. The Sun's framing is that Reformer Pilates is booming in the UK, but classes and equipment both remain costly, which is why cheaper dupes appeal.

I don't have Aldi's or any studio's class prices from the report, so work out your own break-even with this formula:

**Break-even sessions = board price ÷ your price per class**

Example using a hypothetical £15 class (swap in your local price): £19.99 ÷ £15 = 1.3. If you'd otherwise pay £15, the board pays for itself after two sessions you skip. That only counts if you'd really use the board in place of those classes, and it can't give you an instructor's feedback.

## Is it a smart entry point or a trend buy?

Use these checks before you buy.

### Will you use it more than a handful of times?
At £19.99 the downside is small. A board that's used three times is still cheaper than most single purchases in a fitness shop. The risk isn't the price, it's the pile of unused gear.

### Do you want to test the trend or replace the studio?
If you're curious about Reformer Pilates and want to try home movement cheaply, a £20 board is a low-stakes experiment. If you love the specific resistance-and-carriage feel of a reformer, a board is a different experience, and you may end up booking classes anyway. Treat the £19.99 as the cost of finding out.

### Do you have space and a surface for it?
Before buying, measure the area you'd use. Check the dimensions and any weight or usage guidance on Aldi's product page or packaging, since those details aren't in the report.

## Timing: Specialbuys don't stick around

Specialbuys are timed releases, and The Sun's piece is tied to this week's launch. Popular items can sell out, and once they go, they typically don't return on a set schedule. If you want one, check your local store's stock rather than assuming it will be there next week. Also look at Aldi's returns terms at the till so you know what happens if the board isn't what you expected.

## A simple way to decide

1. Write down what you'd pay for a single reformer class near you.
2. Divide £19.99 by that number to get your break-even.
3. Ask honestly whether you'd do that many home sessions.
4. If yes, buy it. If you'd rather have a teacher, spend the money on a class instead.

The saving of nearly £2,000 is only a saving if you wanted the £1,999 machine in the first place. Decide what you actually want to do, then pay the smallest price that gets you there.
