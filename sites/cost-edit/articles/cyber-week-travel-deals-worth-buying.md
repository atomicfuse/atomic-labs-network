Cyber Week is crowded with travel deals, and a big percentage-off banner tells you very little about whether you're saving money. BuzzFeed's shopping team published a frequent traveler's list of 28 Cyber Week deals, and seven of them come with specific discounts we can work with. Below, you'll get those seven, what each is for, and a quick method to check whether any of them is a good price for you.

One caution first: the source gives discount percentages, not list prices, and we don't have dollar figures or retailers for these. So treat the percentages as a starting point and confirm the actual price yourself before you buy.

The list's description also says the Kindle is worth grabbing now because prices have gone up recently, per BuzzFeed. That is the strongest timing argument on the list, and the one most worth verifying.

## Start with the Kindle if you read on trips

The 2024 Kindle Paperwhite at 35% off is the clearest case of a purchase that replaces something you already pay for. A stack of paperbacks takes up bag space and weight, and a single device holds the lot.

To check the deal, find the Paperwhite's regular price at the store you trust, then do the math: regular price x 0.65 = the sale price you should see. If the number at checkout is higher, the listing is using an inflated "original" price or a different model. Make sure it says 2024 Paperwhite.

## Big-ticket gear: the suitcase and backpack

The Away Topsider suitcase and the Beis travel backpack are both 25% off. These are the items where the discount is largest in dollar terms, so they matter most to your budget.

A useful way to compare them is cost per trip. Divide the sale price by the number of trips you realistically take per year, then by the years you'd keep it. A bag you use six times a year for five years is 30 trips. At a hypothetical $300, that works out to $10 per trip. At 25% off, the same bag would be $225, or $7.50 per trip. (Those prices are only an example of the math, not actual prices.)

If you fly twice a year, a cheaper bag may beat a premium one on this measure. If you travel constantly, the pricier one can win.

The two also suit different travelers. A backpack works as a personal item or carry-on, while the suitcase suits people who check or roll bags. Buying one that doesn't match how you fly is the most common way a good discount becomes a waste.

## Small items with surprisingly good savings

### Plackers Floss Picks, 49% off

This is the deepest discount on the list, and it's on a consumable. A 600-count pack is a household stock-up item, not just a travel buy. Compare it by unit price: sale price divided by 600 gives your cost per pick. Check that against the unit price of the smaller pack you normally buy. If you'd use 600 picks within a year or two, a bulk pack is usually the cheaper route; if they'd sit in a drawer for years, it isn't.

### Trtl travel pillow, 25% off

A neck pillow is worth buying only if you'd actually use it. If you sleep on flights and usually wake up sore, a 25% discount makes it a cheap experiment. If you never sleep upright, skip it regardless of the price.

### Tumella windproof travel umbrella, up to 50% off

Note the words "up to." The 50% figure may apply only to certain colors or sizes, so check the exact version in your cart before assuming you're getting half off. A compact umbrella is a small spend, but it's one you can often find cheaply year-round.

### Garnet Hill cashmere wrap, 40% off

Planes run cold, and a wrap doubles as a blanket and a scarf. It's also the most discretionary item here. If you're buying it as a gift or a treat, the 40% is welcome. If you're buying it to save money, it won't.

## A five-minute check before you buy

1. **Write down your regular price.** Use what you've seen at the store, not the "was" price on the sale page.
2. **Do the math.** Multiply by one minus the discount. Confirm it matches the checkout total.
3. **Add the extras.** Shipping, tax, and return fees can erase a small discount.
4. **Note the date and store.** If you're comparing across retailers, record them so you can verify later.
5. **Check the return window.** Holiday purchases often have extended returns, but confirm before you buy.

## Skip anything you wouldn't buy at full price

The discounts on this list are real in the sense that the retailers advertise them, but a deal only saves money on something you were going to buy anyway. Pick the one or two items that fix a problem you actually have, confirm the price, and leave the other five in the cart you never open.
