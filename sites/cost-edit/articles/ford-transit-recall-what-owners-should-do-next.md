Ford is recalling nearly 61,000 2026 Transit vans because a software glitch could cause trailer lights and other features to stop working, which raises the risk of a crash, according to Fox Business. If you own, lease, or manage one of these vans, this guide covers what to check, what to expect from the process, and how to limit the money you lose if your van is sidelined.

The details below come from Fox Business's report. Where I go beyond it, I'll say so, because recall specifics (affected build dates, repair timelines) are best confirmed with Ford or the government's recall database.

## What the recall covers

Per Fox Business, the recall affects nearly 61,000 2026 Ford Transit vans. The problem is a software issue that can shut down key features. Trailer lights are the example named, along with "other features." The risk is a higher chance of a crash.

The report as summarized to me does not list every affected feature or the exact repair steps. So don't assume your van is fine just because your trailer lights work today. Check your specific vehicle.

## Step 1: Confirm your van is included

Not every 2026 Transit necessarily falls under the recall, so verify it.

1. Find your 17-character VIN. It's on the lower corner of the driver's side dashboard (visible through the windshield) and on your registration and insurance card.
2. Enter it in the recall lookup on NHTSA.gov (the U.S. government's vehicle safety site) or on Ford's owner site.
3. Write down the result and the date you checked.

This takes a few minutes and costs nothing. For fleets, run every VIN, not just a sample.

## Step 2: Know what a recall costs you

In general, safety recall repairs are done by the manufacturer's dealers at no charge to the owner. That's the standard U.S. practice, but confirm the specifics for your van when you book. The real cost is usually not the repair but the downtime.

## Step 3: Book the fix and stagger it

Call your dealer's service department and ask these questions:

- Is the software update available now, or are parts or the fix still pending?
- How long will the van be in the shop?
- Can the work be done by appointment so the van isn't down longer than needed?
- Can you get a loaner, or any help with a rental, if the wait is long?

For a fleet, book one or two vans at a time rather than all at once. Prioritize the vans that tow trailers, since trailer lights are the feature Fox Business specifically named.

## Step 4: Check the basics until it's fixed

Until your van is repaired, treat the safety features as less reliable than usual. This is my practical advice, not guidance from the recall notice, so also follow whatever interim instructions Ford provides.

- Before each trailer trip, test the brake lights, turn signals, and running lights with a second person watching.
- Check again after any stop, since a software fault could appear mid-trip.
- Tell drivers on your team what to look for and who to call.
- If lights fail, stop safely and don't continue towing.

A two-minute walk-around costs nothing compared with a crash or a citation.

## Why software recalls hit work vans hard

The general trend is that vehicles run more on software, so a single glitch can affect several features at once. That's an analysis point, not a finding from the Fox Business report. For a commercial van, it means a recall can stop a job, a delivery route, or a trailer haul, not just inconvenience a commuter.

If you're shopping for a work van, factor this in. Ask how a dealer handles recall scheduling, how close the service center is, and whether loaners are available. A van that's cheaper by a few hundred dollars but hours from the nearest service bay can cost more in lost days.

## Keep a recall paper trail

Small businesses should keep a simple record for each van:

- Date you checked the VIN and the result
- Date you booked and completed the repair
- The repair order from the dealer
- Any rental or downtime costs you incurred

That file helps if you need to ask Ford or the dealer about reimbursement, and it supports your records if an insurer or customer ever asks whether the van was properly maintained. Whether reimbursement is available depends on Ford's policies and your circumstances, so ask rather than assume.

## The one thing to do today

Look up your van's VIN on NHTSA.gov right now. If it's on the list, book the repair this week, and test your trailer lights before every haul until it's done.
