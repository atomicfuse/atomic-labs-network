Polymarket's invite code TIMES promises $25 in trading credit after you deposit $10 and make a qualifying trade, per a promotional write-up from the El Paso Times. That sounds like a $15 gain on paper. Before you treat it as free money, this guide walks through what the offer actually requires, what the credit is, and the questions to answer before you put in a dollar.

One caution up front: the details below come from a promotional article, not from Polymarket's own terms. Check the current terms inside the app before you deposit, because promo rules can change.

## What the offer says

According to the El Paso Times piece, new users can:

1. Sign up using invite code TIMES.
2. Deposit $10.
3. Make a qualifying trade.
4. Receive $25 in trading credit.

The key phrase is "trading credit." It is not described as cash you can withdraw right away. The source material does not say what happens to the credit after you use it, whether it expires, or how winnings from it are handled. Those are the details that decide whether the offer is worth your time, and you should find them in the official terms.

## The math, shown simply

Here is the best-case way to read the offer, using the figures reported:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Your deposit | $10 |
| Trading credit offered | $25 |
| Total available to trade | $35 |
| Gross extra value (credit minus deposit) | $15 |

That $15 is a ceiling, not a guarantee. If you place a trade and lose, you can lose the money you put in. A deposit of $10 is the most you are risking with your own cash in this setup, but only if you trade just that amount and nothing more. Treat the $10 as money you are prepared to lose.

## What Polymarket is, in plain terms

Per the source, Polymarket is a prediction market. Instead of betting against a sportsbook, you trade event contracts with other users. That peer-to-peer structure is the main difference the article highlights for people used to traditional sportsbooks.

The promotion leans on a busy November sports calendar: NFL, college football, NBA and college basketball. Polymarket also lists markets on crypto and politics, per the tags on the piece.

## Sportsbook vs. prediction market: what to compare

The source frames Polymarket as an alternative for sports fans. For a fair comparison, check these points for any platform before you sign up:

- **Promo terms:** Is the bonus cash or credit? Does it expire? What counts as a qualifying trade?
- **Deposit and withdrawal method:** The source says it covers how deposits and withdrawals work, but those specifics are not given here. Look for fees and processing times.
- **Availability:** The article discusses where Polymarket is available in the U.S. Confirm your state before depositing, since rules vary by location.
- **Legality and risk:** Event contracts are a newer area, and you should understand the rules where you live.

If a platform cannot give you a clear answer on any of these, wait.

## A quick pre-deposit checklist

Run through this before you enter the code:

1. **Confirm eligibility.** Make sure the service is available where you live.
2. **Read the bonus terms.** Look for the minimum trade size, the expiration date, and any limits on withdrawing credit.
3. **Set a loss limit.** If the $10 is gone, stop. Do not top up to chase it.
4. **Know how contracts pay out.** Before you trade, understand what you win or lose if the event resolves each way.
5. **Note the date.** Promotions like this tend to run for limited periods. The source listing was published November 17, 2026, so verify the offer is still live.

## Is it worth it?

It depends on what you are buying. If you already planned to try prediction markets and can afford to lose $10, the credit lowers your cost of trying one. If you are mostly drawn by the bonus, remember that a promotion is not a reason to start trading. A $15 upside is small next to a $10 downside you could lose in a single trade.

The smart move for most readers is to treat this as entertainment spending with a fixed budget, the same way you would think about a streaming subscription: decide the amount, accept it may not come back, and stick to it.

Before you enter any invite code, read the terms and decide on your $10 limit first.
