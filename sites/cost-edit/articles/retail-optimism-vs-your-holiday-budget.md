On the Monday before Thanksgiving week wrapped up, National Retail Federation President and CEO Matt Shay told Fox Business's "Varney & Co." that consumers are in a pretty good place. According to the segment's description, he discussed expectations for 183 million Thanksgiving weekend shoppers and the growing use of artificial intelligence tools in holiday retail.

That's the industry's read. Your read is the one that matters for your wallet. Below is a practical way to set the headline aside, test your own situation, and spend on purpose this season.

## What the retail industry's optimism does and doesn't tell you

Shay's view, as summarized by Fox Business, is that shoppers remain in a solid position despite economic pressures, with confidence and purchasing power still supporting the market. Retailers, investors, and brands watch this signal closely because it hints at whether people will keep spending or pull back.

Here is our analysis, not Shay's: a trade group's national outlook is an average across a huge number of households. It can be accurate and still tell you nothing about whether your own December is comfortable. Treat it as context, not permission.

The summary also points to inflation, interest rates, and economic uncertainty as forces shaping spending. Those are the things to check in your own numbers.

## Step 1: Set one number before you shop

Pick a total holiday cap, covering gifts, food, travel, and extras, and write it down. Without a cap, every individual purchase looks reasonable.

A simple way to build it:

| Line | Your amount |
|---|---|
| Gifts | $___ |
| Holiday food and hosting | $___ |
| Travel | $___ |
| Extras (cards, wrapping, decor) | $___ |
| **Total cap** | **$___** |

Then ask a hard question: could you pay this from cash on hand, without adding to a balance that will carry interest into the new year? If not, lower the cap until the answer is yes.

## Step 2: Split the cap per person

Divide the gift portion by the number of people on your list. If you have $400 for gifts and 10 people, that's $40 each on average. Adjust up or down for closer recipients, but make sure the adjusted amounts still add up to $400.

Showing the math this way keeps small add-ons from piling up unnoticed.

## Step 3: Compare prices on the same basis

When you check a deal, compare like with like:

- **Total price, not sticker price.** Add shipping, tax, and any membership fee needed to get the deal.
- **Same unit.** For food and household goods, compare price per ounce, per count, or per load, not per package.
- **Same item.** Check model numbers. Some sale versions differ from the standard product.
- **Date and store.** Note when and where you saw the price so you can verify it later.

Example of the math: a $24.00 pack of 48 batteries is $0.50 each. A $14.00 pack of 24 is about $0.58 each. The bigger pack wins on unit price, but only if you'll actually use 48. (These figures are an illustration of the method, not current store prices.)

## Step 4: Use AI tools carefully

The Fox Business description notes growing adoption of AI tools in holiday retail. If you use them for gift ideas or price comparison, treat the output as a starting list. Confirm the price, availability, and return policy on the retailer's own page before buying, and don't let a chatbot's suggestion push you past your per-person amount.

## Step 5: Check your recurring costs while you're at it

Holiday budgets get squeezed most by bills that don't pause. Before the season peaks:

1. List subscriptions and cancel anything you haven't used in the past month.
2. Check whether any streaming, internet, or mobile promo rate ends in the next two months, and note the renewal price.
3. Watch for free trials started for holiday viewing, since they often convert to paid plans automatically.

Even $30 a month freed up is $360 a year, which can fund a good part of next year's gift budget.

## Step 6: Decide what "a good place" means for you

Use a quick self-check:

- Are you paying all bills on time without borrowing?
- Do you have some savings left after essentials?
- Is your credit card balance flat or falling?

Three yeses suggest you're matching the industry's mood and can spend within your cap. Any no suggests trimming the plan, however upbeat the national picture sounds.

## The takeaway

Let the national outlook be background noise. Write down your holiday cap today, divide it by the people on your list, and stick to the number.
