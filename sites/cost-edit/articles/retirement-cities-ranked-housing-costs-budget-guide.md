U.S. News & World Report has ranked the 10 best places to retire in the U.S., as reported by Fox Business. The ranking weighs housing affordability, quality of life, healthcare and the job market. Midland, Michigan, comes in first. The list is a useful starting point, but the real value is learning how to turn a ranking into a number you can check against your own budget. This guide shows you how.

## Read the Ranking by Budget Type

The ranking does not sort cities by budget, but you can. Using only what the report establishes:

### Lower-cost small towns

Altoona, Pennsylvania, is described as an ultra-affordable small town at a $115,000 median home value. Weirton, West Virginia, and Paducah, Kentucky, are also on the list. They are the places to start if keeping housing costs low is your first priority. Confirm their current prices before you count on them.

### Higher-cost, higher-amenity suburbs

The Woodlands and Spring, Texas, rank fourth and fifth. The Woodlands is the pricier one at $473,000. These suit a retiree with more room in the budget who wants a larger, wealthier community.

### Florida options

Homosassa Springs (second) and Palm Coast (tenth) are the two Florida entries. If you want a warm-weather retirement, they are the two to compare first.

## Why a Rank Isn't a Budget

A home's median value is only one line of your retirement spending. Before you commit to any city, price out the recurring costs that don't show up in a ranking:

- **Housing:** mortgage or rent, property tax, homeowners insurance, HOA fees
- **Utilities:** electric, water, internet and mobile service
- **Groceries and takeout:** check prices at the stores you would actually use
- **Healthcare:** premiums and what nearby providers cost you
- **Transportation:** whether you would still need two cars

The ranking covers healthcare as one factor, but it cannot tell you what your own plan would cost in each town.

## A Simple Way to Compare Two Cities

Pick two finalists and build a monthly estimate for each, using the same units (dollars per month) for every line. Use real quotes dated to the day you collect them, and write down the location.

| Monthly line | City A | City B |
|---|---|---|
| Mortgage or rent | | |
| Property tax (annual ÷ 12) | | |
| Home insurance (annual ÷ 12) | | |
| Utilities and internet | | |
| Groceries | | |
| Health premiums and expected costs | | |
| **Total** | | |

Two habits keep this honest. First, convert annual bills to monthly by dividing by 12, so nothing is hidden. Second, for internet and mobile plans, record the renewal rate and not only the promo price, since the promo rate can end and raise your total.

## Test Before You Move

A ranking can't tell you whether you will enjoy a place. Spend a week or two there before buying, ideally in the season that is hardest on you, whether that is summer heat or winter cold. Visit a grocery store, call a local internet provider for a quote, and look at what a home comparable to yours would cost. Those small checks cost little compared with an expensive move you regret.
