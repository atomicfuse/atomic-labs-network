If you are a parent in Nigeria paying a child's university fees, you could be covering a bill that a student loan has already covered. Per a report from Vanguardngr, parents' and students' groups are raising concerns that some students receive NELFUND student loan support while still collecting tuition money from their families. This guide shows how to keep your own household from being part of that overlap, using the steps those groups are reportedly recommending.

I can only work from what that report covers, so there are no price figures or payment amounts here. The checks below cost nothing but a few conversations.

## What "double dipping" means here

In this context, double dipping describes a student whose tuition is supported by the student loan scheme and who also gets tuition fees from a parent. The same bill is funded twice, and the student ends up holding the difference.

The Student Loan Scheme was set up, in the report's words, "to give access to higher education to students who would otherwise drop out because of inability to pay fees." That is the scheme's purpose. Money that goes to a student who has already been covered is money that is not reaching someone who needs it, and the concern raised is that misuse could undermine trust in the scheme.

The report does not say how widespread the practice is, so treat it as a concern raised by stakeholders, not a measured figure.

## Where the confusion starts

Families often assume the other side is handling the bill. A student may have applied for a loan while a parent is still planning to pay fees as usual. Unless someone talks to the other, both can end up funding the same term.

You can close that gap without accusing anyone. Treat it like any shared household bill: before you pay, confirm whether it is already paid.

## A simple check before you pay any fee

These steps are my practical suggestions, built around the transparency theme in the report.

1. **Ask your child directly.** Ask whether they applied for NELFUND support this session and whether any approval has come through.
2. **Ask what the loan covers.** Get a clear answer on whether it is meant to cover tuition for the current session.
3. **Check with the school's bursary or fees office.** Ask whether the fee shows as paid or pending before you transfer anything. Request a receipt or written confirmation either way.
4. **Keep a record.** Note the date, who you spoke to, and what they told you. If a payment is later disputed, you have a trail.
5. **Pay only the balance.** If any part is already covered, pay only what remains and get that documented.

## What parents' and students' groups are recommending

Per the report, the groups, including NANS (the student body named in the headline), have proposed three measures to curb the practice.

### Notify parents

The first idea is that parents be notified when their child receives loan support. That would take the guesswork out of the check above, because the information would come to you instead of depending on your child telling you.

### Publish the beneficiaries

The second is publishing who has received support. Transparency is the theme running through the report. Public lists would let schools, families, and the wider public see where funds are going.

### Re-educate stakeholders

The third is re-educating stakeholders about what the scheme is for and how it should be used. This is aimed at confusion as much as misconduct, since some students and parents may simply not understand how the loan and family support are supposed to fit together.

I would not read these as settled policy. They are recommendations from the groups quoted, and the report does not say any have been adopted.

## Why this matters for your budget

Nigeria is expanding its student loan programme to widen access to tertiary education. At the same time, fees and living costs are pressuring families. When money is tight, an unnoticed duplicate payment is a real loss. It is also a loss for the scheme, since overlapping funding can erode confidence in a programme meant to keep students enrolled.

### If you have already paid and loan support also arrived

The report does not describe a refund process, so I cannot tell you one exists. Start with the school's fees office, show your receipt, and ask in writing what happens to a duplicate payment. Do not assume the money will be credited to a later session unless they confirm it.

## A conversation worth having at home

The cleanest fix is not technical. Sit down with your child before each session and agree on one written line: who is paying which fee, from which source, and by when. If a loan is in play, say so. Students who spend support money on things other than what it was meant for are the heart of the concern, and a plain family agreement makes that harder.

## The takeaway

Before you send a single naira toward tuition, confirm with the school's fees office what has already been paid and who paid it.
