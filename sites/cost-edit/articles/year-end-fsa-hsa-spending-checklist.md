Year-end is when leftover FSA dollars start to feel like a ticking clock. Fox News recently rounded up 18 health and wellness products it pitched as buys for FSA and HSA money before the year runs out. That list is a useful starting point, but a list of products isn't a plan. This guide shows you how to check what you actually have, filter the list down to what fits your life, and avoid the classic mistake of spending money just because it's about to disappear.

## Step 1: Check what you actually have and when it expires

Before you shop, spend ten minutes on the boring part. The right move depends on which account you hold.

- **Look up your balance** in your FSA or HSA portal or app.
- **Check your plan's deadline.** FSA rules vary by employer plan, and some plans offer extra time or a limited carryover. Your plan administrator or HR team can tell you which applies to you. The Fox News roundup frames year-end as a deadline for many FSA users, but it's your plan documents that decide.
- **Know that HSAs work differently.** The Fox News piece pitches HSA purchases alongside FSA ones, but the pressure to spend by December is mostly an FSA issue. If you have an HSA, you generally aren't forced into a year-end spending rush, so confirm with your provider before treating it as a deadline.

Write down one number: the dollars at risk. That's your real shopping budget.

## Step 2: Confirm eligibility before you pay

The Fox News list is a roundup, and eligibility can depend on the specific product and your plan. Some items on a list like this may need a letter of medical necessity or may be eligible only in certain versions or through certain retailers. Before checkout:

1. Look for an FSA/HSA-eligible label on the product page, or check your plan's eligible-expense list.
2. Pay with your FSA/HSA card where possible, since it tends to be the easiest way to confirm eligibility at the point of sale.
3. Save the receipt in case your plan asks for documentation.

## Step 4: Run the real-cost math on the big-ticket picks

This is where leftover-funds shopping goes wrong. A device bought with pre-tax dollars still costs you the full amount of the item in value terms, and it may carry ongoing costs. Before buying a wearable, a smart scale, or a membership, add up:

| Cost to check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Purchase price | Your starting number |
| Subscription or membership fee | Some trackers and wellness services may charge recurring fees. Check the product page for details |
| Replacement parts or refills | Items like nasal-rinse supplies or patches are repeat purchases |
| Whether it's eligible at that price | Bundles and versions can differ |

The math is simple: total first-year cost = upfront price + (monthly fee × 12) + refills. If that total is more than your leftover balance, the rest comes out of pocket, and you should ask whether you'd pay that without the FSA deadline pushing you.

For memberships and subscriptions, also decide now whether you'll keep paying once the FSA money is gone. A recurring charge you drop in February costs you less than one that quietly renews.

You don't need all 18. Pick based on your life:

- **Under about a few dozen dollars left:** stick with consumables you'll use, like sunscreen, hand sanitizer, nasal strips, or a Navage starter bundle.
- **Managing blood pressure or tracking health at home:** the Omron monitor is the most purpose-built item on the list.
- **Sore from workouts:** TheraGun Prime, TheraCup, or the Hyperice knee device.
- **Sitting all day:** the chair massager or the heated and cooling eye massager.
- **Overdue for new frames:** glasses are a classic use for leftover vision funds, and GlassesUSA is the option named in the roundup.

## A quick rule for leftover money

If you wouldn't buy it at full price in March, it's probably not a good use of money in December. Fox News pitches the list as a way to turn remaining healthcare dollars into practical upgrades, and the holiday season makes some of these natural gifts too. Just keep the test honest: buy what you'll use, confirm it's eligible, and skip the gadget that only looks good because the balance is about to vanish.

Open your FSA portal today, write down your deadline and balance, and pick one or two items from the category that fits you. That's the whole plan.
