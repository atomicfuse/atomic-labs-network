Fiber is having a moment. Wellness circles have even coined a term for piling it on: "fibermaxxing." Per Women's Health, fiber is healthy and most people do need more of it, but ramping up too aggressively can upset your gut. By the end of this article, you'll know the signs you may have overdone it and how to bring fiber into hearty, comforting dinners at a pace your stomach can handle.

To be clear, Women's Health describes eating too much fiber as uncommon, but possible. This isn't a reason to fear a bowl of lentil soup. It's a reason to go gently.

## Signs You May Be Getting Too Much Fiber

According to Women's Health, the signals that you may be overdoing it include:

- **Diarrhea**
- **Constipation**
- **Bloating**
- **Loss of appetite**

Yes, constipation and diarrhea are both on the list, which can be confusing. If your digestion has turned unpredictable after a big change in how you eat, fiber is one possible culprit worth considering. If symptoms persist or worry you, check in with your doctor or a registered dietitian rather than guessing.

## Why It's Easier to Overdo Than You'd Think

Women's Health points to the rise of high-fiber packaged foods and low-net-carb products as one reason people may be overshooting without realizing it. Bars, shakes, and "protein" snacks can quietly stack up fiber on top of what you're already eating.

My take: if you've added a few of those products and also started a bean-heavy dinner routine, add it all up before blaming a single recipe.

## Raise Fiber Gradually, One Meal at a Time

The simplest way to avoid trouble is to change one thing at a time rather than overhauling everything on Monday. Here's a practical way to do that (this pacing is common-sense cooking advice from us, not a medical prescription):

1. **Pick one dinner a week** to boost with fiber for the first two weeks.
2. **Notice how you feel** the next day.
3. **Add a second meal** only when things feel comfortable.
4. **Drink water** with your meals, since fiber-rich foods are easier on most people when they're well hydrated.

## Easy Ways to Build Fiber into Comfort Food

You don't need specialty products. Everyday pantry staples do the job, and they're budget-friendly.

### Soups and Stews

Stir a cup of canned beans or lentils into a pot of vegetable or chicken soup. Rinse canned beans first. A can usually costs about a dollar, and a single 15-ounce can stretches a soup that serves four.

Realistic timing: red lentils cook in about 20 minutes straight in the pot, so they work on a weeknight without any soaking.

### Casseroles

Swap half the white rice or pasta in a casserole for brown rice or whole-wheat pasta. Making a half swap instead of a full one keeps the texture familiar and the change mild for your gut.

### Slow-Cooker Meals

Add chopped carrots, celery, and a handful of frozen peas or spinach to chili or a beef stew. Frozen vegetables are an affordable swap for fresh and need no prep.

### Nostalgic Desserts

Fold a little rolled oats into an apple crisp topping, and leave the apple skins on. It still tastes like the dessert you remember, with a bit more going on.

## A Gentle Lentil and Vegetable Soup (Serves 4)

This is a good first "fiber-forward" dinner because the amounts are moderate.

**Ingredients**
- 1 tablespoon oil
- 1 chopped onion
- 2 chopped carrots
- 2 chopped celery stalks
- 2 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 cup red lentils, rinsed
- 6 cups broth or water
- 1 teaspoon cumin
- Salt and pepper
- Optional: a big handful of spinach

**Steps**
1. Warm the oil in a large pot over medium heat. Cook the onion, carrots, and celery for about 6 minutes until softened.
2. Stir in garlic and cumin for 30 seconds.
3. Add lentils and broth. Bring to a boil, then simmer for 20 minutes until the lentils are soft.
4. Stir in spinach, if using, and cook 2 minutes. Season to taste.

**Substitutions:** Use water plus a bouillon cube to save money, or swap the carrots for any root vegetable you have on hand. Serve with a slice of toast, and start with a smaller bowl if beans and lentils are new to your routine.

## If You Think You've Overdone It

Don't panic, and don't swing to the other extreme. Based on Women's Health's description of the problem, which is that the issue is increasing fiber too aggressively, the sensible response is to scale back to what felt comfortable and build up more slowly. Look at packaged foods too, since those are an easy source of hidden fiber. And if symptoms linger, talk to a healthcare professional.

## Your Next Step

This week, add fiber to just one dinner, like the lentil soup above, and see how your body responds before you add another.
