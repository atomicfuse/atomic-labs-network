Most of us won't be in Munich this fall. You can still bring a little of that Oktoberfest feeling to your kitchen table with one pot, about 50 minutes, and a handful of affordable ingredients. This Bavarian-style sausage and cabbage supper serves four, and it's built to be forgiving.

## Why Oktoberfest Is on Our Minds

A recent Forbes piece, republished by WS Insider, looks at how travelers increasingly seek out food and drink at the source, from Oktoberfest in Munich to whisky tourism in Scotland. Its core argument, as summarized, is that provenance (where something is made, who makes it, and the culture around it) adds value, authenticity, and emotional pull.

You don't need a plane ticket to borrow that idea. A dish tastes better when you know where it comes from and why people make it. So here's a supper with a clear sense of place, plus a few ways to make it your own.

## Bavarian-Style Sausage and Cabbage Supper

**Serves:** 4
**Prep:** 15 minutes
**Cook:** 35 minutes
**Pot:** a large Dutch oven or deep skillet with a lid

This is a home-cook riff inspired by German-style pub food, not a traditional recipe from any one kitchen.

### Ingredients

- 1 to 1.5 pounds bratwurst or any fresh smoked-style pork or chicken sausage (about 4 to 6 links)
- 1 tablespoon oil or butter
- 1 large yellow onion, sliced
- 1 small head green cabbage (about 1.5 pounds), cored and sliced into ribbons
- 2 medium apples, cored and sliced
- 1 pound baby potatoes, halved
- 1 tablespoon whole-grain or Dijon mustard, plus more for serving
- 1 tablespoon apple cider vinegar
- 1 cup beer, broth, or apple cider
- 1 teaspoon caraway seeds (optional)
- Salt and pepper

### Steps

1. **Brown the sausages (6 to 8 minutes).** Heat the oil over medium-high heat. Brown the sausages on all sides. They don't need to cook through. Move them to a plate.
2. **Soften the onion (5 minutes).** In the same pot, cook the onion with a pinch of salt, scraping up the browned bits.
3. **Build the base.** Stir in the mustard, vinegar, and caraway. Add the potatoes and pour in the beer, broth, or cider. Bring to a simmer.
4. **Pile on the cabbage and apples.** Add them on top, season with salt and pepper, and nestle the sausages in. Cover.
5. **Simmer (20 to 25 minutes)** over medium-low heat, stirring once or twice, until the potatoes are fork-tender and the cabbage is soft.
6. **Rest and serve.** Let it sit off the heat for 5 minutes. Taste and add a splash more vinegar if it needs brightness.

## Affordable Swaps That Still Work

- **Beer:** Use chicken or vegetable broth, or apple cider for a sweeter finish. Use broth plus an extra teaspoon of vinegar if you want it tangy.
- **Sausage:** Kielbasa, smoked turkey sausage, or even sliced hot dogs in a pinch. For a vegetarian version, use plant-based sausages and add them in the last 10 minutes.
- **Cabbage:** Bagged coleslaw mix saves chopping time. Skip any dressing, of course.
- **Apples:** Any baking apple works. In a pinch, a spoonful of brown sugar plus an extra splash of vinegar fills in.
- **Potatoes:** Russets, cut into 1-inch chunks, are cheaper than baby potatoes.

## Slow-Cooker and Make-Ahead Options

For the slow cooker, brown the sausages first if you have the time, then layer onions, potatoes, cabbage, and apples. Add the liquid (reduce it to 3/4 cup, since the cabbage releases water) and cook on low for 6 to 7 hours. Nestle the sausages in for the last 2 hours if you want them to stay snappy.

Leftovers keep in the fridge for up to 4 days. Reheat gently with a splash of broth.

## Small-Batch Version for Two

Halve everything: 2 to 3 sausages, a quarter to a half head of cabbage, 1 apple, 8 ounces of potatoes, and 1/2 cup liquid. Use a medium skillet with a lid and shorten the simmer to 15 to 20 minutes, checking the potatoes.

## Make It Taste Like Somewhere

The Forbes argument is that people value food more when it's tied to place and tradition. You can do a small version of that at home:

- **Ask your butcher** what sausage they make or recommend locally. A nearby maker gives the dish its own provenance.
- **Pick a local apple or beer** from a nearby orchard or brewery if one is available.
- **Serve it simply:** crusty rye or pretzel rolls, extra mustard, and a pickle on the side.
- **Tell the story at the table.** Say what's in it and where it came from. It makes an ordinary Tuesday feel like an occasion.

## What to Serve Alongside

Rye bread, a crisp cucumber salad, or a simple pot of applesauce all work. For dessert, warm baked apples with cinnamon and a spoonful of yogurt keep the theme going with almost no effort.

## Tonight's Takeaway

Pick one ingredient for this supper that comes from somewhere close to you, whether it's the sausage, the apples, or the beer, and cook the pot around it.
