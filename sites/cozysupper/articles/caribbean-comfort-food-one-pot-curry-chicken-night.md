Forbes recently published a guide to Caribbean restaurants in Washington, D.C., covering everything from Trinidadian street food and Jamaican-inspired seafood to upscale Afro-Caribbean cuisine. The report we have doesn't list the individual restaurants, so we won't name any here. What the guide does remind us is that Caribbean flavors are having a moment in American kitchens.

You don't need a plane ticket to a D.C. dining room to enjoy the idea. By the end of this article you'll be able to make a one-pot curry chicken and chickpea dinner that serves four, takes about 55 minutes, and tastes like a long, slow Sunday. It's a home-cook's riff on the comforting, spiced, scoop-it-up style of food that Trinidadian street stalls are known for. It's inspired by that tradition, not a replica of any restaurant's recipe.

## What You'll Need (Serves 4)

- 1.5 lb boneless, skinless chicken thighs, cut into bite-size pieces
- 2 tablespoons curry powder
- 1 teaspoon salt, plus more to taste
- 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
- 2 tablespoons neutral oil
- 1 large onion, diced
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 tablespoon grated fresh ginger (or 1 teaspoon ground)
- 2 medium potatoes, peeled and cubed (about 3/4 inch)
- 1 can (15 oz) chickpeas, drained and rinsed
- 1 can (14 oz) diced tomatoes
- 1 cup chicken broth or water
- 1 teaspoon dried thyme
- Fresh cilantro or sliced green onions, for serving

### Affordable swaps

- **Chicken:** Bone-in thighs work and are often cheaper. Add 10 minutes to the simmer.
- **Curry powder:** Any basic yellow curry powder from the grocery store is fine. Start there before buying specialty blends.
- **Fresh ginger:** Ground ginger works in a pinch.
- **Tomatoes:** Skip them and use an extra cup of broth for a milder, brothier pot.
- **Heat:** For gentle warmth, add a pinch of red pepper flakes. Keep it optional if kids are at the table.

## Step-by-Step Method

### 1. Season the chicken (5 minutes)

Toss the chicken with the curry powder, salt, and pepper. If you have time, let it sit 15 minutes or even overnight in the fridge. If not, it still works.

### 2. Brown it (8 minutes)

Heat the oil in a large Dutch oven or deep skillet over medium-high heat. Add the chicken in a single layer and cook, stirring occasionally, until lightly browned, about 5 to 6 minutes. It doesn't need to be cooked through. Don't crowd the pot; work in two batches if needed.

### 3. Build the base (5 minutes)

Add the onion to the pot and cook until soft, about 4 minutes. Stir in the garlic and ginger and cook 1 minute more, until fragrant. Scrape up the browned bits from the bottom.

### 4. Simmer (25 to 30 minutes)

Add the potatoes, chickpeas, tomatoes, broth, and thyme. Stir well, bring to a boil, then reduce to a gentle simmer. Cover partly and cook 25 to 30 minutes, until the potatoes are tender and the sauce has thickened slightly.

### 5. Finish and taste

Taste for salt. If the sauce looks thin, simmer uncovered for 5 more minutes. If it's too thick, splash in a little water. Top with cilantro or green onions.

## How to Serve It

The dish is meant for scooping. Warm flatbread, naan, or flour tortillas are an easy stand-in for roti, which is the soft flatbread associated with Trinidadian cooking. Rice is just as good if you want something to soak up the sauce. A simple cucumber salad with lime juice and salt adds a cool, crisp contrast.

## Make It Fit Your Week

### Small-batch version for two

Halve everything. Use a medium saucepan, keep the simmer time the same, and check the potatoes at 20 minutes. Because there's less liquid, cover the pot more fully so it doesn't reduce too fast.

### Slow-cooker version

Brown the chicken and onion on the stove if you have the time, then transfer everything to the slow cooker. Cook on low for 5 to 6 hours or high for 3 to 4 hours. Reduce the broth to 3/4 cup, since slow cookers trap moisture.

### Leftovers

This tastes even better the next day. Refrigerate in an airtight container for up to 3 days, or freeze for up to 3 months. Reheat gently with a splash of water. Spooned over rice in a lunch container, it's an easy next-day meal.

## If You Want the Real Thing

If you do find yourself in D.C., the Forbes guide is a good place to start for Trinidadian street food, Jamaican-inspired seafood, and Afro-Caribbean dining. Order what's unfamiliar, ask the staff what they love, and notice how the spices are layered. Then bring those observations back to your own stove.

## Tonight's Takeaway

Pick a chilly evening this week, grab one pot, and make the curry chicken. Even a simple version teaches you how much flavor comes from blooming spices with onion, garlic, and ginger, and that's a skill you can use in every soup and stew you make from now on.
