Travelers are increasingly choosing destinations for the food, according to The Business Times, which reports that culinary tourism is on the rise: people plan trips around local cuisine, restaurants, markets, and dining culture. You may not have a flight booked. You can still borrow the idea for a cozy night in, and this guide shows you how to do it with one pot, a simple dessert, and a grocery list that won't wreck your budget.

The approach is simple: pick one place, cook one hearty meal that reflects it, and make the evening feel like a small trip.

## Start With One Destination, Not a Whole Menu

Food-first travelers tend to choose a place because of its cuisine. You can do the same at your kitchen table. Pick a single cuisine you're curious about, or one you remember from a trip, and commit to it for one night.

Keep the scope small:

- One main dish (a soup, stew, or casserole)
- One side or something to scoop it up with
- One dessert

That's it. A trip-themed dinner that tries to cover everything turns into a stressful Tuesday.

## The Main: A One-Pot Chickpea and Tomato Stew

This is a flexible, Mediterranean-style stew that feels like a market-day meal. It serves 4 generously and takes about 35 minutes, most of it hands-off.

### What You'll Need

- 2 tablespoons olive oil
- 1 large onion, diced
- 3 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 teaspoon ground cumin
- 1 teaspoon paprika (smoked, if you have it)
- 1 can (28 oz) crushed tomatoes
- 2 cans (15 oz each) chickpeas, drained and rinsed
- 2 cups vegetable or chicken broth
- 4 big handfuls of spinach
- Salt and pepper
- Juice of half a lemon

### Steps

1. Warm the oil in a large pot over medium heat. Cook the onion with a pinch of salt for 5 to 6 minutes, until soft.
2. Stir in the garlic, cumin, and paprika. Cook 1 minute, until fragrant.
3. Add the tomatoes, chickpeas, and broth. Bring to a gentle boil, then lower the heat and simmer, partly covered, for 20 minutes.
4. Stir in the spinach and cook 2 minutes, until wilted.
5. Finish with lemon juice, then taste and adjust the salt and pepper.

Serve with warm bread and a spoonful of plain yogurt on top.

### Affordable Swaps

- No fresh spinach? Use a cup of frozen spinach, added with the broth.
- No paprika? Chili powder works, in a smaller amount (start with half a teaspoon).
- Want it heartier? Add a pound of sliced sausage, browned in the pot before the onion, or a cup of cooked rice at the end.
- Cooking for two? Halve everything and use a medium pot. The simmer time stays about the same.

## Slow-Cooker Option for Busy Days

If you'd rather come home to dinner, put the onion (raw is fine), garlic, spices, tomatoes, chickpeas, and broth in the slow cooker. Cook on low for 6 to 7 hours or on high for 3 to 4. Stir in the spinach and lemon juice just before serving. The flavor is a little mellower, which many families prefer.

## Make the Table Part of the Trip

Markets and dining culture are part of why travelers go, per the report. You can echo that without spending much:

- Serve everything family-style in the middle of the table.
- Put out a few small bowls of toppings: yogurt, chopped herbs, a drizzle of olive oil, crushed red pepper.
- Tear the bread instead of slicing it.
- Put phones away and talk about where each of you would go if the next trip were only about eating.

That last one is surprisingly fun with kids. Let each person pick a dream food destination, then choose next month's theme night from the list.

## The Dessert: Baked Cinnamon Apples for Four

A nostalgic, low-effort finish. Prep takes 10 minutes and baking takes about 35.

1. Heat the oven to 350°F.
2. Core 4 medium apples and set them in a baking dish.
3. Mix 3 tablespoons brown sugar, 1 teaspoon cinnamon, and 2 tablespoons softened butter. Spoon it into the centers.
4. Pour about 1/2 cup of water into the dish around the apples.
5. Bake 30 to 40 minutes, until a knife slides in easily.

Swap in oats mixed with the sugar for a crumble feel, or use any baking apple on sale. Add a spoonful of yogurt or vanilla ice cream if you like.

## A Budget-Friendly Shopping Plan

The full meal comes together mostly from pantry staples. Canned tomatoes, chickpeas, broth, and spices keep for months, so the only fresh items to buy are onions, garlic, spinach, lemon, and apples. Buying the pantry items once means your next theme night costs even less.

## Turn It Into a Habit

If the idea catches on at your house, rotate through a few cuisines over several weeks. Keep a note on the fridge of what you made and what everyone thought. Repeat the winners, adjust the spices, and you'll have a handful of reliable dinners that feel like an outing.

This week, pick one place you're curious about, make the stew, and let dinner be the destination.
