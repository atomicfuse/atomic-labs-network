Forbes recently reported that around La Fortuna, Costa Rica, local ingredients and traditional Costa Rican cooking are becoming the kind of dining luxury travelers prefer. The idea is simple: the most impressive meal is the one that tastes like where it came from.

You don't need a plane ticket to use that idea. You need one pot, about 45 minutes, and whatever is freshest and cheapest near you right now. Below is a stew that serves four, plus the swaps that keep it flexible and affordable.

## What the Trend Means for Your Kitchen

Forbes's point is about restaurants, but the lesson works at home. Cooking with what's local and in season usually means better flavor and often a lower grocery bill. A pot of stew is the easiest place to practice, because the vegetables do the heavy lifting.

This is my own recipe, not one from the Forbes piece. It's just the same principle, scaled down to a weeknight.

## Local-Market Vegetable and White Bean Stew

**Serves:** 4 generous bowls
**Prep:** 15 minutes
**Cook:** 30 minutes

### What You'll Need

- 2 tablespoons olive oil (or any cooking oil)
- 1 medium onion, diced
- 2 carrots, sliced into half-moons
- 2 celery ribs, sliced
- 3 garlic cloves, minced
- 3 cups cubed potatoes or winter squash (about 1 pound), in 3/4-inch pieces
- 1 can (14 oz) diced tomatoes, with juice
- 2 cans (15 oz each) white beans, drained and rinsed
- 4 cups vegetable or chicken broth
- 1 teaspoon dried thyme
- 1 teaspoon salt, plus more to taste
- 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
- 2 big handfuls of chopped greens (kale, chard, or spinach)
- 1 tablespoon lemon juice or vinegar, to finish

### Steps

1. **Soften the base.** Warm the oil in a large pot over medium heat. Add the onion, carrots, and celery with a pinch of salt. Cook for 7 to 8 minutes, stirring now and then, until the onion turns soft and translucent.
2. **Add the garlic.** Stir in the garlic and thyme and cook for 30 seconds, just until fragrant.
3. **Build the stew.** Add the potatoes or squash, tomatoes, beans, broth, salt, and pepper. Bring to a boil, then lower the heat to a steady simmer.
4. **Simmer.** Cover partly and cook for 20 to 25 minutes, until the potatoes are tender when poked with a fork.
5. **Thicken it.** Mash about a cup of the beans and potatoes against the side of the pot with a spoon. Stir them back in. This makes the broth rich without any cream.
6. **Add greens and finish.** Stir in the greens and cook 3 to 5 minutes until wilted. Take the pot off the heat and add the lemon juice. Taste and add salt if needed.

## Affordable Swaps That Still Work

The whole point is cooking with what's local, so treat this recipe as a template.

- **No white beans?** Chickpeas or pinto beans work. Dried beans are cheaper, but they need soaking and longer cooking, so canned is the realistic weeknight choice.
- **Squash out of season?** Use potatoes, sweet potatoes, or turnips.
- **Greens:** Whatever is cheapest and freshest. Frozen spinach works too; add about 1 cup straight from the bag.
- **Broth:** Water plus an extra 1/2 teaspoon of salt and a spoonful of tomato paste will do in a pinch.
- **Want it heartier?** Brown sliced smoked sausage in the pot first, then remove it and add it back at the end. Or stir in leftover shredded chicken with the greens.

## Make It Taste Like a Meal Out

Restaurants that lean on local ingredients tend to let one or two good things shine, and you can do the same at home.

- **Finish with acid.** That splash of lemon at the end brightens everything.
- **Add something fresh on top.** Chopped parsley, cilantro, or green onion makes a bowl look and taste special.
- **Serve with something simple.** Crusty bread, warm tortillas, or a scoop of rice stretches the pot for a bigger family.

## Storing and Reheating

The stew keeps in the fridge for up to 4 days in a covered container. The flavor is often better on day two. Reheat on the stove over medium-low heat, adding a splash of water or broth because the beans and potatoes will have soaked up liquid. It freezes well for about 3 months, though potatoes can turn slightly softer after thawing.

## A Small-Batch Option for Two

Halve everything. Use a medium pot, one can of beans, 2 cups of broth, and about 1 1/2 cups of potatoes. Simmer time stays about the same. If you'd rather cook the full recipe, freeze the second half in two single-serving containers for a no-effort dinner later.

## Start With the Market, Not the Recipe

Next time you plan dinner, walk the produce section or a farmers' market first and ask what looks best and costs least. Build the pot around that answer. It's the same instinct Forbes describes drawing diners to La Fortuna, and it works just as well in your own kitchen.
