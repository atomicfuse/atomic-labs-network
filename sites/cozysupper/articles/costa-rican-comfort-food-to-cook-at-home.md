Afar recently rounded up the foods you have to eat in Costa Rica, and the list reads like a menu for a cozy night in: ceviche, gallo pinto, patacones, heart of palm salad, batidos, rice and beans, and casado. Per Afar, the cuisine is simple, fresh, and deeply tied to place, built on local ingredients and a farm-to-table culture that grows out of the country's biodiversity and regional traditions.

You don't need a plane ticket to borrow that spirit. Below, you'll find how to turn the dishes on that list into a relaxed home dinner, with easy swaps. Afar's piece doesn't supply recipes, so the cooking guidance here is general home-cooking advice, not a traditional or official method.

## Start With the Plate: Build a Casado at Home

Casado is on Afar's list, and it's the easiest one to adapt for a family table. It's a plate that pairs rice and beans with other simple components. Think of it as a template rather than a strict recipe.

A practical version for four people:

- 2 cups cooked rice (about 1 cup dry rice, 20 minutes on the stove)
- 1 can black beans, simmered 10 minutes with a little onion and garlic
- Grilled or pan-seared chicken, fish, or whatever protein is on sale
- A simple salad of cabbage, tomato, and lime juice
- Fried plantain slices on the side

Total time is about 35 minutes. Leftover rice from the night before works well and saves time.

## Gallo Pinto: The Leftover-Rice Breakfast (or Dinner)

Gallo pinto, rice and beans cooked together, is a natural fit for breakfast-for-dinner nights. It's also a smart way to use up leftovers.

1. Warm a tablespoon of oil in a wide skillet over medium heat.
2. Cook half a diced onion and a small chopped bell pepper for 4 to 5 minutes.
3. Add 2 cups cold cooked rice and 1 to 1½ cups cooked beans, with a splash of the bean liquid.
4. Stir for 6 to 8 minutes until everything is heated through and lightly colored.
5. Season with salt and pepper, and a squeeze of lime if you like.

Serve with eggs and toast or tortillas. Serves 3 to 4 for about a dollar or two per person.

## Patacones: A Crispy Side From Green Plantains

Patacones are another item Afar highlights. Home cooks usually make them by frying thick slices of green plantain, flattening them, and frying again until crisp. A few tips:

- Choose firm, green plantains. Ripe, yellow ones turn sweet and soft.
- Fry the thick slices about 3 minutes per side, flatten with the bottom of a glass, then fry 2 to 3 minutes more.
- Salt them right out of the pan.

Can't find plantains? Thick-cut potato wedges roasted at 425°F for 30 minutes give you a similar salty, crunchy side.

## Heart of Palm Salad: The Pantry Shortcut

Heart of palm salad is the easiest dish on the list because the main ingredient comes in a can. Drain one 14-ounce can, slice it into rounds, and toss with diced tomato, a little red onion, olive oil, and lime juice. It takes 10 minutes and serves four as a side.

If canned hearts of palm feel pricey, canned artichoke hearts are a decent stand-in.

## Rice and Beans: The Caribbean Variation

Afar contrasts the everyday rice and beans of Costa Rica with its Caribbean-style version, and the two are worth comparing at home. A common Caribbean approach, which I'm describing from general cooking knowledge rather than from Afar, simmers rice and beans with coconut milk. To try it, replace half the water in your rice pot with canned coconut milk, stir in cooked beans, and simmer covered about 20 minutes. It's richer and a little sweet, and it makes a cozy base for a bowl of anything.

## Ceviche for a Lighter Night

Ceviche, the seafood dish Afar leads with, is the one to treat with care at home. It's made with raw fish cured in citrus, so buy fish from a trusted counter, keep it cold, and eat it the same day. If raw fish isn't for you, quickly poach shrimp for 2 to 3 minutes, chill, and toss with lime juice, red onion, cilantro, and diced tomato. Serves 4, about 20 minutes plus 30 minutes chilling.

## Batidos: The Easy Dessert-Style Finish

Batidos, the fruit-packed drinks on Afar's list, double as a light dessert. Blend 1½ cups frozen fruit (mango, pineapple, or banana), 1 cup milk, and a spoonful of sugar or honey with a handful of ice for about a minute. Makes two glasses. Frozen fruit is the budget-friendly choice, and any milk, dairy or not, works.

## A Simple Menu Plan

For a full dinner for four, make the casado template with patacones and the heart of palm salad, then finish with batidos. Cook the rice first, the beans while it simmers, and fry the patacones last so they stay crisp. Start to finish: about 50 minutes.

Pick one dish this week, probably gallo pinto with the rice already in your fridge, and let it be the start of a new family favorite.
