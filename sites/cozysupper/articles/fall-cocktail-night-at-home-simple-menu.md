When the weather turns, a good drink can make a simple supper feel like an occasion. By the end of this article, you'll have a easy plan for a fall cocktail night: which classics to pick, how to give them an autumn twist, and how to match them to a hearty pot of something warm.

The idea comes from a Forbes roundup of 17 fall cocktails, which spans hot toddies, martinis, margaritas, and whiskey sours, with seasonal flavors from apple to pumpkin. The summary I have names only six of those categories, so I won't pretend to know the other eleven. Instead, here's how to use the ones we do know about without any fuss.

## Start With Two Drinks, Not Seventeen

The biggest mistake with a themed night is overbuilding it. Pick one cold drink and one warm drink. That covers every guest, and you only need to buy a short list of ingredients.

- **Cold:** a whiskey sour. It's a classic, and it's easy to give an autumn feel.
- **Warm:** a hot toddy. It's the one to hand someone who just came in from the chill.

If you're hosting a crowd, add a third option based on what your guests tend to like, such as a martini or a margarita, both of which appear in the Forbes list.

## Hot Toddies for Chilly Evenings

A hot toddy is the most naturally cozy drink on the list. It's served warm, it needs no shaker, and you can make it one mug at a time while the soup finishes simmering.

Keep your setup simple: a kettle, a few mugs, and your usual toddy ingredients laid out on a tray so guests can build their own. Hot drinks are best made fresh, so don't try to batch them hours ahead.

### Smart, affordable swaps

- No fancy spirits? Use a bottle you already own and like drinking.
- Want a no-alcohol version? Skip the spirit and serve the same warm mug with hot water or tea, plus whatever you'd normally add for flavor.

## Whiskey Sours With an Autumn Twist

The whiskey sour is a good canvas for fall because its sharp, bright flavor balances rich food. The Forbes collection pairs classics with seasonal flavors, so think of it as a template: start with the sour you know, then nudge it toward autumn with an apple or other fall note you enjoy.

Taste as you go. If a twist makes the drink too sweet, add a little more citrus. If it's too sharp, ease back.

## Apple or Pumpkin? Pick One

The roundup highlights both apple and pumpkin cocktails, and you don't need both on one night. A quick way to choose:

- **Apple** tends to feel lighter and fresher, which suits a menu with a roast or a creamy casserole.
- **Pumpkin** feels more like dessert, so it works well at the end of the evening instead of the start.

That's my take, not a rule from the roundup. If you can only choose one, go with the flavor your household already reaches for in October.

## Pair Drinks With Supper

Here's where cocktail night meets the dinner table. These pairings are my suggestions, built around simple logic: bright drinks cut through rich food, and warm drinks suit slow evenings.

- **Creamy soup or a cheesy casserole:** serve something citrusy, like a whiskey sour, to cut the richness.
- **Slow-cooker stew or roast:** a hot toddy afterward makes a gentle finish.
- **Apple crisp or other nostalgic dessert:** an apple cocktail echoes the flavors on the plate. Or skip the dessert fork and let a pumpkin drink do the job.

## A Low-Stress Hosting Plan

A plan you can follow without panic:

1. **Earlier in the day:** start the main dish. A slow cooker or a one-pot meal keeps you out of the kitchen when guests arrive.
2. **An hour before:** set out glasses, mugs, ice, and garnishes. Squeeze citrus if your drink needs it.
3. **When guests arrive:** offer the cold drink first, then the warm one as the evening settles in.
4. **With dessert:** put the kettle on again for round two.

## Don't Forget a Zero-Proof Option

Some guests won't drink alcohol, and a cozy night works better when everyone has something special in hand. Warm apple-flavored drinks or a spiced tea in a nice mug feel festive without extra work. Make it as intentional as the cocktails, with the same garnish and glass, so nobody feels like an afterthought.

## The Takeaway

You don't need a long drinks menu to make supper feel special. This week, choose one cold drink and one warm drink, make the main dish ahead, and set a tray out before guests walk in.
