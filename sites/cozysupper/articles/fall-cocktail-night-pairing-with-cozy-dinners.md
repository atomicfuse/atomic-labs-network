Forbes recently rounded up 17 fall cocktails, from whiskey sours and hot toddies to martinis and margaritas, with seasonal flavors like apple and pumpkin. The list is a nice prompt for a cozy night in. This piece takes one angle from it: how to build a relaxed fall cocktail night around a simple, hearty dinner, without turning your kitchen into a bar.

By the end, you'll know which classic drinks suit a chilly evening, how to pair them with soups and casseroles, and how to keep the whole thing easy and inclusive.

A quick note on what's here: the Forbes roundup, per the details available to me, names whiskey sours, hot toddies, martinis, margaritas, apple cocktails and pumpkin cocktails among its 17. I haven't seen the full list or its recipes, so I'm not going to repeat or invent specific measurements for them. The pairing ideas below are my own suggestions, built on cooking basics.

## Start With the Dinner, Then Pick the Drink

On a cozy night, the food is the star. Choose a one-pot dinner you can make ahead, so you're not shaking drinks while something scorches. Good candidates:

- A slow-cooker beef stew or chili that simmers all afternoon
- A baked casserole, such as chicken and rice or a pasta bake, that can rest 10 minutes while you pour drinks
- A big pot of soup, such as potato, tomato or butternut squash, with crusty bread

Aim to have dinner hands-off for the last 30 minutes. That's your window to make a round of drinks.

## Hot Toddies: The Easiest Welcome on a Cold Night

Hot toddies are on the Forbes list, and they suit the cozy-night idea well because they're served warm and made in a mug. In my own pairing logic, a warm drink works best before dinner or alongside something light, like soup, since it won't compete with a rich main dish.

If you want to serve one, use your favorite tested recipe and keep a kettle of hot water ready. Set out the extras (lemon wedges, cinnamon sticks, honey) on a tray so guests can fix their own.

**Affordable swap:** No need for specialty ingredients. Pantry honey and a fresh lemon do most of the work.

## Whiskey Sours: A Classic That Handles Hearty Food

Whiskey sours also made the list. The bright, tart character of a sour is a sensible counterweight to rich, savory dishes like stew or a cheesy casserole. That's my take, not Forbes's: acidity tends to refresh the palate between bites.

Shake these to order, one or two at a time, rather than in a big batch. Serve with a small bowl of salted nuts while dinner finishes.

## Apple or Pumpkin? Choose Based on What's on the Table

The roundup calls out apple and pumpkin as seasonal flavors. Rather than crowning a winner, here's a simple rule of thumb for pairing:

- **Apple drinks** are lighter and fruitier. They go well with pork, roasted chicken or anything with sage.
- **Pumpkin drinks** are richer and spiced. They fit better alongside dessert, or on their own after dinner, since pumpkin-spiced flavors can echo a pie.

If you're already making an apple crisp or a pumpkin loaf for dessert, serve the matching drink with it and skip a separate sweet course.

## Martinis and Margaritas: Fall Twists on Familiar Favorites

The list includes martinis and margaritas, which shows how flexible fall flavors can be. If you have a favorite cocktail you already make well, you don't need a new one. Add one seasonal touch, such as an apple or spiced element, and you've got a fall version.

Stick with a recipe you've tested before. A night at home is not the time to experiment with unknown ratios on guests.

## Plan the Night So You Actually Enjoy It

A few practical habits keep this stress-free:

1. **Limit the menu.** Offer two drinks at most: one warm, one cold.
2. **Prep garnishes in the afternoon.** Slice lemons and apples, and gather cinnamon sticks.
3. **Set up a drink tray** away from the stove so the kitchen doesn't get crowded.
4. **Chill glasses** for cold drinks while the oven is on and dinner is baking.
5. **Serve water too,** with every round.

For a night for two, make one drink per person before dinner and one with dessert. That's plenty.

## Mocktail Swaps Everyone Can Share

Not everyone drinks alcohol, and a good cozy night includes them. You don't need a separate recipe for every guest. Warm apple cider with a cinnamon stick feels like a hot toddy cousin, and sparkling water with lemon and a splash of cider gives a fizzy sour-style option. These are my simple suggestions rather than items from the Forbes list.

Serve them in the same glasses with the same garnishes so no one feels like an afterthought.

## Your Next Step

Pick one dinner you can make ahead this week, choose a single drink to go with it, and make a nonalcoholic version of the same drink. That's a complete fall cocktail night, and it takes about 15 minutes of extra effort.
