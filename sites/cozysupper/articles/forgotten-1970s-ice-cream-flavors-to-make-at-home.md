Per Fox News, five ice cream flavors that once ruled 1970s freezer aisles have largely vanished from mainstream stores, and fans keep asking for them back: Butter Brickle, Rum Raisin, Pink Bubblegum, Heavenly Hash and Maple Walnut. You don't have to wait for a brand to listen. Here's how to get the spirit of each one into a bowl at home, using a simple no-churn base and ingredients you can find at a regular grocery store.

The flavor ideas below are my own kitchen suggestions, not claims about the original recipes. Treat them as starting points and adjust to taste.

## The Easy No-Churn Base (Makes About 1 Quart)

All five flavors start here. You need no ice cream machine.

- 2 cups cold heavy cream
- 1 can (14 oz) sweetened condensed milk
- 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
- Pinch of salt

Whip the cream to stiff peaks, about 3 to 4 minutes with a hand mixer. Gently fold in the condensed milk, vanilla and salt. Fold in your flavorings (below), pour into a loaf pan, cover, and freeze at least 6 hours. That's about 15 minutes of work. Let it sit on the counter 5 minutes before scooping.

**Budget swap:** Whipping cream works if heavy cream costs too much. Store-brand condensed milk is fine.

## 1. Butter Brickle

The appeal is buttery toffee crunch in a sweet, creamy base. To approximate it, fold in 1 cup of chopped toffee bits (the baking-aisle kind) and 2 tablespoons of melted, cooled butter along with the condensed milk.

**Swap:** No toffee bits? Chop up a chocolate-free toffee candy bar, or crush buttery crackers and cook them briefly with brown sugar and butter until bubbly, then cool.

## 2. Rum Raisin

This one rewards a little planning. Soak 3/4 cup raisins in 1/4 cup warm water plus 2 tablespoons dark rum for at least 30 minutes, then drain and fold in. The alcohol adds flavor and keeps the texture a bit softer.

**Swap:** Skip the liquor and use 1 teaspoon of rum extract with the soaking water, or soak the raisins in warm apple juice with a dash of vanilla.

## 3. Pink Bubblegum

This is the most playful of the five, and the trickiest to get right. Bubblegum flavoring is sold online and in some baking shops. Start with 1 teaspoon of the flavoring and add a drop or two of pink food coloring. Taste before freezing, since strength varies by brand.

**Swap:** If you can't find bubblegum flavoring, use a few drops of strawberry or cherry extract with a little extra vanilla for a candy-shop pink treat. It won't taste identical, but kids tend to love it.

## 4. Heavenly Hash

Think of a loaded, everything-in-it scoop. A classic combination for this style is chocolate, nuts and marshmallow, so that's what I'd build: fold in 1/2 cup mini marshmallows, 1/2 cup chopped toasted almonds and 1/2 cup chocolate chips or chopped chocolate. Swirl in 1/4 cup chocolate syrup at the end for ripples.

**Swap:** Use whatever nuts are in your pantry. Walnuts or peanuts work, and chopped chocolate bars are often cheaper than chips.

## 5. Maple Walnut

Use real maple syrup if you can. Replace 1/4 cup of the condensed milk with 1/4 cup maple syrup (the base will be slightly less sweet and a touch softer), and fold in 3/4 cup toasted chopped walnuts. Toast the walnuts in a dry skillet over medium heat for 4 to 5 minutes until fragrant.

**Swap:** Pancake syrup plus 1/2 teaspoon maple extract is a cheaper stand-in, though the flavor is less rounded.

## Why Retro Flavors Keep Coming Back

Per the Fox News report, nostalgia-driven food trends are gaining traction as brands revive retro snacks, diner staples and childhood favorites. The report describes the appeal as "newstalgia": a blend of comfort, memory and novelty. That's easy to believe once you serve a scoop of maple walnut to someone who remembers it from childhood.

## Make It a Family Night

Turn this into a cozy dessert evening. Make two bases on a Sunday, split them into different flavors, and let everyone taste-test and vote on which one deserves a comeback. If you have grandparents or parents nearby, ask which flavor they miss most. The answers tend to come with stories.

## Storage Tips

- Press parchment or plastic wrap directly on the surface to prevent ice crystals.
- Homemade no-churn ice cream tastes best within 2 weeks.
- Chill the loaf pan in the freezer for 15 minutes before filling for a faster set.

Pick the flavor you remember best, whip up the base this weekend, and see how close your freezer can get to the 1970s.
