Farmer Lee Jones of The Chef's Garden appeared on "Fox & Friends Weekend" to show off fresh fall vegetables and Thanksgiving side dishes, per Fox News. The segment's pitch was simple: seasonal produce can bring brighter, fresher flavors to a holiday table full of rich, familiar dishes.

That idea works in a regular home kitchen, too. Below is a practical way to make vegetables the part of Thanksgiving people talk about, without adding stress. The recipes here are my own cozy, tested-style approaches, not dishes from the segment, since the coverage doesn't list specific recipes.

## Start With One Vegetable Side, Not Five

The easiest way to make a fresher spread is to improve one or two sides rather than reinvent everything. Pick the vegetable that looks best at your market this week. Fall produce is at its peak, so it needs less help from butter and cream than you might think.

A good rule: plan about 3/4 to 1 cup of cooked vegetables per person for each side. For eight people, that's roughly 2 pounds of raw vegetables per dish.

## Sheet-Pan Roasted Fall Vegetables (Serves 6-8)

This is the workhorse. It frees up your stovetop and fills the house with a cozy smell.

**You'll need:**
- 2 pounds mixed vegetables (carrots, parsnips, squash, Brussels sprouts), cut into similar-size pieces
- 3 tablespoons olive oil
- 1 teaspoon salt
- Black pepper to taste
- Fresh or dried thyme, if you have it

**Steps:**
1. Heat the oven to 425°F.
2. Toss the vegetables with oil, salt, pepper, and herbs.
3. Spread on two sheet pans in a single layer. Crowding steams them instead of browning them.
4. Roast 25 to 35 minutes, stirring once halfway, until edges are browned and a knife slides in easily.

**Budget swaps:** Use whatever is cheapest: carrots and cabbage wedges roast beautifully. Dried thyme works in place of fresh.

## Upgrade a Classic Instead of Replacing It

If your family insists on the usual dishes, keep them and give them a fresh lift.

### Green beans
Skip the long simmer. Blanch 1.5 pounds of trimmed green beans in boiling salted water for 3 to 4 minutes, then drain and toss with butter and lemon zest. They stay bright and snappy next to soft stuffing and mashed potatoes.

### Mashed root vegetables
Swap half the potatoes for parsnips or celery root. Boil the chunks 20 to 25 minutes until tender, then mash with warm milk and butter. You get more flavor with the same effort.

### Casserole-style sides
A vegetable gratin is a casserole in disguise. Layer thinly sliced squash or potatoes in a buttered 9x13 dish, pour over 1 cup warm cream or milk with salt and garlic, cover with foil, and bake at 375°F for about 40 minutes. Uncover and bake 15 more until golden.

## Quick Plating Tricks That Look Chef-Style

Presentation costs almost nothing and makes a side feel special.

- **Use a warm, shallow serving dish.** A platter shows off roasted vegetables better than a deep bowl.
- **Finish with something fresh.** A handful of chopped parsley, a squeeze of lemon, or a few toasted nuts wakes up a tray of roasted vegetables.
- **Vary the colors.** Orange carrots, deep green sprouts, and cream parsnips look abundant together.
- **Season at the end.** A pinch of flaky salt just before serving adds contrast.

## Make-Ahead Timeline for a Calmer Day

Fresh doesn't have to mean last-minute.

- **Two days before:** Shop, and wash and trim vegetables.
- **One day before:** Cut vegetables and store them in airtight containers in the fridge. Assemble the gratin, cover, and refrigerate.
- **Thanksgiving day:** Roast vegetables while the turkey rests. Reheat the gratin in a 350°F oven for 20 to 25 minutes.

Turkey needs the oven free for resting time, and roasted vegetables are happy to use that window, since the oven can be turned up once the bird comes out.

## Use the Leftovers

Roasted vegetable leftovers make a cozy next-day soup. Simmer 3 to 4 cups of leftover vegetables in 4 cups of broth for 15 minutes, blend until smooth, and season. It serves about four and takes under 30 minutes.

## The Takeaway

This week, choose one vegetable at its seasonal best, roast it simply, and put it front and center on the table. One great side beats five tired ones.
