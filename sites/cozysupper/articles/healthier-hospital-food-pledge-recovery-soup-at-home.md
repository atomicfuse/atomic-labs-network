Robert F. Kennedy Jr. and Dr. Mehmet Oz say 250 U.S. hospitals have joined a voluntary federal pledge, the Make Hospital Food Healthier Pledge, to cut processed food and added sugar from patient meals, according to a Fox News report. Their argument is simple: food in a hospital should support healing.

You can't change your local hospital's menu from your kitchen. You can, though, make the kind of meal that fits that idea: whole ingredients, no added sugar, gentle on a tired body. Below is a one-pot chicken and vegetable soup that serves four, costs little, and works for a sick day, a recovery week, or a chilly Tuesday.

## What the Pledge Actually Says

Per the Fox News piece, Kennedy and Oz frame hospitals as powerful buyers. If enough of them shift toward whole, minimally processed foods, the thinking goes, the broader food system could follow. The pledge is voluntary, not a government mandate, and the details of how each hospital will change its menus weren't spelled out in the report I'm working from. Nutrition has become a bigger political and healthcare issue under the "Make America Healthy Again" movement, with federal leaders linking food quality to chronic disease, recovery and healthcare costs.

That's the policy side. The home-kitchen takeaway is easier to act on: a recovery-friendly meal is mostly about what you leave out and how simply you cook what's left.

## A Recovery-Friendly Chicken and Vegetable Soup

Why this soup: it's built from whole ingredients, has no added sugar, and you control the salt. It also reheats well, which matters when you're not feeling your best.

**Serves:** 4 generous bowls
**Active time:** 15 minutes
**Total time:** about 45 minutes

### Ingredients

- 1 tablespoon olive oil
- 1 onion, diced
- 2 carrots, sliced
- 2 celery stalks, sliced
- 3 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 pound boneless, skinless chicken thighs or breasts
- 6 cups low-sodium chicken broth (or water plus 1 teaspoon salt)
- 1 bay leaf
- 1 teaspoon dried thyme
- 1 cup small pasta, rice, or diced potato
- 2 big handfuls spinach or chopped kale
- Salt, pepper, and a squeeze of lemon

### Steps

1. Warm the oil in a large pot over medium heat. Add the onion, carrots, and celery with a pinch of salt. Cook 6 to 8 minutes until softened.
2. Stir in the garlic and thyme for 30 seconds.
3. Add the chicken, broth, and bay leaf. Bring to a boil, then lower to a gentle simmer. Cover and cook 20 minutes, until the chicken is cooked through and shreds easily.
4. Lift out the chicken, shred it with two forks, and return it to the pot.
5. Add the pasta, rice, or potato. Simmer 8 to 10 minutes, until tender (white rice and small pasta take about this long).
6. Stir in the greens until wilted, about 2 minutes. Remove the bay leaf, add lemon, and season to taste.

## Affordable Swaps

- **No fresh vegetables?** Frozen carrots, peas, or a mirepoix blend work well and cost less. Add them with the broth.
- **Chicken thighs vs. breasts:** thighs stay more tender and are usually cheaper.
- **Broth:** low-sodium boxed broth is fine. Check the label for added sugar, which turns up in some packaged broths and sauces.
- **Slow cooker version:** put everything except the pasta and greens in the cooker, cook on low 6 to 7 hours, then add the pasta and greens for the last 20 to 30 minutes.
- **Smaller batch for two:** halve everything and use a medium pot; the timing stays about the same.

## Making It Gentler or Heartier

For a sore throat or low appetite, use rice or tiny pasta, cook it until very soft, and skip the pepper. For a heartier dinner, add a can of drained white beans in the last 10 minutes and serve with toast.

## Storing and Reheating

Cool the soup within two hours and refrigerate in a sealed container for up to four days. Pasta and rice soak up broth, so if you expect leftovers, cook them separately and add to each bowl. Freeze the soup base without pasta for up to three months, then reheat gently on the stove.

## The Bigger Question

Whether a voluntary pledge is enough, or whether hospitals should face firmer rules, is a live debate, and the report doesn't settle it. What a good patient tray should include is also still being worked out. But you don't need to wait on that conversation to feed your own household well.

This week, make one pot of soup from whole ingredients, portion it into containers, and keep it in the fridge for the next time someone in your house needs something simple and nourishing.
