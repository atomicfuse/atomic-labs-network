Robert F. Kennedy Jr. and Dr. Mehmet Oz announced this week, in a piece reported by Fox News, that 250 U.S. hospitals have joined a voluntary federal pledge, the Make Hospital Food Healthier Pledge, aimed at cutting processed food and added sugar from patient meals. Kennedy and Oz argue that hospital food should support healing, and that hospitals, as big buyers of food, could push the wider food system toward more whole, minimally processed ingredients.

Whatever you think of the politics, the idea is easy to bring home. When someone in your house is under the weather, recovering, or just worn out by a cold week, a bowl of simple, whole-ingredient soup does the job. Below is a tested-style recipe for four servings, with swaps that keep it affordable.

## What the pledge is (and isn't)

Per the Fox News piece, the pledge is voluntary, not a mandate. Its stated goal is to reduce processed food and added sugar in patient meals. The details of how each hospital will do that weren't laid out in the report, so it's too early to say what a pledged hospital tray will look like. What the authors do say is that food quality ties into chronic disease, recovery and healthcare costs.

You don't need a pledge to apply the same thinking in your own kitchen: start with ingredients you can name, and skip the packets and sugary extras.

## Whole-Ingredient Chicken and Vegetable Soup

**Serves:** 4 generous bowls
**Active time:** 20 minutes
**Total time:** about 55 minutes

### Ingredients

- 1 tablespoon olive oil
- 1 medium onion, diced
- 2 carrots, sliced
- 2 celery stalks, sliced
- 3 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 pound boneless, skinless chicken thighs or breasts
- 6 cups low-sodium chicken broth (or water plus 1 teaspoon salt)
- 1 teaspoon dried thyme
- 1 bay leaf
- 1 cup small pasta, or 3/4 cup rice
- 2 cups chopped kale or spinach
- Salt, pepper and a squeeze of lemon, to finish

### Steps

1. **Soften the vegetables.** Heat the oil in a large pot over medium heat. Cook the onion, carrots and celery for 6 to 8 minutes, until the onion turns translucent. Add the garlic for the last minute.
2. **Add the chicken and broth.** Nestle in the chicken, pour in the broth, and add the thyme and bay leaf. Bring to a boil, then lower to a gentle simmer.
3. **Simmer.** Cover and cook 20 to 25 minutes, until the chicken reaches 165°F and shreds easily.
4. **Shred and add starch.** Pull the chicken out, shred it with two forks, and return it to the pot. Stir in the pasta or rice and cook until tender: about 8 to 10 minutes for pasta, 15 to 18 for rice.
5. **Finish.** Stir in the greens for the final 2 minutes. Remove the bay leaf, then season with salt, pepper and lemon.

## Affordable swaps

- **Chicken:** Bone-in thighs are often cheaper. Simmer them 5 to 10 minutes longer, then discard the skin and bones.
- **Greens:** Frozen spinach works fine. Add a cup straight from the freezer.
- **Vegetables:** Diced potato, parsnip or frozen peas can stand in for whatever you're missing.
- **Beans for protein:** Replace the chicken with two cans of drained white beans and use vegetable broth. Cook just 15 minutes.
- **Broth:** Check labels for added sugar and long ingredient lists. Plain stock or a homemade pot of water and aromatics is the simplest option.

## Making it a recovery-friendly meal

The aim of the pledge, as described by Kennedy and Oz, is food that helps people heal. At home, that translates into a few easy habits:

- Keep the salt modest and let lemon, herbs and garlic carry the flavor.
- Choose whole-grain pasta or brown rice if you like, adding a few extra minutes of cooking.
- Serve with a piece of fruit rather than a sugary dessert if appetite is low and you want something light.
- Cook gently and keep the texture soft if chewing or swallowing is tough.

If you're cooking for someone with a medical condition or dietary restriction, check with their care team first. This recipe is a comforting dinner, not medical advice.

## Storing and reheating

Cool the soup within two hours. Refrigerate for up to 4 days, or freeze for up to 3 months. Pasta and rice soften as they sit, so for leftovers cook them separately and add them to each bowl. Reheat on the stove until steaming hot.

## Make a double batch this weekend

Whether or not your local hospital has signed the pledge, you can set your own standard. Make this soup once, freeze half in single-serving containers, and you'll have a whole-ingredient dinner ready the next time a chilly evening or a rough week calls for one.
