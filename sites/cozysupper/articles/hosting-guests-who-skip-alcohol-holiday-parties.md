Holiday party season is here, and one small question can make a guest feel put on the spot: "Why aren't you drinking?" By the end of this article, you'll know what to say instead, how to answer if you're the one being asked, and how to set a table where nobody has to explain their glass.

## The Question to Skip

An etiquette expert told Fox News Digital that asking someone why they aren't drinking is very impolite. Lisa Burdette is the expert cited in the report, which also draws on survey data about drinking habits at gatherings.

The reasons people pass on alcohol vary. Per the report, more people are abstaining for health, religious, personal, or wellness reasons, and sober-curious culture is changing how people think about drinking at parties. A guest might be driving, taking medication, or simply not in the mood. Whatever the reason, it belongs to them.

My own take: the question usually comes from friendly curiosity, not nosiness. But it asks a person to hand over private information in front of a room full of people, and that's the problem.

## What to Say Instead

Swap the question for something that moves the evening forward:

- "Can I get you something to drink?" Simple, and it assumes nothing.
- "We've got hot cider and a ginger-lime sparkler. Want one?" Offering specifics makes the option feel like a real choice, not a consolation prize.
- "Come try the soup, it's been going all afternoon." Food is the best change of subject a host has.

If you notice someone's glass is different from everyone else's, say nothing. Silence is the polite answer.

## If You're the One Being Asked

You don't owe anyone an explanation. Burdette shared a graceful way to deflect the question, and the general idea is to answer briefly and pleasantly, then redirect. Here are a few scripts in that spirit (these are my suggestions, not quotes from her):

- "I'm all set with this one, thanks. Is that your grandmother's pie recipe?"
- "Not tonight, but I'd love some of that cider."
- "I'm good! What can I help carry to the table?"

Keep your tone light and finish with a question or a task. It signals the topic is closed without making anything awkward.

## Host a Table Where Everyone Belongs

The report reminds hosts that connection matters more than alcohol. The easiest way to act on that is to make the nonalcoholic options as appealing as everything else, and to put them out where everyone can reach them.

### A Cozy Warm Drink for a Crowd (Serves 8, about 30 minutes)

This slow-cooker spiced cider works for every guest at once.

1. Pour 8 cups apple cider into a slow cooker.
2. Add 3 cinnamon sticks, 4 whole cloves, and 2 orange slices.
3. Heat on low for 2 to 3 hours, or on high for about 1 hour, until steaming.
4. Keep on the warm setting and ladle into mugs.

Affordable swaps: use 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon if you lack sticks, and skip the cloves if you don't have them. Sliced apple works in place of orange.

### Make It a Real Option

- Set out the cider, a pitcher of sparkling water with citrus, and the wine on the same table.
- Use the same nice glasses or mugs for every drink.
- Put a small card by each pitcher naming what's in it, so guests can choose without asking.

## The Toast Without a Glass Problem

A toast works with any beverage. Raise a mug of cider or a water glass and nobody will think twice. As host, you can set the tone by saying, "Glasses, mugs, whatever you've got!" Then toast to the people in the room, not the drinks in their hands.

## Why Etiquette Is Moving Past the Bar Cart

Per the Fox News Digital report, alcohol norms are shifting, and inclusive hosting matters more as drinking habits change. You don't need to rethink your whole menu. A good pot of soup, a warm dessert, and a few thoughtful drinks make a party feel generous no matter what anyone is sipping.

## Your Takeaway

Before your next gathering, put a pot of spiced cider on to warm, and when someone's glass looks different from yours, offer them a bowl of something hot instead of a question.
