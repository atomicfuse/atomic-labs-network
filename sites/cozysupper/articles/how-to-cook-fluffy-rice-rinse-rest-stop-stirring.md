A pot of rice sits under so many of our coziest dinners: curries, stews, stuffed peppers, a ladleful of soup. When it turns out gummy or clumped, the whole bowl suffers. Per Fox News, chefs say the fix is usually a small habit, not a fancy gadget. Here's how to change those habits so your rice comes out with distinct, fluffy grains.

The short version: rinse, match your method to the rice, leave it alone while it cooks, and fluff gently. Below is how to apply each one.

## Rinse your rice (especially basmati and jasmine)

The step most home cooks skip is the rinse. Chefs told Fox News that rinsing basmati and jasmine rice before cooking is the key to fluffy, distinct grains that don't clump together in the pot.

A simple way to do it:

1. Put the dry rice in a bowl or fine-mesh strainer.
2. Run cool water over it, swishing with your fingers.
3. Drain and repeat until the water looks noticeably clearer.

This takes about a minute. It's free, and it needs no special equipment.

## Don't use one method for every rice

Another common mistake, according to the report, is cooking every rice the same way. Chefs emphasize that rinsing, soaking, water ratios, resting, and fluffing all depend on the variety of rice and the texture you want.

That means a few practical habits:

- **Check the package.** Water ratios differ by variety, so start with the instructions on your bag rather than a rule you memorized for a different rice.
- **Decide on your texture first.** Separate, airy grains call for different handling than a softer, stickier result.
- **Treat soaking and rinsing as options, not rules.** Some rice benefits from them and some doesn't. Basmati and jasmine are the varieties the chefs specifically tied to rinsing.

I'm not going to hand you one universal ratio here, because the point of the advice is that there isn't one. Pick a variety, follow its package, and adjust next time based on what you got.

## Resist stirring while it cooks

Stirring is one of the habits the article flags as a way to ruin texture. Once your rice is covered and simmering, the best move is to leave it alone. Every time you lift the lid and stir, you're working the grains, and that's how you end up with a gluey pot instead of separate grains.

If you tend to hover over the stove, set a timer and walk away. Use that time to get the rest of dinner going, like a soup or a sheet pan of vegetables.

## Let it rest before you serve

Resting is on the chefs' list of things that matter. After the cooking time is up, take the pot off the heat and let it sit, covered, for a few minutes before opening it. The exact timing varies by rice, so your package directions are the best guide.

Resting is also a handy built-in buffer on a busy night. While the rice sits, you can plate the main dish, set the table, or dish up the stew.

## Fluff gently

The final mistake the article names is fluffing too aggressively. Use a fork or a rice paddle and lift the grains rather than mashing or churning them. Think of it as separating, not stirring. Hard, fast fluffing can break the grains and bring back the clumpiness you worked to avoid.

## Troubleshooting a pot that went wrong

If your rice came out gummy, clumpy, or undercooked, work backward through the habits above and change one thing next time:

- **Gummy or clumpy?** Did you skip the rinse? Did you stir during cooking or fluff roughly? Those are the first suspects.
- **Undercooked?** Check that you followed the water ratio for that specific variety, and that it had its resting time.
- **Not sure which?** Change only one habit per batch so you can tell what helped.

The source summary doesn't give chef-specific fixes for rescuing a bad batch once it's cooked, so I'd rather not invent a trick. Treat the miss as a lesson for the next pot.

## Making rice work for cozy dinners

Rice is one of the most affordable ways to stretch a meal, and getting it right makes everything you serve over it better. A few ways to put it to work:

- Spoon a thick stew or chili over a scoop of fluffy rice.
- Serve a creamy curry with basmati or jasmine.
- Cook a little extra and use leftovers for lunches during the week.

None of that requires a new recipe, just a better habit at the start.

## The takeaway

Next time you cook rice, rinse it until the water runs clearer, follow the ratio for that specific variety, put the lid on, and don't touch it until it has rested. Then fluff gently with a fork.
