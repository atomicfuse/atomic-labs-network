Researchers in Western Australia have launched Food Waste PRO, an online toolkit that helps small and medium food businesses measure their waste and judge whether reduction or upcycling ideas are technically, legally and financially viable, per Phys. It's built for businesses, not home cooks. But the core idea travels well to your kitchen: measure first, then decide what's worth rescuing.

By the end of this article you'll have a simple one-week waste audit, a use-it-up soup that handles almost any vegetable odds and ends, and a small-batch muffin recipe for those spotty bananas.

## Why the business idea works at home

Per the Phys report, food waste is increasingly seen as both a sustainability problem and a missed commercial opportunity, especially as businesses face tighter margins. Coverage of the toolkit also points to a near-zero-waste trial by a business called Sweeter Banana. I don't have the details of that trial, so I won't guess at them.

The home version of the same logic is plain: what you throw away is money you already spent. You can't fix what you haven't measured, and a quick tally shows patterns fast.

## A one-week waste audit (10 minutes total)

You don't need software. You need a bowl and a notepad.

1. **Set a "waste bowl" on the counter.** Everything you'd normally bin goes in: wilted greens, stale heels of bread, soft fruit, leftover scoops of rice.
2. **Jot a line each evening.** Write what went in and why ("forgot it," "cooked too much," "didn't like it").
3. **Review on day seven.** Look for repeats. If celery goes limp every week, buy a smaller bunch or chop and freeze half on day one.
4. **Sort into three groups:** *could have been eaten*, *could have been stretched* (scraps for broth, stale bread for croutons), and *truly inedible* (compost if you can).

Most households find one or two culprits. Fixing those is where the savings are.

## Use-It-Up Vegetable Soup

This is the soup that lives in the "could have been eaten" group. It's forgiving, cheap and good on a chilly evening.

**Serves:** 4 to 6 
**Time:** 10 minutes prep, 35 minutes cooking

### Ingredients

- 2 tablespoons olive oil or butter
- 1 onion, diced
- 2 carrots and 2 celery stalks, diced
- 2 garlic cloves, minced
- About 4 cups chopped vegetable odds and ends (potato, zucchini, broccoli stems, cabbage, wilting greens)
- 1 can (14 oz) diced tomatoes
- 6 cups broth or water plus a bouillon cube
- 1 cup cooked beans (canned, drained) or 1/2 cup small pasta
- 1 teaspoon salt, plenty of black pepper, 1 teaspoon dried herbs

### Steps

1. Warm the oil in a large pot over medium heat. Cook the onion, carrots and celery for 8 to 10 minutes until softened.
2. Stir in the garlic for 30 seconds.
3. Add the firm vegetables (potato, broccoli stems, cabbage), tomatoes, broth, salt and herbs. Bring to a boil, then simmer 20 minutes.
4. Add the beans or pasta and any tender vegetables like zucchini. Simmer another 10 to 12 minutes, until the pasta is tender.
5. Stir in leafy greens for the last 2 minutes. Taste and adjust the salt.

### Affordable swaps

- No broth? Water plus a bouillon cube and a splash of soy sauce works.
- Rind of Parmesan in the fridge? Drop it in with the broth and remove before serving.
- Stale bread? Toast cubes in a 400°F oven for 8 minutes for croutons.
- Slow cooker: sauté the onion mixture first if you have time, then cook everything except pasta and greens on low for 6 to 7 hours. Add those in the last 30 minutes.

It keeps 4 days in the fridge and freezes well for up to 3 months, which makes it a built-in lunch plan.

## Small-Batch Banana Muffins

Soft, brown-spotted bananas are the classic "missed opportunity." This makes just six muffins, so you're not stuck with a whole loaf.

**Makes:** 6 muffins 
**Time:** 10 minutes prep, 20 to 22 minutes baking

### Ingredients

- 2 very ripe bananas, mashed
- 1/4 cup melted butter
- 1/3 cup sugar (brown sugar is lovely)
- 1 egg
- 1 teaspoon vanilla
- 1 cup all-purpose flour
- 3/4 teaspoon baking soda
- 1/4 teaspoon salt

### Steps

1. Heat the oven to 350°F and line 6 muffin cups.
2. Whisk the bananas, butter, sugar, egg and vanilla in a bowl.
3. Stir in the flour, baking soda and salt until just combined. A few lumps are fine.
4. Divide among the cups and bake 20 to 22 minutes, until a toothpick comes out clean.

**Swaps:** Use oil instead of butter, or add a handful of oats, chopped nuts or chocolate chips if you have a little left in the bag. Too many bananas at once? Peel and freeze them in a bag, and they'll be ready whenever you bake.

## Turn your audit into a habit

After your first week, pick one change. Plan one "clear-out" dinner each week, a soup, a casserole or a fried rice, built from whatever is closest to its end. Keep a freezer bag for vegetable trimmings and add to it until it's full enough for broth.

Start your waste bowl tonight, and on day seven, make the soup.
