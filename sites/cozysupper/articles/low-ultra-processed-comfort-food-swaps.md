Women's Health recently reported on new research linking higher intake of ultra-processed foods (UPFs) to elevated markers of inflammation, which are associated with risks like cardiovascular disease, diabetes, and cancer. That sounds heavy for a food site about cozy suppers. But the experts quoted in that coverage framed the goal as reducing UPFs without aiming for perfection, and that fits comfort cooking well.

By the end of this article, you'll know how to spot the hidden UPFs in common soup, casserole, and slow-cooker recipes, and you'll have a handful of swaps that cost little and take almost no extra time. There's also a one-pot soup to put them into practice.

## Start With What Counts as Ultra-Processed

Per Women's Health, the reporting breaks down what counts as a UPF, and the practical takeaway is that many are packaged foods that look harmless on the shelf. I can't give you a complete official definition from that coverage, so here is a simple kitchen test that is my own rule of thumb, not a clinical standard: read the ingredient list. If it's mostly things you wouldn't keep in your own pantry, it's worth a second look.

In comfort cooking, the usual suspects tend to be:

- Canned cream-of soups used as a casserole base
- Boxed stuffing and seasoning mixes
- Flavored instant noodles and rice packets
- Bottled sauces with long ingredient lists
- Packaged dessert mixes and canned frosting

These are convenient, and nobody needs to feel guilty about them. They're just the easiest places to make a small upgrade.

## Why Small Swaps Beat a Total Overhaul

The message from the experts in the Women's Health piece was not to cut out everything packaged. It was to reduce UPFs gradually. That's good news for busy households, because one swapped ingredient in a dish you already make weekly adds up over a year more reliably than a strict plan you abandon by Thursday.

Pick one recipe you cook often and change one thing. That's the whole strategy.

## Five Easy Swaps for Cozy Dinners

### 1. Replace canned cream soup with a quick homemade white sauce

Melt 2 tablespoons butter in a saucepan, whisk in 2 tablespoons flour, then slowly whisk in 1 1/2 cups milk or broth. Simmer 5 minutes until thick. Season with salt, pepper, and a pinch of garlic powder. This replaces one can of cream soup in most casseroles. Total time: about 8 minutes.

### 2. Swap boxed stuffing for toasted bread cubes

Cube 4 cups of day-old bread (a great use for heels and stale loaves), toss with 2 tablespoons olive oil, and bake at 350°F for 15 minutes. Add dried thyme or sage from your spice shelf.

### 3. Use plain rice and your own seasoning

Cook plain rice and season with broth, onion powder, and herbs. Cooking a double batch costs pennies and covers two dinners.

### 4. Choose simple broth and canned tomatoes

Look for broth and canned tomatoes with short ingredient lists. Canned and frozen vegetables are budget-friendly, and a short label is easy to scan in the aisle.

### 5. Bake dessert from basic pantry staples

A simple apple crisp needs apples, oats, flour, butter, brown sugar, and cinnamon. No mix required, and it's the kind of nostalgic dessert that makes the house smell like fall.

## A One-Pot Soup to Try: Chicken and Rice Soup for Four

This is a flexible, low-UPF soup built from basic ingredients. Serves 4. Active time 15 minutes, total time about 40 minutes.

**You'll need:**

- 1 tablespoon olive oil
- 1 onion, diced
- 2 carrots, sliced
- 2 celery stalks, sliced
- 2 cloves garlic, minced
- 6 cups low-sodium chicken broth
- 1 cup uncooked long-grain rice
- 2 cups shredded cooked chicken (a rotisserie chicken works)
- 1 teaspoon dried thyme
- Salt and pepper to taste

**Steps:**

1. Warm the oil in a large pot over medium heat. Cook the onion, carrots, and celery for 6 to 8 minutes until softened.
2. Add the garlic and thyme and stir for 30 seconds.
3. Pour in the broth and bring to a boil. Stir in the rice, reduce to a simmer, and cover. Cook 15 to 18 minutes until the rice is tender.
4. Stir in the chicken and heat through for 3 to 4 minutes. Season to taste.

**Affordable substitutions:** Use leftover turkey or canned chickpeas instead of chicken. Frozen mixed vegetables can replace fresh carrots and celery. Brown rice works too, but add about 15 minutes of simmering time and an extra cup of broth.

## Make It Work for Slow Cookers and Casseroles

For slow-cooker meals, skip packet seasoning mixes and build flavor with onion, garlic, a spoonful of tomato paste, and dried herbs. Cook chuck roast or chicken thighs with vegetables on low for 6 to 8 hours.

For casseroles, use the white sauce above, add frozen vegetables, and top with toasted bread crumbs or a little shredded cheese. Bake at 350°F for 25 to 30 minutes, until bubbling.

## Keep Convenience, Just Choose Better Shortcuts

You don't have to give up shortcuts. Rotisserie chicken, frozen vegetables, canned beans, and dried pasta are all handy and affordable, and they have short, familiar ingredient lists. Keep them stocked and a homemade dinner is rarely more than 30 minutes away.

## Your Next Step

This week, pick the one packaged ingredient you reach for most often in your dinners, and replace it with a homemade version once. Make the white sauce, toast the bread cubes, or stir up the soup above. One swap is enough to start.
