Dark chocolate gets most of the credit for magnesium, but according to Health, it isn't the richest source. The outlet names six foods that beat it: pumpkin seeds, spinach, amaranth, Brazil nuts, chia, and soybeans. The good news for anyone who cooks comfort food is that all six fit easily into meals you probably already make.

By the end of this article, you'll have a simple way to work each one into a soup, a one-pot dinner, or a cozy dessert, without buying anything fancy.

A quick note on scope: the Health piece lists the foods but the details I have don't include exact magnesium amounts, so I won't quote numbers. The portions and cooking times below are general kitchen guidance for the ideas, not nutrition claims. Magnesium is a popular wellness topic, often linked to sleep, stress, and muscle recovery, but this is a food article, not medical advice. If you have a health concern, ask your doctor.

## Spinach: the easiest one to start with

Spinach is the no-effort entry point because it disappears into hot food.

- **Soup:** For a pot serving four, stir in 5 ounces (a standard clamshell) of fresh spinach during the last 2 minutes of cooking. It wilts almost instantly.
- **Casseroles:** Squeeze thawed frozen spinach dry and fold in about 10 ounces per 9x13 pan. Frozen is cheaper and works just as well here.
- **Pasta bakes:** Layer it between the noodles and sauce.

Substitution tip: no spinach on hand? Any tender green works for texture, but spinach is the one Health names, so stick with it when you can.

## Pumpkin seeds: the crunchy finisher

Pumpkin seeds work as a topping, which means no recipe changes at all.

- Toast 1/4 cup in a dry skillet over medium heat for 3 to 4 minutes, until they start to pop.
- Scatter over butternut squash soup, chili, or baked potatoes.
- Stir a handful into oatmeal cookie dough or a crumble topping.

If you carved a pumpkin this season, you can roast the seeds yourself. Rinse, dry, toss with a little oil and salt, and roast at 325°F for about 20 minutes, stirring once. A bag from the store is the budget-friendly shortcut.

## Amaranth: a cozy porridge or soup thickener

Amaranth is a tiny whole grain that turns soft and a little creamy when cooked. If you've never used it, think of it as a cousin to oatmeal.

**Simple breakfast porridge for two:**

1. Combine 1/2 cup amaranth with 1 1/2 cups water or milk and a pinch of salt.
2. Bring to a boil, then simmer covered for about 20 minutes, stirring now and then.
3. Top with cinnamon, sliced apples, and a spoonful of pumpkin seeds.

You can also add a few tablespoons to a pot of vegetable soup. It thickens the broth as it cooks, about 20 to 25 minutes.

## Chia: the quiet pudding maker

Chia is a nostalgic-dessert helper in disguise. It gels when it sits in liquid, so it makes a rice-pudding-style treat without the stove.

**Cozy chia pudding for two:**

1. Whisk 3 tablespoons chia seeds with 1 cup milk, 1 tablespoon maple syrup or honey, and a pinch of cinnamon.
2. Stir again after 5 minutes to break up clumps.
3. Chill at least 2 hours, or overnight.

Spoon baked cinnamon apples over the top for a warm-and-cold dessert. You can also stir a spoonful into jam, or into pancake batter.

## Brazil nuts: a small, rich addition

Brazil nuts are large, buttery, and a bit pricey, so use them where they count.

- Chop 3 or 4 and sprinkle over a baked apple or fruit crisp.
- Fold chopped nuts into banana bread or brownies.
- Add to a homemade trail mix with raisins and pumpkin seeds.

Budget swap: if the cost stings, buy a small bag and use them as a topping rather than a main ingredient. A little goes a long way.

## Soybeans: a hearty one-pot upgrade

Soybeans, including edamame and dried or canned versions, bring body to meals that need a protein.

- **Chili or stew:** Swap one can of beans for a can of soybeans, drained and rinsed. Add it in the last 15 minutes.
- **Slow cooker:** Stir in 1 1/2 cups of shelled edamame (frozen is fine) for the final 30 minutes on low.
- **Fried rice or skillet dinners:** Toss in a cup of frozen edamame for the last 3 minutes.

Canned beans save time on a weeknight, and dried soybeans save money if you plan ahead and soak them overnight.

## One cozy menu that uses all six

You don't need to eat all six in one day, but here is how a chilly-evening menu might look:

- **Breakfast:** Amaranth porridge with pumpkin seeds.
- **Dinner:** Chili with soybeans, finished with a handful of spinach.
- **Dessert:** Chia pudding topped with chopped Brazil nuts.

That's every food on the list in three ordinary meals.

## Keep the chocolate

Health's point isn't that dark chocolate is a bad choice. It's that it isn't the top source. Keep the square of chocolate after dinner if you love it, and let the others do more of the heavy lifting in your pots and baking dishes.

This week, pick just one: stir a handful of spinach into whatever soup you're already making.
