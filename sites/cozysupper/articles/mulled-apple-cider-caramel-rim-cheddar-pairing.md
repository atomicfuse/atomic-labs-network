Bill Michaels of Fly Creek Cider Mill & Orchard recently joined "Fox & Friends Weekend" to demonstrate autumn cider recipes, per Fox News. The showpiece was hot mulled cider in a caramel-rimmed glass, topped with whipped cream and mulling spices, served alongside extra-sharp cheddar cheese. This piece shows you how to build that same idea at home: a warm, dessert-style cider and a simple cheese pairing you can pull together in about 30 minutes.

A note on the recipe: the segment, as reported, names the elements (cider, mulling spices, caramel rim, whipped cream, extra-sharp cheddar) but not exact measurements. The quantities and timings below are my own home-kitchen suggestions, not Michaels' recipe.

## What Michaels Put in the Glass

Per Fox News, the demonstration featured:

- Hot mulled cider
- A caramel-rimmed glass
- A whipped cream topping
- Mulling spices
- Extra-sharp cheddar cheese on the side

That's a short list, and it's a good one. Nothing here needs a special trip beyond fresh cider and a good block of cheese.

## Warm Mulled Cider for Four

This is my version, built around those elements. It makes about four mugs.

**You'll need:**

- 1 quart (4 cups) apple cider
- 3 cinnamon sticks
- 6 whole cloves
- 3 allspice berries
- 2 strips orange peel (optional)
- Caramel sauce, for rimming
- Whipped cream, for topping

**Steps:**

1. Pour the cider into a medium saucepan. Add the cinnamon, cloves, allspice, and orange peel if using.
2. Heat over medium-low until steaming, with tiny bubbles at the edges. This takes about 8 to 10 minutes. Don't let it boil hard, which can dull the fresh apple flavor.
3. Reduce the heat to low and let the spices steep 10 to 15 minutes. Taste at 10. Stop when it suits you.
4. Strain out the spices, or ladle carefully around them.

For two people, halve everything and use a small saucepan. Leftovers keep in the fridge for a few days and reheat gently.

## How to Rim a Glass With Caramel

This is the step that makes a mug of cider feel like a café drink.

1. Warm a few tablespoons of caramel sauce for 10 to 15 seconds in the microwave so it spreads easily.
2. Spoon it onto a small plate.
3. Dip the rim of each glass or mug into the caramel, turning to coat about a quarter inch.
4. Let it set for a few minutes while the cider steeps.

Use heatproof glasses or sturdy mugs, since you're pouring in hot liquid. If you want extra crunch, press the caramel rim lightly into crushed graham crackers or chopped toasted nuts. That's my own add-on, not part of the segment.

## Top It Like Dessert

Ladle the hot cider into the rimmed glasses and add a generous spoonful of whipped cream. Store-bought whipped cream from a can is perfectly fine on a busy night. If you have heavy cream, whip 1/2 cup with a tablespoon of sugar until soft peaks form, about 2 to 3 minutes with a hand mixer.

Finish with a pinch of cinnamon, or a cinnamon stick as a stirrer. The cream melts into the cider as you sip, which is half the fun.

## Why Sharp Cheddar Belongs on the Plate

Michaels paired the cider with extra-sharp cheddar, a classic fall combination. My take on why it works: cider is sweet and a little tart, and aged cheddar is salty, tangy, and rich. Each one makes the other taste brighter.

A simple way to serve it:

- Cut 4 to 6 ounces of extra-sharp cheddar into small cubes or thin slices.
- Arrange them on a board with apple slices, if you like.
- Add crackers or a few walnuts for crunch.

Plan on about 1 to 1½ ounces of cheese per person as a snack.

## Affordable Swaps

- **No whole spices?** Use 1/2 teaspoon ground cinnamon and a pinch of ground cloves. The cider will be a little cloudier.
- **No caramel sauce?** Skip the rim and drizzle honey or maple syrup over the whipped cream.
- **No fresh cider?** Plain apple juice works in a pinch. Add a splash of lemon juice for brightness, since juice tends to be sweeter and flatter.
- **Cheese on a budget?** A store-brand sharp cheddar still pairs nicely. Buy a block and cut it yourself, which usually costs less than pre-sliced.
- **Slow cooker for a crowd?** Combine the cider and spices in a slow cooker on low for 2 to 3 hours, then switch to warm. This is my suggestion for hosting, and it frees up your stove.

## A Cozy Way to Serve It

This is a good fit for a chilly weekend afternoon or a small gathering. Set out the warm cider, the caramel-rimmed glasses, a bowl of whipped cream, and the cheese board, and let everyone top their own. It's dessert and snack in one, with very little cleanup.
