New Haven, Connecticut, has an official "pizza guru," and people are traveling to meet him. You probably can't get there this weekend, but you can borrow the spirit of the trip: a pizza night at home that feels like an event, not just dinner.

Here is what's behind the buzz, and how to turn it into a cozy, affordable evening in your own kitchen.

## Make Pizza Night a Small Pilgrimage

The appeal of food tourism is that it turns eating into a story. You can do that at home with almost no effort.

### Pick a theme

Choose one style or place you're curious about and make it the night's focus. Read a little about it beforehand. Even ten minutes of reading gives dinner a narrative.

### Do a two-pizza "crawl"

A crawl is just tasting more than one thing. Make two small pizzas instead of one large one: one classic (sauce and cheese) and one with a topping you love. Cut each into four slices and compare. That's a real pizza flight for two, and it keeps portions sensible.

### Set the scene

A lamp on, a checkered cloth or plain parchment on the table, and a simple salad on the side is enough. The goal is to feel like you went somewhere.

## A Simple Pizza Night for Two

This is a general homemade pizza plan, not a New Haven recipe. It makes two 10-inch pizzas, serves two generously, and takes about 30 minutes of active time plus rising.

### What you need

- 2 store-bought dough balls (about 8 oz each), or 1 lb of dough from your grocery's bakery or refrigerated case
- 1 cup canned crushed tomatoes, seasoned with a pinch of salt and a drizzle of olive oil
- 6 oz shredded mozzarella
- Toppings: basil, sliced onion, mushrooms, or pepperoni
- Olive oil and flour for the pan and counter

### Steps

1. Set the dough out for 30 to 45 minutes so it relaxes and stretches easily.
2. Heat your oven to its highest setting, usually 475 to 500°F, and put a heavy baking sheet inside to preheat for at least 15 minutes.
3. Stretch each dough ball by hand on a floured surface to about 10 inches. If it springs back, rest it for 5 minutes and try again.
4. Lay the dough on parchment. Spread a thin layer of sauce, leaving a half-inch border. Go light: too much sauce makes a soggy center.
5. Add cheese and toppings. Keep toppings modest so everything cooks through.
6. Slide the parchment onto the hot sheet. Bake 10 to 14 minutes, until the crust is browned and the cheese is bubbling with a few dark spots.
7. Rest 3 minutes before slicing.

## Affordable Swaps

- **No dough?** Use plain naan or flatbread. Bake at 425°F for 8 to 10 minutes.
- **Mozzarella pricey?** Buy a block and shred it yourself. It's usually cheaper and melts better than pre-shredded.
- **No pizza stone?** An upside-down baking sheet works fine.
- **Fresh tomatoes out of season?** Canned crushed tomatoes are the better choice anyway.

## Keep It Cozy on Chilly Nights

Pizza pairs well with something warm on the side. A small pot of tomato soup, simmered 20 minutes from a can of crushed tomatoes, a little broth, and a spoonful of butter, turns two pizzas into a full cold-weather dinner. Leftover slices reheat well in a skillet over medium heat for 2 to 3 minutes, covered, which crisps the bottom and melts the top.
