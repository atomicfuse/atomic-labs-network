Every holiday season, a few New York City restaurants become nearly impossible to get into. Elle recently rounded up the toughest tables to book, and the list is a who's who of the city's dining scene: The Polo Bar, The Jazz Club at Aman New York, The Corner Store, 4 Charles Prime Rib, L'Artusi, Rolf's, Pebble Bar and L'Avenue at Saks. It also includes special-event meals like Thanksgiving Brunch at Per Se, Thanksgiving Dinner at The Nines, Christmas Eve Dinner at Chez Fifi and Christmas Day Dinner at Galo.

Most of us won't land those reservations, and that's fine. This article gives you the next best thing: a plan for a candlelit, steakhouse-style dinner for two in your own kitchen, built on a small, affordable roast, with a make-ahead dessert so you can actually sit down and enjoy it.

## Why Recreate It at Home

Per Elle, the appeal of these tables is partly the food and partly the occasion. A hard-won reservation feels sweeter this time of year. You can borrow that feeling without the booking scramble. The trick is treating a weeknight or weekend dinner like an event: set the table, dim the lights, and serve something that feels special.

One item on Elle's list, 4 Charles Prime Rib, points to the dish we'll cook. Prime rib is the classic celebration roast, and a small version is surprisingly doable.

## The Menu: Small-Batch Roast Dinner for Two

- Herb-crusted small beef roast
- Creamy mashed potatoes
- Buttered green beans
- Warm baked apples for dessert

Total hands-on time is about 40 minutes. Everything fits in one oven and two pots.

## Step 1: Choose an Affordable Roast

A full prime rib is expensive and serves a crowd. For two people with leftovers, buy a **2 to 2½ pound boneless beef roast**. Good budget-friendly options:

- **Top sirloin roast** is lean, tender enough when sliced thin, and usually the cheapest.
- **Chuck eye roast** has more marbling and a richer flavor.
- **Small ribeye roast**, if you want to splurge a little.

Ask the butcher counter to tie it so it cooks evenly.

## Step 2: Season Ahead

Seasoning the roast early gives you better flavor and less to do later.

1. Pat the roast dry with paper towels.
2. Mix 1½ teaspoons kosher salt, 1 teaspoon black pepper, 1 teaspoon garlic powder and 1 tablespoon chopped fresh rosemary or thyme (or 1 teaspoon dried).
3. Rub the mixture all over with 1 tablespoon olive oil.
4. Refrigerate uncovered for at least 1 hour, or up to overnight.

Let it sit on the counter for 30 minutes before roasting.

## Step 3: Roast It

1. Heat the oven to 450°F.
2. Place the roast on a rack in a small pan, or on a bed of thick onion slices.
3. Roast for 15 minutes to build a crust.
4. Lower the heat to 325°F and keep roasting for about 35 to 50 minutes more.
5. Use a meat thermometer. Pull the roast at **125°F for medium-rare** or **135°F for medium**. The temperature will rise about 5 to 10 degrees as it rests.
6. Rest the roast, loosely tented with foil, for 20 minutes before slicing.

Times vary with the cut and shape of your roast, so trust the thermometer over the clock.

## Step 4: Make the Sides While It Rests

### Creamy Mashed Potatoes (serves 2 with leftovers)

1. Peel and cube 1½ pounds Yukon Gold or russet potatoes.
2. Boil in salted water for 15 to 20 minutes until very tender.
3. Drain, then mash with 3 tablespoons butter, ¼ to ⅓ cup warm milk and ½ teaspoon salt.

**Substitution:** No milk? Use a splash of the potato cooking water. Plain yogurt or sour cream also works.

### Buttered Green Beans

1. Trim ½ pound green beans.
2. Simmer in salted water for 4 to 5 minutes, then drain.
3. Toss in a skillet with 1 tablespoon butter and a squeeze of lemon.

Frozen green beans work fine; cook them straight from the freezer for 5 to 6 minutes.

### Quick Pan Sauce (optional)

If you roasted on a pan, pour off most of the fat, then add ½ cup beef broth and a teaspoon of Dijon mustard. Simmer 3 to 4 minutes, scraping up the browned bits. Whisk in 1 tablespoon cold butter.

## Step 5: Dessert You Make Ahead

The restaurant experience feels easy because someone else does the work. Make dessert earlier in the day so you don't.

### Warm Baked Apples (serves 2)

1. Heat the oven to 350°F.
2. Core 2 large baking apples (Honeycrisp, Braeburn or Granny Smith), leaving the bottoms intact.
3. Mix 2 tablespoons oats, 1 tablespoon brown sugar, 1 tablespoon softened butter and ½ teaspoon cinnamon.
4. Fill the apples, set them in a small dish with 2 tablespoons water, and bake 35 to 45 minutes until tender.

Serve warm with vanilla ice cream. They reheat in 10 minutes at 300°F while you clear the plates. Don't have oats? Crushed graham crackers or chopped walnuts work.

## Step 6: Set the Scene

This is the part that borrows from the big-night-out feeling.

- Use cloth napkins and real plates.
- Light a couple of candles away from the food so they don't compete with the aromas.
- Put on a jazz playlist. (Elle's list includes The Jazz Club at Aman New York, so it's a fitting nod.)
- Slice the roast at the table or on a board and serve it family-style.

## Leftovers Plan

A 2-pound roast gives you leftovers for a second meal. Slice cold beef thin for sandwiches with horseradish and mustard, or warm slices gently in leftover pan sauce. Store in an airtight container for up to 3 days in the refrigerator.

## Your Move

Pick a night this month, buy the roast this weekend, and season it the evening before. You won't need a reservation, and the best table in the house will be yours.
