New research out of Australia, reported by Medical Xpress, finds that meal delivery apps like Uber Eats and DoorDash are seen as "treat food" platforms, and that deals and app design can nudge people toward less healthy choices. The researchers argue governments should update nutrition policy to cover digital food environments.

You don't need a policy change to act on that tonight. This article gives you a simple way to spot a promo-driven order, plus a 35-minute one-pot soup that is about as easy as opening an app on a chilly evening.

## What the research says (and what it doesn't)

Per Medical Xpress, the national Australian study found that people think of delivery apps as places for treats. The apps' deals and design, the report says, steer users toward less healthy options. The researchers' call is aimed at governments, not at individual diners.

That matters for tone. Nobody here is being scolded. If an app puts a discount in front of you at 6:30 on a tired Tuesday, saying yes is a normal human response. The useful move is noticing when the deal is making the decision for you.

## A quick check before you tap "order"

Try these three questions. They take about ten seconds.

1. **Did I want this before I opened the app?** If the craving started with a banner or a discount, pause.
2. **Is the deal saving money, or just making the total feel smaller?** A minimum spend can push an order well past what you'd planned.
3. **Could I have dinner on the table in 35 minutes at home?** If yes, the soup below may win.

Treat nights are lovely. The goal is choosing them on purpose.

## Easy Chicken and Rice One-Pot Soup

**Serves:** 4 generously, with leftovers possible
**Active time:** 10 minutes
**Total time:** about 35 minutes

### Ingredients

- 1 tablespoon olive oil or butter
- 1 medium onion, diced
- 2 carrots, sliced
- 2 celery stalks, sliced (optional; see substitutions)
- 3 garlic cloves, minced
- 6 cups chicken broth (or water plus 2 bouillon cubes)
- 1 pound boneless chicken thighs or breasts
- 3/4 cup long-grain white rice
- 1 teaspoon dried thyme
- 1 bay leaf
- Salt and pepper
- Juice of half a lemon
- Handful of frozen peas or chopped parsley

### Steps

1. **Soften the vegetables.** Heat the oil in a large pot over medium heat. Add the onion, carrots, and celery with a pinch of salt. Cook 5 minutes, stirring now and then.
2. **Add garlic and liquid.** Stir in the garlic for 30 seconds, then pour in the broth. Add thyme and bay leaf.
3. **Cook the chicken.** Nestle the whole chicken pieces into the pot. Bring to a gentle boil, then reduce to a simmer, cover, and cook 12 minutes.
4. **Shred and add rice.** Lift the chicken out onto a plate. Stir the rice into the pot and simmer, uncovered, for 12 to 15 minutes, until tender. Meanwhile, shred the chicken with two forks.
5. **Finish.** Return the chicken to the pot with the peas. Simmer 2 minutes. Remove the bay leaf, add lemon juice, and season with salt and pepper to taste.

Check that the chicken is cooked through (165°F / 74°C) before shredding.

## Affordable swaps

- **No fresh vegetables?** Use a bag of frozen soup vegetables or mixed vegetables, added with the rice.
- **Chicken costs too much?** Use a can or two of drained white beans, or leftover roast chicken. Add leftovers at the end, just to heat through.
- **No lemon?** A splash of vinegar works.
- **Brown rice?** Use it, but it needs about 40 minutes, so add it before the chicken goes in.
- **Want it creamier?** Stir in a splash of milk at the end. Don't boil it afterward.

## Make it a delivery-night rival

Part of delivery's appeal is that it feels like an event. You can borrow that.

- Serve the soup with buttered toast or a warm bread roll.
- Put it in the good bowls.
- Let everyone add their own toppings: black pepper, grated Parmesan, hot sauce, a little extra lemon.

A simple dessert helps too. Baked apples (core, fill with a spoonful of brown sugar and cinnamon, bake at 375°F for 30 to 35 minutes) can go in the oven while you eat the first course prep, and they feel like a treat without a menu.

## Storing and reheating

The rice keeps absorbing broth, so leftovers thicken. Refrigerate in airtight containers for up to 3 days. Add a splash of water or broth when reheating on the stove until it's steaming hot. The soup also freezes, though rice softens a bit; for the best texture, freeze it without the rice and cook fresh rice when serving.

## A small plan for the nights you'll still order

You don't have to give up delivery. A few habits keep it a choice:

- **Decide first, then open the app.** Know what you're after before the promos appear.
- **Keep one backup dinner ready.** A frozen batch of this soup is a good one.
- **Order a treat, then add something fresh at home.** A bag of salad or some fruit on the side rounds out the meal.

Next time a discount banner pops up, ask whether you want the food or just the deal, and if you're unsure, put a pot on the stove.
