A beloved piece of Los Angeles is coming back. The Original Pantry Cafe, a downtown diner that closed after a century in business, is set to reopen Nov. 19, per Fox Business. If you can't get to LA, you can still bring some of that diner spirit to your own table with a simple breakfast-for-dinner night. Below: what we know about the reopening, then a small-batch pancake recipe for two to four people that takes about 25 minutes.

## What We Know About the Reopening

Per Fox Business, the Original Pantry Cafe will reopen under a nonprofit social-enterprise model. All profits are slated to support Hope the Mission's homelessness programs. The plan also keeps the diner's classic recipes and brings back longtime union employees.

The closure was reportedly tied to labor disputes, which makes the return of those workers a central part of the story. ## Why Diner Food Feels So Comforting

Diner food works because it is unfussy. Nothing on the plate is trying to impress you. It's warm, filling, and familiar, and it's cheap to make at home. That's why breakfast for dinner is one of the easiest ways to make a chilly evening feel special on a weeknight.

## Diner-Style Pancakes for Two to Four

**Makes:** about 8 pancakes (serves 2 hungry people or 4 with sides)
**Time:** 10 minutes prep, 15 minutes cooking

### Ingredients

- 1 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
- 2 tablespoons sugar
- 2 teaspoons baking powder
- 1/2 teaspoon salt
- 1 1/4 cups milk
- 1 large egg
- 3 tablespoons melted butter, plus more for the pan and serving
- 1 teaspoon vanilla (optional)

### Steps

1. Whisk the flour, sugar, baking powder, and salt in a large bowl.
2. In a second bowl, whisk the milk, egg, melted butter, and vanilla.
3. Pour the wet mixture into the dry. Stir just until combined. A few lumps are fine and keep the pancakes tender.
4. Rest the batter for 5 minutes while the pan heats over medium heat.
5. Lightly butter the pan. Pour in about 1/4 cup batter per pancake.
6. Cook 2 to 3 minutes, until bubbles form and the edges look set. Flip and cook 1 to 2 minutes more, until golden.
7. Keep finished pancakes on a baking sheet in a 200°F oven while you cook the rest.

### Affordable Substitutions

- **No milk?** Use water plus an extra tablespoon of butter, or thinned plain yogurt.
- **No buttermilk?** This recipe doesn't need it, but you can stir 1 tablespoon of vinegar or lemon juice into the milk for a tangier stack.
- **No vanilla?** Skip it, or add a pinch of cinnamon.
- **No maple syrup?** Warm jam with a splash of water, or simmer brown sugar with a little butter and water for a quick syrup.

## Round Out the Plate

A diner breakfast is more than pancakes. Add these low-effort sides while the batter rests:

- **Scrambled eggs:** 4 eggs, a splash of milk, and a pat of butter. Cook over low heat for 3 to 4 minutes, stirring gently.
- **Crispy bacon or sausage:** Bake bacon on a sheet pan at 400°F for 15 to 20 minutes, no splatter on the stovetop.
- **Fried potatoes:** Dice 2 medium potatoes small, then cook in a skillet with oil for 12 to 15 minutes, turning occasionally, until crisp.

## Make It a Tradition

Pick one night a week, set the table, and cook breakfast for dinner. Let the kids flip the pancakes. It costs very little, and it turns a hectic weeknight into something that feels like a small occasion.

If you want to tie it to the news, you could even donate what you saved by skipping a restaurant meal to a local food program. Either way, the takeaway is the same: this week, make a stack of pancakes and call everyone to the table.
