A pot of soup is only as good as what goes into it. According to Fox News, Los Angeles-based cookbook author Carmen Perr, who wrote "Cook What Grows," argues that better home cooking starts before you enter the kitchen, with how you choose your fruits, vegetables and herbs. Her approach, as reported, is to pay attention to texture, color and aroma while you shop.

By the end of this article, you'll have a simple three-sense routine for the produce aisle or farmers market, plus ideas for putting it to work in the hearty, cozy meals this site is all about.

## Why the Shopping Trip Matters

When you cook affordably and seasonally, ingredients do the heavy lifting. A simple vegetable soup or a one-pot dinner has few components, so there's nowhere for a sad carrot or a limp bunch of parsley to hide. Choosing well at the store means you can season lightly and let the food taste like itself.

It also helps with waste. Produce you picked carefully tends to be produce you'll actually use, rather than something that wilts in the crisper drawer.

## Use Three Senses Instead of Labels

Perr's tips, per Fox News, center on texture, color and aroma. Here's how I'd turn those into a quick routine. The specific examples below are my own general kitchen guidance, not quotes from Perr.

### Texture: Pick It Up

Don't just look. Hold the produce. Good vegetables and fruits generally feel sturdy for their type, without soft spots, slimy patches or wrinkling. Leafy greens and herbs should look perky rather than limp. Onions, potatoes and squash should feel firm, not squishy.

A good habit: gently handle two or three of the same item and pick the one that feels best. It takes ten seconds.

### Color: Look for Vibrancy

Color tells you a lot. Look for bright, even color that suits the item. Herbs should be lively green, not yellowed or blackened at the edges. Tomatoes, peppers and carrots should look rich rather than dull or faded.

A little imperfection is fine. Odd shapes and small blemishes on a farmers market tomato usually matter less than overall freshness.

### Aroma: Lean In

Smell is the most underused tool in the produce section. Fresh herbs should smell fragrant the moment you bring them close. Ripe fruit often smells like itself, and a bland or musty smell can be a hint to keep looking. If you can't smell anything at all on something that should be aromatic, consider choosing another.

## Put It to Work: A Chilly-Evening Plan

Here's how these habits play out when you're shopping for a cozy week. These are flexible, general ideas rather than a tested recipe from Perr.

### A Simple Vegetable Soup (serves 4, about 45 minutes)

Shop for: 3 carrots, 2 celery stalks, 1 onion, 2 potatoes, a small bunch of parsley or thyme, and a can of white beans.

1. Dice the onion, carrots and celery. Cook in a little oil in a large pot over medium heat for 8 to 10 minutes until softened.
2. Add diced potatoes, the drained beans and 5 cups of water or broth. Season with salt.
3. Simmer 20 to 25 minutes until the potatoes are tender.
4. Stir in chopped fresh herbs at the end, so their aroma stays bright.

This is where your shopping pays off: firm carrots, fragrant herbs and sturdy potatoes make a plain pot taste far more interesting.

### Affordable Swaps

- No fresh herbs? Use about 1 teaspoon dried in place of a tablespoon fresh, added earlier with the broth.
- Potatoes pricey? Use extra carrots or any firm root vegetable in season.
- No broth? Water plus a bit more salt and a splash of lemon or vinegar works in a pinch.

## Farmers Market Tips for Beginners

Farmers markets can feel intimidating, but your three senses work just as well there. Walk the whole market once before buying. Compare the same item at different stalls, using your hands and nose. Buy what looks and smells best that day rather than sticking rigidly to a list. That flexibility is the heart of seasonal cooking: let what's good decide what's for dinner.

If you're shopping for two, buy smaller amounts. Two carrots and one onion bought thoughtfully beat a big bag that spoils.

## Common Grocery Store Habits to Rethink

- **Grabbing the first one.** Take a moment to compare.
- **Buying only by the recipe.** If the recipe calls for parsley and the parsley looks tired, swap in another fresh herb or adjust the plan.
- **Ignoring your nose.** Aroma costs nothing to check.
- **Overbuying.** Pick what you'll cook this week, especially delicate items like herbs and greens.

## Your Next Step

On your next shopping trip, choose just one item for the week's soup or casserole and test it with all three senses: feel it, look at it, smell it. Do that with every ingredient and you'll notice dinner tasting better before you've even turned on the stove.
