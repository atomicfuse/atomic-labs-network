Fewer of us are going out to eat every week, and if you're one of them, you probably still want a dinner that feels like an occasion. Below is a tested-style one-pot dinner that serves four, takes about 50 minutes, and gives you that "someone else made this" feeling without the check.

## What the numbers say

Per a Gallup poll reported by Fox News, only 39% of U.S. adults now eat at a restaurant at least once a week, a record low and a sharp drop from pre-pandemic levels. The report says middle- and upper-income adults are cutting back the most.

The takeaway in that coverage isn't that Americans have given up on restaurants. It's that they're getting pickier, with value, costs, food safety concerns and changing habits all playing a part. Dining out is shifting from a routine to more of a treat.

I can't tell you from that poll exactly how much any household saves by staying in. But I can offer a simple idea: if restaurant night is becoming the special occasion, home dinners can borrow some of that feeling.

## The plan: a one-pot dinner that feels like a treat

This creamy lemon chicken and rice skillet uses one pan, pantry staples, and cheap cuts. It's the kind of dish that smells like a restaurant kitchen by minute fifteen.

**Serves:** 4 
**Prep:** 15 minutes 
**Cook:** 35 minutes 
**Pan:** a large, deep skillet or Dutch oven with a lid

### Ingredients

- 1 1/2 to 2 pounds bone-in or boneless chicken thighs
- 1 teaspoon salt, divided, plus black pepper
- 2 tablespoons olive oil or butter
- 1 medium onion, diced
- 3 cloves garlic, minced (or 1 teaspoon garlic powder)
- 1 1/2 cups long-grain white rice
- 2 3/4 cups chicken broth (or water plus 2 bouillon cubes)
- 1 teaspoon dried thyme or Italian seasoning
- 1 lemon, zested and juiced
- 1/2 cup milk, half-and-half, or a splash of cream
- 1 1/2 cups frozen peas or chopped broccoli
- Grated Parmesan, for serving (optional)

### Steps

1. **Season and sear.** Pat the chicken dry and season with half the salt and pepper. Heat the oil over medium-high heat. Brown the chicken 4 to 5 minutes per side, then move it to a plate. It won't be cooked through yet.
2. **Build the base.** In the same pan, cook the onion in the drippings for 4 minutes, until soft. Add the garlic and thyme for 30 seconds.
3. **Toast the rice.** Stir in the rice and cook for 1 minute so it coats in the fat.
4. **Add liquid.** Pour in the broth and lemon juice, scraping up the browned bits. Stir in the remaining salt. Nestle the chicken on top.
5. **Simmer covered.** Bring to a boil, reduce to low, cover tightly, and cook 20 minutes. Don't lift the lid early.
6. **Finish.** Stir in the milk, lemon zest and frozen peas. Cover off the heat for 5 minutes. Check that the chicken reaches 165°F (175°F is better for thighs, if you want them tender).
7. **Serve** with Parmesan and an extra squeeze of lemon.

## Affordable swaps

The point is to keep this cheap, so use what you have.

- **No fresh lemon?** Use 2 tablespoons bottled lemon juice and skip the zest.
- **No cream?** Plain milk works. For a thicker sauce, stir 1 tablespoon cream cheese into the pan.
- **Different protein?** Smoked sausage slices or a can of drained chickpeas can replace the chicken. Cut the simmer time as needed for precooked ingredients.
- **Veggies:** carrots, spinach, or green beans all work in place of peas.
- **Brown rice?** It needs more liquid and about 40 minutes, so plan ahead.

## Make it feel like going out

A few small touches do a lot of work:

- Set the table with cloth napkins or a candle.
- Serve in shallow bowls rather than eating from the pan.
- Warm some bread with butter on the side.
- Finish with something sweet. Baked apples are easy: core 4 apples, fill with a spoonful of oats, brown sugar and cinnamon, add a pat of butter, and bake at 350°F for 35 to 40 minutes.

## Scale it down for two

Cooking for two? Halve everything and use a medium skillet. The simmer time stays about the same, maybe a couple of minutes less. Leftovers reheat well with a splash of broth or water in the microwave, so cooking the full batch for lunches is also a fine choice.

## Make one night the "restaurant night"

If eating out is turning into a treat for you too, pick one evening a week and give it that status at home. Choose the dish ahead of time, shop for it on the weekend, and put the candle on the table. You'll look forward to it the way you would a reservation.
