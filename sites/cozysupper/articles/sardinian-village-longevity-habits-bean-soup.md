In the small Sardinian village of Bolotana, 10 of the 2,262 residents are centenarians. That's the figure cited in a Fox News report on National Geographic's study of the village as a potential new "blue zone." You don't need to move to Italy to borrow what's there. This article covers the three everyday habits reported from Bolotana and gives you a hearty, budget-friendly bean minestrone that puts two of them on your table this week.

## What Bolotana Is Reportedly Doing

Per Fox News, National Geographic researchers are looking at Bolotana because centenarians appear there at unusually high rates. The three habits highlighted are:

1. Constant, natural movement
2. A mostly plant-based diet
3. Strong social connection

None of these is a gadget, supplement, or strict program. That's the appealing part. Researchers are still studying the village, so treat this as an encouraging pattern rather than a guarantee. Still, it's hard to argue with a routine built around walking, beans, and good company.

## Habit 1: Move Without "Exercising"

The reported habit is movement woven into the day, not a scheduled workout. Some easy ways to copy that at home:

- Walk to one errand a week instead of driving.
- Take a 10-minute stroll after dinner while the dishes soak.
- Stand and stir: cooking from scratch is a small, steady kind of activity.

A chilly evening walk makes a bowl of hot soup taste even better, which doesn't hurt.

## Habit 2: Make Beans and Vegetables the Center of the Bowl

A mostly plant-based diet doesn't have to mean fancy ingredients. It can mean a big pot of minestrone, which is cheap, forgiving, and better the next day. Here's a version that fits a weeknight.

### Cozy Bean Minestrone

**Serves:** 4 generous bowls (leftovers reheat well)
**Time:** 15 minutes prep, about 40 minutes simmering

**Ingredients**
- 2 tablespoons olive oil
- 1 onion, diced
- 2 carrots, diced
- 2 celery stalks, diced
- 3 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 can (14 oz) diced tomatoes
- 6 cups vegetable broth or water with a bouillon cube
- 2 cans (15 oz each) white beans or chickpeas, drained and rinsed
- 1 zucchini or 2 cups chopped cabbage
- 1 cup small pasta (ditalini or elbows), optional
- 1 teaspoon dried oregano or Italian seasoning
- Salt and pepper to taste
- Handful of chopped greens such as kale or spinach

**Steps**
1. Warm the olive oil in a large pot over medium heat. Cook the onion, carrots, and celery for 8 minutes, stirring now and then, until soft.
2. Add the garlic and herbs and stir for 1 minute.
3. Pour in the tomatoes and broth. Bring to a boil, then lower the heat and simmer 15 minutes.
4. Add the beans and zucchini or cabbage. Simmer 10 more minutes. If using pasta, add it now and cook until tender, about 8 to 10 minutes.
5. Stir in the greens for the last 2 minutes. Season with salt and pepper.

**Serve** with crusty bread and a drizzle of olive oil.

### Affordable Swaps

- Dried beans instead of canned: soak overnight and simmer until tender before adding, or use a slow cooker on low for 6 to 8 hours with the broth.
- Frozen mixed vegetables can replace fresh carrots, celery, and zucchini.
- No pasta? Add an extra can of beans or a diced potato.
- Out of tomatoes? Use 2 tablespoons of tomato paste stirred into the broth.

### Small-Batch Option for Two

Halve everything: 1 tablespoon oil, 1 carrot, 1 celery stalk, 1 can of beans, 3 cups broth. Use a medium pot and keep the same timing.

## Habit 3: Share the Table

The third habit reported from Bolotana is strong social connection. At home, the easiest way to practice that is a shared meal. A pot of minestrone stretches well, so it's a natural reason to invite a neighbor, a parent, or a friend over. Ask someone to bring the bread. Keep it casual; the point is the company, not a perfect table.

If you live alone, make the full pot and drop a container off with someone nearby, or set a standing weekly soup night with a friend, in person or by phone.

## A Simple Week to Try It

- **Monday:** Make the minestrone. Walk after dinner.
- **Wednesday:** Reheat leftovers for lunch and walk to one errand.
- **Sunday:** Cook for someone else, or with someone else.

Three small changes, no special equipment, and a pot that costs just a few dollars to fill.

The takeaway: this week, simmer one pot of bean soup, share a bowl with someone, and walk to the mailbox or corner store on your way back.
