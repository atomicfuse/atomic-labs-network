The idea is appealing, especially if takeout fatigue and grocery bills have you wanting to cook more. The published summary doesn't spell out what the individual products are, so I won't guess at them. Instead, here is a pared-down version for chilly evenings: the handful of tools that cover soup, a casserole, a one-pot dinner, and one nostalgic dessert, with a first week to put them to work.

You don't need 26 things. You need a few good ones and a plan for using them.

## Start With the Meals, Not the Gadgets

The easiest way to overspend is to buy tools for a cook you imagine becoming. Buy for the meals you'll actually make in the next two weeks. For cozy cooking, that usually means:

- A soup or stew (serves 4 to 6, with leftovers)
- A one-pot dinner, like pasta or rice with vegetables and chicken
- A baked casserole
- A simple dessert, such as baked apples or a fruit crisp

Every tool below earns its spot by serving at least two of those.

## The Short List Worth Buying First

### A big pot with a lid

A 5- to 6-quart Dutch oven or heavy stockpot does soup, stew, chili, and one-pot pasta. If a Dutch oven is out of budget, a sturdy stockpot from a restaurant supply or discount store works fine. Heavy bottoms resist scorching, but you can compensate with lower heat and more stirring.

### One good knife and a cutting board

A sharp 8-inch chef's knife makes chopping onions, carrots, and celery quicker and safer than a dull one. Pair it with a board big enough that vegetables don't slide off. A damp towel under the board keeps it from slipping.

### A 9x13-inch baking dish

This is your casserole dish, your crisp dish, and your roasting pan for sheet-style dinners. Glass or ceramic is fine. Cover it with foil if you don't own a lid.

### A wooden spoon, a ladle, and a heatproof spatula

Three utensils handle almost all stovetop work. Skip the 12-piece sets.

### Measuring cups and spoons

Baking and casseroles reward accuracy. Soups forgive eyeballing; desserts don't.

### A slow cooker, if you're ready for one more purchase

If your evenings are packed, a 6-quart slow cooker lets you start dinner in the morning. Look for secondhand ones in good condition. This is the one "maybe" on the list, and you can wait on it.

## A Beginner Soup That Teaches the Basics

This simple chicken and vegetable soup uses the pot, knife, and spoon, and teaches the rhythm behind most soups: soften, simmer, season.

**Serves 4 to 6. Active time 15 minutes, total about 45 minutes.**

1. Heat 2 tablespoons oil in your pot over medium heat.
2. Add 1 chopped onion, 2 chopped carrots, and 2 chopped celery stalks with a pinch of salt. Cook 6 to 8 minutes, stirring, until soft.
3. Add 3 minced garlic cloves and cook 30 seconds.
4. Pour in 6 cups broth and add 1 pound boneless chicken thighs or breasts. Bring to a gentle boil, then reduce to a simmer.
5. Simmer 20 minutes, until the chicken is cooked through (165°F inside).
6. Lift out the chicken, shred it with two forks, and return it to the pot.
7. Add 1 cup small pasta or rice if you like and simmer until tender, about 10 minutes more. Taste, then add salt and pepper.

**Affordable swaps:** water plus bouillon for broth, frozen mixed vegetables for fresh, and any leftover cooked chicken (skip the simmering step and just heat it through).

## A Small-Batch Option for Two

Halve the recipe above and use a 3-quart saucepan. Use 3 cups broth, half a pound of chicken, and half the vegetables. The timing stays about the same. Leftovers reheat well for lunch the next day.

## A Nostalgic Dessert With Almost No Skill Required

Baked apples need only your baking dish.

**Serves 4. Active time 10 minutes, bake 40 minutes at 375°F.**

1. Core 4 apples and set them in the dish.
2. Mix 3 tablespoons oats, 2 tablespoons brown sugar, 2 tablespoons softened butter, and 1 teaspoon cinnamon.
3. Spoon the mixture into the apples and pour about 1/2 cup water into the dish.
4. Bake until the apples are tender when poked with a knife.

No oats? Use crushed crackers or just extra brown sugar.

## Your First Week, Mapped Out

- **Sunday:** Make the soup. Plan on leftovers.
- **Tuesday:** Reheat the soup or make a one-pot pasta with whatever vegetables you have.
- **Wednesday:** Bake a simple casserole in the 9x13 dish, such as rice, chicken, and frozen broccoli with cheese.
- **Friday:** Make baked apples while dinner finishes.

Cooking three or four nights at home is a realistic goal for a beginner. You don't have to cook every night to see the difference in your grocery and takeout spending.

## What to Hold Off On

If a gadget does one narrow job, wait. Buy it only after you've caught yourself wishing for it three separate times. Tools you reach for weekly are the ones worth owning.

This week, pick one soup, buy a pot and a sharp knife if you lack them, and cook it on Sunday. That single pot of soup will teach you more than a cart of 26 products.
