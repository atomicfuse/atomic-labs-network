Southern food has a reputation for being heavy. Texas-based chef and restaurateur Tiffany Derry is pushing back on that. Per Fox News, she talks about wholesome ingredients, colorful vegetables, and simple preparation as the real heart of what has traditionally been served across the South.

That idea is easy to take home. Below is a one-pot supper for two that follows it: smoky greens, sweet potato, and beans, ready in about 50 minutes. The recipe is my own interpretation of that spirit, not one of Derry's dishes.

## What Derry's Point Means in Your Kitchen

The takeaway from Derry's perspective, as reported by Fox News, is that Southern cooking leans on fresh, colorful produce and simple methods. So the plan is straightforward: let vegetables do most of the work, and keep the seasoning honest.

That suits a chilly evening. A pot of vegetables simmered until tender is every bit as comforting as a casserole, and it tends to cost less.

## Smoky Greens, Sweet Potato, and Bean Supper

**Serves:** 2 generous bowls, with a little left for lunch
**Active time:** 15 minutes
**Total time:** about 50 minutes

### What You'll Need

- 1 tablespoon oil or bacon drippings
- 1 small onion, diced
- 2 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 medium sweet potato (about 10 ounces), peeled and cut into 3/4-inch cubes
- 1 bunch collard greens or kale (about 8 ounces), stems removed, leaves chopped
- 1 can (15 ounces) black-eyed peas or white beans, drained and rinsed
- 2 cups low-sodium broth or water
- 1 smoked ham hock, a few slices of chopped bacon, or 1/2 teaspoon smoked paprika
- 1 tablespoon apple cider vinegar
- Salt, black pepper, and hot sauce to taste

### Steps

1. **Soften the base (5 minutes).** Heat the oil in a medium pot over medium heat. Cook the onion with a pinch of salt until soft and golden at the edges. Add the garlic for the last 30 seconds.
2. **Add the smoke and liquid.** Stir in the broth and your smoky element. If you're using a ham hock, simmer it for 15 minutes first so the flavor reaches the broth.
3. **Add the sweet potato (10 minutes).** Simmer, partly covered, until the cubes are just starting to soften.
4. **Add the greens (15 minutes).** Stir in the collards a handful at a time. Cover and cook until tender. Collards need the full time; kale needs closer to 8 to 10 minutes.
5. **Finish.** Add the beans and heat through for 5 minutes. Pull out the ham hock, shred any meat back into the pot, and stir in the vinegar. Taste, then adjust the salt, pepper, and hot sauce.

Serve it in bowls with cornbread or over a spoonful of rice.

## Affordable Swaps That Still Work

- **Greens:** Frozen chopped collards or spinach work well. Use about 10 ounces frozen, and add them straight to the pot.
- **Smoke:** Skip the meat entirely and use smoked paprika for a vegetarian pot. Add a splash more vinegar to keep it bright.
- **Beans:** Any canned bean you already have in the pantry will do.
- **Sweet potato:** Butternut squash or regular potatoes cook in about the same time.
- **Broth:** Water plus an extra pinch of salt and a bay leaf is fine if you're out of broth.

## Making It Heartier Without Making It Heavy

This pot is filling because of the beans and sweet potato, not because of added fat. If you want more heft, here are a few easy options:

- Stir in a handful of cooked rice or small pasta during the last 10 minutes.
- Top with a fried or soft-boiled egg.
- Serve alongside a skillet of cornbread.

## Storing and Reheating

The soup keeps in the fridge for up to 4 days in a covered container. The flavors settle in overnight, so day two is often better. Reheat gently on the stove with a splash of water, since the beans and sweet potato soak up liquid. It also freezes for up to 3 months, though the sweet potato will be a little softer after thawing.

## Your Next Step

Next time you plan a cozy dinner, start with the vegetables in your crisper drawer instead of the heaviest dish you can think of. Simmer them with something smoky and finish with vinegar, and you'll see what Derry means about Southern cooking.
