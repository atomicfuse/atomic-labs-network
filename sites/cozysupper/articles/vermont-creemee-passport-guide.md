Vermont's famous creemee now has its own passport. If you plan to road-trip through the state, or you just like the idea of a dessert with a stamp collection attached, here is what you need to know and how to make the most of it. And because most of us can't hop in the car for soft serve every night, you'll also get a simple way to bring the creemee spirit into your own kitchen.

## What the Vermont Creemee Passport Is

Per Afar, the Vermont Creemee passport launched in April 2026. It is a 20-page booklet paired with a digital directory, and it covers 189 places to try the state's creemee, a richer, higher-butterfat soft serve.

The booklet has pages for collecting stamps, so each stop becomes a small souvenir. Afar also reports that proceeds support Vermont nonprofits, which means your dessert habit helps a good cause.

The details beyond that, such as where to buy a copy or how stamps are verified, weren't spelled out in Afar's report, so check the official passport listing before you go.

## Why a Creemee Isn't Just Soft Serve

The key difference, according to Afar, is richness. A creemee is made with higher butterfat, which gives it a fuller, creamier texture than the airy swirl you may be used to. That's the whole appeal: a dessert that tastes like it was made with a little extra care.

## Plan Your Stops Like a Road Trip

With 189 shops on the map, you won't hit them all in one trip. A few practical ways to approach it:

### Pick a region, not the whole state
Choose one area and stay there for a weekend. Fewer miles means more time to sit with your cone and chat with whoever is behind the window.

### Limit yourself to two or three a day
Creemees are rich. Sharing a small one with a travel partner lets you collect more stamps without feeling overdone.

### Use the digital directory to cluster stops
The directory is there to help you map shops. Group nearby ones together so you aren't backtracking.

### Favor small businesses
The passport is a nudge to explore independent shops across the state. Treat it as an excuse to turn off the highway and into a town you would have skipped.

## Bring the Creemee Mood Home: Small-Batch Maple Cream Cups

This is not a true creemee, which needs a soft-serve machine. It is a cozy, no-churn dessert for two that borrows the creamy, maple-leaning spirit. It's my own simple recipe, not part of the passport.

**Makes:** 2 generous servings
**Hands-on time:** 10 minutes
**Freeze time:** 4 to 6 hours

### Ingredients
- 1 cup cold heavy cream
- 1/3 cup sweetened condensed milk
- 2 tablespoons maple syrup, plus more for drizzling
- 1/2 teaspoon vanilla extract
- Pinch of salt

### Steps
1. Whip the cream in a chilled bowl until it holds soft peaks, about 3 minutes with a hand mixer.
2. Stir the condensed milk, maple syrup, vanilla, and salt together in a separate bowl.
3. Gently fold the whipped cream into the maple mixture until just combined.
4. Spoon into two small cups or a loaf pan. Cover and freeze 4 to 6 hours, until scoopable.
5. Let it sit on the counter for 5 minutes, then drizzle with a little more maple syrup.

### Affordable swaps
- No heavy cream? Whipping cream works the same way.
- Pancake syrup in a pinch works, though real maple gives better flavor. Use a touch less, as it is sweeter.
- Add a pinch of cinnamon for a fall feel.

## Make the Passport a Habit

The real charm here is that a dessert becomes a reason to wander. Pick one weekend, choose a handful of shops within a short drive, and let the stamps tell the story of your trip. Then come home and put a batch of maple cream in the freezer for the next chilly evening.
