Matt got the promotion. Now, per Vulture's recap of *Below Deck* Season 13, Episode 7, "Who's the Boss?", he seems intent on a second goal: ruining the vibes on the ship. Vulture describes the episode as one in which the newly minted bosun alienates the crew with arrogance, drunken hostility, and bullying behavior. The open question is whether Captain Kerry will keep tolerating a leader whose ego is, by Vulture's account, actively hurting morale.

That question is the whole episode, and it's a better one than it first sounds.

## The Promotion Is the Plot Twist

Reality TV loves a rise-and-fall arc, but the fall usually takes a few episodes. Here, per the recap, the promotion goes sideways almost at once. Matt has the title, and the crew has opinions about how he's wearing it.

It's hard not to see why this works as television. A promotion is supposed to be the payoff. On a charter yacht, where everyone lives, works, and sleeps in close quarters, it's the start of a much harder test: can you lead people who were your peers last week? Going by Vulture's summary, Matt's answer is a loud no.

## Leadership vs. "Leadership"

The sharpest contrast in the episode is between two ways of running a boat. Vulture's recap points to Captain Kerry's zero-tolerance stance on bullying, while Matt's version of authority leans on arrogance and hostility, drunken hostility included. One is empathetic leadership. The other, to borrow the recap's framing, is a self-serving take on it.

This is my read, not Vulture's: the show's best drama has always come from a gap between a person's title and their actual skills. A bosun is the link between the deck crew and the captain, so when the link snaps, everyone feels it. Matt may want to be the boss, but being the boss and being good at it are different jobs.

## The Supporting Cast Earns Its Keep

Matt shouldn't get all the oxygen, and the recap's angles suggest he doesn't.

**Alex's demotion.** Vulture's angles frame Alex's demotion as a surprisingly graceful reality-TV arc. That is a rare sight in a genre where a loss of status usually produces confessional-booth fireworks. Grace under pressure is underrated TV, and it makes a useful foil to Matt's behavior.

**Moe as the calm.** The recap also pegs Moe as the calm comic relief amid total crew chaos. Every ship needs one person who looks at the wreckage and keeps folding napkins. Moe, apparently, is that person.

## Good TV, Bad Management

Here's the tension fans will likely argue about this week. The episode sets up a possible staffing shake-up, and viewers get to pick a side: is this the drama they tune in for, or is it simply a workplace where someone is behaving badly and others are paying for it?

Both can be true, and that's the genre's perennial trick. Audiences have grown more attuned to power dynamics, bullying, and accountability on reality TV, so a bosun who bulldozes the crew plays differently than it might have a decade ago. Watching Captain Kerry decide what to do about it is less about gossip and more about a boss deciding what kind of ship she wants to run.

What happens next hasn't been spelled out in Vulture's recap, so no one should treat a firing as a done deal. But the episode clearly sets the table for a reckoning.

## The Bottom Line

Matt wanted authority, and he got it. What he apparently didn't get is the part where authority has to be earned over and over, by people who can walk away from it. Captain Kerry's next move will say a lot about whether this season's real lesson is that a title is only as good as the crew willing to follow it.
