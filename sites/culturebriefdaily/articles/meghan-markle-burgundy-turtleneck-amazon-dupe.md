Beige, it was a nice run. Per Us Weekly, Meghan Markle recently stepped out in a burgundy fall outfit built around a fitted turtleneck from the designer label Joseph, and the outlet is calling the shade the season's "It" sweater color. Its budget answer: a slim-fit burgundy top on Amazon for $13.

That's the news. Here's the take: the more interesting story isn't the price gap, it's the color.

## The Case for Burgundy as the New Neutral

Us Weekly frames burgundy as a richer fall alternative to beige and the other quiet-luxury neutrals that have dominated closets. That framing is the outlet's, but it's easy to see the logic. Beige is safe to the point of vanishing. Burgundy keeps the same grown-up, no-logo mood while actually looking like a decision someone made.

It's also hard not to notice what Markle's outfit is doing structurally. Per the report, the look is a polished, monochrome burgundy ensemble, which is a smart trick: one deep shade head to toe reads as intentional and expensive, even when the pieces are simple. The fitted turtleneck is the anchor, and that's the piece the report says you can echo for less.

## The Designer Look vs. the $13 Version

The comparison, as Us Weekly lays it out:

- **Markle's version:** a fitted burgundy turtleneck from Joseph, a designer label.
- **The stand-in:** a slim-fit burgundy turtleneck on Amazon, priced at $13.

The report details I have don't include the Joseph sweater's price, fabric, or the Amazon top's reviews or materials, so I won't pretend to rule on whether it's a true twin. What the report does support is the shared silhouette and color: slim, fitted, rich burgundy. Treat it as an echo of the look, not a clone. Fabric quality at $13 is always worth checking against the listing's details and customer reviews before you commit.

## How to Wear a Fitted Turtleneck Without Overthinking It

The appeal of a slim turtleneck is range. Us Weekly's piece is pitched around recreating Markle's polished monochrome moment, and the same shirt can stretch across a few settings. These are my styling suggestions, not part of the report:

### For work
Pair it with tonal or dark bottoms and keep accessories minimal. The color does the talking, so let it.

### For the weekend
Throw it under something casual and let the burgundy be the one saturated note. A fitted base layer is ideal when you want a coat or jacket on top without bulk.

### For evening
Go monochrome in the spirit of Markle's look: stay within the burgundy family and vary the textures so it feels layered rather than matchy.

## Why This Kind of Story Keeps Working

Celebrity outfit plus affordable Amazon alternative is a durable fashion formula, and cooler weather is exactly when people refresh their wardrobes. The format works because the barrier to entry is a few clicks and about the cost of lunch. There's also something quietly appealing about the "rich mom" aesthetic being reduced to its essentials: good color, good fit, no loud branding.

Of course, a $13 top won't make anyone look like they're headed somewhere with a driver. But burgundy is forgiving, and a well-fitted turtleneck in it does most of the heavy lifting. If fall's neutral was always going to get a little more interesting, a deep red-wine shade is a pretty painless way to let it.

The sharpest point here may be the simplest: the new "It" color isn't a trend that demands a new wardrobe. It asks for one good sweater.
