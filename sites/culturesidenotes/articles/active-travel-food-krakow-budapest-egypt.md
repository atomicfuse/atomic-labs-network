## Why These Destinations Are on the Radar

According to the report, active travel has surged since the pandemic as people look for immersive, healthier, experience-led vacations beyond traditional sightseeing. ## Krakow to Budapest: A Route Worth Eating Through

A trip linking Krakow and Budapest connects two cities with strong food cultures. Here are dishes and habits worth seeking out on any visit to Poland and Hungary. These are general regional traditions, not confirmed stops on an itinerary.

### In and around Krakow

- **Pierogi:** Poland's best-known dumplings, filled with anything from potato and cheese to meat or fruit.
- **Zapiekanka:** a popular street-food baguette-style toast with mushrooms and cheese, a good refuel option.
- **Obwarzanek:** a ring-shaped bread sold from street carts, easy to carry on a walk.

### In and around Budapest

- **Goulash (gulyás):** Hungary's paprika-rich beef soup, hearty enough to count as a full meal.
- **Lángos:** fried flatbread, typically topped with sour cream and cheese.
- **Market halls:** Budapest is known for its covered markets, a handy place to assemble a picnic of cheese, cured meats and pickles.

## Egypt: Fuel for Long, Hot Days

Egypt is the less expected name on the list for an active vacation, and its everyday food suits movement. Common staples include:

- **Ful medames:** slow-cooked fava beans, a traditional breakfast that keeps you full for hours.
- **Koshari:** a street-food mix of rice, lentils, pasta and chickpeas topped with tomato sauce and crispy onions.
- **Ta'ameya:** Egyptian falafel made with fava beans, usually tucked into bread.

On any trip involving exertion in warm weather, plan around water, shade and sensible meal timing, and ask your trip operator how they handle food and hydration.

## How to Plan a Food-Forward Active Trip

A few doable steps, whichever operator or route you choose:

1. **Ask about meal structure.** Find out which meals are included and which are free, so you can leave room for local restaurants and markets.
2. **Flag dietary needs early.** Tell the operator before departure rather than at the table.
3. **Keep one evening open.** A free night lets you chase a recommendation instead of a schedule.
4. **Pack a small snack kit.** Nuts or dried fruit cover the gap between a long morning and a late lunch.
5. **Learn five food words.** "Thank you" and the name of one dish go a long way at a market stall.

## A Simple Recipe Inspired by the Route: Weeknight Paprika Beef Soup

This is a quick, goulash-inspired soup, not a traditional Hungarian recipe. It serves four and takes about 1 hour 15 minutes.

### Ingredients

- 1 lb (450 g) beef stew meat, cubed
- 2 tablespoons oil
- 2 onions, chopped
- 3 cloves garlic, minced
- 2 tablespoons sweet paprika
- 1 tablespoon tomato paste
- 2 potatoes, cubed
- 1 carrot, sliced
- 4 cups (1 liter) beef broth
- Salt and pepper

### Steps

1. Heat the oil in a heavy pot and brown the beef in batches. Set aside.
2. Cook the onions in the same pot until soft, about 8 minutes. Add the garlic.
3. Take the pot off the heat, stir in the paprika and tomato paste, then return to the heat. (Taking it off the heat first helps keep the paprika from scorching and turning bitter.)
4. Add the beef back with the broth. Simmer, partly covered, for 40 minutes.
5. Add potatoes and carrot and cook another 20 minutes, until tender. Season and serve with bread.
