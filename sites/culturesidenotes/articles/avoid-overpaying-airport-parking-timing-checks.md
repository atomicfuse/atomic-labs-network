Food trips are won or lost on the budget. Every pound or dollar that disappears into an avoidable parking fee is money not spent on a market lunch, a tasting menu or a second round of pastries. Here is how to get your airport parking timing right so you pay for the days you actually use.

The advice comes from travel expert Ricky Durrance, as reported by Yahoo, who laid out a few simple checks to make before you book. The core message: understand how your car park charges, plan around more than just your flight times, and add only a sensible buffer.

The timing is practical for anyone heading out around October half-term, when more families are at airports and trying to trim holiday costs. Small timing mistakes can quickly become expensive. The advice itself is evergreen, though, and works for any trip.

## Start With How Your Car Park Charges

This is the first check, and it shapes everything else. Car parks do not all count time the same way. Some charge by calendar day, and others charge in 24-hour blocks from the moment you enter. The difference can decide whether your trip costs you one extra day of parking or not.

Here is a simple illustration of the principle (the numbers are my own example, not Durrance's):

- Under a 24-hour system, if you drive in at 10:00 on Saturday and drive out at 11:00 the following Saturday, you have gone one hour past seven full days, and you may be billed for an eighth day.
- Under a calendar-day system, what matters is which dates you were on site, not the clock times.

Before paying, find out which system your car park uses. The booking terms should say so. If they are unclear, ask the operator before you commit.

## Plan Around Your Real Times, Not Just Your Flight Times

Your flight departure and arrival are not the times your car enters and leaves the car park. Think through the whole journey in order:

1. **Entry time:** You arrive well before takeoff, so your car goes in hours ahead of your flight.
2. **Return flight landing:** Your plane lands, and then you still have passport control, baggage reclaim and, at some car parks, a shuttle bus.
3. **Exit time:** Only after all that do you actually drive out.

If you book using only your flight times, you can end up short. Write down your likely entry time and your realistic exit time, then check them against how the car park counts days.

## Watch Late Returns and the Midnight Problem

Late-evening landings are where the extra charges tend to creep in. If your flight is due in close to midnight, even a modest delay can push your exit into the next day. Depending on how the car park charges, that can mean a whole additional day on your bill.

If you are due back late at night, work out what happens if you are an hour or two behind schedule. Then decide whether to book a little longer to cover it.

## Add a Buffer, but Keep It Sensible

Durrance's advice is to add only a sensible buffer. That cuts both ways:

- **Too little buffer:** A delayed flight, slow baggage or a shuttle queue pushes you over and you pay extra.
- **Too much buffer:** You pay for a full extra day you never needed.

A good approach is to think about the specific risks on your trip. Is your flight late at night? Does the car park rely on a shuttle bus? Are you travelling at a busy time? If the answers point to real risk, build in some room. If not, don't pay for a cushion you won't use.

## Don't Forget the Shuttle

If your car park is off-site, the shuttle transfer adds time at both ends of your trip. Going out, it affects when you need to leave the car. Coming home, it adds minutes between the terminal and your car, which can matter if you are near a day boundary.

## A Quick Pre-Booking Checklist

Before you click "pay", run through these:

1. Does the car park charge by calendar day or in 24-hour blocks?
2. What time will my car realistically enter?
3. What time will I realistically leave, after landing, passport control, baggage and any shuttle?
4. If my flight is late, would I cross into another charging day?
5. Is my buffer sized to real risks rather than guesswork?
6. Are there any grace period rules in the terms?

That last point matters because some car parks may allow a short window past your booked time. Check the terms rather than assuming.

## Why This Matters for a Food-Led Trip

A few avoided extra days of parking can pay for something far more memorable: a street food crawl, a market breakfast or a regional dish you would otherwise skip. Sorting out the dull logistics first leaves more room for the good part of the trip.

## The Takeaway

Before you book, spend five minutes working out your real entry and exit times against your car park's charging rules. That one habit is the simplest way to avoid paying for a day you didn't use.
