Planning a trip through southern Italy and Greece, and want to build it around what you eat? Traveler Claudia Siron recently shared an itinerary with Thatslife covering Palermo, the Amalfi Coast, Athens, Paros and Naxos. Her guide is broader than food, but it contains a handful of meals and food stops that make a useful backbone. Here is how to turn them into a food-first route, with a simple recipe to bring it home.

One note up front: the itinerary names only a few specific dishes, so this guide sticks to those and treats everything else as places to explore, not menus to promise.

## Start in Palermo: Street Food First

Siron's Palermo stop is built around street food, architecture, gelato and shopping. That order makes sense for a food traveler. Palermo is a city to walk, snack and walk again.

A practical way to use it:

- Eat in small portions, several times a day, rather than sitting for one big lunch.
- Pair every snack stop with something to look at, since the city's architecture is part of the draw.
- Save gelato for the afternoon gap between meals.

### Day trips from Palermo

Three easy additions from the itinerary:

- **Mondello Beach:** golden sand and beach bars. Good for a relaxed lunch or a drink after the city.
- **Cefalu:** a coastal town where Siron mentions clam pasta and exploring the rocks. Order the pasta, then walk it off along the shore.
- **Monreale:** an ancient cathedral and quiet markets. Markets are where you can browse local ingredients without the crowds.

## Sorrento and Capri: Eat Smart in Expensive Places

Sorrento sits on the Amalfi Coast, known for cliffs, a marina and secret beaches. Siron points to Bagni Regina Giovanna as one of them. Capri is a day trip by boat, with views from the Monte Solaro chairlift.

The itinerary frames these as places you can enjoy on a budget. A sensible food reading of that: use Sorrento as your base and treat Capri as a single day out, rather than paying to stay on the island. That keeps your spending flexible, so you can put your money into one good meal instead of a hotel bill.

## Athens: Gyros and a Rooftop View

In Athens, Siron highlights rooftop bars, Acropolis views and affordable gyros. The combination is easy to copy: grab an inexpensive gyro for a meal, then spend a little more on a rooftop drink where the Acropolis is the scenery. You get the view without building the whole day's budget around it.

## Paros: Quiet Villages and a Dinner in Marpissa

Siron describes Paros as quieter in Parikia village, and she explored by quad bike. Marpissa is where she recommends dining. The itinerary also contrasts Parikia with the more tourist-heavy Naousa, so if you want a calmer pace, Parikia is the base to consider.

Quad bikes make it easy to reach a village for dinner, though that is your call on comfort and safety. Plan your ride home before the evening starts.

## Naxos: Olive Oil at the Source

Naxos is the largest Greek island, and the itinerary points to Chora village and the Eggares Olive Oil Museum. This is the most ingredient-focused stop on the route. Visiting a place that explains olive oil gives context to every plate you eat afterward in Greece, where it is a defining ingredient.

From there, head inland to Apeiranthos, a mountainous marble village that Siron describes as rich in traditional Greek culture. It is a good place to slow down and see a different side of the island.

## A Recipe to Bring It Home: Simple Clam Pasta

Cefalu's clam pasta is the one named dish on this route, so here is an approachable home version. This is a general method, not a recipe from Siron or a Cefalu restaurant.

**Serves 2**

- 200 g spaghetti
- 500 g fresh clams, rinsed well
- 3 tablespoons olive oil
- 2 garlic cloves, sliced
- Pinch of chili flakes
- Splash of white wine
- Chopped parsley

### Steps

1. Boil the spaghetti in well-salted water until just shy of done.
2. Warm the olive oil with garlic and chili over medium heat until fragrant, not browned.
3. Add the clams and wine, cover, and cook a few minutes until the shells open. Discard any that stay shut.
4. Add the drained pasta with a splash of its cooking water. Toss for a minute so the sauce clings.
5. Finish with parsley and a drizzle of good olive oil.

If you pick up a bottle of olive oil on Naxos, this is the dish to use it on.

## How to Use This Route

The route moves from Sicily to the Amalfi Coast, then to Athens and the islands. Each stop gives you one food anchor: street food in Palermo, clam pasta in Cefalu, gyros in Athens, a dinner in Marpissa, olive oil on Naxos. Pick those five and let the rest of the day stay loose.

Before you book anything, choose your one anchor meal for each stop and build the day around it.
