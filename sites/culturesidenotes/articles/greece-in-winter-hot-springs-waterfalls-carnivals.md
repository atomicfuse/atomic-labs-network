## The idea: Greece as a winter wellness trip

Per Ynetnews, Greece has more to offer in the cold months than its summer beach image suggests. The article points to natural hot springs, heated spa pools, snow-covered mountains, waterfalls and colorful carnivals as lesser-known winter attractions.

The timing fits a wider trend. Greece is actively promoting year-round tourism, reportedly to reduce its dependence on peak summer travel and to draw attention to wellness, rural, culinary and mountain destinations. For you, that means a winter itinerary built around soaking, walking and local life rather than sunbathing.

## Athens add-on: a Loutraki spa weekend

If you're already in or near Athens, Loutraki is the easiest add-on in this set of ideas. It's a spa town you can reach without a ferry, which makes it a simple weekend extension rather than a full logistical project.

A practical way to use it:

- Plan the spa as the centerpiece, not an afterthought. Heated pools are the point of a winter visit.
- Keep the rest of the schedule loose. Spa weekends work best with slow mornings.
- Look for a local taverna in town for a long, warm meal after your soak. Ask the staff what's seasonal rather than ordering from a generic list.

## Northern Greece: Loutra Pozar, waterfalls and snow

For a bigger escape, the article points north to Loutra Pozar, a natural hot springs site. Ynetnews pairs it with waterfalls and snowy mountain scenery, which makes for a trip with real contrast: cold air, steaming water, and wet green landscapes.

Some ways to build that trip:

- Treat the hot springs as your recovery stop after outdoor time, not the whole day.
- Add the waterfalls and mountain areas as daytime outings.
- Rural and mountain regions are among those Greece is promoting for culinary tourism, so plan meals around local producers and village eateries. Ask where the locals eat and what's in season.

## Don't skip the carnivals

The article lists colorful carnivals among Greece's winter draws. They're a way to see local life up close: costumes, community events and, as with most Greek festivals, plenty of communal eating is a reasonable expectation. Exact dates and locations vary, so check the local calendar for whichever town you're considering before you book.

## What to check before you go

Winter changes how travel works in Greece. Before booking, sort out these basics:

1. **Opening hours.** Seasonal businesses may run reduced schedules in the off-season. Confirm that your spa, hotel and restaurants are open on your dates.
2. **Transport.** Mountain areas can see snow. Check road and weather conditions, and allow slack in your schedule.
3. **Packing.** You'll want layers for cold outdoor stops, plus swimwear for the springs and pools.
4. **Booking.** Lower crowds are an advantage, but still reserve spa time and lodging at popular spots.

## A simple way to bring the trip home: a warming bean soup

Greek winter cooking leans on pantry staples, and a classic like fasolada (white bean soup) suits the mood of a soak-and-stroll trip. It's an approachable recipe rather than something drawn from the Ynetnews report, so treat it as a suggestion.

**Easy fasolada**

- Soak 2 cups of dried white beans overnight, then drain.
- Simmer in fresh water for 30 minutes, then drain again.
- In a clean pot, soften a chopped onion, 2 carrots and 2 celery stalks in olive oil.
- Add the beans, a couple of spoonfuls of tomato paste or a cup of chopped tomatoes, a bay leaf and enough water to cover by an inch or two.
- Simmer 1 to 1.5 hours until creamy. Season with salt and pepper, and finish with more olive oil.

Serve with bread and a squeeze of lemon if you like.
