Standing in front of a wall of Italian whites, most of us grab the Pinot Grigio. This guide gives you a better plan: a few label words that signal quality, and a short list of alternatives worth trying instead. The ideas come from a tasting video by WineOceans, which compared well-known and lesser-known Italian whites, from Pinot Grigio and Gavi to Vermentino and Fiano.

The video's core argument is simple: don't stop at the familiar bottles. Italy is full of white wines that are more interesting and reasonably priced, and with wine prices climbing, supermarket and shop picks that balance quality and cost matter more than ever.

## Three Label Words That Help You Choose

You don't need to memorize hundreds of grapes. Per the video's takeaways, a few words on the label do a lot of work.

### Alto Adige

Pinot Grigio from Alto Adige is presented as a step up from the generic version, with more freshness and clearer flavor. If you like Pinot Grigio and want to stay in familiar territory, this is the easiest upgrade.

### Gavi di Gavi

Gavi is a white wine from Italy, and the video found that Gavi di Gavi is more complex than basic Gavi. The video also tasted a Gavi from Lidl, so a budget supermarket bottle was in the mix. If you try both styles side by side, you can judge the difference yourself.

### Classico

According to the video, "Classico" often signals the original home region of a wine and better quality. Note the word "often": it's a useful hint, not a guarantee. Soave Classico was among the bottles covered.

## The Bottles the Video Covered

The video worked through 13 wines in total:

- Tesco Finest Pinot Grigio DOC

- Gavi from Lidl

That spread is the real lesson. These wines come from different regions, from Veneto in the north to Campania and Puglia in the south, and the video explains how region, grape and climate shape what ends up in the glass.

## The Standouts to Try First

The video's final picks highlighted Vermentino, Lugana and Fiano. If you want a short shopping list, start there.

- **Vermentino:** a good first move if you want something different but not intimidating.
- **Lugana:** a name worth looking for when you want to move beyond the usual suspects.
- **Fiano:** the Fiano in the video came from Salento in Puglia, which shows how a southern, warmer region can shape a white wine.

## The One to Skip

Frascati was the video's worst experience, described as lacking flavor. That's one taster's verdict on specific bottles, not a ruling on the whole category. Still, if you're choosing among several unfamiliar whites, you might begin elsewhere.

## A Simple Way to Taste at Home

You can copy the video's comparison approach without a formal setup.

1. Pick a base: a standard Pinot Grigio.
2. Add its upgrade: an Alto Adige Pinot Grigio.
3. Add a Gavi and, if you can find it, a Gavi di Gavi.
4. Choose one wildcard from the southern list, such as Fiano or Falanghina.

Pour small amounts, taste in that order, and note which feels fresher, which has more flavor and which feels flat. Four bottles and a few friends make the differences easy to spot, and you'll learn your own preferences faster than any label can teach you.

## Pairing Is Up to You

The video's brief doesn't give specific food pairings, so rather than invent them, use your tasting notes. A crisp, fresh bottle suits light starters; a more complex one can handle a more flavorful dish. Let what you taste guide what you cook.

## Your Next Move

Next time you reach for Pinot Grigio, put it back and pick a Vermentino, a Lugana or a Fiano from Puglia instead. Then check the label for Alto Adige, Gavi di Gavi or Classico, and let those words do the sorting.
