Holiday travel often means weather delays, crowded airports, and steep prices. If you live in New York City, there's another option: stay put and travel through your own appetite. Per a travel piece originally reported by the Haitian Times (with JPMorganChase), four tips can make a holiday staycation feel like a real getaway. Below, each one is paired with a way to put food at the center of the plan.

The source doesn't name specific restaurants or dishes, so the food ideas here are suggestions of mine, not part of its advice. You'll find the framework first, then ways to fill it in with your own neighborhood knowledge.

## 1. Treat your hometown like a destination

The first tip is a mindset shift: approach the city the way a visitor would. Visitors research, make a plan, and pick a few things worth doing rather than drifting through the day.

For eaters, that means doing what you'd do before a trip abroad. Pick one neighborhood you rarely eat in and look up what it's known for. Then build a short list:

- One place for a morning bite
- One casual stop for lunch, such as a street cart, a counter, or a market stall
- One sit-down dinner

Three food stops and a walk between them make a day. Seasonal markets, lights, and performances are part of what makes the city staycation-friendly right now, per the original report, so fold one of them in as your dessert-and-stroll finale.

## 2. Spend the night away

The second tip is to book a hotel. Even a night in a different part of the city changes the feel of the trip, because you stop being on your way home.

The practical food payoff is real. Staying overnight lets you:

- Have a leisurely dinner without watching the clock for the last train
- Have a drink or dessert after the meal
- Eat breakfast somewhere new the next morning

The source points to hotel deals this season as a reason the timing works. Check for them, and if you have rewards points or discounts available, it's worth looking at whether they can lower the cost. Choose a neighborhood by what you want to eat, then book within walking distance of it.

## 3. Be flexible

The third tip is flexibility, and it's especially helpful in a food-driven plan. Holiday crowds, weather, and closures can shift things quickly.

A few ways to build in slack:

- **Have a backup for every reservation.** Note a second option nearby.
- **Eat at off-peak hours.** An early dinner or late lunch can ease the crowd problem.
- **Keep one slot open.** Leave an afternoon unscheduled so you can follow a good smell or a long line.

A staycation's advantage is that nothing is riding on a flight. If a plan falls through, you can try again another day.

## 4. Try something new

The fourth tip is to try something new. For a food-focused staycation, make it concrete: choose one dish, cuisine, or ingredient you've never ordered, and seek it out.

Some ways to pick:

- Choose a cuisine from a community in the city you haven't eaten from before.
- Ask the person behind the counter what they'd recommend, and order that.
- Order the dish you can't identify on the menu.

Setting a rule like "one new thing per meal" keeps the day curious without turning it into homework.

## A simple 24-hour sketch

Here's how the four tips could fit together, as a template rather than a prescription:

1. **Late morning:** Check in or drop bags at your hotel, then walk the neighborhood you chose.
2. **Lunch:** Pick something casual from a market or counter, and try one new item.
3. **Afternoon:** Visit a seasonal market or see the lights, with a flexible gap for detours.
4. **Dinner:** Take a sit-down meal you booked ahead, with a backup noted.
5. **Next morning:** Have breakfast somewhere you wouldn't normally go before heading home.

## Keep it budget-friendly

A staycation skips airfare, which is part of its appeal. To keep the rest in check, lean on hotel deals and any rewards points you have, and mix cheap bites with a single splurge meal rather than spreading money evenly.

## Your next step

Open a map, pick a neighborhood you've never eaten in, and write down three places to try. Once you've named them, you've already started the trip.
