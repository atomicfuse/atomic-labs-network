Travel + Leisure recently asked six chefs and travel experts to name their favorite food destination. According to the outlet, they all landed on the same answer: the Iberian Peninsula, with San Sebastián and Lisbon singled out as the places to go. If you're planning a food-focused trip, that kind of agreement is worth paying attention to. This guide helps you decide which of the two cities suits you, and gives you a way to start eating the part before you book.

## Why the Iberian Peninsula keeps winning

When six people with different jobs and different tastes choose one region, the region is doing something right. Per Travel + Leisure's reporting, the draw is a combination: acclaimed restaurants, markets, coastal flavors, and food culture that visitors can actually get into without insider connections.

That mix matters for planning. Some food destinations are all high-end dining, and others are all street snacks. A place that offers both lets you set your own budget and pace.

## San Sebastián or Lisbon: pick by travel style

The experts named both cities, so the real question is which one fits you. Here's a practical way to decide. This is our framing, not a ranking from the original report.

### Choose San Sebastián if you plan around restaurants

San Sebastián was highlighted for its standout culinary experiences. If your ideal trip is built around meals, with reservations, a few splurges, and a compact destination where food is the main event, it's a natural fit. Book the restaurants you care about first, then fill the gaps with casual stops.

### Choose Lisbon if you like a city to wander

Lisbon was also singled out, and it suits travelers who want food woven into a broader city visit. Markets, coastal flavors, and neighborhood eating all make it easy to graze through a day instead of scheduling it. If you'd rather stumble on lunch than plan it, start here.

### Choose both if you have time

The fact that the experts treated the peninsula as one food destination suggests the two cities complement each other. A trip that includes both lets you compare a restaurant-centered city with a market-and-coast city. Give each at least three full days so you aren't rushing meals.

## How to eat beyond the obvious spots

Generic tourist dining is the main risk in any famous food city. A few habits help in both places:

- **Visit markets early.** Markets are where local ingredients are most visible, and they're good for a first-day orientation to what's in season and what locals buy.
- **Eat where the coast shows up.** Coastal flavors were part of the appeal, so look for places that lean on seafood and the day's catch.
- **Mix one big meal with small ones.** Plan one serious restaurant meal per day at most, then keep the rest flexible and casual.
- **Ask for recommendations at each meal.** Servers, market vendors, and shopkeepers will usually point you to something better than a search result will.
- **Book the headline restaurants early.** Acclaimed dining rooms fill up, so reserve those before flights if the meal is the reason for the trip.

## A simple three-day framework for either city

You don't need a rigid itinerary. Try this template and fill in the specifics with local advice:

1. **Day one: orient.** Start at a market, eat a light lunch, walk the center, and keep dinner casual.
2. **Day two: the big meal.** Book one standout restaurant, and keep breakfast and lunch small so you arrive hungry.
3. **Day three: go local.** Spend the day in neighborhood spots away from the main sights, and ask the people you meet where they eat.

## Bring the trip home: a coastal-market plate

You can get into the spirit before you travel, or extend the trip after. This isn't a traditional recipe from either city. It's a simple, market-style plate inspired by the coastal, ingredient-first approach these destinations are known for.

**What you need (serves two):**

- A good crusty loaf, sliced
- Best-quality olive oil
- Ripe tomatoes, sliced and lightly salted
- A tin or jar of quality seafood, such as sardines or anchovies
- Flaky salt and a squeeze of lemon

**How to make it:**

1. Toast the bread until crisp at the edges.
2. Drizzle with olive oil.
3. Layer on the tomato and let it sit for a minute so the salt draws out the juices.
4. Top with the seafood, a pinch of flaky salt, and a little lemon.
5. Serve right away with something cold to drink.

The lesson is the same one the experts seem to be pointing to: with the right ingredients, very little cooking is needed.
