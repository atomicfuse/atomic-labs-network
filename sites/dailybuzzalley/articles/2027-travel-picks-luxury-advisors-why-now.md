Forbes has published its list of the seven best places to visit in 2027, drawn from luxury travel advisors at OvationNetwork. The outlet's description points to three headline flavors: destinations tied to two sporting events, a wetland that, per Forbes, most Americans can't find on a map, and a bicultural Alpine region.

The full roster of seven isn't something we can lay out here, so we won't pretend to. What the Forbes framing does give us is a clear pattern, and it's a more interesting story than any single pin on a map.

## The Real Trend: Every Trip Needs a 'Why Now'

The common thread in those three teasers is a reason to go at a specific moment. Two of the picks hang on sporting events. One is an overlooked natural area. One is a region defined by two cultures meeting in the mountains. None of these is the standard postcard bucket-list stop.

That tracks with a broader shift in luxury travel: people are planning big-ticket trips years in advance, especially around global events and harder-to-access places. When you're booking this far out, a hook matters. "Because it's lovely" is true of everywhere. "Because something is happening there in 2027" gets a calendar invite.

## Sports as the New Velvet Rope

Forbes notes that two of the picks are linked to major sporting events. It's hard not to see why advisors like that angle. A big event gives a trip a fixed date, built-in drama and a story you can tell afterward. It also, we'd guess, creates a scramble for rooms, which is where advisors earn their keep.

That's our read, not Forbes's, but the logic is simple: if you want the good hotel near the big moment, you don't wait until the moment is on TV. Anyone who has watched fans try to book lodging after a host city is announced knows how quickly the sensible options vanish.

The smart move is to treat the event as the anchor and the destination as the real prize. Go for the match or the race, stay for the region.

## The Wetland Nobody Can Find

The most charming detail in the Forbes teaser is the wetland "most Americans can't find on a map." That's a delightful sales pitch, and a slightly self-aware one. Obscurity is now a luxury good.

Why might that appeal? For travelers who've already done the famous cities, the scarce thing isn't a thread count, it's the feeling of arriving somewhere before the crowds do. A little-known wetland offers exactly that: wide-open nature, no line for the photo spot, and bragging rights that begin with "Have you heard of it?"

We can't tell you from the available reporting where it is, so check Forbes for the specific pick. But the category itself, underrated nature worth planning a trip around, is one to watch.

## Why a Bicultural Alpine Region Fits the Moment

The third highlight is an Alpine region described as bicultural. Without naming it, we can still say why the idea is appealing. Places where two cultures overlap tend to offer double the everything: two culinary traditions, two sets of festivals, perhaps two ways of ordering the same coffee. You get a destination that feels layered rather than one-note.

Mountains also carry a built-in advantage for planners, since the scenery doesn't need a special occasion to justify itself. Pair that with cultural depth and you have a trip that works for people who want both fresh air and something to talk about at dinner.

## How to Use a List Like This

A few practical takeaways, offered as our suggestions rather than anything from the Forbes piece:

- **Pick your hook first.** Decide whether you're chasing an event, a landscape or a culture, then look for the destination that delivers it.
- **Plan early for event-linked trips.** Fixed dates tend to compress availability, so the earlier you start, the more choice you have.
- **Consider the lesser-known pick.** If everyone is heading to the famous option, the quiet one may give you more room to breathe.
- **Talk to an advisor if the logistics are heavy.** That's the whole premise of OvationNetwork's picks: these are where its luxury advisors are sending clients, per Forbes.

## The Takeaway

The most useful thing about a "best places" list this far ahead isn't the list. It's the reminder that the best trips now come with a reason attached. Whether it's a stadium, a wetland or a mountain valley with two languages on the road signs, 2027 is already rewarding the traveler who asks "why now?" before everyone else does.

For the complete seven, head to Forbes. Then start a calendar note, because the people who book first are the ones who get to be insufferable about it later.
