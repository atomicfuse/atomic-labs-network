With Thanksgiving week here, CBS News reports that AAA has put out holiday travel tips, a Thanksgiving travel forecast, and a look at where vacation habits are headed. One trend stands out: cruises are expected to be a major travel choice in 2027.

That last part is the interesting one. The summary of AAA's guidance doesn't spell out every number or the full list of do's and don'ts, so I won't invent them. But the pairing of the two topics tells a story on its own.

## Two Kinds of Travel Stress, One Common Fix

Holiday week is peak stress for families. Per the CBS News report, crowded roads and airports are shaping how people plan. Cruises, meanwhile, are being flagged as a bigger vacation trend.

Those may sound like separate stories, but it's hard not to see a thread. The stress of Thanksgiving travel is mostly a stress of logistics: when to leave, how to get there, where to stay, and what could go wrong in between. A cruise answers many of those questions in one purchase. That's my read, not AAA's, but it fits the broader shift toward bundled, easier-to-plan trips that the coverage points to.

## What AAA's Holiday Guidance Is Aimed At

The CBS News piece frames AAA's advice around avoiding headaches, saving money, and planning smarter. That's the right framing for the moment. Anyone who has sat in holiday traffic or stood in a security line knows the problem isn't a lack of destinations. It's the friction of getting there.

## Why Cruises Keep Getting the Spotlight

AAA's expectation that cruises will be a major choice in 2027 is a forward-looking signal. People aren't just planning this week's trip to Grandma's. They're already thinking about next year's vacation.

The appeal, as the coverage frames it, is ease. A bundled trip handles lodging, transport, and a lot of the itinerary in one go. For a family that just survived a holiday road trip, the idea of unpacking once is probably persuasive. (That's a joke with a grain of truth, not a statistic.)

## The Takeaway for Your Holiday Week

You can't bundle your way out of Thanksgiving traffic. But the same instinct driving cruise demand applies to a road trip: reduce the number of decisions you have to make on the day. Settle your departure time, route, and plans before you're in the car or at the gate.

The pattern in the news this week is simple. Travelers are tired of friction, and the trips gaining ground are the ones that remove it. If AAA is right about 2027, the cruise ship's biggest selling point may not be the ocean at all. It's the fact that someone else has already done the planning.
