With October half-term travel underway and families heading to airports while trying to trim holiday costs, travel expert Ricky Durrance has shared a few simple checks to make before booking airport parking, per Yahoo. The aim: avoid paying for time you didn't actually need.

The main takeaway, as Yahoo reports it, is to understand how your car park charges, plan beyond your flight times, and add only a sensible buffer. It sounds almost insultingly basic. It's also exactly the kind of thing that turns a bargain into a bill.

## The Real Villain Is Your Own Arithmetic

Most of us book parking by looking at flight times. Depart Saturday at 7 a.m., land Saturday the following week at 9 p.m., done. The trouble, per the advice Yahoo relays, is that your car's time in the car park isn't your flight's time in the air. You have to get to the car park before you fly, and you have to get out of it after you land.

That gap is where small timing mistakes can quickly become expensive, as the piece frames it. It's hard not to see the pattern: the cost isn't in the headline price, it's in the minutes on either side of your trip.

## Step One: Know How Your Car Park Counts a Day

The report takeaway from Durrance's advice is to understand how your car park charges. Two common approaches come up in the angles around this story: calendar-day charging and 24-hour charging. They sound similar. They are not.

- **Calendar-day parking** is tied to the date on the clock. Cross midnight and, in a car park that charges this way, you may be into another day.
- **24-hour parking** counts elapsed time from when you enter.

That's our plain-language reading of the distinction, not a quote from Durrance, and the specific rules will vary by operator, so check the terms of your own booking. But it's easy to see how the difference could matter. A flight that lands just before midnight and a slow trudge to the car can tip you over the line.

## Step Two: Work Out Your Real Exit Time

The idea running through the advice is to plan beyond flight times. A practical way to do that is to build your timeline backward and forward from the flights:

### Before you fly
Work out when you'll actually be entering the car park, not when you'll be at the gate. If a shuttle bus is part of the process, that's more time between pulling in and being airside.

### After you land
This is where the extra day can sneak in. Between touchdown and driving out, there's usually a chain of events: getting off the plane, passport control, baggage, the shuttle back to your car, and the queue to leave. Any one can stretch. The midnight returns and shuttle buses flagged in the story's framing are the obvious pressure points.

Our suggestion, and it is only that: write down your landing time, then add each step in order and see where midnight falls. If the answer is uncomfortably close, you know what to check on your booking terms.

## Step Three: Buffer, But Don't Go Overboard

Durrance's advice, as summarized by Yahoo, is to add only a sensible buffer. That phrasing matters. Flights get delayed, shuttles take their time, and nobody wants to plan a trip with zero slack. But a buffer is insurance, not a second holiday. Pad every end by hours "just in case" and you may buy the very extra day you were trying to avoid.

The smart move is to size the cushion to the real risks: a delayed flight, a slow shuttle transfer. Then check how your car park treats a late exit, including whether there's any grace period, before you hit pay.

## A Quick Pre-Booking Checklist

Pulling the advice together, here's what to confirm before you book:

1. **How does this car park charge?** Calendar day or 24-hour.
2. **When will you really enter?** Not your flight time, your arrival time.
3. **When will you really leave?** Landing plus the walk, the baggage wait, and the shuttle.
4. **Does your exit time cross midnight?** If so, read the fine print.
5. **Is there a grace period?** Find out before you need one.
6. **Is your buffer sensible?** Enough for plausible delays, not a blank check.

## The Bottom Line

There's a certain irony in airport parking: you can spend weeks hunting for a cheap flight, then lose the savings to a 40-minute shuttle queue after midnight. The good news in Durrance's advice is that none of it requires a hack or a coupon code. It requires reading how your car park counts time, doing a few minutes of arithmetic, and resisting the urge to pad your plans into a surprise extra day.

The cheapest parking spot, it turns out, is the one you leave before the clock ticks over.
