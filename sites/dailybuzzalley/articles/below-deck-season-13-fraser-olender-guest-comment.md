Below Deck Season 13 has barely left the dock, and the chief stew is already talking about a guest in language Bravo's bleep department knows well. Per Us Weekly, Fraser Olender called a charter guest a "shady bitch" after she criticized the crew's teamwork. That's an opening-charter moment, and it's the kind that sets the tone for a season.

## The Moment That Lit the Fuse

Here's what Us Weekly reports: the season opened in chaos, the charter overwhelmed the crew, and one guest took issue with how the team was working together. Fraser, the chief stew, responded by labeling her "shady" in less polite terms.

The reporting doesn't give us more detail than that, so we won't pretend to know the full exchange, the guest's exact words, or what the edit left out. What we do know is the shape of it: a guest critiques the team, and the person leading the interior department takes it personally.

## My Take: A Chief Stew Defending the Room

Here's where the spin starts, and it's mine. It's hard not to read Fraser's reaction as a leader protecting a brand-new team. A chief stew who is also running a crew that has barely met has two jobs on charter one: keep guests happy and keep the department from cracking. A complaint about teamwork hits both at once.

That doesn't make the language wise. Insulting a paying guest, even once the guest is out of earshot, is not the textbook way to run an interior. But Below Deck fans know the franchise's central tension: the job demands a smile, and the people doing it are humans who have been up since dawn. The gap between those two is where the show lives.

Whether Fraser was right to call her "shady" is a harder question, because nothing in the reporting shows what the guest did beyond criticizing teamwork. Criticism isn't automatically shady. Only the unedited footage could settle it, and viewers never get that.

## Why First Charters Go Sideways

There's a reason the first charter of a Below Deck season tends to be a stress test. The crew is new to each other, the boat, and the routines. Every small hiccup lands harder because nobody has built up trust yet. Per Us Weekly, this season's start fits that pattern: overwhelmed crew, unhappy guest, tempers up.

That's useful context for anyone tempted to write off a cast member after one rough episode. Opening-charter behavior is often the most stressed version of a person, not the average one.
