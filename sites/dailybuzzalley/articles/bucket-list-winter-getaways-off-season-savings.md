Winter has a reputation problem in travel circles: cold, dark, and best spent under a blanket. A recent Yahoo travel piece makes the opposite case. It names four bucket-list getaways where visiting in winter can save money, thin out the crowds and, in some cases, actually improve the weather.

The four picks: Glacier National Park and Kalispell, Montana; Death Valley (California-Nevada) and Arches (Utah) national parks; Ireland; and Colombia. The shared theme, per Yahoo, is value. Off-season timing can mean lower prices and fewer people between you and the view.

The angle worth committing to here: the best winter trips aren't the ones that tolerate the season, they're the ones where winter is the better product. Here's how each pick fits.

## 1. Glacier National Park and Kalispell, Montana

Glacier is the kind of destination that tends to live on summer vision boards. Yahoo's list puts it, along with nearby Kalispell, on the winter roster as a way to see a marquee national park without peak-season pricing and crowds.

The trade is plain enough to state as my own take: you swap summer's easy access for a quieter, colder, more deliberate version of the trip. Yahoo's pitch is that the savings and the elbow room are the reward for packing a heavier coat.

## 2. Death Valley and Arches National Parks

This is the pick where the logic flips most cleanly. Yahoo groups Death Valley, which straddles California and Nevada, with Utah's Arches, and the case rests on the weather argument: winter can be the better time for these parks.

It's hard not to see the appeal. Desert parks are famously a different proposition in the heat of summer, so choosing the colder months is less a sacrifice than a smart scheduling decision. Fans of the classic Western road-trip aesthetic (wide skies, red rock, a rental car and a questionable playlist) get the postcard without the crowd-and-price premium, according to the outlet's framing.

The pairing also hints at a natural budget road trip. Two parks in the same list, both with winter advantages, make for the sort of itinerary you can build around driving rather than flying. Treat that as my suggestion, not a route Yahoo laid out.

## 3. Ireland

Ireland is the one international pick most readers will already have bookmarked. Yahoo includes it as a bucket-list destination that becomes more budget-friendly when you visit in winter.

The pop-culture pull here is real: it's a place many people picture through film and TV landscapes, and it tends to be crowded in the busy season. Going in winter, per the article's logic, trades some of that crowd for better value. What you give up is the long, golden-hour summer feel. What you gain is a trip that costs less, which for plenty of travelers is the difference between "someday" and "booked."

## 4. Colombia

Colombia rounds out the list as the wild card. Yahoo presents it as another bucket-list destination where a winter visit can stretch your budget.

It's the pick that best illustrates why "winter" is a Northern Hemisphere habit of thinking. Reading the list, the common thread isn't snow at all. It's timing: travel when demand is lower and the price tag follows.

## The Tradeoffs, Honestly

Yahoo's argument is about savings, fewer crowds and sometimes better weather, but a fair reading includes the catch. Off-season trips ask for flexibility and a little more planning. Cold-weather destinations like Glacier demand the right gear and a realistic itinerary. That's my caution, not the outlet's, but it's the part that separates a bargain from a regret.

### A quick way to use the list

- **Want the lowest-friction road trip?** The Death Valley and Arches pairing is the one where winter is framed as an advantage.
- **Want a mountain escape with fewer people?** Glacier and Kalispell.
- **Want a passport stamp on a budget?** Ireland or Colombia.

Note that Yahoo's piece, as summarized here, doesn't put specific dollar figures on any of these. So treat the savings as directional and price your own dates before you get attached.

## The Takeaway

We tend to treat peak season as the "real" version of a destination and winter as the consolation prize. This list flips that script: the crowd is smaller, the bill is lighter, and for the desert parks the weather may be the main event. The smartest bucket-list move might be the one that looks like the off-beat choice on the calendar.
