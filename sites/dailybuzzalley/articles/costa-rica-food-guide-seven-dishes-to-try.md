Travel outlet Afar has put out a list of must-eat Costa Rican foods, headlined as 12 dishes, from fried snacks to fruit smoothies. The summary of the piece we have covers seven of them, so those are the seven we're talking about here: ceviche, gallo pinto, patacones, heart of palm salad, batidos, rice and beans, and casado.

Afar's larger argument, as relayed in that summary, is that Costa Rica's cuisine is simple, fresh, and deeply tied to place. It credits the country's biodiversity, regional traditions, and farm-to-table culture. My take: this is a food scene that doesn't need a lot of tricks, because the ingredients are doing the heavy lifting.

## The Angle: Simple Is the Whole Pitch

Food travel has been leaning hard into authenticity, sustainability, and local sourcing, and Costa Rica has a strong story to tell on all three. Afar's piece also touches on growing interest in Blue Zone eating and biodiversity-driven cuisine. It's hard not to read the list as a pitch for a country where "eat local" isn't a trend but just how lunch works.

So instead of a ranking, think of these seven as a menu with a shared philosophy: fresh, regional, uncomplicated.

## The Seven

### Ceviche

The seafood entry. Afar points to fresh seafood ceviche as a flagship of the country's cooking. It's the dish that best shows what "fresh" means when you're near the coast, since there's nowhere for a weak ingredient to hide.

### Gallo Pinto

One of the dishes Afar names as essential, and a staple of Costa Rican cooking. If you want a single dish to stand in for the country's everyday food, this is a strong candidate. It also sets up a fun comparison with the Caribbean rice and beans further down.

### Patacones

The fried-snack portion of the headline. Patacones are the kind of thing you order to share and then quietly regret not ordering two of. I can't tell you more about them from Afar's summary, and I won't pretend to, but the category (fried, snackable, built for lingering) speaks for itself.

### Heart of Palm Salad

The produce-forward pick, and a nod to that biodiversity-driven angle. It's a reminder that Costa Rican food isn't only rice, beans, and seafood; the plants get a starring turn too.

### Batidos

The fruit-packed smoothie from Afar's headline. The piece singles out batidos as a must-try, and it's easy to see why they're on the list: in a country with this much biodiversity, the fruit is the point. Order one and you've done your sustainability-minded, locally sourced good deed for the afternoon.

### Rice and Beans

Afar frames this as the Caribbean-side counterpart to gallo pinto. The two are cousins, and the regional difference between them is the kind of detail that rewards a curious eater. A food-nerd travel plan writes itself: eat both, compare notes, argue with your travel companion about which won.

### Casado

The last on our list, and a classic plate-of-many-things in the Costa Rican tradition. If the other six are individual notes, casado is the chord. It's the sort of order that lets you sample the country's staples in one sitting.

## How to Eat Like You Mean It

Afar's emphasis on regional traditions suggests a simple strategy: eat where you are. Seafood near the coast, rice and beans if you're on the Caribbean side, and a batido whenever the heat gets ideas. That's my read of the piece, not a rule Afar lays out, but it follows from its central point that this cuisine is rooted in place.

The full Afar list runs to 12 dishes, and the summary I worked from covers only these seven, so the remaining five are worth a visit to the original.

## The Takeaway

The most appealing thing about this list is what it doesn't promise. No foam, no fusion, no tasting-menu choreography. Just fresh ingredients, local habits, and a country that seems to have figured out that the best food story is the one you can taste in the first bite.
