Dark chocolate has spent years as the wellness world's favorite permission slip: it's a treat, but it's also *good for you*, so go ahead. According to a recent roundup from Health, the magnesium part of that story holds up. Dark chocolate is high in magnesium. It just isn't the richest source, and six foods beat it: pumpkin seeds, spinach, amaranth, Brazil nuts, chia, and soybeans.

This isn't a takedown. Think of it as a casting update: dark chocolate keeps its spot in the ensemble, but a few scene-stealers from the pantry deserve more screen time.

## Why Magnesium Keeps Trending

Magnesium is one of those wellness topics that never really leaves the feed. It's tied in popular conversation to sleep, stress, muscle recovery, and overall health, which is a résumé that practically writes its own hashtag. As more people look for food-first approaches rather than reaching straight for a supplement, the question becomes practical: what's already in the kitchen that does the job?

That's the angle here. Not a miracle fix, just a smarter swap list. (I'm not offering medical advice, and the Health piece as summarized doesn't give specific milligram figures, so I won't invent any. If you're thinking about your own intake, a doctor or dietitian is the right call.)

## The Six Foods That Outrank the Chocolate Bar

### 1. Pumpkin seeds

First on the list, and the easiest to slip into a routine. They're a snack on their own, but they also work as a crunchy topper. Sprinkle them over soup, yogurt, or a salad and you've upgraded a meal without cooking anything.

### 2. Spinach

The leafy green with the old cartoon-strongman reputation also lands on the list. Toss it into eggs, blend it into a smoothie, or wilt it into pasta. It shrinks dramatically when cooked, which makes a big handful a very low-effort move.

### 3. Amaranth

The wildcard. If quinoa is the grain everyone knows, amaranth is the one that makes you sound well-read at a dinner party. The Health roundup counts it among the foods with more magnesium than dark chocolate, so it's worth a spot in the rotation as a porridge-style breakfast or a side in place of rice.

### 4. Brazil nuts

Nuts are an easy grab-and-go item, and Brazil nuts earn their place here. Keep a small container in your bag or desk drawer and you have a snack that can compete with the candy aisle.

### 5. Chia

Chia is the pantry's best example of doing a lot with very little. Stir it into oatmeal, yogurt, or a jar of overnight oats. It's less a dish than a quiet upgrade you can apply to something you were already eating.

### 6. Soybeans

The legume of the group rounds out the list. Think edamame as a snack or a side, or soybeans added to a grain bowl for something hearty.

## The Swap Strategy

Here's my take, and it's visibly mine: the most useful way to read this list is as a set of "and" moves, not "instead of" moves. Nobody needs to give up chocolate. But if your whole magnesium plan has been a square or two after dinner, the list suggests you have plenty of company to add.

A few easy pairings from the list:

- **Breakfast:** chia stirred into oats or yogurt, topped with pumpkin seeds.
- **Lunch:** a spinach salad with soybeans or edamame.
- **Dinner side:** amaranth in place of your usual grain.
- **Snack:** a few Brazil nuts, or pumpkin seeds from the jar.

Notice that none of these requires a recipe, a special trip, or a blender the size of a small appliance. That's the quiet appeal of a food-first approach: it's mostly about what you add to meals you already make.

## The Plot Twist Is Mostly Plants

It's hard not to notice that every item on the list is a plant food: seeds, greens, a grain, a nut, and a legume. If you've been looking for a reason to put more of those on the plate, the magnesium conversation hands you a neat one.

Dark chocolate remains a perfectly good player, and the Health roundup is clear that it's high in magnesium. It just turns out the pantry has been holding a stronger bench all along. Keep the chocolate for dessert, and let the seeds, spinach, and soybeans do the heavy lifting at the main event.
