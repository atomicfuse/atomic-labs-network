Forbes has put out a roundup of 17 fall cocktails, and the lineup reads like a greatest-hits album for sweater weather. Per the piece, the collection spans hot toddies, martinis, margaritas and sours, with seasonal flavors ranging from apple to pumpkin. Whiskey sours and hot toddies get top billing.

The full list of 17 isn't something we can walk you through item by item. What the roundup does make clear is the shape of the season's drinking: comfort, hosting, and ingredients that taste like October. So let's take one angle and run with it: the best fall cocktail strategy is a classic with an autumn upgrade, not a brand-new drink that needs a shopping trip.

## What the roundup actually tells us

Here's what's reported. The Forbes collection includes whiskey sours, hot toddies, martinis and margaritas, plus drinks built around apple and pumpkin. That's the whole picture we have of the list, and it's a telling one.

Look at the structure. Four of those six entries are established cocktail families, and the other two are flavors. In other words, the roundup isn't mostly about inventing new drinks. It's about taking drinks people already know and dressing them for the season.

That's our read, not Forbes' claim, but it's hard to miss. A margaritas-in-fall entry is the tell: nobody needs a new cocktail, they need permission to keep drinking the one they like while wearing a scarf.

## Why the classics-first approach works

### Familiar drinks lower the stakes

A fall party is not the time for a recipe with nine components and a technique you learned from a video at 1 a.m. A whiskey sour is a known quantity. So is a martini. Guests recognize them, and you already have a rough sense of how they should taste.

### The upgrade does the seasonal work

This is where apple and pumpkin come in. Those are the two autumn flavors the roundup names, and they function as the costume change. Same bones, different outfit. Apple is the natural partner for something like a sour or a martini in spirit, while pumpkin leans richer and dessert-adjacent. Those pairings are suggestions from us, not recipes from Forbes, so treat them as a starting point.

### Hot drinks fit the mood

The hot toddy is the roundup's obvious cold-weather anchor. It's the one entry on the named list that is defined by temperature, and it's a reminder that fall drinking isn't only about what's in the glass but how it warms your hands. For a night in, it's hard to beat a drink that doubles as a heating pad.

## Apple vs. pumpkin: a friendly feud

The two named flavors invite an argument, so here's our take, clearly labeled as opinion.

Apple is the safer bet. It reads as bright, familiar and flexible, which makes it easy to put on the menu for a mixed crowd. Pumpkin is the more divisive pick: you either want the full pumpkin-spice-season experience or you'd happily skip it. Pumpkin drinks have the pop-culture weight of a seasonal event, the liquid cousin of every fall-flavored thing that shows up the second the leaves turn. Apple just quietly shows up and does its job.

If you're hosting, a good move might be one of each and letting guests settle it. A tiny, low-stakes rivalry is great party fuel.

## Building a fall drink night without losing your mind

The broader appeal of this kind of list is that it's easy. The goal isn't a bar. It's a few reliable options that feel festive. Here's one way to think about it, again our suggestion rather than anything from the Forbes list:

- **Pick one shaken classic.** A whiskey sour is the obvious choice since it's on the list.
- **Pick one hot option.** A hot toddy covers the chilly-night slot.
- **Pick one seasonal wild card.** Apple or pumpkin, depending on how your guests feel about pumpkin.
- **Have a non-alcoholic option ready.** Not everyone's drinking, and a warm spiced alternative keeps the table included.

That's four drinks, and none of them require a mixology degree.

## The takeaway

We can only vouch for what the roundup shows: whiskey sours, hot toddies, martinis, margaritas, apple and pumpkin among its 17 picks. But that mix says plenty. The most appealing thing about fall cocktail season is that it rarely asks you to reinvent anything. Take the drink you'd order anyway, add a little orchard or a little pumpkin, and let the weather do the rest of the marketing.
