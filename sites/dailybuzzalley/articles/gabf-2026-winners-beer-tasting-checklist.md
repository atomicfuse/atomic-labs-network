## Why a Winners List Is Better as a Checklist Than a Ranking

It's tempting to read competition results like a sports leaderboard: who's number one, who got robbed. That's fun for about ten minutes. The more rewarding move, in my view, is to treat the list as a guided tour.

A beer competition at this scale is, by its nature, a sampler of what brewers around the country are proudest of at a given moment. That makes the results a ready-made shopping list from people who spend their lives obsessing over this. You don't need to agree with the judges on every pick. You just need a reason to try something you wouldn't have grabbed on your own.

### Step 1: Start with your local winners

Once you have the full results, scan for breweries in your region first. Fresh beer from a nearby taproom is usually the easiest to find and the cheapest to try. There's also a small civic pleasure in discovering that the place down the road, the one you've walked past a dozen times, is now holding an award.

### Step 2: Pick one style you'd normally skip

This is the real value of the exercise. If you're a hazy IPA loyalist, find a winning lager or a stout. If you live on pilsners, go for something darker or funkier. Winning entries in a style you don't usually drink are a low-risk way to test whether your taste buds are as set in their ways as your coffee order.

### Step 3: Compare a winner to your everyday beer

Here's a simple experiment. Buy a winner and pour it next to your regular go-to in the same style. Smell both. Sip both. Notice what's different: aroma, bitterness, finish, how it behaves as it warms up. You may find the award-winner is a revelation. You may find your usual is just fine. Either answer is useful, and both beat arguing about it on the internet.

## How to Taste Like You Know What You're Doing

You don't need a sommelier-style vocabulary. A few habits go a long way:

- **Use a real glass.** Drinking from the can flattens the aroma, which is half the experience.
- **Smell first.** Give it a few seconds before the first sip.
- **Take notes, even lazy ones.** "Citrusy, dry finish, would buy again" is a perfectly good review.
- **Share a bottle or can.** Splitting a pour with a friend lets you try more without regretting it by beer three.

## The Trend-Watcher's Bonus Round

If you like reading the tea leaves, the full winners list is where the real nerd fun lives. Forbes frames the results as a snapshot of what's leading the U.S. craft beer scene, and that's the right way to read them: a single moment, not a final verdict. ## The Takeaway

Awards are only as good as what you do with them. A medal on a can is a nudge, not a command. Use the 2026 GABF results as permission to wander a little: one local brewery, one unfamiliar style, one side-by-side with your usual. The best beer in America, it turns out, is probably the one you hadn't tried yet.
