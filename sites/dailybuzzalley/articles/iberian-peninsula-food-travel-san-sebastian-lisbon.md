Ask six chefs and travel experts to name their favorite food destination in the world, and you'd expect a scattered answer: a ramen alley in one corner, a taco stand in another, someone getting defensive about Lyon. Instead, per Travel + Leisure, all six landed on the same place: the Iberian Peninsula. The outlet's piece points especially to two cities, San Sebastián and Lisbon.

That kind of unanimity is rare. Food people are famously opinionated, and getting six of them to agree on lunch is a minor miracle, let alone an entire region.

## What Travel + Leisure Found

According to the outlet, the case for Iberia rests on a combination of acclaimed restaurants, markets, coastal flavors, and accessible culinary discovery. San Sebastián and Lisbon are the two cities the experts keep returning to when they talk about standout food experiences.

The details of each expert's individual picks aren't something I can responsibly invent here, so I'll stick to what the reporting establishes: the consensus is real, and the two cities at the center of it are San Sebastián and Lisbon.

## The Angle: Why This Pairing Makes Sense

Here's my take, and it's visibly just that: the interesting thing isn't that Iberia won, it's that two such different cities share the podium.

San Sebastián sits in Spain's Basque Country, and Lisbon is the Portuguese capital on the Atlantic. Both are coastal, and both have the sort of food culture that makes visitors plan days around meals instead of squeezing meals around sights. It's hard not to wonder whether that's exactly the appeal for chefs, who spend their working lives thinking about food and presumably want a vacation where the food thinking is the vacation.

If you've ever watched a travel-and-food show, you'll recognize the genre: the host lands in a coastal city, wanders a market, sits down to a long lunch, and says something wistful about the sea. Iberia is the destination that makes that formula feel earned.

## San Sebastián: The Chef's Pick

Travel + Leisure highlights San Sebastián as a standout, and the reason it's on a chef's shortlist is easy to guess even without a quote to lean on: it's a city that treats eating as its main event. The outlet frames the region's appeal around acclaimed dining paired with strong local food culture, which is the combination serious food travelers chase.

If you go, the smart move is to plan around the local experience and not just the famous names. The outlet's angle is to experience each city beyond the obvious tourist stops, and that's advice worth taking at any food destination.

## Lisbon: The Easier Yes

Lisbon is the other half of the pairing. Where San Sebastián reads as the destination for a dedicated food pilgrimage, Lisbon, in my read, looks like the one that makes a great food trip feel casual. Markets and coastal flavors are part of the Iberian draw Travel + Leisure describes, and a capital city on the water is a natural place to find both.
