Lanterns just wrapped its first season on HBO Max, and the finale did something the genre rarely dares to do: it answered big questions without a single skyline-flattening brawl. According to Cosmopolitan's breakdown, the episode revealed the identity of the second Manhunter, exposed who killed Hal Jordan, and handed John Stewart an unconventional new job as Earth's protector, one that blends green and yellow Lantern powers.

That's a lot of plot for a finale that, per the same report, took a deliberately understated approach. My angle: the quiet is the point, and it's the smartest thing the show did.

## The Reveals, Quickly

Here's what Cosmopolitan says the finale delivered:

- **The second Manhunter is Noah**, who is Kelly's son. Noah is reportedly both her child and a secret Manhunter, and the finale leans on sci-fi mechanics to explain how his existence was possible.
- **Kerry killed Hal Jordan.** Per the breakdown, this is a shocking subversion of typical superhero storytelling.
- **John Stewart becomes Earth's protector**, using a hybrid of green and yellow Lantern powers. Cosmopolitan frames this as a fresh take that sets him apart from comic canon.

I'm deliberately keeping the mechanics light here. The finer details of how Noah's existence works are best taken from the full episode and Cosmopolitan's write-up rather than from my paraphrase.

## Why the Lowkey Finale Works

The usual superhero finale comes with a checklist: a sky beam, a CGI horde, a hero lifted out of the rubble in slow motion. Lanterns skipped the checklist. Cosmopolitan describes the finale as deliberately understated, and it's hard not to read that as a choice about what the show values.

Think about what a loud finale would have required. Kerry killing Hal Jordan is the kind of moment that, in most superhero stories, gets a thunderclap and a swelling score. Here, per the report, it lands as a subversion. When the genre's default is to make death a spectacle (and then undo it), treating it as a plot fact instead is a bold swerve. My read: it tells the audience this is a show about people first and power rings second.

The same logic applies to Noah. A kid who is both someone's son and a secret Manhunter is a premise that could have been staged as a Big Twist with a dramatic music sting. Keeping the tone grounded makes the reveal feel like a family problem that happens to have cosmic consequences, which is a more interesting place to live.

## John Stewart, Mixing Colors

The most fan-bait detail in the finale is John Stewart's new setup. Per Cosmopolitan, he becomes Earth's protector by combining green and yellow Lantern powers, and the outlet notes this departs from comic canon.

For anyone who grew up on the color-coded Lantern system, where each hue is tied to an emotion and a corps, mixing two is a loaded move. I'll stay in my lane and not claim more about what the combination means in the show's lore than the report supports. But as a character beat, it's tidy: the Lantern who's left holding Earth is the one who doesn't fit neatly into a single box.

It's also a quietly confident choice for a first season. Rather than reset John to the familiar template, the show lets him become something new, and that gives the DCU a protector with a distinct identity instead of a rerun.

## What Comes Next

Cosmopolitan's piece points to the DCU's future, with potential crossovers looming, as part of why fans are digging into the finale now. It also floats a theory: that Hal could return as a Black Lantern, which would reshape the stakes for season 2.

To be clear, that's speculation, not confirmation. But the idea is irresistible precisely because the finale was so restrained. A show that treats Hal's death as a quiet fact has set up a return that would hit much harder than any rubble-crawling resurrection. If it happens, the earlier subtlety is what would make it feel earned rather than cheap.

## The Bottom Line

The temptation after a season like this is to ask where the explosions were. The better question is why we expected them. By choosing a low-key finale, Lanterns trusted its twists, its characters, and its audience to carry the weight. If season 2 does bring Hal back in some darker shade of green, the loudest thing about it might be how long the show made us sit in the silence first.
