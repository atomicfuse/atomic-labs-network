Elle has published its guide to New York City's toughest tables to book this holiday season, and the list reads like a seating chart for the city's social calendar. It runs to twelve spots, from Midtown icons to downtown hot spots to one-night-only festive meals. Elle's pitch, in its own words: scoring a difficult reservation feels "that much sweeter this time of year."

That framing is the whole game. In December, a booking is less a dinner plan than a small flex you can screenshot. So let's sort the list the way a regular would: by what kind of flex each table delivers.

## The Perennial Power Tables

Three names on Elle's list are the kind that regulars drop without explanation.

**The Polo Bar** leads the list. It's the sort of place where a confirmed reservation tends to get mentioned casually and repeatedly. **4 Charles Prime Rib** is also on the list, and the name alone tells you what the kitchen is about. And **L'Artusi** and **Rolf's** round out the restaurant-as-institution group.

Rolf's is worth a second look for a seasonal reason. It's a restaurant that Elle includes in a holiday roundup, and it's hard not to wonder whether the festive setting is part of the draw. That's my read, not Elle's. The magazine's list simply puts it alongside the others as a table that's tough to get.

## The Newer Hot Spots

Not every impossible table is a decades-old fixture. Elle also flags **The Corner Store**, **Pebble Bar** and **The Jazz Club at Aman New York**. The Jazz Club, tucked inside a hotel, adds a live-music angle to the evening, which is a different kind of status from a great steak.

These are the picks for people who want to say they were somewhere before it became everyone's answer to "where should we go?"

## The Holiday-Only Tickets

Here's where the list gets seasonal, and where competition is likely fiercest because the calendar does the limiting for you. Elle highlights:

- **Thanksgiving Brunch at Per Se**
- **Thanksgiving Dinner at The Nines**
- **Christmas Eve Dinner at Chez Fifi**
- **Christmas Day Dinner at Galo**

There's only one Christmas Eve and one Christmas Day, so these tables have a built-in scarcity that no restaurant has to manufacture. If you're reading this on the weekend Elle published it, in mid-November, note that Thanksgiving is the closest of the four. Whether specific slots remain is something to confirm directly with each restaurant, since the guide's details are all I have to go on.

## The Shopping-Day Splurge

Last on the list is **L'Avenue at Saks**. Its place on a holiday list makes intuitive sense: it pairs naturally with a day of department-store browsing. That pairing is my inference rather than anything Elle spelled out, but the logic holds for anyone planning a festive Midtown day.

## How to Play This List Like an Insider

Elle's roundup doesn't offer reservation strategy, so here's a practical read of the list itself, clearly my own take.

**Start with the date-bound tables.** The Thanksgiving and Christmas meals can't be rescheduled, so they're the ones to chase first.

**Pick one big swing and one safe landing.** The list is a good menu for a split strategy: aim for a headline name like The Polo Bar, and keep a more attainable table in your back pocket.

**Mix your categories.** A hotel jazz club, a classic Italian spot and a prime rib house deliver three very different evenings. If one falls through, you can pivot by mood rather than by starting over.

**Check directly.** Availability changes fast, and I can't tell you which of these have open tables. Contact each restaurant or its booking platform for current openings.

## The Real Currency

The amusing thing about a list like this is how little of it is about food. Nobody lists a reservation as a achievement because of the bread basket. The scarcity is the point, and December supplies a built-in audience: relatives in town, friends who want a story, a feed full of twinkling lights.

But the best version of this list is the one you actually use. A table that's hard to get is only worth it if you're glad you sat down at it. So chase one, book one, and let the other eleven be somebody else's screenshot.
