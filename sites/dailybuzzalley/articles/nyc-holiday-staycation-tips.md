Every December, a familiar ritual unfolds: people queue at airports, watch departure boards flicker to "delayed," and pay premium prices to get somewhere else. Per a recent piece from the Haitian Times, there's another option for New Yorkers. Stay put, and treat the city like the destination everyone else flies in to see.

The article lays out four tips for a holiday staycation in NYC. It also makes the case for why now is a good moment: holiday travel often brings weather delays, airport crowds and high costs, and the city's seasonal markets, lights, performances and hotel deals make it especially staycation-friendly. Here's how those four tips play out, with my own spin clearly marked as such.

## 1. Treat your hometown like a destination

The first tip is a mindset shift: look at New York the way a visitor would. That means actually planning, instead of defaulting to the same three restaurants and the same commute.

It's hard not to see the appeal. Anyone who has ever hosted out-of-town relatives knows the quiet shame of discovering they've seen more of the city in a weekend than you have in a decade. A staycation flips that script. You're the visitor now, and the itinerary is yours.

The Haitian Times' framing points toward the seasonal draws: markets, lights and performances. My suggestion (this is take, not reporting): pick one neighborhood you rarely visit and build a day around it, the way a guidebook would. Pick a market, pick a meal, pick one thing you'd normally walk past.

## 2. Spend the night away

Tip two is where a day out becomes a getaway: book a hotel. Even in your own city, the article suggests, spending the night away is what makes it feel like a real trip.

There's a certain logic to this. A day trip ends with the same subway ride home and the same pile of laundry. A hotel night gives the outing an edge, a check-in, a view that isn't yours, and a bed someone else will make. The piece notes that hotel deals are part of what makes the season staycation-friendly, so it may be worth watching for them.

A few practical thoughts of my own, which are not from the article: consider a neighborhood across the city from your own, so the change of scenery is real. And if you have rewards points or loyalty perks from other travel, a staycation is a low-stakes place to spend them.

## 3. Be flexible

The third tip is the least glamorous and probably the most useful: stay flexible. Holiday plans are famous for collapsing, and the article's whole premise is that a staycation sidesteps the weather delays and crowds that can derail longer trips.

The upside of vacationing at home is that the failure modes are gentler. If the line at an attraction is absurd, you can pivot to the next thing. If it rains, nobody's missed a connecting flight. A flexible itinerary lets you lean into that advantage rather than cramming in every must-see like you only have one shot at it.

That's the quiet joke of a staycation: you live here. You can come back. Treat the schedule as a loose suggestion, and the trip gets better.

## 4. Try something new

The last tip is to try something new, and it's the one that makes the other three worthwhile. A staycation that replicates your normal weekend with a hotel robe isn't much of a vacation.

The report wording is simple, but the possibilities are wide. A performance you've never seen, a market you've never browsed, a cuisine you've never ordered. Familiar neighborhoods can hold unfamiliar corners, and the holiday season gives you plenty of reasons to go looking.

Here's a pop-culture framing, strictly mine: New York has been the backdrop for countless holiday movies, and a lot of them lean on the same idea, that the city looks different when you slow down enough to see it. You don't need a rom-com meet-cute to try it. You need a free afternoon and a willingness to wander.

## Why this works for the budget-minded

The Haitian Times piece is aimed at travelers, families and budget-conscious readers, and the staycation pitch is mostly about easing the pressure. No airfare, no airport, no packing for three climates. You can scale the whole thing to what you're comfortable spending, from a single hotel night to a loose weekend of wandering.

That's the real value here. The holidays can feel like a season of obligation and expense, and a staycation offers a version of rest that doesn't require a boarding pass.

## The takeaway

New York spends the whole year being somebody else's dream trip. The four tips boil down to something simple: for a weekend, let it be yours too. Book the room, skip the rigid schedule, and go look at the lights like you flew in for them.
