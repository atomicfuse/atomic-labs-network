Taye Diggs, 55, says he's still looking for love. In an exclusive interview with the Daily Mail, the actor opened up about being single, returning to therapy to "get himself in order," and staying open to romance more than a decade after his 2013 divorce from Idina Menzel.

The conversation comes as Diggs stars in the thriller *Forever, This Ring*, where he plays a wealthy bachelor who swears off marriage until he meets a woman with a dark secret. Per the Daily Mail, that character's commitment to the bachelor life makes a pointed contrast with Diggs himself, who is still hoping to find a partner.

## The Plot Twist: The Bachelor Is Not Actually the Bachelor

Casting a man who is open to love as a man who has sworn it off is a classic bit of actor-versus-role irony. Diggs spends the movie playing a guy allergic to commitment, while telling the Daily Mail that off screen he's looking.

It's hard not to enjoy the symmetry. Thrillers love a man who says he'll never marry, because the genre exists to punish that kind of confidence. Diggs, meanwhile, seems to be taking the more grown-up route: skipping the dark-secret subplot and going to therapy first.

## Therapy as Pre-Season Training

The most interesting detail in the Daily Mail piece is how Diggs frames his therapy. He's returning to it, per the report, to get himself "in order." That's not the language of a crisis. It sounds more like a man doing the prep work before opening a new chapter.

That's our read, not his words, but the logic holds: if you're looking for a relationship, working on yourself first is about as sensible as it gets. Celebrity romance coverage tends to treat singlehood as a problem to be solved. Diggs, by this account, treats it as a project with a to-do list.

## The Career Reset Running in Parallel

The personal reset has a professional twin. Diggs is reinventing his career, per the Daily Mail, by launching a mobile-first streaming platform called Microhouse Films and taking on edgier roles under director Chris Stokes.

That's a notable pivot for an actor many viewers still associate with a particular brand of smooth, charming leading man. Stepping into producing and a platform built for phones suggests he's not waiting for the phone to ring. He's building the thing the phone rings from.

### Why the Mobile-First Bet Makes Sense

Microhouse Films fits the rise of short-form, mobile-first serialized content, the so-called vertical drama format that has been pulling viewers into binge-sized episodes made for scrolling thumbs. We can't say from the Daily Mail report how Diggs's platform will be structured, so we'll leave the specifics to future announcements. But the timing of a veteran actor planting a flag in this space is worth noticing.

## Why This Midlife Moment Lands

There's a familiar script for famous men in their fifties: a flashy comeback, a rebrand, perhaps a motorcycle. Diggs's version, as reported, is quieter and arguably more relatable. Therapy. A new business. A willingness to say out loud that he'd like to meet someone.

That openness is the real hook here. He isn't announcing a romance or performing a victory lap. He's saying he's mid-process, which is a more honest place to be than most press tours allow.

## The Takeaway

*Forever, This Ring* gives Diggs a character who has decided love isn't for him. The man playing him is doing the opposite: working on himself, building something new, and leaving the door open. In a thriller, that's the guy who gets the better ending.
