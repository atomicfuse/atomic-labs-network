Los Angeles has a reputation for movie sets and sunshine, but its museums are a reason to visit all on their own. Afar's roundup of the city's best museums makes that case, and four of them cover very different tastes. Here's how to pick the one that fits you, and what each offers according to Afar's guide.

The short version: love movies, go to the Academy Museum of Motion Pictures. Want contemporary art, go to The Broad. Drawn to ancient history, head to the Getty Villa. Would rather wander outdoors, choose the Huntington Library, Art Museum, and Botanical Gardens.

## If you love movies: Academy Museum of Motion Pictures

L.A. is the city that made the movies, and this museum is devoted to the art and history of cinema. Afar frames it as part of the city's cultural identity beyond Hollywood, tied to film history in an immersive way.

**Pick it if:** you want your film obsession to go deeper than a studio tour or a star-spotting walk.

**Practical tip:** check the museum's current exhibitions before you go. Afar notes that long-running exhibitions and expansions are already shaping visitor plans in L.A., so what's on view can change.

## If you want contemporary art: The Broad

Afar includes The Broad among the city's powerhouse museums for contemporary art. It's the pick when your idea of a good afternoon is standing in front of bold, modern work and deciding what you think of it.

**Pick it if:** you prefer the art of right now over the art of centuries ago, and you like a museum visit that's focused rather than sprawling.

**Practical tip:** the guide highlights neon signs among the things to see in L.A. museums, so keep an eye out for that kind of work. Confirm current visiting details on the museum's own site before you plan your day.

## If you're fascinated by the ancient world: The Getty Villa

Afar points to ancient Roman sculptures as one of the draws of L.A.'s museum scene, and the Getty Villa is the antiquities stop on this list. It pairs classical art with the architecture and setting that suit it.

**Pick it if:** you'd happily spend an hour with a marble figure that's been around for millennia.

**Practical tip:** this is a good one to schedule on a clear day, since part of the appeal is the setting as well as the collection. Check the museum's website for current entry requirements.

## If you'd rather be outside: The Huntington

The Huntington Library, Art Museum, and Botanical Gardens is the all-in-one option. Afar describes L.A.'s verdant gardens as a highlight, and this is where the museum visit and the garden stroll become the same outing.

**Pick it if:** you're traveling with someone who wants galleries and someone who wants fresh air. It satisfies both.

**Practical tip:** give it more time than a typical museum stop. With a library, an art museum, and botanical gardens under one name, you can easily fill a long visit.

## Putting them together

These four don't need to compete. If you have a long weekend, you could give each a half day and end up with a tour through film, modern art, antiquity, and nature. With only one day, choose the one that matches how you like to spend free time.

Afar notes that Los Angeles is leaning into its cultural side ahead of the 2028 Summer Olympics, with expansions and renovations underway. That's a good reason to check each museum's website for current exhibitions and any closures before you commit to a date.

## Choose one and book it

Don't try to see everything. Pick the museum that matches your biggest interest, look up what's on view this month, and build the rest of your day around it.
