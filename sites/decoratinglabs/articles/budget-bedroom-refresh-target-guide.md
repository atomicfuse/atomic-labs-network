BuzzFeed Shopping recently rounded up 28 affordable Target finds for building a dream bedroom without a big spending spree. The published list doesn't give us product names or prices to work from, so rather than guess at them, this guide does something more useful: it gives you a plan for deciding what to buy, in what order, and how to make a handful of inexpensive pieces look like one deliberate room.

By the end, you'll know where a small budget does the most work, and how to walk through Target with a shortlist instead of a cart full of impulse buys.

## Start with the three changes that transform a bedroom

BuzzFeed's pitch is that a major room upgrade doesn't require major spending. In practice, the biggest visual shifts in a bedroom come from three areas: lighting, bedding, and storage. Small decor swaps come after those.

Spend your first dollars here, in this order:

1. **Lighting.** Overhead light alone flattens a room. Add one or two lamps at different heights, such as a bedside lamp and a floor lamp in a corner, and the space instantly feels softer.
2. **Bedding.** The bed is the largest surface in the room. New bedding changes the whole color story.
3. **Storage.** Clutter makes any bedroom feel smaller and cheaper than it is.

Only then move on to the small stuff: art, a throw, a plant, a tray.

## Lighting: swap the glare for glow

If you do only one thing, change how the room is lit at night.

- Turn off the overhead fixture in the evening and use lamps instead.
- Place lamps at different heights. A table lamp on the nightstand plus a taller floor lamp creates depth.
- Check the bulb before you buy a fancy lamp. A warm-white bulb in a basic lamp beats a cool-white bulb in a pretty one.

Buying a shade or bulb is often cheaper than replacing a whole lamp, so if you already own a sturdy base you like, refresh just the shade.

## Bedding: build in layers, not sets

A full bedding bundle can be tempting, but layering lets you spread the cost and change the look later.

- **Base layer:** sheets in a calm color.
- **Middle layer:** a duvet or comforter in a solid or subtle pattern.
- **Top layer:** a throw blanket folded at the foot of the bed and two or three pillows in contrasting textures.

Keep the palette to two or three colors. Texture does the heavy lifting: a chunky knit throw next to smooth cotton looks more expensive than five different prints fighting each other.

## Storage: hide the mess, keep the surfaces clear

Clear surfaces are the cheapest luxury look there is. Aim to get at least these zones working:

- **Under the bed:** shallow bins or boxes for off-season clothes and extra linens.
- **Nightstand:** a tray or small basket to corral chargers, a book, and lip balm, so the top looks intentional.
- **Door or wall:** hooks for bags, robes, and the jacket that always lands on the chair.

Baskets with a uniform look read as décor, not clutter. Pick one material, such as woven or fabric, and stick with it.

## Small swaps that finish the room

Once the big three are sorted, add personality with a few low-cost details:

- A framed print or two, hung at eye level above the bed or dresser
- A plant or faux stem in a simple pot
- A small mirror to bounce light around
- A rug under the foot of the bed to warm up bare floors

For renters, students, and first-apartment shoppers, who BuzzFeed's audience skews toward, removable and freestanding items are the safest bet. Lamps, rugs, bedding, and baskets all move with you and need no drilling.

## How to shop Target on a budget

Since the roundup points to Target as a go-to for accessible decor, here's how to use a store trip well:

- **Set a cap before you walk in.** Choose a number, say $100, and divide it: roughly a third for lighting, a third for bedding, and a third for storage and extras. Adjust to your own room.
- **Measure first.** Know your bed size, nightstand width, and the floor space available for a rug.
- **Bring a color swatch.** Snap a photo of your wall color and current bedding so you can match undertones in the store.
- **Buy the biggest-impact item first.** If your budget runs out, you want it to run out after the lamp and bedding, not after the candle.
- **Check the app or shelves for sale tags** before you commit, since prices shift.

## A simple weekend plan

**Saturday morning:** Declutter and clear every surface. Note what you actually need to store.

**Saturday afternoon:** Shop with your cap and list. Prioritize lamps, bedding, and storage.

**Sunday:** Make the bed with your layers, set up the lamps, tuck bins under the bed, and style the nightstand with one tray and one personal item. Step back and see what the room still needs before buying more.

## The one rule to remember

Before you add anything new, fix the light and clear the surfaces. A tidy, warmly lit room with simple bedding looks better than a crowded one full of new purchases.
