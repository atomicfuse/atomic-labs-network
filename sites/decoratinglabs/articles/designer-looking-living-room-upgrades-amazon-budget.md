A living room rarely looks unfinished because of the sofa. It looks unfinished because of what's missing around it: the layered rug, the stack of books, the framed photos, the side table within reach. Those are also the cheapest things to fix.

Real Simple recently rounded up 11 living room decor picks from an interior designer, all available on Amazon, with prices starting at just $9. The published summary names coffee table books, coasters, area rugs, side tables and picture frames among the categories. It doesn't list every item or price, so rather than guess at specific products, this guide focuses on how to use those categories to get a polished look.

## Start With the Pieces That Do Two Jobs

The best budget upgrades earn their spot by being useful and good-looking at once. From the designer's categories, three fit that description:

- **Coasters** protect your table and, when they're a cohesive set, read as a deliberate choice rather than an afterthought.
- **Side tables** give guests somewhere to put a drink and give the room another surface to style.
- **Coffee table books** add height and color to a tabletop, and they're something to flip through.

If you only buy one category this week, make it the one that solves a daily annoyance. A room that works well tends to look better too.

## Use Coffee Table Books as Your Styling Base

A bare coffee table looks flat. A small stack of books fixes that quickly. Here's a simple way to do it:

1. Stack two or three books, largest on the bottom.
2. Choose covers that suit your palette, so the stack looks intentional.
3. Set something small on top, such as a candle, a small dish or a coaster set.

This is my suggestion rather than part of the designer's roundup, but it's an easy way to put the book category to work.

## Let an Area Rug Anchor the Room

The area rug is likely the biggest item on the list, and the one with the most visual impact. A rug defines the seating area and makes a room feel pulled together. It also helps renters in particular, since it adds color and texture without touching the floor or walls.

When you shop, measure first. As a general styling rule (mine, not the designer's), aim for a rug large enough that at least the front legs of your sofa and chairs sit on it, so the furniture feels connected rather than floating.

## Add a Side Table Where Your Hand Naturally Lands

Sit in your usual spot and notice where you reach for your drink or remote. That's where a side table belongs. A small table next to a sofa or chair does more than add storage-free surface space. It breaks up a long run of furniture and gives you a place for a lamp, a plant or a book.

## Fill the Walls and Shelves With Picture Frames

Frames are among the cheapest ways to make a room feel personal. A few tips for getting a designer look:

- Pick one frame finish, such as black, wood or brass, and stick with it.
- Mix sizes, but keep the spacing between frames consistent.
- Use them on shelves and tables, too, not just walls.

That last point matters for renters, since leaning a frame on a shelf means no nails.

## Why Small Swaps Beat One Big Purchase

Spread across several categories, a modest budget goes further than a single splurge. With prices starting at $9, you could refresh several corners of your room for the cost of one decorative accent from a specialty shop. Real Simple's roundup reflects a familiar reality: shoppers want quick, accessible ways to update a home without sacrificing style, and Amazon is a convenient place to find them.

The tradeoff is that online listings can look different in person. Check dimensions carefully, read recent reviews, and confirm the return policy before ordering anything you can't easily eyeball, especially rugs and furniture.

## A Simple Order to Shop In

If you're not sure where to begin, this sequence builds the room from the bottom up:

1. **Rug**, to set the foundation and color story.
2. **Side table**, to fix function.
3. **Coffee table books and coasters**, to style the main surface.
4. **Picture frames**, to add personality last.

Buying in this order keeps you from picking small accessories that clash with the larger pieces later.

## Your Next Step

Stand in your living room and find the emptiest surface. Fill that one spot first, with a book stack, a set of coasters or a frame, and see how much more finished the room feels. Then decide what the space needs next.
