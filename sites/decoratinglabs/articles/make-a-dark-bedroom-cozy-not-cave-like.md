Dark walls don't have to make a bedroom feel like a cave. Per Good Housekeeping's roundup of 20 dark bedroom ideas, designers say deep colors like plum, navy, black, brown and deep green can read as cozy and calming when they're balanced with texture, contrast, lighting and natural materials. Below are the moves from that list that do the most work, in the order you'd actually use them.

The broader shift is away from all-white minimalism and toward richer, more cocoon-like rooms. If you want a bedroom that feels restful and personal, here's how to go dark without going gloomy.

## Start With a Color That Feels Rich, Not Flat

The roundup covers a wide range of moody shades, so you have options beyond black:

- **Plum:** a deep, slightly warm shade that feels dramatic without feeling cold.
- **Deep green:** a good partner for natural materials like wood and woven pieces.
- **Navy:** a classic dark choice that's easy to live with.
- **Warm brown:** the coziest-sounding option on the list.
- **Moody purple:** more saturated than plum if you want real drama.
- **Black or dark gray:** the boldest route, including pairing black with dark gray or going black on black.

If you're nervous, pick a color with some warmth in it, such as plum, brown or green, before reaching for pure black.

## Choose Your Level of Commitment

You don't have to paint every surface. The list offers a few scales of dark:

### Paint one accent wall
Behind the bed is the natural spot. You get the depth of a dark color while the rest of the room stays lighter.

### Drench the ceiling
One idea is to paint the ceiling gray. A dark ceiling lowers the visual "lid" of the room and makes it feel wrapped around you.

### Go all in
For a full cocoon, layer shades of one color (the list suggests blues) or go tone on tone, like black on black or black with dark gray. Varying the shades keeps the room from looking like a single flat block.

## Add Dimension With Board-and-Batten

Board-and-batten is on the list as a way to add drama. Painted in a dark color, the vertical lines create shadow and structure, so walls look architectural instead of just dark. It's a good fit if you want a full-color room to feel designed rather than simply painted.

## Layer Textures So Dark Doesn't Look Flat

Texture is one of the main fixes the designers lean on. A dark wall with smooth, matching bedding can feel heavy. The same wall with mixed materials feels inviting. Think a knit throw, linen or velvet pillows, a woven basket, a wood nightstand. Aim for at least three different surfaces on and around the bed, and you'll see the room soften.

Natural materials help in particular. The roundup suggests mixing dark green with natural materials, which keeps a saturated color grounded and organic.

## Balance Dark Walls With Lighter Pieces

Contrast is what keeps a dark room from swallowing itself. Three ways the list does it:

1. **White furniture.** A pale dresser or nightstand pops against a deep wall.
2. **Light, airy curtains.** Sheer or pale drapes soften the window and bounce some brightness into the room.
3. **Classic black and white.** A high-contrast pairing gives the room crispness rather than murk.

Black furniture also appears on the list, which works the other way around: dark pieces against dark walls for a layered, enveloping look. Choose one approach per room so the scheme stays clear.

## Bring In Pattern or Cheerful Color

A dark room can still be playful. The roundup suggests patterned wallpaper, and blending cheerful colors with moody ones. A bright accent, such as a colorful throw or artwork, keeps the mood from tipping into somber. It even suggests experimenting with dark color in a kids' room, which shows the look isn't reserved for serious, grown-up spaces.

## Use Warm Lighting

The designers' advice, as summarized in the article's description, pairs deep colors and layered textures with warm lighting. That's the piece many people skip. Dark paint absorbs light, so a single bright overhead fixture tends to look harsh against it. Instead, use lamps on each side of the bed and consider warm-toned bulbs, so the walls glow rather than go flat. This is our practical read on the advice rather than a specific number from the list, but it's a cheap thing to test before you buy any paint.

## Renting? Borrow the Idea Without the Paint

The roundup is about painted rooms, but you can borrow its principles if you can't repaint. These are our suggestions, not the article's:

- Get the dark from bedding, a large headboard or curtains.
- Add dark furniture, which the list endorses, against lighter walls.
- Layer textures and warm lamps exactly as above.

## Your Next Step

Before you commit to a gallon, pick one wall or the ceiling, choose a color with some warmth in it, and plan your texture and lighting at the same time. Dark paint only feels cozy when the room around it is soft, layered and well lit.
