A kitchen in a once-forgotten Malibu mobile home just earned a Design 9 Awards nod as Southern California's best kitchen. The takeaway for your own house: a small, overlooked space can become the most-used room in the home if you design it around how people actually live in it.

Per Modern Luxury, Alana Marie Interiors transformed the property, steps from the Malibu sand, into a coastal escape where three generations can cook, gather and, in the outlet's words, "live a little louder." The published details are limited, so this piece doesn't invent specifics about finishes or layout. Instead, it pulls out the design thinking the project demonstrates and shows how to apply it at home.

## What the Project Shows

Here is what's reported: a neglected mobile home, a coastal Malibu location, a multigenerational household, and a kitchen built as the center of gathering. That combination is the lesson. The kitchen wasn't treated as a showpiece to admire. It was treated as the place where a family spends its time.

## Start With Who Uses the Room

Designing for three generations means designing for very different bodies and habits. Whatever your household looks like, a quick audit helps before you buy anything:

- List everyone who uses the kitchen in a typical week, including kids and grandparents.
- Note what each person does there: cooking, homework, coffee, entertaining.
- Mark where traffic jams happen, usually near the fridge or sink.

Those answers should drive your layout and storage decisions before any style choices do.

## Make Gathering Space a Priority

The project's stated purpose is a place to cook and gather together. In your own kitchen, that usually means giving people somewhere to sit or lean that isn't in the cook's path. A counter with seating, a bench, or even a clear spot at the end of the room can do it. Aim for a layout where one person cooking doesn't push everyone else out.

## Treat Small Homes as an Opportunity

Modern Luxury frames this as an elevated mobile-home renovation, which fits a broader appeal of compact-home upgrades. Small footprints reward decisive choices. A few ways to make a compact space feel considered rather than cramped:

- **Edit hard.** Keep only what you use weekly on the counters.
- **Pick a tight palette.** Fewer colors and materials make a small room read as calm and cohesive.
- **Spend where you touch.** Faucets, drawer pulls and counters get handled daily, so quality there pays off most.

These are general design practices, not details confirmed from this project.

## Borrow the Coastal Mood Without a Beach

You don't need Malibu sand to bring in the feeling. Coastal style generally leans on light, easygoing materials and a relaxed attitude. Practical ways to get there:

1. Choose light, natural-feeling surfaces and textures over glossy, formal ones.
2. Let daylight in by keeping window areas clear of clutter.
3. Pick durable, forgiving finishes so the room can handle sandy feet, spills and crowds.

The point of a coastal kitchen is that it feels easy to be in. If a choice makes the room precious, skip it.

## Budget-Minded Ways to Apply This

An award-winning renovation likely isn't a weekend project, but the principles scale down:

- Repaint cabinets and swap hardware before replacing anything.
- Add one seating spot where people can gather.
- Reorganize storage so the things each generation needs are within reach.
- Upgrade lighting so the room works for both cooking and lingering.

## The Bigger Lesson

This kitchen won attention because it started with a neglected property and a clear purpose: bring a family together. Before you shop for a single tile or fixture, write down who needs your kitchen and what they do in it. Design for that, and the room will earn its place whatever its size.
