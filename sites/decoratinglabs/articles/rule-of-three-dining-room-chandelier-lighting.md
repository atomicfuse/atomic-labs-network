Three chandeliers over one dining table sounds like a lot. According to Homes & Gardens, that is exactly what Kris Jenner has done in her California dining room, and the outlet calls the result bold, eye-catching and a lesson in modern luxury. The good news: the idea behind it is a simple design rule you can borrow, scaled to your own table and budget.

Below, you'll get the logic behind the "rule of three," plus practical guidance on sizing, spacing and dimming so the look feels warm instead of overdone.

## What Kris Jenner Did

Per Homes & Gardens, the setup uses three crystal chandeliers hung over a single dining table. The outlet describes the effect as creating rhythm, warmth and modern luxury, and frames it as a heritage-inspired look that suits where luxury interiors are heading: spaces that feel more inviting and less cold or formal.

The takeaway for you isn't "buy three crystal chandeliers." It's that repeating one statement fixture across a table can add drama while still feeling welcoming.

## Why Odd Numbers Feel Balanced

The piece points to the principle that odd-numbered groupings feel more balanced than even ones. Two fixtures can read as a matched pair, a bit static. Three gives your eye a center point with something on either side, which creates movement across the room.

This works well over a long dining table, where one fixture can look lost and two can leave an awkward gap in the middle. The same thinking applies elsewhere in the room: three vases on a sideboard, three frames on a wall, three candlesticks down the table.

## How to Copy the Look at Home

You don't need a mansion-size table. Here's a practical way to approach it.

### Start with the table, not the fixture

Three lights only make sense if your table is long enough to carry them. A rectangular table that seats six or more is the natural candidate. On a small or round table, a single larger fixture is usually the better call, and you can still use the rule of three elsewhere in the room.

### Keep the fixtures in the same family

Repetition is what makes the trio look intentional. Choose three of the same fixture, or three clearly matched ones, rather than a mix. This is also where budget helps: buying three identical simpler fixtures often looks more deliberate than one expensive piece that doesn't relate to anything.

### Space them evenly

A simple method: measure your table's length, divide it into three equal sections, and center one fixture over each. Mark the ceiling spots with painter's tape and check the layout from a seated position before drilling anything. Make sure the outer two sit comfortably inside the table's ends rather than hanging off them.

### Size them so they don't crowd

Because there are three, each fixture should be smaller than a single statement chandelier would be. Think of the trio as one lighting composition. If they feel close together when you mock them up with cardboard circles, go smaller or move to a more slender profile.

### Mind the hanging height

Hang them low enough to feel connected to the table but high enough that nobody has to dodge them or can't see the person across from them. A common starting point is roughly 30 to 36 inches above the tabletop; adjust to your ceiling height and your tallest dinner guest.

## Add a Dimmer, Always

Warmth is the whole point of this trend, and a dimmer is how you get it. Three bright fixtures at full power can feel harsh. On a dimmer, you can run them low and glowing for dinner and raise them for homework or cleanup. Pair them with warm-toned bulbs to keep the glow flattering.

If your dining room has no existing wiring for multiple fixtures, talk to an electrician before planning the layout. Adding ceiling points is not a casual weekend task.

## Budget Ways to Get the Same Effect

- **Buy three matching mid-priced fixtures** instead of one pricey chandelier. Repetition does the heavy lifting.
- **Choose simpler shapes.** You don't need crystal to get rhythm. Clean, slim designs can feel modern while still nodding to classic glamour.
- **Refresh what you own.** Cleaning an older fixture and swapping in warmer bulbs can change its look more than you'd expect.
- **Use the trio idea on a smaller scale.** Three pendants over a kitchen island or three wall sconces along a dining wall borrow the same logic.

## Don't Overdo the Rest of the Room

Three statement lights are the show. Keep the table setting, art and seating calmer so the lighting can lead. Warm wood, soft textiles and simple tableware let the fixtures read as luxurious rather than loud.

## Your Next Step

Before you buy anything, cut three cardboard circles to the size you're considering, tape them to the ceiling over your table, and sit down. If the rhythm feels right at your table's length, you've got your answer.
