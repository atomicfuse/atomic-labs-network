Most rooms don't look off because of one big thing. They look off because of five small things, and you can fix every one of them in an afternoon. A recent roundup of common styling mistakes, originally reported by Yahoo, lists five that interior designers spot immediately, from poorly scaled art to backwards books. The main message: a home looks better when its choices feel intentional, personal, and properly scaled.

Here's each mistake, why it reads as "off," and what to do instead.

## 1. Hanging Too Many Small Pieces of Art

A wall dotted with lots of little frames can look scattered, as if nothing was planned. The issue is scale: small pieces get lost on a big wall.

**Try this:**
- Swap a cluster of tiny frames for one or two larger pieces.
- If you love your small pieces, group them tightly so they read as a single unit rather than a scatter.
- Before drilling, tape a paper template to the wall to check how the size feels in the space.

## 2. Relying on So-So Pillows

The source calls out "so-so" pillows, the limp, forgettable kind that make a sofa look like an afterthought. Pillows are among the cheapest ways to change how a seating area feels, which makes mediocre ones a missed opportunity.

**Try this:**
- Replace the flattest, saggiest pillows first. Fuller inserts make covers look better instantly (my suggestion, not the source's).
- Mix textures and patterns instead of buying a matching set.
- Spend your budget on a few good ones rather than a pile of filler.

This is classic budget decor: a small purchase with a big visual return.

## 3. Choosing Mass-Market Art Only

Art that everyone owns can make a room feel generic. That ties into a bigger shift toward personality-driven interiors, vintage finds, and meaningful decor over copy-paste trends.

**Try this:**
- Add at least one piece with a story: a vintage find, something from a trip, or a kid's artwork in a good frame.
- Mix mass-market pieces with personal ones rather than banning the store-bought entirely.
- Ask of each piece: would a guest know this is *yours*?

## 4. Turning Books Backwards

Books turned with the pages facing out have become a styling habit, but designers reportedly notice it right away, and not in a good way. It can read as trying too hard, and it hides the thing that makes books personal: the spines.

**Try this:**
- Put books back the right way around.
- Stack some horizontally and stand others upright for variety.
- Use the books you actually read. Real titles say more about you than a color-coordinated wall of pages.

## 5. Treating Every Shelf the Same with Trendy Items

When every shelf gets the same trendy accessories in the same arrangement, the whole unit feels like a showroom display. It's styled, but it's not yours.

**Try this:**
- Vary what each shelf holds: books on one, a framed photo and a favorite object on another, empty breathing room on a third.
- Replace at least one trendy item per shelf with something personal or vintage.
- Step back and squint. If every shelf looks identical, change one.

## Do a Quick Designer Audit This Weekend

Walk through each room and ask five questions:

1. Does any wall hold lots of small art that would be stronger as fewer, larger pieces?
2. Are the sofa pillows full and interesting, or just there?
3. Is anything on the walls actually personal?
4. Are the books facing the right way?
5. Do all the shelves look like copies of each other?

As more homes are styled with social media in mind, spaces risk looking generic rather than lived-in. These five fixes push in the opposite direction. None needs a big budget or a renovation, just a little attention to scale and a willingness to put your own things on display.

Start with the easiest one today: turn those books around, then take a hard look at the nearest shelf.
