A living room rarely looks unfinished because of one big missing piece. It usually looks that way because of the small stuff: bare surfaces, a rug that doesn't fit, frames that never got filled. The good news is that the small stuff is cheap to fix.

RealSimple recently ran a roundup of 11 living room decor picks from an interior designer, all available on Amazon, with prices starting at just $9. The published summary names categories like coffee table books, coasters, area rugs, side tables, and picture frames, plus other living room pieces. It doesn't spell out all 11 here, so I won't pretend to list them. What I can do is show you how to use the categories that were named to make a room feel more put together, without spending much.

## Start With the Surfaces You Look at Most

Your coffee table and side tables do the heavy lifting in a living room. When they're empty or cluttered with remote controls and mail, the whole room feels unplanned.

### Coffee table books

This is one of the easiest upgrades on the list. A couple of books stacked on the coffee table give the surface height and a focal point. Pick titles whose covers and spines you actually like, since they'll be on display. Choose subjects that match your interests, such as travel, art, food, or architecture, and the table starts to say something about you.

A simple way to style them:

- Stack two or three books, largest at the bottom.
- Set a small object on top, like a candle or a bowl.
- Leave some of the table empty so it doesn't look crowded.

### Coasters

Coasters sound boring, but they do two jobs. They protect your furniture from rings and heat, and they're a small chance to add texture or color. Keep a set within arm's reach of every seat so guests actually use them. Stored in a small tray on the table, they look intentional rather than like an afterthought.

## Fix the Floor With an Area Rug

A rug is the item most likely to change how the whole room feels. It defines the seating area, adds warmth underfoot, and ties your furniture together. It's also especially useful if you rent, because it adds color and pattern without touching the floors or walls.

Before you buy, measure. Here's a practical way to do it:

1. Mark out the rug's size with painter's tape on your floor.
2. Check that the front legs of your sofa and chairs would sit on it, or at least that the rug sits comfortably in front of them.
3. Leave a border of bare floor around the edges so the rug looks like a deliberate layer.

A rug that is too small is one of the most common reasons a room looks off. Taping it out takes five minutes and saves you a return.

## Add Function With a Side Table

A side table gives guests a place to set a drink and gives you a spot for a lamp. It also fills the awkward gap next to a sofa or armchair. Look at the height of your seat arms and aim for a table that's close to that height, so reaching it feels natural.

Keep the top simple. One lamp, one small plant or object, and room for a mug is plenty. If you need to tuck it into a tight corner, a compact round table is easier to walk around than a square one.

## Fill the Walls With Picture Frames

Blank walls make a room feel temporary. Picture frames are an inexpensive way to fix that, and they work whether you're hanging photos, prints, or a favorite postcard.

Some ways to make them look considered:

- Stick to one or two frame finishes, such as black or wood, so the group feels unified.
- Use matching mats if you can. They make mixed images look like a set.
- Lay everything on the floor first and arrange it before putting a single nail in the wall.
- Renters can use removable hanging strips, if your lease is strict about holes.

## Think in Swaps, Not Overhauls

The real idea behind a roundup like this is that a room can look noticeably better through a handful of small changes. You don't need a full redesign. You need to replace the things that look default with things that look chosen.

Here's a sensible order if your budget is tight:

1. **Frames and coasters first.** They cost the least and fix the most visible gaps.
2. **A coffee table book or two.** Instant styling for a bare surface.
3. **A side table, if you need one.** Only buy it if it solves a real problem, like nowhere to put a drink.
4. **A rug last.** It costs more and takes more thought, so measure carefully.

## A Quick Word on Shopping Smart

Amazon makes it fast to compare options, but quick shopping can lead to impulse buys. Before you add anything to your cart, check the dimensions, read the reviews that include photos, and look at the return policy. Prices and availability change, so confirm the details on the product page, and check RealSimple's original roundup for the designer's exact 11 picks.

## Your Next Step

Walk into your living room tonight and look at the emptiest surface or wall. Pick one item from this list that would fill it, measure the space, and buy only that. One good swap beats a cart full of maybes.
