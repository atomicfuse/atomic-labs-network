You don't need to knock down a wall to make a boring house feel like yours. Interior designer Alexandra Brownlow shows how in her Melbourne townhouse, a bland 1990s semi that she turned into a richly layered, art-filled home, as reported by Homestolove. Her approach leans on colour, pattern, antiques, contemporary art and English country-house references rather than structural upheaval.

I'll stick to what the report supports and translate it into steps you can use in your own place, whether it's a rental-friendly tweak or a weekend project.

## Decorate First, Demolish Later

The most useful idea here is the "ugly duckling" approach: take a plain, characterless house and work out what decoration alone can do before you reach for a builder. Brownlow's home started as a standard 1990s semi, and the transformation came through layering rather than rebuilding.

Try this in your own home:

1. Live in the space for a few weeks and note what actually bothers you (light, colour, emptiness) versus what you assume needs renovating.
2. Fix the cheap things first: paint, wallpaper, lighting, art.
3. Only then decide whether a structural change is still worth the money.

You may find the room was never the problem. It was just blank.

## Let Colour Do the Heavy Lifting

Maximalism is gaining momentum as a reaction against minimalist, beige interiors, and colour is its clearest tool. Chartreuse is one of the shades associated with this home, and it's a good example of how a single bold hue can change a room's whole mood.

If a full-room commitment feels scary, scale it:

- **Start small:** a hallway, powder room or the back of a bookcase.
- **Test big swatches:** paint a large sheet of card and move it around the room through the day.
- **Pair it with something familiar:** a bold wall looks calmer next to timber furniture or antiques.

## Use Wallpaper as a Statement

Pattern is part of what makes a maximalist room feel layered instead of flat. A statement wallpaper gives a plain room a point of view fast, and you can limit it to one wall, a nook or a ceiling if you want impact without overwhelm.

The country-house link matters here. English country-house style is built on pattern and a sense of accumulated history, so a traditional-feeling print can make a modern shell feel older and more settled.

## Build an Art Wall That Feels Collected

The home is described as art-filled, with contemporary art playing a major part. The lesson isn't to buy expensive pieces. It's to hang art with confidence and let it crowd the walls a little.

A few practical habits:

- Group pieces tightly rather than spacing them out evenly.
- Mix sizes and frames so it looks gathered over time.
- Include pieces that mean something to you, not just pieces that match the sofa.

That last point is what separates a curated home from a showroom. The current appetite is for interiors that feel storied and emotional, and personal art is the quickest route there.

## Mix Antiques With Contemporary Pieces

Brownlow's home pairs antiques with contemporary art, and that contrast is what keeps traditional style from feeling like a museum. An old piece gives warmth and history, while a modern painting adds tension and energy.

To try it without a big budget:

- Hunt for one good secondhand piece, such as a chest, mirror or side table, and give it a prominent spot.
- Place something modern or abstract directly above or beside it.
- Add personal objects on top: books, ceramics, things with a story.

This is also the eco-friendly angle. Buying and reusing older pieces keeps furniture in circulation and gives your rooms character that new flat-pack can't.

## Bring Country-House Charm Into a City Home

The appeal of this house is that it feels like a lived-in retreat despite being a city townhouse. You can borrow that feeling without owning a manor:

- **Layer pattern:** florals, checks or stripes alongside each other.
- **Choose heritage references:** antique furniture, traditional prints, collected objects.
- **Prioritise comfort:** a home that looks rich but feels stiff isn't doing its job.

## A Quick Plan for This Weekend

Pick one room and give it three moves: one bold paint or wallpaper choice, one tight gallery of art, and one antique or secondhand piece with a personal object on it. Live with it for a month before adding more.

The real takeaway from Brownlow's townhouse is permission. Start with the plainest room you have, and make one confident, colourful decision there today.
