A mobile home steps from the Malibu sand doesn't sound like the setup for an award-winning kitchen. But that's the story behind a winner in this year's Design 9 Awards, which Modern Luxury names Southern California's best kitchen. Here's what that project can teach you about making a compact, overlooked home work harder, whatever your budget.

Per Modern Luxury, Alana Marie Interiors took a once-forgotten mobile home and turned it into a coastal escape where three generations can cook, gather and, in the outlet's words, "live a little louder." The source material doesn't give us a materials list or a floor plan, so I won't invent one. What it does give us is a clear design philosophy, and that's the useful part.

## Start With How People Actually Gather

The heart of this project is the people, not the square footage. The home was reworked for a multigenerational family, which means the kitchen has to serve very different needs: someone cooking, someone hovering, someone who just wants to sit nearby and talk.

You can borrow that thinking in any home. Before you pick finishes, answer a few practical questions:

- Who is in the kitchen at the same time, and doing what?
- Where do people naturally stand when they're not cooking?
- Which tasks collide (prep, serving, homework, coffee)?

A kitchen designed around those answers feels generous even when it's small.

## Treat an Overlooked Home as an Opportunity

The starting point here was a neglected property. That's worth sitting with. Homes that others pass over, whether older, compact or mobile, often come with a lower barrier to entry and fewer rules about what "should" be done. Alana Marie Interiors' result shows that a thoughtful redesign can change how a home is perceived, not just how it looks.

If you're looking at a tired space of your own, ask what it could do for your life rather than what it lacks. Decide the one or two ways you want to live in it, then let those priorities guide every choice.

## Let the Setting Shape the Design

The home sits just steps from the Malibu sand, and the project is framed as a coastal escape. A kitchen like this works best when it feels connected to its surroundings. You don't need an ocean to apply the idea:

- **Pull from your own landscape.** Desert, woods, city or shoreline, echo the colors and light outside.
- **Keep it livable.** Coastal style is often about ease: surfaces and layouts that handle sandy feet, quick meals and crowds.
- **Make it personal.** The goal is a space that feels like the family, not a showroom.

## Make a Small Home Feel Luxurious

Modern Luxury's framing suggests that luxury here comes from how the space lives rather than how big it is. A few principles that hold up in small homes generally (these are my own general advice, not details from the project):

### Edit hard
A small kitchen shows clutter instantly. Keep counters clear and give everyday items a defined home.

### Choose one standout feature
One memorable element, such as a distinctive surface, a bold light fixture or a striking color, does more than five competing ones.

### Make the kitchen the social center
When cooking and gathering share the same zone, a compact room feels like the heart of the house instead of a cramped afterthought.

## Plan for Flexibility

Multigenerational households change: kids grow, grandparents visit, dinners swell from four people to twelve. Flexible furnishings help, like movable seating, surfaces that serve double duty and storage that's easy for everyone to reach. Design for the busiest day, then make sure it still feels calm on the quietest one.

## Budget-Minded Ways to Borrow the Approach

Award-winning doesn't have to mean a full gut renovation for your own home. Try this order of operations:

1. **Define the gathering moment.** Pick the one scenario you most want the kitchen to support.
2. **Fix the layout friction first.** Clear the path before you buy anything decorative.
3. **Spend on the touchpoints.** Prioritize what you use and see daily: counters, lighting, hardware.
4. **Reuse and refresh where you can.** Repainting, refinishing and re-hanging are kinder to both your wallet and the planet.
5. **Add personality last.** Art, textiles and found objects make it yours.

## The Takeaway

The Malibu project shows that a kitchen's value isn't measured in square feet. It's measured in how many people can comfortably fit into one good moment. Before you spend a dollar on your own space, decide who you want around the table, and design for them first.
