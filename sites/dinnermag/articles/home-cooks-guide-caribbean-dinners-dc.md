## Why the Forbes Guide Is a Useful Dinner Map

Per Forbes, the restaurants offer "everything from Trinidadian street food and Jamaican-inspired seafood to upscale Afro-Caribbean cuisine." That's three very different kinds of dinner:

- **Casual and handheld:** Trinidadian street food
- **Seafood-forward:** Jamaican-inspired cooking
- **Dressed-up and special-occasion:** Afro-Caribbean fine dining

That spread is the real takeaway. If you've only tried one Caribbean dish, you've tasted a small slice. Treating these as three separate dinners, not one theme, is the easiest way to explore.

## Trinidadian Street Food: The Weeknight Mindset

Street food is built for speed, eating with your hands, and sharing. The headline in Forbes's title mentions roti, a flatbread-based favorite, so that's a natural place to start.

What to borrow for your own weeknights:

- **Think in components.** Street food works because a few cooked items (a curried filling, a soft bread, a sharp sauce) get assembled to order. Cook the filling once, then let everyone build their own.
- **Keep the bread simple.** Store-bought flatbread is a fair stand-in if you don't want to make roti from scratch. Warm it in a dry skillet for about 30 seconds per side so it's pliable.
- **Add something bright.** A quick cucumber or onion pickle (thinly sliced, tossed with vinegar and a pinch of salt, ready in 15 minutes) cuts through rich, spiced fillings.

If you're ordering out, this is the style to try first. It's the lowest-commitment way to learn what you like.

## Jamaican-Inspired Seafood: The Fast Weekend Main

Seafood cooks quickly, which makes it ideal for relaxed entertaining. A fillet of firm white fish usually needs only 8 to 10 minutes in a hot pan or oven, depending on thickness, so most of your time goes to the seasoning and sides.

Practical ways to get that Jamaican-leaning flavor profile at home:

- **Season ahead.** Rub fish with a bold spice blend and let it sit 20 to 30 minutes before cooking. Jerk-style seasoning blends are widely sold; check the label for heat level before serving guests.
- **Substitute freely.** Snapper, cod, or even shrimp all work. Shrimp needs only 2 to 3 minutes per side.
- **Pair with something cooling.** Mango or pineapple salsa balances heat. Rice cooked with a splash of coconut milk makes an easy base.

Cook for four with about 1½ pounds of fish, and you have a dinner-party main that takes under 40 minutes from fridge to table.

## Upscale Afro-Caribbean Dining: Where to Go for a Special Night

Forbes includes upscale Afro-Caribbean cuisine in its roundup, and it's the one style that's hardest to copy at home. That's a reason to book a table, not a reason to skip it.

When you go, a few approaches help:

- **Order to share.** Pick several plates, not one entrée each, so you taste more.
- **Ask the server** which dishes best represent the kitchen. It's a simple way to learn about an unfamiliar menu.
- **Notice what you like**, then bring those flavors home in a simpler form, such as a spice, a sauce, or a side.

## How to Plan a Caribbean-Inspired Week

You don't need three recipes or three grocery trips. A shared pantry covers most of it:

1. **Monday: flatbread night.** Curried chickpeas or chicken with warm flatbread and quick pickles.
2. **Wednesday: seafood night.** Spiced fish or shrimp with coconut rice and fruit salsa.
3. **Weekend: go out or go big.** Book an upscale spot, or cook a slower dish for guests.

Overlapping ingredients like onions, garlic, limes, and rice keep the list short. Chickpeas can be swapped for chicken, and any firm fish can replace another, so use whatever is fresh and affordable.

## If You're Visiting D.C.

Match the style to the moment. Street food suits a quick lunch between sights. Seafood suits a relaxed evening. The upscale option suits a celebration. Check the full Forbes guide for actual restaurant names and details before you go, and confirm hours directly with each place.

Pick one of the three styles this week, street food, seafood, or a splurge dinner, and try it. One new dish teaches you more about Caribbean cooking than any list will.
