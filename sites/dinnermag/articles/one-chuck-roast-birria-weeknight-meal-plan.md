A BuzzFeed shopping roundup of cooking helpers highlights one pantry shortcut: EZ Bombs birria cooking bombs, seasoning bombs meant to deliver quick, flavorful birria-style meals without a cabinet full of spices. Per BuzzFeed, they work for tacos, quesadillas, ramen, nachos, and more. That list of uses is the interesting part, because it points to a smart weeknight strategy: cook one big batch of seasoned beef once, then change it into different dinners.

Here's how to do that with a single chuck roast. One note up front: the roundup doesn't give package directions, bomb-to-meat ratios, or cook times, so the quantities and timings below are general guidance for braising chuck roast. Follow the instructions on the EZ Bombs package for how many bombs to use, and adjust to taste.

## Why this works: one cook, many dinners

Chuck roast is a forgiving braising cut. It's well marbled, it turns tender with long, moist cooking, and it shreds easily. Seasoning it with a single bomb replaces the usual birria shopping list of multiple dried spices, so your grocery list stays short.

A 3 to 4 pound roast typically yields roughly 6 to 8 servings of shredded beef, enough for several meals for a household of two to four.

## Step 1: Cook the beef (hands-on: about 15 minutes)

1. Pat the roast dry and cut it into 3 or 4 large chunks. Smaller pieces cook faster and absorb more flavor.
2. Season with the birria bomb following the package directions.
3. Add the beef and enough liquid to come about halfway up the meat. Water or broth both work.
4. Cook until fork-tender and shreddable.

### Pressure cooker or slow cooker?

General guidelines for chuck roast: a pressure cooker usually takes about 60 to 75 minutes at high pressure, plus time to come up to pressure and release. A slow cooker takes about 8 hours on low, or 4 to 5 on high. Pressure cooking suits a Sunday afternoon you can't spare; the slow cooker suits a day when you can start it at breakfast. Either way, the beef is done when it pulls apart with little resistance.

### Save the broth

Shred the beef, then keep the cooking liquid. Skim off some of the fat, and store the rest. You'll use it throughout the week.

## Step 2: Plan the week

Refrigerate the shredded beef in its broth in airtight containers, and use it within 3 to 4 days. Freeze the rest for later.

### Night one: tacos

Warm tortillas, pile on the beef, and top with chopped onion, cilantro, and lime. Serve a small cup of warm broth alongside for dipping. Plan about 3 tacos per person.

### Night two: quesadillas

Fill a tortilla with beef and a melting cheese such as Monterey Jack or mozzarella. Cook in a skillet over medium heat, about 3 minutes per side, until crisp and melted. Dip in the broth.

### Night three: birria ramen

The roundup specifically names ramen as a use. Heat a cup or two of the reserved broth per bowl, add cooked noodles, and top with beef, a soft egg if you like, and scallions. Instant ramen noodles (without the seasoning packet) work in a pinch, and cooking them separately keeps the broth from turning cloudy.

### Night four: sheet-pan nachos

Spread tortilla chips on a sheet pan, scatter with beef and shredded cheese, and bake at 400°F for about 8 to 10 minutes until bubbly. Finish with pickled jalapeños, sour cream, and chopped cilantro. This is also the easiest way to feed a casual crowd.

## Substitutions that hold up

- **No chuck roast:** Beef short ribs or brisket flat work in a similar braise; lean cuts like round tend to dry out.
- **No tortillas:** Serve the beef over rice or in baked potatoes.
- **Cheese:** Any good melter will do. Use what's in your fridge.
- **Broth:** If you're short on cooking liquid, stretch it with store-bought beef broth.

## One grocery list

For all four dinners, you need: one 3 to 4 pound chuck roast, the birria bomb, tortillas, cheese, chips, onion, cilantro, limes, noodles, scallions, and eggs if you want them. Pickled jalapeños and sour cream are optional extras.

## Why seasoning bombs are worth a look

BuzzFeed frames this kind of product around a real appeal: restaurant-style flavor at home without buying lots of individual spices or spending hours prepping. That's an honest trade. You give up control over exact seasoning, and you gain a lot of time. If a pre-blended flavor makes you actually cook on a Tuesday, it has done its job. Taste the beef before you serve it and add salt or lime juice if it needs brightness.

## The takeaway

This weekend, braise one chuck roast with a birria bomb, save the broth, and decide on your four dinners before you shred a single bite. The cooking is the easy part. The plan is what makes the week.
