House Beautiful recently rounded up 20 elegant Halloween decorations, mostly from Pottery Barn, that aim to look festive rather than tacky. You don't need all 20. The list works best as a lesson in how to decorate: pick a few well-made pieces, repeat a material or color, and let a single spooky detail do the talking. Here's how to apply that to your own home.

## Start with materials, not motifs

Look at what the list leans on: antique brass, smoked amber glass, linen, ceramic, enameled cast iron. These are materials you'd happily own in March. The Halloween part comes from a small twist in the shape or detail.

Examples from the roundup:

- An antique brass spider-web mirror
- A smoked amber glass candle holder
- Linen napkins with an oak vine design
- Pumpkin-shaped enameled cast iron cocottes

The rule to borrow: if the object would look decent without the spooky element, it will look decent with it.

## Put the drama on the walls and entry

Wall pieces make a strong first impression without taking up surface space. The list includes two mirror options: the spider-web mirror and a snake-wrapped mirror that's Nagini-inspired. It also features a serpentine wall hook, which is a small way to add a creepy detail to an entryway.

A few ways to use this:

1. Choose one statement mirror and keep the surrounding wall bare.
2. Swap a regular hook near the door for a serpentine one, then hang a coat or tote from it.
3. Add a woodland critter doormat outside so guests see the theme before they step in. The roundup includes one.

## Build a candlelit mood

Light does more for atmosphere than any plastic decoration. The list offers a few ways in:

- **Smoked amber glass candle holder** for a warm, moody glow.
- **Hanging taper candles**, described as Hogwarts-inspired, for a floating-candle effect.
- **Ghost incense smoker** if you want scent along with the visuals.

If you try hanging tapers, keep them well away from anything flammable and never leave them burning unattended. Many people find that battery-operated tapers give the same look with less worry, though that's my suggestion rather than something from the roundup.

## Set a Halloween table that works for dinner

The food-and-drink section of the list is the largest, which makes sense: tableware is where elegant and playful meet most easily. You could assemble an entire party from it:

- **Serving:** a skull charcuterie board, a lidded snack bowl, and a gold-decaled "Hex Mix" snack bowl
- **Small bowls:** ghost-shaped pinch bowls for nuts, salt, or candy
- **Cooking and serving:** pumpkin-shaped enameled cast iron cocottes
- **Drinks:** scorpion-topped cocktail picks and autumn leaf inlay coasters
- **Linens:** napkins with an oak vine design
- **Statement set:** a skeleton serving collection with wine glasses

A simple way to keep this from looking busy: choose one "loud" item, like the skull board, and let everything else stay quieter. The leaf coasters and oak vine napkins are the calm pieces here. They signal autumn, not costume party.

## Add softness with a single playful piece

Elegant doesn't have to mean serious. The list includes a Gus the Ghost throw pillow with a bouclé pumpkin, which brings in texture and a bit of humor. On a neutral sofa, one pillow like this is plenty. Two or three starts to feel like a theme park.

## Bring Halloween outside and up high

Two items on the list suit porches and shelves:

- A light blue ceramic pumpkin planter, which gives you a pumpkin without the usual orange
- Antique brushstroke birdcages, which have a gothic feel and can hold candles, faux florals, or small ornaments

A sculptural golden snake is another option for a mantel or console. Treat it like a small sculpture and give it breathing room.

## Choose a cohesive palette

Looking across all 20 items, the palette skews toward brass, gold, amber, soft blue, and natural linen, with black and bone as accents. That's why they feel more like home decor than seasonal clutter. When you shop or thrift, pick two or three of these tones and stick to them. A gold-and-black scheme, for example, would tie together the Hex Mix bowl, the brass mirror, and the golden snake.

## Keep it from looking cheap

The best takeaways from the list, in practice:

- **Limit the count.** Five to seven well-chosen pieces beat twenty scattered ones.
- **Repeat a shape.** Snakes show up in the hook, mirror, and sculpture. Ghosts appear on the pillow, bowls, and incense smoker. Repetition reads as intentional.
- **Favor reusable pieces.** Many of the items, including the mirror, planter, and linens, can stay out well past October.
- **Mix high and low.** Pottery Barn is the main source here, but nothing stops you from thrifting brass candlesticks, old birdcages, or plain linens and adding one spooky accent. That's a styling approach, not a claim about specific prices.

## Your next step

Pick one room, usually the entry or dining table, and choose a single anchor from the list's categories: a mirror, a set of candles, or a serving piece. Build around it with two quieter items in the same finish. Stop there, step back, and see if you still want to add more. Usually you won't.
