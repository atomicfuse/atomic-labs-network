You don't need a full renovation to make a bedroom feel like a boutique hotel suite. Good Housekeeping recently collected 57 bedroom ideas designers say add a luxurious, personal feel. The summary available to me covers 20 of them, so that's what this guide works from. Pick one change, do it well, and let the room build from there.

The ideas fall into a few groups: the big swings (color and wallpaper), the layers (curtains, lighting, texture), and the structural or style moves. Start with whichever fits your budget and your tolerance for risk.

## Start With Curtains and Lighting

These are the easiest places to add polish, and neither requires touching a wall.

### Turn curtains into a focal point

Good Housekeeping's list suggests treating window treatments as a feature, not an afterthought. Choose a fabric or pattern that earns attention, and let it frame the bed wall or the window.

### Hang bedside pendant lights

Another idea on the list is swapping table lamps for pendants hanging on either side of the bed. This frees up nightstand space and adds a custom look. If hardwiring isn't an option, plug-in versions are a common workaround (that tip is mine, not the list's).

## Choose a Color Direction

The list offers a range of moods, so you can match color to how you want to feel in the room.

- **Calming green:** The list includes both a general green idea and a specific one built around sage green. Both point toward a restful, natural palette.
- **Light and bright:** If you want airy and open, keep the palette pale.
- **Dark and moody:** The opposite approach: deep tones that make a bedroom feel like a cocoon.
- **Dramatic red or warm reds:** Two separate entries lean on red, from a bold statement to layered warm tones.

My take: if you're nervous about committing, test the idea in small doses first. Warm reds or deep colors can arrive through bedding and curtains before they ever reach the walls.

### Consider a glossy finish

The list also suggests a glossy paint finish. Shine reflects light, so it changes how the same color reads in the room. Test a sample patch and look at it in morning and evening light before you paint the whole space.

## Use Wallpaper Strategically

Wallpaper shows up twice, in two different strengths.

### Wallpaper the "fifth wall"

That's the ceiling. It's a way to add drama without crowding the walls, and it's the part of the room you see while lying in bed.

### Wallpaper the entire room

The all-in option. It's the bolder commitment, but it creates a wrapped, intentional feel.

If you're renting, check whether removable wallpaper suits your lease. That's my suggestion rather than part of the list.

## Layer Texture and Pattern

Luxury often comes from richness, not expense.

- **Show off natural textures:** Let materials with a natural feel do the work.
- **Play with pattern:** Pattern adds visual interest and personality.
- **Embrace the "more is more" mentality:** Instead of editing everything away, layer on purpose.
- **Embrace eclectic style:** Mix pieces that don't match but share a sensibility.
- **Mix vintage with modern:** An older piece next to clean-lined furniture gives a room history.
- **Let travel inspire decor:** Bring in objects and ideas from places you've been, so the room feels like yours.

A practical way to apply this: choose one anchor (a pattern, a vintage find, a travel souvenir) and build around it, rather than adding everything at once.

## Change the Architecture

Some ideas work with the room's bones or add structure.

### Design a cozy canopy bed

A canopy gives the bed its own enclosure and instantly makes it the centerpiece.

### Make the most of an A-frame

If your bedroom has sloped ceilings, the list suggests leaning into them, not fighting them.

### Create contrast with shiplap

Shiplap paneling adds texture and a contrast against other surfaces in the room.

## How to Choose Your First Move

With this many options, use a simple filter:

1. **Lowest commitment:** curtains, pendant lights, pattern in textiles.
2. **Medium commitment:** a new paint color or glossy finish.
3. **Highest commitment:** wallpapering the whole room, adding shiplap, or building a canopy bed.

The designers behind Good Housekeeping's list cover nearly every style, from pale and airy to dark and layered. The common thread is that each room feels deliberate and personal, not matched from a catalog.

Pick one idea this week, such as new curtains or a pair of pendants, and live with it before you add the next. A bedroom that feels luxurious is usually built in layers, not in a single weekend.
