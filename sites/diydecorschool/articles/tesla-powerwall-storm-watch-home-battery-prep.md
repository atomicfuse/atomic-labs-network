## What Storm Watch Mode Is, in Plain Terms

Per BASENOR, Storm Watch Mode is part of how Powerwall prepares for storm-related outages. The idea is simple: a home battery is most useful when it's full before the lights go out, not after. A feature built around bad weather exists to get ahead of the outage rather than react to it.

That framing matters for how you think about backup power. A battery that's half-drained when a storm rolls in gives you half the runtime you were counting on.

## Check Your Settings Before the Forecast Turns

Don't wait for a warning to learn where your settings live. On a calm day:

1. Open your Tesla app and find your Powerwall's settings.
2. Look for any storm-related or backup-related options and read what each one says.
3. Confirm the app shows your system as connected and online.
4. Note your current battery charge level so you know your normal baseline.

If something looks unfamiliar, check Tesla's own support materials or ask your installer. Ten minutes now beats guessing mid-storm.

## Decide What Your Battery Needs to Run

Backup power only helps if you know what you want it to cover. Before storm season, write a short list in two columns.

### Must-run items
- Refrigerator and freezer
- Phone and laptop charging
- Internet router and modem
- A few lights

### Nice-to-have items
- TV and gaming gear
- Space heaters or other high-draw appliances
- Extra kitchen appliances

Prioritizing keeps you from draining the battery on comforts in the first hours. Check with your installer about which circuits your system backs up, since that varies by setup.

## Prep the Rest of the House Too

A battery doesn't replace basic storm readiness. Pair it with a few low-tech steps:

- Keep flashlights and spare batteries where you can find them in the dark.
- Charge portable power banks and devices ahead of time.
- Know where your breaker panel and any manual shutoffs are.
- Secure outdoor furniture, planters, and decor that could become projectiles.
- Clear gutters and drains so heavy rain has somewhere to go.

These are standard home-prep habits, not claims about Powerwall. They're worth doing either way.

## Questions to Ask Your Installer

If you're shopping for a home battery or already own one, these questions turn a vague feature name into real answers:

- What does the system do automatically when severe weather is forecast?
- Can I adjust that behavior, and where?
- Which parts of my home are covered during an outage?
- About how long could the battery run my must-run list?
