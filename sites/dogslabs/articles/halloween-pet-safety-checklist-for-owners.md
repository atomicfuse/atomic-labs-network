Halloween is fun for people and, per News3lv's pet safety roundup, can be legitimately scary for pets. The good news: a little preparation covers most of the risk. Run through the checklist below before the first trick-or-treater shows up, and you'll know your candy is secured, your pet can't bolt out the door, and your decorations aren't a hazard.

This guide is ordered by what matters most, so if you only have five minutes, read the first two sections.

## Lock Down the Candy First

Halloween packs several hazards into one night, and candy is the big one. The reporting flags two ingredients in particular: chocolate and xylitol. Both are a problem for dogs and cats.

Things you can do tonight:

- **Store the bowl high and out of reach.** Not on the coffee table, not on a low shelf, not in a bag on the floor.
- **Keep trick-or-treat hauls out of pet range.** Kids' pumpkin buckets left by the couch are an easy target. Give kids a designated spot, like a closed closet or high counter.
- **Check labels for xylitol.** Sugar-free candy and gum are worth separating and putting away entirely.
- **Tell kids the rule.** "Candy stays off the floor" is simple enough for most children to follow.

If your pet does get into candy, don't wait to see what happens. Call your veterinarian or a pet poison hotline and have the wrapper handy so you can describe what and roughly how much was eaten.

## Prevent Door Dashes

Frequent doorbell rings are one of the unusual stressors Halloween brings into the home. Every open door is a chance for a nervous or excited pet to slip outside, especially in the dark, among strangers and costumes.

A few practical setups:

- **Put your pet in a separate room** with a closed door, water, a comfortable bed, and something to chew or lick.
- **Use a gate or leash** if you want your dog nearby but not at the door.
- **Move the candy station outside** (a table on the porch or driveway) so the door stays closed more often.
- **Make sure ID is current.** Collar tags and microchip details should match your current phone number before the night begins.

Cats deserve the same plan. A quiet back bedroom works well for them.

## Think Twice About the Costume

Not every pet enjoys dressing up. If you do try a costume, treat it as a trial run rather than a requirement.

### Signs your pet hates it

The costume is probably a no if your pet freezes, tries to back away, paws at it, hides, or won't move normally. Take it off and don't push it. A festive bandana is often an easier compromise than a full outfit.

### Make it safer if they tolerate it

- Try it on days ahead, for a few minutes at a time, with treats.
- Make sure they can see, breathe, hear, and move freely.
- Skip anything that covers the face or restricts their legs.
- Take it off if they seem stressed, even if the photo isn't done.

## Rethink Decorations, Candles, and Scents

The same things that make a house look spooky can tempt pets. A few safer swaps:

- **Candles:** Use flameless LED candles instead of open flames in pumpkins. A wagging tail or curious cat can knock over a real one.
- **Fog machines and strong scents:** Strong smells and fog can bother sensitive noses. Use them sparingly, in areas your pet doesn't share, or skip them.
- **Dangling or chewable décor:** Keep cords, small pieces, and anything a pet might chew or swallow out of reach.

Walk through your house at pet height once. You'll spot things you'd never notice standing up.

## Give Your Pet a Calm Place

Loud activity, doorbells, and strangers in costume add up. Set up a quiet room before the evening starts with a bed, water, and a familiar toy. Soft background noise, like a TV or radio, can help mask the commotion. Check on your pet periodically, and let them stay put if they seem happier there.

## Do the Morning-After Cleanup

Halloween isn't over when the lights go out. Wrappers and dropped candy can end up in your yard, on the sidewalk, and around your porch, and your dog will find them on the first morning walk.

1. **Walk the yard and porch** before you let your pet out.
2. **Pick up wrappers, candy, and decorations** that blew around overnight.
3. **Empty the trash** or move it somewhere secure, since candy wrappers smell like what was inside.
4. **Check on a leash** during the first walk, watching for anything your dog tries to grab.

## The One Thing to Do Right Now

Pick your pet's quiet room today and set it up before Halloween night. If your pet is secure and the candy is out of reach, you've handled the biggest risks.
