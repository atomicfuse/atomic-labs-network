## The Plight of Senior Dogs
Lica's story is all too common among senior dogs. Often overlooked in favor of younger, more energetic pets, older dogs face unique challenges in shelters. Many people mistakenly believe that senior dogs are untrainable or incapable of bonding deeply, which is far from the truth. With patience and love, older dogs can adapt beautifully to new living environments, forming strong connections with their new families. 

### Why Older Dogs Deserve Homes Too
1. **Potty Trained**: Most senior dogs come already house-trained, sparing new owners the challenge of teaching a puppy.
2. **Calm Demeanor**: Older dogs tend to have a more laid-back personality, making them perfect companions for families, singles, or retirees looking for a gentle pet.
3. **Less Destructive**: Less likely to chew on furniture or dig up the yard, senior dogs can fit seamlessly into any home.
4. **Deep Bonds**: With a little encouragement, senior pets form profound bonds with their humans, often valuing this relationship even more than their younger counterparts. 

## Recovery and Rehabilitation: Building Trust
After her rescue, Lica was introduced to shelter life—a process full of challenges and triumphs. The team at the shelter worked diligently to ensure Lica felt safe and secure, creating an environment where rehabilitation could happen gradually. 

### The Process of Healing

- **Positive Reinforcement**: Successful rehabilitation involves reinforcing small victories. Treats, affection, and encouragement went a long way in helping Lica adjust.
- **Routine and Stability**: Establishing a consistent daily routine helped Lica find comfort. Structured feeding and walking schedules can offer predictability in an otherwise daunting world.
