After six years of "maybe someday" energy, the Monterey Five appear to be finally, actually, genuinely returning. Multiple cast members — Nicole Kidman, Reese Witherspoon, Laura Dern, Zoë Kravitz, and Shailene Woodley — publicly confirmed in early 2025 that work on *Big Little Lies* season 3 is moving forward. HBO has not yet made an official renewal announcement, but when every actress in a five-person ensemble starts saying the same thing in interviews, that's not a coincidence. That's a coordinated signal flare.

For fans who have been sitting with the show's unresolved season 2 threads since 2019, the wait has been genuinely painful. So let's talk about the cast's statements, the real obstacles that kept season 3 from happening sooner, and what this moment actually signals — because the road here was a lot rockier than the current enthusiasm suggests.

## From "Probably Not" to "We're Working On It"

It's worth remembering how different the vibe was just a few years ago. Series creator David E. Kelley stated as far back as 2019 that a third season was unlikely — not impossible, but not something anyone should count on. The original barrier was brutally practical: five A-list actresses with five wildly incompatible schedules. Kidman alone was filming projects on multiple continents. Stacking those calendars felt close to impossible.

Then, in December 2021, director Jean-Marc Vallée died unexpectedly at 58. Vallée had been the creative architect of both seasons — his handheld intimacy, his music-video-adjacent editing rhythms, his instinct for the fragile and the ferocious all at once — and his loss cast a genuine shadow over whether the show could or should continue without him. That's not a scheduling problem. That's a grief problem, and it deserved time.

The shift in tone from the cast in early 2025 suggests that time has now done its work. Saying they're "working on the next chapter" is a meaningfully different sentence than anything any of them was saying in 2022 or 2023. Per reports, the scheduling conflicts that originally stalled the project also appear to be resolving — which, given the individual trajectories of five of the most in-demand actresses working right now, is its own minor miracle.

## What Jean-Marc Vallée's Legacy Means for Season 3's Tone

This is the angle that deserves more attention than it's getting in the general excitement. *Big Little Lies* wasn't just well-cast — it was visually and rhythmically distinct in a way that was inseparable from Vallée's direction. Season 1 won eight Emmy Awards, including Outstanding Limited Series. Season 2 was more divided in its critical reception, but the visual language remained unmistakably his.

Whoever steps into the director's chair for season 3 inherits both an incredible opportunity and a genuinely complicated legacy. The show has an established aesthetic vocabulary — those Monterey coastal shots, the slow-motion memory sequences, the way violence and beauty were edited to sit uncomfortably close together. A new director will have to decide: honor that language faithfully, or find a way to evolve it that feels like a natural next chapter rather than an imitation.

That creative question is probably the most consequential decision *Big Little Lies* season 3 will make, and as of now, no director has been publicly attached to the project. It's hard not to wonder whether the right answer is someone who worked closely with Vallée and understands the show's DNA from the inside — or whether a genuinely fresh eye, someone who can respectfully acknowledge what came before while steering their own direction, might actually serve the material better.

## The Shailene Woodley Thread

The description attached to this story specifically flags Shailene Woodley — who played Madeline's daughter Abigail — as someone who has shared her hopes for the season. That detail matters more than it might seem. Abigail was a character whose season 2 arc left genuine threads hanging: her relationship with her mother, her own moral awakening about the Monterey Five's cover-up, her position as someone who knew more than she was supposed to. She was, in some ways, the show's conscience in miniature.

Woodley's enthusiasm for season 3, per reports, puts her in the same camp as the five leads — and that ensemble energy across the whole cast is one of the clearest signals that this project has genuine momentum rather than just good PR intentions.

## Why the 2025 Timing Makes Sense

The "scheduling conflicts are resolving" framing is worth unpacking, because it tells you something about where everyone is in their careers right now. Kidman's output has remained prolific but the fever pitch of her mid-2010s and early 2020s volume has settled into something more selective. Witherspoon has been building Hello Sunshine into a production empire, meaning she arguably has more control over her own calendar than she did six years ago. Dern, Kravitz, and Woodley have all had projects land in the interim that may have moved them to moments of relative availability.

In other words: the stars, literally and figuratively, may have just aligned in a way that wasn't structurally possible in 2019.

## What Fans Actually Need From Season 3

The season 2 finale left Bonnie's legal fate genuinely unresolved — she was moving toward turning herself in, carrying the weight of Perry's death differently than the other four women. Renata's financial collapse and her volcanic rage at her husband were mid-arc when the credits rolled. Celeste's custody battle and her emerging sense of self were left in a hopeful but unfinished place. Mary Louise Wright — Meryl Streep playing Perry's mother with unsettling, coiled precision — was circling the truth in ways the season never fully paid off.

Those are not small loose ends. Those are structural cliffhangers that a fandom has been sitting with for the better part of a decade. The cast's enthusiasm is wonderful. But the real measure of season 3 will be whether the writing honors how much has been left unfinished — and whether a post-Vallée vision can carry the emotional weight that made this show worth caring about in the first place.

The Monterey Five kept each other's secrets for six years. Turns out, so did the show.
