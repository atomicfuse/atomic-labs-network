Deadline's review of *Half* calls Samjad's Malayalam action-horror film "fast, fun" and super-violent, and the outlet's description gets right to the soundtrack: bhangra beats scoring brutal beatings. The film, about twin vampires taking revenge on a desert crime network in a dystopian future, premiered in TIFF's Midnight Madness and has since played Sitges. That's a very respectable genre-festival one-two punch.

## The Pitch: Twins, Fangs, Grudges

Per Deadline, the setup is lean: twin vampires, a desert crime network, and a dystopian future where revenge is the main item on the agenda. The review praises that economy, along with the film's inventive violence and its world-building. It's hard not to read that as a sign the movie knows what it is and doesn't waste your time pretending to be anything else.

Deadline also points to a mix of influences: vampire horror, revenge thriller, neo-noir and spaghetti western aesthetics. That's a lot of genres for one film to juggle. Based on the review, though, the juggling is part of the fun rather than a sign of strain.

## Why the Score Is the Story

The detail that jumps out is the techno-bhangra score. Pairing the pounding energy of bhangra with on-screen brutality is the kind of tonal collision that tends to separate a forgettable genre exercise from something fans quote at midnight screenings. Deadline's write-up frames the film as having real cult-movie energy, and the music appears to be a big reason why.

My take: vampire movies often lean on gothic hush, all candlelight and brooding. A film that instead scores its bloodletting with dance-floor propulsion feels like a deliberate dare to the genre's habits.

If you like your horror quick, mean and rhythmically unhinged, *Half* sounds like the kind of title to track down as it moves beyond the festivals.
