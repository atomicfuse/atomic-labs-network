Kylie Jenner is headed to Hulu, and she is reportedly not making it out of the bachelorette party alive. Per Vulture, Jenner stars in a new series called *Bachelorette Weekend*, playing a bride who is murdered on her bachelorette trip. Her bridesmaids then become the suspects. Vulture's headline summed up the vibe neatly: it's basically *Pretty Little Liars*.

The tagline writes itself: Who killed Kylie?

## The Premise, Per Vulture

Here is what's been reported: Jenner plays the bride. The bride dies on the bachelorette trip. The friends who were supposed to be popping champagne are now people of interest. That's the extent of what we can responsibly say about the plot, and honestly, it's enough to get the gist.

The *Pretty Little Liars* comparison comes from the shape of the thing. That show hooked viewers on a group of close friends, a dead girl at the center, and the nagging question of who among them was lying. A murdered bride and a circle of bridesmaids with something to hide runs on much the same engine. It's our read, not a confirmed creative lineage, and nobody has said the show is intentionally modeled on anything.

## Why This Formula Keeps Working

It's hard not to see the appeal. The "friend group turns on itself" mystery is a durable one, because every suspect doubles as someone the audience is meant to like. Add a weekend away, where everyone is cooped up together, and you have a ready-made whodunit with built-in drama.

The bachelorette setting adds its own spin. It's a pressure-cooker occasion: matching outfits, old grudges, and a group chat's worth of history all in one place. If you were going to design a setup for secrets to spill, you could do worse.

There's also the casting wrinkle. Reportedly, the murder victim is the biggest name in the cast. Killing off your star is a classic mystery move, and it makes the dead bride the character everyone will keep talking about. Whether Jenner appears in flashbacks or only as a body is not something we know, so we won't pretend to.

## Kylie Follows Kim Into Scripted Territory

The bigger story may be the career pivot. Per Vulture, Jenner is following Kim Kardashian into scripted acting, which suggests a broader Kardashian-Jenner push into entertainment beyond reality TV. For a family that built an empire on being watched while being themselves, playing a character is a real change of pace.

Jenner also arrives with some goodwill. Vulture notes she received praise for a comedic turn in Charli XCX's *The Moment*. That gives her a credible recent credit to point to, and it's a smart bridge: a funny cameo reads as self-aware, and self-awareness is the currency that makes a celebrity actor easier to root for.

The timing suggests a deliberate strategy, though we can't know that from the outside. What we can say is that the path from a well-received comedic turn to a lead role in a streaming mystery looks like a tidy progression on paper.

## The Vanity-Project Question

Celebrity-led thrillers have a mixed track record, and skeptics will no doubt be sharpening their takes. The usual worry is that the casting becomes the whole marketing plan. But a murdered-bride role carries a built-in advantage: the character's defining trait is being the mystery. The audience is invited to speculate about her, not necessarily to be dazzled by her range.

That could be a savvy match of talent and material, or it could simply be a case of the premise doing the heavy lifting. Without having seen a frame of the show, we're not going to pick a side.

## What to Watch For

A few things will tell us whether this is a hit or a curiosity:

- **The ensemble.** In a whodunit, the bridesmaids carry the show. If they're sharp and distinct, the Jenner casting becomes a bonus rather than a crutch.
- **The tone.** Is it a straight thriller, a wink-heavy satire, or a blend? Her *The Moment* experience hints at comfort with comedy, but that's our inference.
- **The Kim comparison.** It's inevitable. Two sisters, two scripted careers, and a public that loves a head-to-head.

## The Last Word

A reality star who spent years being the most scrutinized person in any room is now cast as the person everyone else is being scrutinized over. Whatever else *Bachelorette Weekend* turns out to be, that's a neat bit of irony, and it's the one part of the pitch that needs no plot twist.
