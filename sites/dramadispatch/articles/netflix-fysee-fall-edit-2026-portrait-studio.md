Netflix's fourth annual FYSee: The Fall Edit has come and gone, and Deadline's portrait studio has the photos to prove it. Per Deadline, the event gathered stars and creators from the streamer's TV awards contenders, including the casts of *Nobody Wants This*, *The Diplomat*, *Beef*, *East of Eden*, *The Gentlemen* and *Monster: The Lizzie Borden Story*, among others.

The names attached to those portraits include Florence Pugh, Kristen Bell, Keri Russell, Melissa McCarthy and Charlie Hunnam. That's a lot of star wattage for a Netflix event, and it's the point.

## The Portrait Is the Pitch

Here's our take: a portrait studio at an awards event is never just a photo op. Each shot is a tiny campaign ad, designed to be scrolled past by the exact people who vote, and also by the fans who make voters feel a show is Having A Moment.

That's how it reads from where we sit, anyway. Deadline's framing is straightforward: the event spotlights Netflix's TV awards contenders. The dual purpose, Emmy campaigning on one side and fan-facing promo on the other, is our read of what an event like this does, not something Netflix has spelled out to us.

What the lineup tells you is which shows Netflix wants in the conversation. Putting them all in one room, in one fall event, is a way of saying: look at the breadth.

## The Range on Display

The slate Deadline lists is a deliberately mixed bag:

- **A rom-com:** *Nobody Wants This*, with Kristen Bell among the names tied to the event.
- **A political thriller:** *The Diplomat*, with Keri Russell.
- **A dark-comedy anthology-style hit:** *Beef*, which makes the list alongside the more conventional crowd-pleasers.
- **A literary adaptation:** *East of Eden*, with Florence Pugh among the stars.
- **A crime romp:** *The Gentlemen*, with Charlie Hunnam.
- **A true-crime drama:** *Monster: The Lizzie Borden Story*.

Melissa McCarthy is also among the big names Deadline connects to the event.

That spread is the interesting part. Comedy, spy-adjacent drama, a classic-novel adaptation and true crime all share one portrait wall. It's hard not to read it as Netflix arguing that its awards case isn't one genre or one prestige lane, but a whole buffet.

## Why East of Eden Stands Out

Of the titles, *East of Eden* is the one that feels most like a statement. The report details we have don't say how the series is being received or what Pugh's role entails, so we won't guess. But its inclusion beside the comedies and thrillers shows Netflix wants the book-to-screen crowd in its corner too.
