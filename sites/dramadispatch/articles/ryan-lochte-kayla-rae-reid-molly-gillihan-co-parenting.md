## The Stories Tab Is Not a Courtroom

Celebrity breakups used to leave a paper trail of tabloid covers and carefully worded joint statements. The joint statement, "we ask for privacy at this time," was a small act of choreography. Both sides signed off, and the story had edges.

Instagram Stories removed the edges. A 24-hour post can work as a rebuttal, a character reference, a subpoena and a press release all at once. Nobody has to sign off. A response can arrive within minutes, and the audience supplies the jury, usually without the full file.

The format also rewards the wrong things. Calm, measured posts rarely travel. The sharpest line does, and "desperate for attention" is a sharp line. When a disagreement about a family gets compressed into whatever fits on a phone screen, nuance is usually the first thing cut.

## Who Gets to Talk About the Kids?

The reported heart of this dispute is a question many blended families wrestle with privately: when does a new partner's voice help, and when does it add heat? Per Us Weekly, Reid objected to Gillihan's public commentary. We haven't been given the specifics of what was said, so it would be unfair to characterize it here.

What we can say is that the question is bigger than these three people. Parents and stepparent figures often disagree about where the line sits. Some see a partner speaking up as loyalty. Others see it as stepping into a conversation that belongs to the people who share legal responsibility for the children. Reasonable people land on both sides, and the answer often depends on the tone, the audience and who started it.

It's hard not to notice that the children themselves are the ones with the least say in any of this, and for that reason this piece will leave them out of the discussion. They didn't choose the platform.

## What Anyone Can Take From It

Strip away the famous names and this is a recognizable situation. Two former partners disagree about how to handle shared parenting, a new partner enters the conversation, and a private tension gets an audience. A few observations follow, none of them verdicts on anyone involved:

- **Private channels exist for a reason.** Disagreements about custody and parenting roles are usually easier to resolve when they aren't being performed.
- **A response is not the same as a resolution.** Replying online can feel like defending yourself, but it also keeps the story alive for another news cycle.
- **Attention is a loaded accusation.** Calling someone "desperate for attention" is a claim about motive, and motive is the one thing observers can't verify from the outside. Nobody outside the family can say what anyone intended.

## The Takeaway

We don't know what has been said in private, what has been agreed on, or how any of the adults involved actually feel. We know that a custody conversation ended up on a public feed and that Lochte answered there. The most useful thing the rest of us can do is resist the urge to hand down a ruling.

The Stories tab will keep supplying drama as long as people keep feeding it. The question for any family, famous or not, is whether the audience was ever supposed to be part of the conversation.
