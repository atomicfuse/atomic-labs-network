What follows is general cooking guidance plus a plan for the leftovers.

## Why a seasoning bomb makes sense for beginners

Birria is a rich, deeply seasoned beef dish from Mexico, often served in tacos or with a dipping broth. The appeal of a pre-built flavor bomb is simple: you skip buying many separate spices for one dish. If you only cook birria a few times a year, that saves money and cabinet space.

The trade-off is control. You can't adjust what's inside as easily as you can with your own spice mix. Taste your finished meat and broth before serving, and add salt, a squeeze of lime, or a little water as needed.

## The plan: cook once, eat several times

The idea is to turn a basic chuck roast into a base you can reuse. Chuck is a budget-friendly cut that does well with long, moist cooking, which is why it's a common choice for shredded beef.

### Step 1: Cook the roast with the bomb

Follow the package directions for how many bombs to use and how much liquid to add. Chuck roast is typically cooked in a slow cooker or pressure cooker. Cook until the meat shreds easily with a fork. If it resists, it needs more time, not more heat.

### Step 2: Shred and save the liquid

Pull the beef apart with two forks. Keep the cooking liquid. It's your flavor insurance: spoon some over the meat so it stays juicy, and save the rest for dipping or soup.

### Step 3: Divide it up

Split the shredded beef into portions before you refrigerate it. Store the meat with some of its liquid in airtight containers, and use it within a few days or freeze what you won't eat.

## Four ways to use the leftovers

The Buzzfeed pick names tacos, quesadillas, ramen, and nachos. Here is how I'd approach each as a quick weeknight meal.

### Tacos

Warm tortillas in a dry skillet, add the beef, and top with chopped onion, cilantro, and lime. It's the fastest option, and you can have dinner on the table in the time it takes to reheat the meat.

### Quesadillas

Put cheese and beef between two tortillas or on one folded in half. Cook in a skillet over medium heat until the outside is crisp and the cheese has melted, flipping once. Serve with some of the reserved liquid for dipping.

### Ramen

This is the most creative use of the lot. Cook instant ramen according to its package, then top it with the shredded beef and a spoonful of the cooking liquid. It turns a pantry staple into a more substantial meal.

### Nachos

Spread tortilla chips on a sheet pan, scatter beef and cheese over them, and bake until the cheese melts. Add toppings like jalapeños, scallions, or sour cream after baking so they stay fresh.

## Pressure cooker or slow cooker?

Both can work for chuck roast. A pressure cooker is faster, which suits a weeknight if you're home and planning ahead. A slow cooker lets you start dinner in the morning and come home to it. Choose based on your schedule, and check the product directions for any guidance specific to the bombs.

## If you don't have the bombs: a swap

Birria flavor generally comes from dried chiles, warm spices, garlic, and a little acidity. If you want to cook the same plan from scratch, a basic version can use chili powder, cumin, garlic, oregano, and a splash of vinegar or lime. It won't taste identical, and I can't promise it matches the product, but it will get you to a savory, shreddable beef with a similar spirit.

## A few tips to make it work

- **Salt at the end.** Seasoning mixes vary, so taste first.
- **Add brightness.** Lime, pickled onions, or fresh cilantro balance rich beef.
- **Label your containers.** You'll thank yourself midweek.
- **Vary the toppings, not the beef.** That's what keeps the same meat from feeling repetitive.

## The takeaway

On Sunday, cook one chuck roast with a flavor shortcut, shred it, and portion it out. By Wednesday, you'll have plenty of ways to turn it into a different dinner without starting over.
