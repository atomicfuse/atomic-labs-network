By the end of this article, you'll have a 35-minute, budget-friendly pot of Brazilian-style black beans with rice that uses mostly pantry staples.

A note on authenticity: this is a simplified weeknight version inspired by the beans-and-rice pairing that is a familiar part of Brazilian home cooking. Traditional versions often simmer dried beans for hours with smoked meats. This one takes shortcuts so you can eat tonight.

## What You'll Make

A savory pot of black beans seasoned with garlic, onion, and bay leaf, served over fluffy white rice with a squeeze of lime. It serves four, costs very little, and keeps well for lunches.

**Time:** 10 minutes prep, 25 minutes cooking.

## Ingredients

- 2 tablespoons neutral oil or olive oil
- 1 medium onion, finely chopped (about 1 cup)
- 4 garlic cloves, minced
- 2 cans (15 oz each) black beans, undrained
- 1 bay leaf
- 1/2 teaspoon salt, plus more to taste
- 1/4 teaspoon black pepper
- 1 cup long-grain white rice
- 2 cups water (for the rice)
- 1 lime, cut into wedges
- Optional: 2 slices bacon, chopped, for a smoky note
- Optional: chopped fresh parsley or green onion to finish

## Step-by-Step

### 1. Start the rice

Rinse the rice in a fine strainer until the water runs mostly clear. Combine it with 2 cups water and a pinch of salt in a saucepan. Bring to a boil, cover, reduce the heat to low, and cook 15 minutes. Turn off the heat and let it sit, covered, for 5 more minutes. Fluff with a fork.

### 2. Build the flavor base

While the rice cooks, warm the oil in a medium pot over medium heat. If you're using bacon, cook it first until crisp, about 5 minutes, then proceed with the onion in the same fat. Add the onion and cook 4 to 5 minutes, until soft and translucent. Stir in the garlic and cook just 30 seconds. Garlic burns fast, and burnt garlic turns bitter.

### 3. Simmer the beans

Pour in both cans of beans with their liquid. Add the bay leaf, salt, and pepper. Bring to a gentle bubble, then lower the heat and simmer 15 minutes, uncovered, stirring now and then.

### 4. Thicken it

Use the back of a spoon to mash about a quarter of the beans against the side of the pot. This gives the broth a creamy body without any added thickener. If it gets too thick, splash in a little water. If it's too thin, simmer a few minutes longer.

### 5. Season and serve

Remove the bay leaf. Taste and add salt if needed. Spoon the beans over rice and finish with a squeeze of lime and a sprinkle of parsley or green onion.

## Easy Ingredient Swaps

- **No black beans?** Pinto or kidney beans work. The flavor shifts slightly but the method is identical.
- **Dried beans instead of canned:** Soak overnight, then simmer until tender, usually 1 to 1.5 hours, before starting step 2. Use about 1 cup dried beans for every can you're replacing, and add enough of the cooking liquid to keep it saucy.
- **No fresh garlic:** Use 1 teaspoon garlic powder, added with the beans.
- **Vegetarian:** Skip the bacon and add 1/2 teaspoon smoked paprika for depth.
- **Brown rice:** Works well, but allow about 40 minutes of cooking time and start it first.

## Simple Sides That Make It a Meal

Beans and rice are the foundation. Round out the plate with whatever you have:

- **Sautéed greens:** Heat 1 tablespoon oil, add a bunch of kale or collards with a minced garlic clove, and cook 5 minutes until wilted.
- **Fried egg:** A runny yolk over the beans adds richness for pennies.
- **Sliced orange:** Citrus alongside savory beans is a refreshing contrast.
- **Farofa-style crunch:** Toast 1/2 cup breadcrumbs in 1 tablespoon butter until golden, about 3 minutes, and sprinkle on top. It's a quick stand-in for the toasted cassava flour topping often served with Brazilian meals.

## Troubleshooting for Beginners

**Beans taste flat:** Add salt a pinch at a time, then a squeeze of lime. Acid wakes up bland beans.

**Rice is gummy:** You likely skipped rinsing or lifted the lid too often. Next time, rinse well and keep it covered.

**Beans are too salty:** Canned beans vary. Stir in a splash of water and a little extra rice on the plate to balance.

## Storing and Reheating

Cool leftovers within two hours and refrigerate in airtight containers for up to four days. Reheat beans on the stove with a splash of water, stirring until hot. They also freeze well for up to three months, and the flavor often improves overnight.
