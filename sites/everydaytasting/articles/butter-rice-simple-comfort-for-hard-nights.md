Some nights you don't want a recipe. You want something warm, something you can make half-asleep, something that makes a strange kitchen feel a little less strange. Butter rice is that dish, and below you'll find a reliable way to make it in about 25 minutes with ingredients you probably already own.

## The Essay Behind This Recipe

In a personal essay for Vogue titled "First, End Your Marriage. Then, Cook Rice," the author describes her first night in a sublet apartment, separated from her children, her husband, and a home of 24 years. Her description, per the essay: "I cooked and my sister came over."

According to the piece's framing, the cooking was a grounding ritual, with butter rice at the center. The essay is not about drama. That's the practical lesson here. Butter rice asks almost nothing of you, and it works in a rented kitchen with mismatched tools.

## Basic Butter Rice (Serves 2 to 3)

**Time:** about 5 minutes prep, 20 minutes cooking, 5 minutes resting.

**You'll need:**
- 1 cup long-grain white rice (jasmine or basmati both work)
- 2 tablespoons butter
- 1 3/4 cups water (see swaps below if your rice is different)
- 1/2 teaspoon salt

**Equipment:** a medium saucepan with a tight lid. If your lid is missing, a flat plate or a sheet of foil pressed over the rim works.

### Step by step

1. **Rinse the rice.** Put it in a bowl, cover with cold water, swirl, and drain. Repeat two or three times until the water looks mostly clear. This removes surface starch so the grains stay separate.
2. **Melt the butter.** Over medium heat, melt 1 tablespoon of the butter in the saucepan.
3. **Toast the rice.** Add the drained rice and stir for about 1 minute, until it smells slightly nutty. This step is optional but adds flavor.
4. **Add water and salt.** Pour in 1 3/4 cups water and the salt. Stir once and bring to a boil.
5. **Cover and cook low.** As soon as it boils, turn the heat to the lowest setting, cover, and cook for 15 minutes. Don't lift the lid.
6. **Rest.** Take the pot off the heat and leave it covered for 5 minutes.
7. **Finish.** Add the remaining tablespoon of butter, fluff with a fork, and taste for salt.

## Troubleshooting Without Special Gear

Borrowed pots and unfamiliar stoves make rice unpredictable. A few fixes:

- **Gummy rice:** you probably skipped rinsing or used too much water. Next time, rinse more and trim the water by a couple of tablespoons.
- **Crunchy center:** add 2 to 3 tablespoons of hot water, cover, and cook on low for 5 more minutes.
- **Scorched bottom:** your burner runs hot. Move the pot to the smallest burner or use a heat-diffusing trick like a thin pan underneath. Scoop out the unburnt rice from the top and leave the crust behind.
- **No measuring cup:** use any mug as your measure. Use the same mug for rice and water, filling water to a little under 2 mugs for each mug of rice.

## Ingredient Swaps

- **No butter?** Use olive oil or any neutral oil. You'll lose some richness but the dish still works.
- **Salted butter:** reduce the added salt to 1/4 teaspoon and taste at the end.
- **Brown rice:** use 2 cups water per cup of rice and cook for about 40 minutes, then rest 10.
- **Stock instead of water:** chicken or vegetable stock adds depth. Cut the salt and taste first.
- **Leftover rice:** if you cooked too much, cool it quickly and refrigerate within an hour or so for safe next-day fried rice.

## Ways to Turn It Into Dinner

Plain butter rice is a complete comfort on its own, but it's also a base. Try one of these, each adding about 5 minutes:

- **Soft egg on top:** fry an egg in a little butter, set it over the rice, and add a splash of soy sauce.
- **Garlic and pepper:** stir in a minced garlic clove with the butter in step 2, then finish with black pepper.
- **Frozen peas:** stir in a handful during the 5-minute rest. The residual heat cooks them.
- **Grated cheese:** a spoonful of Parmesan melted in at the end makes it feel like a risotto shortcut.
- **Canned fish or beans:** warm a can of tuna, sardines, or chickpeas and spoon over the top for protein.

Rice-and-butter pairings show up in many home kitchens across cultures, from pilafs that start by toasting grains in fat to simple buttered rice served beside stews. Once you're comfortable with the base, those traditions are easy to explore.

## Cooking for One, or for Two

The essay's scene includes a second person: a sister who came over. That detail is worth borrowing. This recipe scales easily either way:

- **For one:** use 1/2 cup rice, 3/4 cup plus 1 tablespoon water, 1 tablespoon butter, and a pinch of salt. Use a smaller pot and cook for the same 15 minutes.
- **For company:** double everything and use a larger pot with a lid. Cooking time may stretch by a few minutes.

You don't need to host or talk. Sometimes the most useful thing anyone can do is show up, sit nearby, and eat what you made.

## Make It Tonight

If your week has been heavy, skip the ambitious plan. Rinse a cup of rice, melt a spoonful of butter, set a timer for 15 minutes, and let the pot do the work. Then sit down and eat it warm.
