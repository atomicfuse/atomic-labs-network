NFL Week 7 has a full slate, with the Chiefs, Packers, Lions, Rams, Raiders, and Seahawks all featured in prime windows, per Fox News. That's a lot of hours on the couch, and nobody wants to spend them stuck at the stove. Here are three snacks that cost little, need basic equipment, and each take 30 minutes or less. Make one, or make all three and call it dinner.

## Plan the Spread Before You Start

The trick to stress-free hosting is picking dishes that use different tools, so nothing competes for the same burner or oven rack.

- **Sheet-pan nachos:** oven only
- **Elote-style corn dip:** one skillet, then a bowl
- **Gochujang meatballs:** one skillet, ideally started while the nachos bake

Start the corn dip first (it's good warm or at room temperature), then the meatballs, then slide the nachos into the oven right before guests settle in. Nachos are the one thing that really can't wait.

## Sheet-Pan Nachos

**Serves:** 4 to 6 | **Time:** 20 minutes (10 prep, 10 bake)

The common nacho mistake is piling everything in one heap, so the chips at the bottom go soggy and naked. Two thin layers fix that.

### What you need

- 10 oz tortilla chips (a standard bag)
- 1 can (15 oz) black beans, drained and rinsed
- 2 cups shredded cheese (about 8 oz), such as a Mexican blend, cheddar, or Monterey Jack
- 1/2 cup pickled jalapeños, optional
- Toppings: salsa, sour cream, chopped cilantro, diced avocado

### How to make it

1. Heat the oven to 425°F. Line a large rimmed baking sheet with parchment or foil.
2. Spread half the chips in a single layer. Scatter on half the beans and half the cheese.
3. Add the remaining chips, beans, and cheese on top. Add jalapeños if using.
4. Bake 8 to 10 minutes, until the cheese is fully melted and the edges of the chips are just turning golden.
5. Top with salsa, sour cream, cilantro, and avocado right before serving.

**Swap it:** No black beans? Pinto beans work the same way. Want it heartier? Stir a cup of leftover shredded chicken or taco-seasoned ground beef into the layers.

## Elote-Style Corn Dip

**Serves:** 6 to 8 as a dip | **Time:** 15 minutes

Elote is Mexican street corn: charred kernels with something creamy, something salty, something sharp, and lime. Turning it into a scoopable dip keeps the flavor and ditches the mess of eating it off the cob.

### What you need

- 1 bag (16 oz) frozen corn, thawed and patted dry
- 1 tablespoon oil
- 1/2 cup mayonnaise
- 1/2 cup sour cream
- 1/2 cup crumbled cotija or feta
- 1 small garlic clove, grated
- 1 teaspoon chili powder, plus extra for sprinkling
- Juice of 1 lime
- 2 tablespoons chopped cilantro

### How to make it

1. Heat the oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat.
2. Add the corn in one layer and leave it alone for 2 to 3 minutes so it browns. Stir and cook another 3 minutes. Some dark spots are exactly what you want.
3. Let the corn cool for 5 minutes. Hot corn will make the dressing runny.
4. Stir together the mayo, sour cream, cheese, garlic, chili powder, and lime juice. Fold in the corn and cilantro.
5. Taste and add salt if needed. Cotija and feta are salty, so you may not need much.

Serve with tortilla chips, or with cut-up cucumber and bell pepper for something lighter. It keeps in the fridge for up to 3 days and tastes good cold.

**Swap it:** Canned corn (drained very well) works in a pinch. Plain Greek yogurt can replace the sour cream.

## Gochujang Meatballs

**Serves:** 4 to 6 | **Time:** 20 minutes

Gochujang is a Korean fermented chili paste, and it's one of the easiest ways to bring big flavor to a weeknight pantry. It tastes savory, a little sweet, and gently spicy. Find it in the international aisle or at an Asian grocery. A tube or tub lasts for months in the fridge.

This recipe uses frozen, pre-cooked meatballs, so there's no rolling or browning.

### What you need

- 1 lb frozen cooked meatballs (about 24)
- 1/4 cup gochujang
- 2 tablespoons honey
- 1 tablespoon soy sauce
- 1 tablespoon rice vinegar (or lime juice)
- 1 teaspoon grated fresh ginger, optional
- 1/4 cup water
- Sliced scallions and sesame seeds for topping

### How to make it

1. In a large skillet, whisk the gochujang, honey, soy sauce, vinegar, ginger, and water.
2. Add the frozen meatballs and bring to a gentle simmer over medium heat.
3. Cover and cook 8 minutes, stirring once or twice, until the meatballs are heated through.
4. Uncover and cook 3 to 4 more minutes, until the sauce thickens enough to coat them.
5. Top with scallions and sesame seeds. Serve with toothpicks, or spoon over rice for a quick dinner.

**Adjust the heat:** Gochujang brands vary. Start with 3 tablespoons if you're cautious, taste the sauce, and add more. A little extra honey calms it down.

## A Simple Game-Day Timeline

If you're serving at kickoff, work backward:

- **40 minutes before:** Char the corn and mix the dip.
- **25 minutes before:** Start the meatballs.
- **15 minutes before:** Assemble the nachos and heat the oven (if it isn't already hot).
- **10 minutes before:** Bake the nachos while you finish the meatballs.
- **At kickoff:** Top the nachos and put everything out.

Put out napkins and a few small plates ahead of time. Guests will graze across several games, so the dip and meatballs can sit out while the nachos disappear first.

## Make One Tonight

If you only have time for a single recipe, start with the corn dip. It takes 15 minutes, uses ingredients you can get anywhere, and works whether you're feeding a crowd or just yourself with a bag of chips.
