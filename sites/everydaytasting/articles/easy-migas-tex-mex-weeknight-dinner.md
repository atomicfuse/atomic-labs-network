Migas is the dinner you make when the fridge looks empty and you still want something that tastes like effort. Eggs get scrambled with crispy tortilla chips, a little onion and chile, and a handful of cheese. By the end of this guide you'll be able to make a pan of it in about 15 minutes for roughly the cost of a fast-food combo, and you'll know how to keep the chips pleasantly crisp-chewy instead of mushy.

Migas is a staple of Tex-Mex cooking. The name means "crumbs" in Spanish, which tells you the basic idea: it's a way to use up leftover tortillas or the sad bottom of a chip bag.

## What You'll Need (Serves 3)

- 6 large eggs
- 2 big handfuls tortilla chips (about 2 ounces, or roughly 1 packed cup once crushed)
- 1 tablespoon neutral oil or butter
- 1/4 cup diced onion (about 1/4 of a small onion)
- 1 jalapeño, seeded and finely diced (optional, but recommended)
- 1 small tomato, diced, or 1/2 cup fresh salsa
- 1/2 cup shredded cheese (about 2 ounces), such as cheddar, Monterey Jack, or a Mexican blend
- 1/4 teaspoon salt, plus more to taste
- Chopped cilantro, hot sauce, and lime wedges for serving

Total time: about 15 minutes. Active time: all of it, but it's mostly stirring.

## How to Make It, Step by Step

### 1. Prep before you turn on the heat (3 minutes)

Dice the onion, jalapeño, and tomato. Crush the chips with your hands into pieces about the size of a quarter. Crack the eggs into a bowl, add the salt, and beat until no streaks of white remain. Eggs cook fast, so having everything ready is the real secret to this dish.

### 2. Soften the vegetables (3 minutes)

Heat the oil in a large nonstick skillet over medium heat. Add the onion and jalapeño and cook, stirring, for about 3 minutes until the onion turns soft and translucent. Add the tomato and cook 1 minute more, just to warm it through. If you're using salsa instead, pour it in and let it bubble for 1 minute so the extra liquid cooks off.

### 3. Add the eggs (2 minutes)

Turn the heat down to medium-low. Pour in the eggs and let them sit for about 15 seconds, then stir gently with a spatula, pushing from the edges to the center. You want soft, large curds. Stop when the eggs look about 80 percent set and still a little glossy.

### 4. Fold in the chips and cheese (1 minute)

Scatter in the chips and stir for 30 to 60 seconds. The leftover heat in the eggs softens the chips just slightly while the edges stay crunchy. Sprinkle the cheese on top, turn off the heat, and cover for 30 seconds so it melts.

### 5. Serve right away

Migas doesn't wait well. The chips keep absorbing moisture, so bring the pan to the table and top with cilantro, a squeeze of lime, and hot sauce.

## Three Ways to Keep It From Getting Soggy

**Use sturdier chips.** Thick, restaurant-style chips hold their texture better than thin, delicate ones. Slightly stale chips are actually ideal.

**Cook off the extra moisture.** Watery tomatoes or loose salsa are the usual cause of soggy migas. Let them simmer a minute before the eggs go in.

**Add the chips last.** Stirring them in at the very end, rather than cooking them with the eggs from the start, is the difference between crisp-chewy and porridge.

## Easy Ingredient Swaps

Migas is forgiving, which makes it great for beginner cooks. Use what you have.

- **No jalapeño?** Use a spoonful of canned green chiles or a pinch of red pepper flakes.
- **No fresh tomato?** Canned diced tomatoes work. Drain them well first.
- **No chips?** Cut 3 corn tortillas into thin strips and fry them in the oil for 2 to 3 minutes until crisp before adding the vegetables. Flour tortillas work too.
- **Dairy-free?** Skip the cheese and add diced avocado at the end.
- **Want more protein?** Stir in 1/2 cup rinsed canned black beans with the tomato, or crumble in a cooked breakfast sausage or leftover chorizo.
- **Vegetable boost:** A handful of baby spinach wilts into the onions in about 30 seconds.

## What to Serve With It

The simplest approach is to warm a few corn or flour tortillas in a dry skillet (about 20 seconds per side) and scoop the migas into them as tacos. Refried beans or a pot of canned black beans seasoned with a little cumin make it a fuller meal. Sliced avocado and a bright, tart salsa balance the richness of the eggs and cheese.

## What It Costs and How to Scale It

A dozen eggs, a bag of chips, an onion, a jalapeño, a tomato, and a small bag of cheese will cover several rounds of migas, and most of those ingredients probably live in your kitchen already. To feed two, use 4 eggs and a generous handful of chips. To feed six, use a 12-inch skillet or cook in two batches, since a crowded pan steams the eggs rather than scrambling them.

## Leftovers and Reheating

Honestly, migas is best eaten fresh. If you do have extra, refrigerate it in an airtight container for up to 2 days and reheat in a skillet over medium-low heat for a couple of minutes. The chips will be softer, but it works well stuffed into a warm tortilla for lunch.

## Your Next Move

Next time you reach the bottom of a chip bag, don't toss the crumbs. Set them aside, crack six eggs, and make migas. Once you've done it once, you'll be able to make it without a recipe.
