By the end of this article, you'll be able to put a skillet of migas on the table in about 20 minutes, using eggs, a few tortillas, and whatever odds and ends are in your fridge. It's a Tex-Mex classic of scrambled eggs, crisp tortilla strips, and melty cheese, and it's one of the most forgiving dinners a beginner cook can make.

## What Migas Actually Are

Migas is a skillet dish built on a simple idea: fry tortilla strips until crisp, then scramble eggs right over them. The tortillas soften slightly where the egg touches them and stay crunchy elsewhere. Add onion, chile, tomato, and cheese, and you have a savory, slightly spicy meal with a lot of texture.

It works for breakfast, but it's even better as a fast dinner. Everything cooks in one pan, and the ingredients are cheap.

## Ingredients (Serves 2 to 3)

- 4 corn tortillas, cut into strips about 1/2 inch wide (or about 2 cups of crushed tortilla chips)
- 2 tablespoons neutral oil, such as vegetable or canola
- 1/2 small onion, diced (about 1/2 cup)
- 1 jalapeño, seeds removed for mild heat, finely diced
- 1 medium tomato, diced (about 3/4 cup)
- 6 large eggs
- 1/2 teaspoon salt, plus more to taste
- 1/2 cup shredded cheese (Monterey Jack, cheddar, or a blend)
- Chopped cilantro, for serving
- Salsa, hot sauce, and lime wedges, for serving

**Total time:** about 20 minutes. **Prep:** 8 minutes. **Cook:** 10 to 12 minutes.

## Step by Step

### 1. Prep before you turn on the heat

Stack the tortillas and slice them into strips. Dice the onion, jalapeño, and tomato. Crack the eggs into a bowl, add the salt, and whisk until no streaks of white remain. Migas cook fast, so everything should be ready before the pan gets hot.

### 2. Crisp the tortilla strips

Heat the oil in a large nonstick or well-seasoned skillet over medium heat for about 1 minute. Add the tortilla strips in a single layer as much as you can. Cook for 3 to 4 minutes, stirring every 30 seconds or so, until the edges are golden and the strips feel stiff. Don't walk away. They go from golden to burnt quickly.

### 3. Soften the vegetables

Add the onion and jalapeño to the pan. Cook for 2 to 3 minutes, stirring, until the onion looks translucent. Add the tomato and cook for 1 more minute, just to take the raw edge off.

### 4. Add the eggs

Lower the heat to medium-low. Pour in the eggs and let them sit for about 20 seconds, then gently fold with a spatula. Keep folding slowly for 2 to 3 minutes. Pull the pan off the heat when the eggs look mostly set but still slightly glossy. They'll finish cooking from the residual heat.

### 5. Cheese and serve

Sprinkle the cheese over the top, cover the pan for 1 minute, or just stir it in. Top with cilantro and serve right away, with lime and salsa on the side. Migas get soggy if they sit, so eat them while the tortillas still have crunch.

## Ingredient Swaps That Work

You don't need a special shopping trip for this one. Try these substitutions:

- **Tortillas:** Stale or leftover flour tortillas work, though they'll be chewier. Plain tortilla chips are the fastest option. Skip the frying step and stir them in just before the eggs, about 2 cups roughly crushed.
- **Jalapeño:** Use a serrano for more heat, half a bell pepper for none, or a spoonful of canned diced green chiles (drained) in a pinch.
- **Fresh tomato:** Swap in 1/3 cup of drained canned diced tomatoes or 1/4 cup of thick salsa.
- **Cheese:** Pepper jack adds heat. Crumbled queso fresco or feta, added at the end, gives a tangy finish.
- **Add protein:** Stir in 1/2 cup of cooked chorizo, diced ham, or black beans along with the vegetables.
- **Dairy-free:** Skip the cheese and add sliced avocado on top.

## Fixes for Common Problems

**Soggy tortillas?** The pan was probably overcrowded or the strips weren't crisp before the eggs went in. Cook them a minute longer next time, and use a bigger skillet.

**Dry, rubbery eggs?** The heat was too high or they stayed on the burner too long. Take the pan off while the eggs still look a little wet.

**Bland?** Salt is usually the culprit. Taste a bite and add a pinch, then finish with lime juice and hot sauce. A squeeze of acid brightens the whole dish.

## What to Serve With Migas

Migas are filling on their own, but a few easy sides turn them into a full meal:

- Warm refried or black beans (a canned one heated in a small pot takes 5 minutes)
- Sliced avocado with a pinch of salt
- Warm flour or corn tortillas, if you want to wrap everything into tacos
- Sliced oranges or a simple cabbage slaw with lime for something fresh

## Make It Your Own

Once you've made migas once, you can change them freely. Some cooks add a handful of spinach, a spoonful of leftover rice, or the last bit of roasted vegetables from earlier in the week. The method stays the same: crisp the tortillas, soften the vegetables, scramble the eggs gently, and serve fast.

Next time you're staring at a fridge with eggs and not much else, pull out a package of tortillas and a skillet. Twenty minutes later, dinner's done.
