When you're settled in for a long evening of screen-watching, whether that's a big game, a movie marathon, or following news coverage like NBC News's hub on the 2026 Texas midterm elections, you want food that cooks itself and feeds a few people without fuss. This beanless Texas-style chili does that. You'll have it on the table in about 75 minutes, with only 15 of those minutes spent actually cooking.

It uses pantry spices, one can of tomatoes, and ground beef, so it's affordable and forgiving. It serves 6 to 8.

## What Makes Chili "Texas-Style"

The classic Texas version is a bowl of red: beef and chile flavor, no beans, and no pasta. The focus is a deep, savory, slightly smoky sauce. Beans aren't banned from your kitchen, but leaving them out lets the meat and spices carry the pot. If you want beans, see the swaps below.

## Ingredients

- 2 lb ground beef (80/20 gives the best flavor)
- 1 large yellow onion, diced
- 4 garlic cloves, minced
- 3 tablespoons chili powder
- 1 tablespoon ground cumin
- 1 teaspoon smoked paprika
- 1 teaspoon dried oregano
- 1 1/2 teaspoons salt, plus more to taste
- 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
- 2 tablespoons tomato paste
- 1 can (14.5 oz) crushed or diced tomatoes
- 1 1/2 cups beef broth (or water)
- 1 tablespoon masa harina, or 1/4 cup finely crushed tortilla chips, to thicken
- 1 tablespoon apple cider vinegar or the juice of 1 lime
- 1 tablespoon oil, only if your beef is very lean

## Method

### 1. Brown the beef (10 minutes)

Heat a large heavy pot or Dutch oven over medium-high heat. Add the beef in an even layer and leave it alone for 3 to 4 minutes so it browns rather than steams. Break it up and cook another 4 to 5 minutes until no pink remains. Don't drain the fat unless there's a lot of it; a couple of tablespoons is flavor.

### 2. Cook the aromatics and spices (5 minutes)

Add the onion and cook 4 minutes until softened. Stir in the garlic, chili powder, cumin, paprika, oregano, salt, and pepper. Cook for 1 minute, stirring constantly. Toasting the spices in the fat wakes them up and removes the raw, dusty taste.

### 3. Add the liquids (2 minutes)

Stir in the tomato paste and cook 1 minute, then add the tomatoes and broth. Scrape up any browned bits from the bottom of the pot. Those bits are concentrated flavor.

### 4. Simmer (45 to 60 minutes)

Bring to a gentle boil, then turn the heat to low and partly cover. Stir every 10 to 15 minutes. If it looks dry, add a splash of broth or water. The chili is ready when the meat is tender and the sauce coats a spoon.

### 5. Thicken and brighten (5 minutes)

Whisk the masa harina with 3 tablespoons of warm water until smooth, then stir it in. Simmer uncovered for 5 minutes. Finish with the vinegar or lime juice, then taste. Chili almost always needs a bit more salt, and the acid makes the flavors pop.

## Ingredient Swaps

- **No chili powder?** Use 2 tablespoons paprika, 1 tablespoon cumin, and 1/2 teaspoon cayenne, adjusting the heat to your taste.
- **Ground turkey or chicken:** Works fine, but add 1 tablespoon of oil and an extra 1/2 teaspoon of salt, since leaner meat has less built-in flavor.
- **Want beans?** Stir in one drained and rinsed 15 oz can of pinto or kidney beans during the last 15 minutes.
- **No masa harina:** Crushed tortilla chips do the same job and add a toasty corn flavor. Cornmeal also works in a pinch, though you should simmer it a little longer.
- **No tomato paste:** Add an extra 1/2 cup of crushed tomatoes and simmer a few minutes longer.
- **Beef broth swap:** Water plus 1 teaspoon of soy sauce adds savory depth.
- **Smoky heat:** Stir in 1 to 2 chopped chipotle peppers in adobo for a deeper, spicier chili.

## How to Serve It

Chili is a great base for a toppings bar, which keeps everyone happy and makes serving easy:

- Shredded cheddar or Monterey Jack
- Sour cream or plain yogurt
- Sliced green onions or diced raw onion
- Pickled jalapeños
- Corn chips for scooping, or a pan of cornbread on the side

For a cheap and filling plate, spoon it over baked potatoes or rice.

## Make-Ahead and Storage

Chili tastes better the next day because the spices have time to settle in. Cool it, then refrigerate in airtight containers for up to 4 days. For longer storage, freeze it for up to 3 months. Reheat gently on the stove with a splash of water, since it thickens as it sits.

## Troubleshooting

- **Too thin:** Simmer uncovered for 10 more minutes, or add another teaspoon of masa slurry.
- **Too thick:** Stir in broth a few tablespoons at a time.
- **Tastes flat:** It almost always needs salt or acid. Add a pinch of one, then a squeeze of lime, and taste again.
- **Too spicy:** Stir in a spoonful of sour cream or a small pinch of sugar, and serve with extra toppings to cool it down.
- **Greasy surface:** Skim it with a spoon, or lay a paper towel briefly on top to blot it.

## The One Thing to Remember

Give the chili its full simmer. The 45 minutes of low, slow cooking turns a plain pot of beef and tomatoes into something rich, so start it early and let the stove do the work while you settle in.
