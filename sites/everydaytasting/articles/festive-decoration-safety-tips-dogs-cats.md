Festive season fills a home with string lights, flickering diyas, fresh flowers, colorful rangoli and extra guests. To a curious dog or cat, that is a brand-new playground. By the end of this piece, you'll have six practical ways to keep the celebration going while keeping your pets out of trouble.

The guidance here comes from a veterinarian-recommended list reported by ABP Live. Its core message is simple: you don't need to skip decorating. You need to decorate thoughtfully.

A quick note on scope: this is a food site, and we're stepping slightly outside the kitchen. But festive cooking and festive decorating happen in the same busy house, often on the same afternoon, so a little pet planning belongs alongside your menu.

## 1. Secure Electrical Wires and Lights

Decorative lights mean cords, and cords look like something to chew, bat or tug. Exposed wires are one of the hazards the ABP Live piece flags for dogs and cats.

Try this as you set up:

- Run cords along walls and tuck them out of paw's reach.
- Hang lights higher than your pet can jump or climb.
- Walk through the room at pet height once you finish. If you can see a dangling wire, they can too.

## 2. Choose Sturdier, Pet-Safe Décor

Fragile ornaments are easy to knock over, and broken pieces on the floor are a risk for paws and mouths. The tip is to pick sturdier decorations, especially anything that will sit low or on a surface your pet can reach.

A simple rule: if it would shatter when it hits the floor, put it up high or leave it for another year. Keep your showpieces on shelves and let durable items fill the lower spaces.

## 3. Keep Diyas and Candles Out of Reach

Open flames and curious animals are a bad mix. A wagging tail or a cat's leap can topple a diya in a second. Place diyas and candles where pets simply cannot get to them, such as high surfaces or rooms they are shut out of.

If your pet tends to explore everything, consider keeping flame decorations in one pet-free area, and never leave them lit with an unsupervised animal nearby. That's our practical take on the vets' advice, not a quote from the list.

## 4. Be Careful With Flowers and Garlands

Flowers and garlands are a festive staple, but the list warns that some flowers can be toxic to cats and dogs. Pets also tend to nibble on garlands that hang or drape within reach.

Before you buy or hang anything:

1. Check whether each flower type is safe for dogs and cats. If you can't confirm it, ask your vet.
2. Hang garlands high, away from jumping and climbing routes.
3. Sweep up fallen petals promptly so they don't become floor snacks.

## 5. Prevent Access to Rangoli Powders and Glitter

Rangoli is made on the floor, which is exactly where pets live. Colored powders and glitter can be a problem if they get licked, sniffed or walked through and then groomed off paws. The tip is to prevent access.

Practical ways to do that:

- Make your rangoli while pets are in another room, and keep them out until it's finished.
- Consider placing it in a spot you can block off with a door or gate.
- Store leftover powder and glitter in closed containers on a high shelf.

## 6. Create a Designated Pet-Friendly Space

Guests, noise and unfamiliar decorations can be stressful for animals. The final tip is to set aside a space just for your pet, a calm corner away from the action.

Make it feel like theirs:

- Put their bed, water and a favorite toy there.
- Keep it free of decorations, flames and powders.
- Let them retreat there whenever the house gets loud.

This also gives you a safe place to park them while you cook, light diyas or greet visitors at the door.

## A Simple Walk-Through Before the Celebration

Before guests arrive, do a ten-minute check at your pet's eye level. Look for loose cords, low ornaments, open flames, flowers within reach and powders on the floor. Fix what you find, then point your pet to their calm corner.

Decorating with pets in mind takes only a little extra planning, and it spares you an emergency vet visit in the middle of the festivities. Before you plug in a single light this season, spend ten minutes at floor level fixing what a curious nose could reach.
