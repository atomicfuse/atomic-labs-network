By the end of this page, you'll have a creamy, tangy French-style chicken dinner on the table in about 30 minutes, using one skillet and ingredients you probably already own. Dijon chicken is a classic of French home cooking: browned chicken, a quick pan sauce, and a spoonful of mustard doing most of the flavor work.

**Time:** 10 minutes prep, 20 minutes cooking. **Serves:** 4.

## What You'll Need

- 1½ lb (680 g) boneless, skinless chicken thighs
- ½ teaspoon salt, plus more to taste
- ¼ teaspoon black pepper
- 1 tablespoon neutral oil or olive oil
- 1 shallot or ½ small onion, finely chopped
- 2 garlic cloves, minced
- ½ cup (120 ml) dry white wine, or use chicken broth
- ½ cup (120 ml) chicken broth
- 2 tablespoons Dijon mustard
- ⅓ cup (80 ml) heavy cream or crème fraîche
- 1 teaspoon dried thyme (or 1 tablespoon fresh leaves)

A 12-inch skillet is ideal. Anything smaller and the chicken steams instead of browns.

## Step-by-Step Method

### 1. Season and dry the chicken (3 minutes)

Pat the thighs dry with paper towels. Dry meat browns; wet meat turns gray. Sprinkle both sides with the salt and pepper.

### 2. Brown the chicken (10-12 minutes)

Heat the oil in the skillet over medium-high heat until it shimmers. Add the chicken in a single layer and leave it alone for 5 to 6 minutes, until the underside is deeply golden and releases easily. Flip and cook another 5 to 6 minutes. The thickest piece should read 165°F (74°C) on an instant-read thermometer. Move the chicken to a plate.

### 3. Build the sauce (5 minutes)

Reduce the heat to medium. Add the shallot and a pinch of salt and cook 2 minutes, stirring, until soft. Add the garlic and thyme for 30 seconds.

Pour in the wine and scrape up the browned bits from the bottom of the pan. Those bits are concentrated flavor. Let the wine bubble for about 2 minutes until it has reduced by half, then add the broth and simmer 2 more minutes.

### 4. Finish with mustard and cream (2 minutes)

Lower the heat. Whisk in the Dijon and the cream. Simmer gently for 1 to 2 minutes until the sauce coats the back of a spoon. Return the chicken and any juices to the pan and spoon sauce over the top. Taste and add salt if needed.

## Easy Ingredient Swaps

This dish is forgiving, which makes it a good one for beginners.

- **No wine?** Use all broth (1 cup total) plus 1 teaspoon white wine vinegar or lemon juice for brightness.
- **No cream?** Use 3 tablespoons full-fat plain Greek yogurt. Take the pan off the heat before stirring it in, or it can curdle.
- **No Dijon?** Smooth French-style mustard is best. In a pinch, use 1 tablespoon yellow mustard plus 1 teaspoon honey, and taste before adding more.
- **Chicken breasts instead of thighs?** Slice them in half horizontally so they cook evenly, and reduce browning time to about 4 minutes per side.
- **Want more vegetables?** Add 8 oz (225 g) sliced mushrooms after you remove the chicken. Cook them 5 minutes before adding the shallot.

## What to Serve It With

The sauce is the star, so pair it with something that catches it:

- Boiled or mashed potatoes
- Buttered egg noodles (cook 8 to 10 minutes while the chicken browns)
- Crusty bread
- Green beans or a simple salad with lemon and olive oil

## Why Mustard Works So Well

Dijon brings sharpness, a little salt, and acidity, which cuts through rich cream. It also helps thicken the sauce slightly, so you don't need flour or cornstarch. That's the flavor lesson worth remembering: when a creamy sauce tastes flat, a spoonful of something tangy usually fixes it.

## Storage and Leftovers

Refrigerate leftovers in an airtight container for up to 3 days. Reheat gently over low heat with a splash of broth or water to loosen the sauce. Avoid a rolling boil, which can cause cream sauces to separate.

## Make It Your Own

Once you've made it once, try stirring in a tablespoon of whole-grain mustard for texture, a handful of spinach at the end, or a squeeze of lemon. Next time you open the fridge and see only chicken, mustard, and a bit of cream, you already have dinner.
