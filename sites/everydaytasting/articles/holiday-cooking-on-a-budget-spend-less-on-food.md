Affordability was a big word this election season, and in his Nov. 23 column for Civicmedia, Iowa Politics host Jeff Stein points to an interesting tension. Households say prices are too high, yet Stein cites Thanksgiving travel, Halloween spending, and Christmas retail forecasts as signs that people are still spending on the things they care about. His takeaway, as the column frames it: we make our choices.

That idea is useful in the kitchen, too. You probably don't want to skip the holiday table. You can, though, choose where the food money goes. Below are five practical ways to keep the traditions and trim the grocery bill, plus one cheap, easy dish you can make in about 30 minutes.

## Decide What Matters Most, Then Save on the Rest

Pick one or two dishes that are the heart of your gathering. Maybe it's the main roast, or maybe it's grandma's pie. Spend normally on those. For everything else, go simple.

This is the same logic Stein describes: people make tradeoffs, putting money toward priorities. Try it with a quick exercise. Write down your menu, circle the two dishes people would truly miss, and look for cheaper versions of the rest.

## Use Ingredient Swaps That Nobody Will Notice

Some swaps cut cost with almost no change in flavor:

- **Frozen vegetables for fresh.** Green beans, peas, and corn work well in casseroles and sides, where they get cooked anyway.
- **Canned pumpkin for a fresh pumpkin.** It saves prep time and works well in pie, soup, and muffins.
- **Dried beans for canned.** Dried beans cost less per serving, though they need soaking overnight or a longer simmer.
- **Yogurt for sour cream.** Plain yogurt works in dips and as a topping, and often costs less.
- **Store-brand staples.** Flour, sugar, broth, and butter are usually interchangeable with name brands in baking and cooking.

## Build a Meal From Pantry Staples

Before you shop, check what you already have. Rice, pasta, canned tomatoes, beans, onions, and spices can anchor many meals. Cooking from the pantry in the days around a big holiday meal frees up money for the feast itself.

### Easy Spiced Chickpea and Tomato Stew

This dish is inspired by North African and Middle Eastern flavors, and it uses cheap pantry ingredients. It serves 4 and takes about 30 minutes.

**You'll need:**

- 2 tablespoons olive oil
- 1 onion, chopped
- 3 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 teaspoon ground cumin
- 1 teaspoon paprika
- 1/2 teaspoon ground cinnamon (optional, but it adds a warm note)
- One 14.5-ounce can diced tomatoes
- Two 15-ounce cans chickpeas, drained and rinsed
- 1 cup broth or water
- Salt and pepper to taste
- Rice, bread, or couscous for serving

**Steps:**

1. Heat the oil in a pot over medium heat. Add the onion and cook 5 minutes, until soft.
2. Stir in the garlic, cumin, paprika, and cinnamon. Cook 1 minute, until fragrant.
3. Add the tomatoes, chickpeas, and broth. Bring to a simmer.
4. Cook 15 minutes, uncovered, stirring now and then, until slightly thickened.
5. Season with salt and pepper. Serve over rice or with bread.

**Swap ideas:** Add a handful of spinach at the end, or stir in a spoonful of yogurt before serving.

## Stretch Your Leftovers

Holiday leftovers are free ingredients if you plan for them. A few easy ideas:

- **Turkey or chicken:** Shred it into soup, tacos, or fried rice.
- **Mashed potatoes:** Mix with an egg and a little flour, shape into patties, and pan-fry for 3 to 4 minutes per side.
- **Stale bread:** Cube it, toss with oil, and bake at 375°F for about 10 minutes for croutons.
- **Bones and vegetable scraps:** Simmer them in water for an hour or more to make a basic broth.

Store leftovers in the fridge promptly and plan to use them within a few days, or freeze them.

## Cook Once, Eat Twice

Double a recipe when you can. The chickpea stew above freezes well, so make a full batch and save half for a busy night. Having a ready meal on hand keeps you from ordering takeout when the holiday calendar fills up.

## Make Your Own Choices

Stein's column argues that people make choices even when prices bite. You can apply that to your own kitchen. Keep the dishes that matter, swap where it's painless, and let your pantry do some of the work. This week, pick one swap from the list above and try it.
