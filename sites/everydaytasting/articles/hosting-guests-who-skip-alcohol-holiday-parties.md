One question tends to creep into holiday parties right alongside the appetizers: "Why aren't you drinking?" Etiquette expert Lisa Burdette told Fox News Digital that asking it is very impolite. This guide gives you what to say if you're the one declining, and how to set up a table where nobody has to explain anything.

## Why the Question Misses the Mark

People skip alcohol for plenty of reasons: health, religion, personal choice, wellness goals. Per the Fox News Digital report, those reasons are part of a broader shift in alcohol norms, with sober-curious culture and changing dining habits making abstaining more common.

The issue with asking "why" is that it puts someone on the spot. They either share something private or invent an answer. Neither is a fun way to start a party. The source article's core message is that connection matters more than alcohol, and a guest's drink order is not an invitation to investigate.

## If You're Not Drinking: Simple Ways to Respond

The report shares a graceful way to deflect the question, and the general idea is easy to borrow: keep it short, warm, and forward-moving. These are my own suggested scripts, not quotes from the expert:

- "I'm all set for now, but that smells amazing. What's in it?"
- "I'm sticking with this tonight. Have you tried the dip?"
- "Not tonight, thanks. How was your trip?"

The pattern is the same each time: a brief, friendly no, then a pivot to food or the person you're talking to. You don't owe an explanation, and a cheerful redirect usually ends the topic.

### Hold a Drink Anyway

If you'd rather not field questions at all, grab a glass of something you like right away. Sparkling water with a lime wedge or a mug of warm spiced cider tends to stop the questions before they start. This is a practical tip, not a rule.

## If You're Hosting: Make the Nonalcoholic Table Real

The easiest way to avoid awkward moments is to make the nonalcoholic options as appealing as the rest. Think of it as one more dish on the menu, not an afterthought of leftover soda.

### A Two-Minute Spiced Cider Pitcher

This is a flexible starting point, and you can scale it up for a crowd.

1. Pour 8 cups of apple cider into a large pot.
2. Add 3 cinnamon sticks, 4 whole cloves, and 2 strips of orange peel.
3. Warm over low heat for about 15 minutes. Don't let it boil hard.
4. Ladle into mugs and add an orange slice.

Total time is around 20 minutes, mostly hands-off. Keep it on the lowest heat setting so guests can serve themselves.

### A Sparkling Option for Toasts

Set out a bucket of ice with sparkling water or a nonalcoholic sparkling juice, plus a bowl of cranberries, rosemary sprigs, and citrus slices. Guests can build a festive glass in under a minute. Put it where the other drinks are, not hidden in the kitchen, so choosing it feels as normal as choosing wine.

### Label Things

A small card on each pitcher saying what's in it helps everyone. It also saves guests from asking whether a drink contains alcohol, which is another question that can feel awkward in a crowd.

## Toasting Without Alcohol

A toast is about the moment, not what's in the glass. If you're hosting, raise whatever you have and make the toast to the people in the room, the food, or the year. If you're a guest, simply lift your glass and join in. No one needs to inspect what's in it.

## Ask About the Food Instead

If you're tempted to ask a question when someone passes on a drink, redirect your curiosity to something safer. "Did you make this?" or "What's your favorite thing on the table?" opens a conversation without anyone feeling cornered.

## The Takeaway

This season, drop the "why" and put a good nonalcoholic drink on the table. Then, when someone says no thanks to a glass, simply say "Great, can I get you something else?" and move on to the food.
