Country singer and author Jessie James Decker has a new cookbook, "The Secret Sauce," and the idea behind it is one any home cook can use tonight: the emotions you bring into the kitchen shape the food you make. Per Fox News, which reported on her cooking lesson, the book treats food as a reflection of care, mood, and connection.

That's the whole premise, and it's worth taking seriously, because it changes how you approach a weeknight dinner. Below are practical ways to apply it. These are my own suggestions built on that idea, not Decker's instructions or recipes.

## Start With a Check-In Before You Touch a Knife

Per Fox News, Decker's philosophy is that cooking is shaped by the feelings people carry into the kitchen. You can turn that into a habit that takes about 30 seconds.

Before you start, ask yourself: am I rushed, tired, stressed, or relaxed? You don't need to fix the answer. You just need to know it, because it tells you how to plan the meal.

- **Rushed or frazzled:** Pick a dish with few steps and forgiving timing, like a sheet-pan dinner or a one-pot pasta.
- **Tired:** Lean on pantry staples such as canned beans, rice, eggs, or frozen vegetables.
- **Calm and curious:** This is your window to try something new, like a spice blend you've never used.

## Match the Recipe to Your Energy

A lot of kitchen frustration comes from choosing a 90-minute recipe on a 20-minute night. Cooking mistakes often teach the best habits, and this is a common one: ambition that doesn't fit your mood.

A simple rule of thumb: on busy nights, choose recipes with five or fewer main ingredients and a single pan. Save layered, multi-step dishes for weekends when you actually want to stand at the stove.

## Try a Low-Stress Dinner You Can Make in 20 Minutes

Here's a flexible fried rice that works on tired nights and uses what you already have. It serves two to three.

**You'll need:**
- 3 cups cooked, cold rice (day-old is best)
- 2 tablespoons neutral oil
- 2 eggs, beaten
- 1 to 1½ cups frozen peas and carrots, or any chopped vegetables
- 2 cloves garlic, minced
- 2 to 3 tablespoons soy sauce
- 1 teaspoon sesame oil (optional)
- 2 sliced scallions

**Steps:**
1. Heat 1 tablespoon oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Add the eggs, scramble for about 1 minute, and move them to a plate.
2. Add the remaining oil, then the vegetables. Cook 3 to 4 minutes until hot.
3. Add the garlic and stir for 30 seconds.
4. Add the rice, breaking up clumps. Cook 4 to 5 minutes, stirring often, until it starts to look slightly toasted.
5. Stir in the soy sauce, eggs, and sesame oil. Top with scallions.

**Ingredient swaps:** Use leftover chicken, ham, or tofu for protein. Swap soy sauce for tamari if you need gluten-free. No scallions? A pinch of black pepper does fine.

This is the kind of dish where a calm, unhurried pace helps: the rice needs a few minutes of undisturbed contact with the pan to crisp up.

## Treat Family Recipes as Stories

Decker's book, per Fox News, centers on family-focused food. You can borrow that approach without a cookbook deal. Pick one dish your family already loves and add a small ritual to it.

- Write the recipe on an index card with exact quantities, since many family recipes live only in someone's head.
- Add one line about who made it first or when you eat it.
- Let a kid or a guest handle one step, such as stirring, tearing herbs, or setting the table.

Those little additions are what turn a meal into something people remember.

## Slow Down on Purpose, Even on Busy Nights

Cooking with intention doesn't require extra time. It means choosing one step to do carefully. Taste as you go. Season in small pinches. Let onions soften for the full 5 to 7 minutes instead of rushing them. A single deliberate habit tends to make the whole meal feel better.

## Know When to Walk Away

If you're truly frayed, it's fine to simplify. Scrambled eggs on toast with a handful of greens is a real dinner. The takeaway from Decker's philosophy isn't that every meal must be an act of love and ceremony. It's that your state of mind is an ingredient, so notice it and cook accordingly.

## Your Next Step

Tonight, take 30 seconds before you cook and name your mood. Then pick a recipe that fits it, not the one you wish you had energy for.
