Cumin in a fizzy drink sounds like a dare. In India, it is a growing business. Per the BBC, cumin-flavored sodas such as Lahori Zeera, Campa Jeera and Jeeru are winning a bigger share of the soft drink market as demand for homegrown flavors rises. By the end of this article, you will know why these drinks are catching on, what they taste like in concept, and how to start exploring the flavor in your own kitchen.

## What is happening: cumin soda versus the cola giants

The BBC reports that these so-called "ethnic beverages" are challenging Coca-Cola and Pepsi in the mass-market soft drinks segment. Its reporting points to three forces behind the shift: nostalgic Indian flavors, low pricing and wider distribution.

The broader backdrop, per the same report, is a fast-expanding non-alcoholic beverages market in India. Online demand for cumin sodas has surged, and local brands are gaining ground as consumers lean toward flavors that feel familiar, authentic or healthier.

I can't tell you from the reporting exactly how big each brand's share is, so treat any precise number you see elsewhere with care. The direction of travel is the story.

## Why a cumin soda works as a flavor

If cumin only lives in your spice rack next to the chili powder, the idea may feel odd. Think of what jeera does in savory cooking: it is earthy, warm and slightly citrusy. Add salt, a little sweetness and bubbles, and you get something closer to a savory-leaning refresher than a sugary cola.

That is my take, not the BBC's, but it explains the pull. These drinks tap into nostalgia, a flavor many people grew up with in home-style or street-style drinks, now sold in a bottle.

## Try the idea at home: a quick jeera fizz

This is a simple home riff on the flavor, not a copy of any brand's recipe. It takes about 10 minutes, mostly toasting and steeping.

### What you need (makes 2 glasses)

- 1 teaspoon whole cumin seeds
- 2 tablespoons lemon or lime juice
- 1 to 2 tablespoons sugar or honey, to taste
- A pinch of salt (about 1/8 teaspoon)
- 1 1/2 cups cold sparkling water
- Ice
- Fresh mint leaves, optional

### Steps

1. Toast the cumin seeds in a dry pan over medium heat for 1 to 2 minutes, until fragrant. Don't let them darken.
2. Crush them lightly with a mortar and pestle, or the back of a spoon on a cutting board.
3. Stir the crushed cumin, sugar and salt into 2 tablespoons of hot water. Steep 5 minutes, then stir in the citrus juice.
4. Divide the syrup between two glasses over ice.
5. Top each with sparkling water and stir gently. Add mint if you have it.

Taste and adjust. More lemon brightens it, more sugar softens the earthiness, and extra salt makes it more savory.

## Ingredient swaps and pantry shortcuts

- **No whole cumin?** Use 1/2 teaspoon ground cumin, toasted briefly in the pan. Strain if you dislike grit.
- **No sparkling water?** Plain cold water makes a still version, closer to a quick spiced lemonade.
- **Want it sweeter and more familiar?** Start with a little extra sugar, then dial it back on your second glass.
- **Want more depth?** A small pinch of black pepper is a common companion to cumin in savory cooking, and it works here in tiny amounts.

## Why this story is bigger than one drink

The BBC frames the rise of cumin soda as part of a wider consumer move toward homegrown, culturally familiar flavors. For home cooks, that is an invitation. A spice you already own can show up somewhere unexpected, and the result can taste both new and oddly familiar.

You can also see why low pricing matters in this story. A drink that is cheap, local in flavor and easy to find has an advantage, according to the BBC's reporting, over a global brand that feels the same everywhere.

## How to taste it if you find it

If you spot a jeera soda at an Indian grocery or specialty shop, chill it well and try it plain first. Then try it with something salty or fried, such as samosas or chips, where the earthy, tangy flavor has something to play against. Brand availability varies by region, so check what your local store carries.
