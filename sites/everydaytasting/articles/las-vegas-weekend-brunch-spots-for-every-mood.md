Las Vegas weekend brunch is no longer just eggs and a mimosa. Per Las Vegas Weekly, four spots at Resorts World, STK, Play Playground and South Point show how far it has stretched: poolside Pilates, live DJs, 20-plus games and a prime rib buffet. This guide matches each one to the kind of morning you want, so you can book once and not regret it.

One note before we start: the original roundup was sponsored, and it doesn't list prices, hours or reservation details. Check each venue's own site before you plan around cost or timing.

## If You Want a Wellness Reset: Resorts World

Resorts World pairs movement with a meal. According to the roundup, you can take Poolside Pulse Pilates at AYU Dayclub and follow it with brunch at FUHU.

That's the one to pick if you want to feel a little virtuous before you eat. Exercise first, then food, makes the meal feel earned and gives the morning a clear shape.

Resorts World also offers a Latin-inspired brunch at ¡VIVA!. If your group is split between the Pilates crowd and people who only want to eat, that gives you a second option under the same roof.

### How to use it

- Choose the Pilates and FUHU route if you want a structured, active morning.
- Choose ¡VIVA! if the flavors matter more than the workout. Latin-inspired brunch is a good chance to try dishes outside the usual egg-and-pancake lineup.
- Confirm class times and booking requirements directly, since the roundup doesn't specify them.

## If You Want a Party Brunch: STK Las Vegas

STK's All You Can Brunch combines chef-driven bites, cocktails and live DJs, per Las Vegas Weekly. This is brunch as a social event, not a quiet meal.

It suits a group celebrating something, or anyone who treats brunch as the opening act of a bigger day. The all-you-can format means you can keep sampling instead of committing to one plate.

### A simple plan

- Go with friends who like energy and music.
- Pace yourself. With cocktails and unlimited bites, the second hour matters more than the first.
- Eat something substantial early, then sample lighter items as the music picks up.

## If You Want Fun for a Mixed Group: Play Playground Las Vegas

Play Playground offers a brunch menu, bottomless mimosas and Bloody Marys, and more than 20 interactive games, according to the roundup.

The games are the draw. They give a group something to do between rounds of food, which helps when people have different energy levels or the conversation needs a boost. The roundup doesn't say anything about age policies or family suitability, so if you're bringing kids, call ahead and ask.

### Bottomless tips

- Decide on your drink pace before the first round arrives.
- Pick one Bloody Mary and one mimosa phase rather than mixing endlessly.
- Ask about any time limits on the bottomless offer. The roundup doesn't mention them.

## If You Want a Classic Feast: South Point Garden Buffet

South Point's Garden Buffet offers a prime rib and Champagne brunch with global buffet stations, per Las Vegas Weekly.

This is the one for big appetites and groups who can't agree on what to eat. Global stations mean everyone finds something, and prime rib gives the meal a centerpiece.

### Buffet strategy for beginners

- Do a lap before you grab a plate, so you know what's worth the space.
- Start small. Take a few bites from several stations, then go back for favorites.
- Save room for the prime rib if that's why you came.

## Which One Should You Book?

Match the spot to your mood, not the hype:

- **Wellness reset:** Resorts World, with Pilates at AYU Dayclub and brunch at FUHU.
- **Party energy:** STK Las Vegas, for chef-driven bites, cocktails and DJs.
- **Games and bottomless drinks:** Play Playground Las Vegas.
- **Big, traditional feast:** South Point Garden Buffet, for prime rib and Champagne.

If you're trying new flavors, the Latin-inspired brunch at ¡VIVA! and the global stations at South Point are the most likely to take you beyond standard brunch fare.

## Bring the Idea Home

You don't need a Strip resort to borrow the format. A home version takes about the same planning: pick one theme, then build around it. A stretch or walk first, a pitcher of mimosas, or a small buffet of a few dishes from different cuisines all work.

The real takeaway: choose your brunch by the kind of morning you want, then confirm the details with the venue before you go.
