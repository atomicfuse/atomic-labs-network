## Why New Haven Is Worth a Trip

Per Fox News, food tourism is booming as travelers look for hyper-local experiences, and New Haven's pizza reputation is increasingly a reason to visit on its own. The city is turning its culinary identity into a broader tourism draw.

That's the useful frame for you: you're not visiting a city that happens to have good pizza. You're visiting a place where pizza is the local tradition, with people like Kaplan whose job is to explain it. Plan the trip around eating, not around sightseeing with a pizza stop squeezed in.

## Step 1: Hire a Guide or Learn From One

Kaplan's role is the clearest takeaway. A pizza historian can tell you what you're tasting and why it matters, which turns a meal into a lesson you remember.

Before you go:

- Search for guided pizza tours in New Haven and check current availability and pricing directly with the operator.
- If you'd rather go solo, read up on the city's pizza history first so you know what to look for at each stop.
- Write down two or three questions to ask staff, such as what the shop is known for and how long it has been around.

## Step 2: Build a Crawl You Can Actually Finish

Pizza is filling, so the biggest crawl mistake is overcommitting. These are general pacing rules, not Kaplan's:

1. **Pick two or three pizzerias, not six.** Spacing them out beats rushing through.
2. **Share one pie per stop.** With a group of two to four, a single pizza is plenty for each location.
3. **Order the shop's signature first.** Ask what the place is famous for and start there before trying toppings.
4. **Walk between stops** when distances allow, and drink water.
5. **Take notes on your phone.** A line or two on crust, sauce, and char helps you compare later.

A realistic crawl runs three to four hours with a break between stops. Adjust if your group likes to linger.

## Step 3: Taste Like a Historian

You don't need expertise to compare pizzas well. At each stop, look at the same few things:

### Crust
Is it crisp, chewy, or both? Notice the underside and the edges. Compare how each shop handles them.

### Sauce and Cheese
Is the sauce bright or mellow, sparse or generous? Does the cheese cover the pie or play a supporting role?

### Overall Balance
Ask yourself which element you'd remember tomorrow. That's often the shop's identity.

By the third stop, differences that seemed subtle at the first will be obvious.

## Bring the Crawl Home

Can't travel yet? You can still run a mini pizza crawl at home, and it's a good way to practice cooking basics.

### A Simple Home Pizza Night

This is a general beginner approach, not a New Haven recipe:

- **Prep time:** about 20 minutes with store-bought dough, plus 30 minutes to let the dough rest at room temperature.
- **Oven:** heat it as high as it goes, usually 475 to 500°F, with a baking sheet or pizza stone inside for 30 minutes.
- **Toppings:** keep it simple. Crushed canned tomatoes with a pinch of salt, a little cheese, and olive oil.
- **Bake:** roughly 8 to 12 minutes, until the crust is browned and the cheese bubbles.

Make two pies with different sauce and cheese amounts, then taste them side by side the way you would on a crawl. You'll learn quickly what you like.

### Easy Swaps

- No pizza stone? Flip a baking sheet upside down and preheat it.
- No fresh dough? Many grocery stores sell it in the refrigerated or bakery section.
- Want less cleanup? Use parchment paper under the dough.

## The Takeaway

New Haven shows that a city's food identity can be a destination in itself. Pick one pizza tradition near you or far away, find someone who knows its story, and eat your way through it slowly, comparing as you go.
