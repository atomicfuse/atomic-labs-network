Every holiday season, New York City's toughest reservations turn into a kind of sport. Elle recently rounded up 12 of the hardest tables to land this time of year, and the list is a tour of the city's most coveted dining: The Polo Bar, The Jazz Club at Aman New York, The Corner Store, Thanksgiving Brunch at Per Se, Thanksgiving Dinner at The Nines, 4 Charles Prime Rib, L'Artusi, Rolf's, Christmas Eve Dinner at Chez Fifi, Christmas Day Dinner at Galo, Pebble Bar and L'Avenue at Saks.

If you didn't get one of those tables, or never planned to chase them, here's the payoff: you can borrow what makes these meals feel special and recreate it in your own kitchen, without a wait list. Below is a practical plan for a holiday dinner at home that feels like an occasion.

## What These Restaurants Have in Common

The listings span very different rooms, from an iconic Midtown-style dining room to downtown hotspots and one-night-only holiday menus. Per Elle, the appeal is partly the food and partly the occasion: reservations here double as social currency in the city's busiest season.

That's useful to you, because the occasion is the part you can copy. A few things any of these meals deliver, and any home cook can too:

- **A clear start and finish.** A set time, a set menu, a sense of ceremony.
- **One showpiece dish.** The thing everyone remembers.
- **A room that feels different.** Lighting, music, a set table.

## Step 1: Pick One Showpiece, Not Five

The biggest beginner mistake is making an ambitious menu across every course. Restaurants with famous dishes tend to be known for one or two things. Do the same.

Pick a single centerpiece you can make with confidence, then keep everything else simple:

- **A roast.** Rubbing a roast with salt, pepper and garlic and cooking it until a thermometer reads your preferred doneness is far less work than it looks. Prime rib is a classic steakhouse-style splurge if you want to echo that kind of menu.
- **A big pot of pasta.** Italian-American dining rooms earn their crowds with simple things done well. A pound of dried pasta feeds four to six, and a sauce can simmer while you set the table.
- **A comforting brunch spread.** If a holiday brunch is the dream, eggs, something baked and something fresh make a satisfying table.

Choose the dish first, then build around it.

## Step 2: Make a Realistic Timeline

A calm host is the real luxury. Work backward from the time you want to sit down.

- **Two days ahead:** Shop, and make anything that improves with time, like a sauce or dessert.
- **The day before:** Set the table, chill drinks, wash and chop vegetables.
- **Four hours before:** Season the main dish and bring it out of the fridge if it needs to come to room temperature.
- **One hour before:** Start sides, light candles, and put on music.
- **At serving time:** Rest the main dish for about 15 minutes while you finish plating.

Cooking times vary by recipe and oven, so check your own recipe and rely on a thermometer rather than the clock alone.

## Step 3: Cheap Swaps That Still Feel Fancy

Holiday restaurant menus can be pricey. You can get the same feeling on a budget:

- **Roast chicken instead of a prime cut.** A whole chicken with lemon and herbs reads as celebratory and costs far less.
- **Butter and herbs for sauce.** Melt butter, add chopped parsley and a squeeze of lemon, and spoon it over anything.
- **Frozen peas or green beans, dressed up.** Toss with a little butter, salt and lemon zest.
- **Sparkling water with citrus** for a festive non-alcoholic option.

## Step 4: Borrow the Atmosphere

Part of what draws people to places like a jazz club or a hotel dining room is the mood. You can fake that convincingly.

- Dim the overhead lights and use lamps or candles.
- Play a jazz playlist at low volume.
- Use cloth napkins and real glasses, even if everything else is casual.
- Put out a small bowl of something to snack on as guests arrive.

It costs almost nothing and changes how the meal feels.

## If You Still Want a Table

The list from Elle is a good starting point if you'd like to try for a real reservation. Several entries are tied to specific occasions, such as Thanksgiving Brunch at Per Se, Thanksgiving Dinner at The Nines, Christmas Eve Dinner at Chez Fifi and Christmas Day Dinner at Galo. Those are likely to be limited, so check each restaurant's own booking page for dates, availability and pricing, since details can change.

A good backup strategy is to book one outing and plan one night at home. If the reservation falls through, you already have a showpiece dish in mind.

## Your Move

Pick the one dish you'd be proud to put in the middle of the table, and put it on your calendar today. A great holiday meal is mostly a plan, and you can make that yourself.
