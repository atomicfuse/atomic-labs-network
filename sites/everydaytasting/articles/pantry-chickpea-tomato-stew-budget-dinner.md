## What You Need

This is a loose, beginner-friendly take on chana masala, a chickpea dish popular across northern India. It is not a strict traditional version, but it hits the same notes: warm spice, tangy tomato, and creamy chickpeas.

- 2 tablespoons neutral oil (vegetable, canola, or olive)
- 1 large onion, diced
- 3 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 tablespoon fresh grated ginger (or 1 teaspoon ground ginger)
- 2 teaspoons curry powder or garam masala
- 1 teaspoon ground cumin
- ½ teaspoon ground turmeric
- 1 can (14 to 15 oz) diced tomatoes, with their juice
- 2 cans (15 oz each) chickpeas, drained and rinsed
- 1 cup water or vegetable broth
- ½ teaspoon salt, plus more to taste
- 1 tablespoon lemon juice (or a splash of vinegar)
- Optional: 2 big handfuls of spinach
- To serve: cooked rice, flatbread, or toast

**Time:** about 10 minutes of prep, 20 minutes of cooking.

## How to Make It

### 1. Soften the onion (5 to 7 minutes)

Heat the oil in a large pot or deep skillet over medium heat. Add the onion and a pinch of salt. Stir now and then until it turns soft and golden at the edges. Don't rush this step; the onion is the flavor base.

### 2. Bloom the aromatics and spices (1 to 2 minutes)

Add the garlic and ginger. Stir for 30 seconds, until fragrant. Add the curry powder, cumin, and turmeric and stir for another 30 seconds. Toasting spices in oil wakes them up and removes the raw, dusty taste. If things start to stick, add a splash of water.

### 3. Add tomatoes and chickpeas (10 to 15 minutes)

Pour in the diced tomatoes, chickpeas, water, and ½ teaspoon salt. Scrape up any browned bits from the bottom. Bring to a gentle bubble, then lower the heat and simmer uncovered for 12 to 15 minutes, stirring occasionally.

For a creamier texture, press a few chickpeas against the side of the pot with your spoon. The starch thickens the sauce without any cream.

### 4. Finish with brightness

Stir in the lemon juice, and the spinach if you're using it. It wilts in about a minute. Taste, then adjust: more salt if it tastes flat, more lemon if it tastes heavy. Serve over rice or with warm bread.

## Swaps That Save a Trip to the Store

Strict recipes are the enemy of budget cooking. Use what you already have:

- **No fresh ginger or garlic?** Use ½ teaspoon each of ground ginger and garlic powder, added with the other spices.
- **No curry powder?** Use 1 teaspoon each of cumin and paprika, plus a pinch of cayenne or black pepper.
- **No canned chickpeas?** White beans, kidney beans, or lentils work. Red lentils (¾ cup, rinsed) cook right into the sauce in about 15 minutes; add an extra ½ cup of water.
- **No diced tomatoes?** Use 2 tablespoons tomato paste plus 1½ cups water, simmered a few minutes longer.
- **No lemon?** A teaspoon of vinegar or a spoonful of plain yogurt on top adds the same tang.
- **Want more protein?** Stir in a handful of frozen peas, or top each bowl with a fried or soft-boiled egg.

## Make It Stretch

This stew improves overnight as the spices settle in, which makes it ideal for planning ahead.

- **Fridge:** keeps well in an airtight container for up to 4 days.
- **Freezer:** freeze in single-serving portions for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge, then reheat with a splash of water.
- **Second-day ideas:** spoon leftovers into a warm pita or tortilla with a little yogurt, or thin it with broth for a quick soup.

## Why Pantry Cooking Pays Off

Canned beans, canned tomatoes, onions, and a small collection of spices are the backbone of cooking in many parts of the world, from Indian curries to Mediterranean stews to Latin American bean dishes. Learn the pattern once (onion, spices, something canned, something acidic) and you can make dozens of variations without a new recipe.

Start with the spice jars you already own, add one can of beans and one can of tomatoes to your next grocery list, and cook this stew once this week. By the second time, you won't need the recipe at all.
