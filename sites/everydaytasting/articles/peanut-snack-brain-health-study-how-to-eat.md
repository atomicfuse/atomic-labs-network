A small new study suggests the humble peanut may do more than satisfy a snack craving. Per Fox News, researchers at Maastricht University found that healthy older adults who ate 60 grams of unsalted, skin-roasted peanuts every day for 16 weeks showed improved insulin responsiveness in memory-related brain regions. They also showed a slight increase in brain blood flow.

By the end of this article you'll know what that result does and doesn't mean, and you'll have a few cheap, simple ways to work a daily handful of peanuts into your meals.

## What the Study Actually Found

The details, as reported by Fox News:

- **Who:** healthy older adults
- **What they ate:** 60 grams of unsalted, skin-roasted peanuts daily
- **How long:** 16 weeks
- **What changed:** better insulin responsiveness in memory-related brain regions, plus a slight rise in brain blood flow

The study was small, and that matters for how much weight you give it.

## What It Does Not Prove

Experts cautioned, per the report, that the findings do not show peanuts prevent Alzheimer's or cognitive decline. Improved insulin responsiveness in the brain is an interesting signal, since research increasingly links metabolism and insulin function to cognitive aging. But a signal is not an outcome. Nobody measured whether these participants stayed sharper or avoided dementia.

A sensible way to read a small nutrition study: treat it as a reason to try a low-risk habit, not a reason to expect a medical result. Peanuts are a food, not a treatment.

## How Much Is 60 Grams?

Sixty grams is roughly two small handfuls. If you have a kitchen scale, weigh it once so you know what it looks like in your own hand or a favorite small bowl. Then eyeball it from there. Peanuts are calorie-dense, so this is a swap for another snack, not an addition on top of everything else.

Two details from the study are worth copying: the peanuts were **unsalted** and **skin-roasted**. Look for packages labeled that way. Skin-on peanuts are usually easy to find and typically cost about the same as other roasted peanuts.

## Easy Ways to Eat Your Daily Peanuts

Plain peanuts every day gets dull fast. These ideas take minutes and use pantry basics.

### Spiced Stovetop Peanuts (about 5 minutes)

Toast 60 grams of unsalted peanuts in a dry skillet over medium heat for 2 to 3 minutes, shaking the pan often. Take them off the heat and toss with a pinch of chili powder, a pinch of cumin, and a squeeze of lime. Let them cool before eating. Keep the salt light, or skip it, to stay close to the study's unsalted approach.

### Peanut Topping for Grains and Veggies

Roughly chop a handful and scatter over rice, roasted vegetables, or a bowl of lentils. This borrows from the way many Southeast Asian and West African dishes use crushed peanuts for crunch and richness. You get texture and flavor without a new recipe.

### Yogurt and Fruit Bowl

Stir peanuts into plain yogurt with sliced apple or banana. It takes two minutes and works as breakfast or an afternoon snack.

### Quick Peanut Sauce

Whisk 2 tablespoons of peanut butter with 1 tablespoon of lime juice, 1 tablespoon of warm water, and a splash of soy sauce. Spoon over noodles or steamed greens. Note that peanut butter is not the same as the skin-roasted whole peanuts used in the study, so count this as a tasty extra rather than a substitute.

## Where Peanuts Fit in a Mediterranean-Style Plate

The study didn't test a whole diet, but peanuts fit comfortably into a Mediterranean-style pattern, which leans on vegetables, legumes, whole grains, and olive oil. Peanuts are technically legumes, so they sit naturally beside chickpeas and lentils. A few easy pairings:

- Peanuts over a chickpea and cucumber salad
- A small handful with fresh fruit instead of a packaged snack
- Crushed peanuts on a tomato and olive oil flatbread

## A Quick Safety Note

Peanuts are a major allergen, so this habit isn't for anyone with a peanut allergy. If you take medication or manage a condition such as diabetes, check with your doctor before making a big change to your eating.

## The Takeaway

Pick up a bag of unsalted, skin-roasted peanuts this week, measure out 60 grams once, and swap them in for one regular snack each day. It's cheap, it's tasty, and it's a reasonable habit to try. Just don't expect it to be a cure for anything.
