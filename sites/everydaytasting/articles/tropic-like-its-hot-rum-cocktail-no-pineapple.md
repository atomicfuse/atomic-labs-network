Tropical cocktails usually lean on pineapple juice and coconut cream. A video from WineOceans shows a different route: a rum drink called "Tropic Like It's Hot" that tastes tropical without either one. You get the effect from herbs and aromatic balance instead. Here is how the drink works, how to build it, and what to do if you don't have the key herb.

## What Goes in Tropic Like It's Hot

Per WineOceans, the cocktail uses four ingredients:

- Aperol infused with pineapple sage
- Yellow Chartreuse
- White rum
- Lemon juice

There is no pineapple juice and no coconut. The tropical character comes from the pineapple sage, which gives the Aperol a light, fruity, herbal note.

## The Ratio: Equal Parts of Everything

The recipe uses equal measures of lemon juice, infused Aperol, yellow Chartreuse, and rum. That makes it easy to remember and easy to scale. If you're new to mixing drinks, this is a friendly format: no fractions, no fussy math.

A single serving, using 3/4 ounce (22 ml) of each, looks like this:

- 3/4 oz lemon juice
- 3/4 oz pineapple sage-infused Aperol
- 3/4 oz yellow Chartreuse
- 3/4 oz white rum

The exact measure is my suggestion; the source describes the equal-parts structure. Shake all four with ice, then strain into a chilled glass. Shaking a citrus drink with ice for about 10 to 15 seconds is standard practice and chills and dilutes it just right.

## Why This Combination Works

Each ingredient covers a job:

- **Lemon juice** brings the acidity that keeps the drink fresh.
- **Infused Aperol** brings bittersweet notes, plus the fruity herbal touch of pineapple sage.
- **Yellow Chartreuse** adds a soft herbal character with a hint of licorice.
- **White rum** supplies the base spirit without weighing things down.

The result, according to the video, is a light, refreshing drink with an unusual tropical feel. Bitter, sour, and herbal elements are balanced against each other rather than buried under fruit juice.

## How to Infuse Aperol With Pineapple Sage

The source doesn't give an infusion time, so treat these steps as a starting point and taste as you go:

1. Pour some Aperol into a clean jar.
2. Add a few fresh pineapple sage leaves, lightly bruised so they release their aroma.
3. Seal and let it sit, tasting every so often.
4. When the herbal, fruity note is noticeable but not overpowering, strain out the leaves.

Taste early rather than late. Herbs can turn grassy if left too long, and you can always infuse longer but can't undo it.

## No Pineapple Sage? Try These Swaps

The source centers on pineapple sage, and I can't promise a substitute matches it. Still, if you can't find it, a few practical options are worth a try:

- **Regular Aperol:** Skip the infusion. You'll lose the fruity herb note, but the drink keeps its bitter-sour-herbal structure.
- **Another soft garden herb:** Infuse with a different mild herb and taste often. Expect a different drink, not a copy.
- **Grow your own:** The video's interest in garden-grown ingredients is a reminder that one herb plant can supply many drinks.

These are suggestions, not tested equivalents from the video.

## Troubleshooting the Balance

Because the recipe is equal parts, small tweaks are easy to understand:

- Too sharp? Pull back the lemon slightly.
- Too sweet or herbal? Add a touch more lemon.
- Too boozy and flat? Shake a little longer with plenty of ice.

Change one thing at a time so you know what made the difference.

## A Note on Yellow Chartreuse

Chartreuse is a specialty bottle, and it can be pricey. WineOceans' framing is that it has become easier to find again. If you buy a bottle for this drink, remember it's a sipping-strength herbal liqueur, so a bottle goes a long way when each drink uses only 3/4 ounce.

## The Takeaway

You don't need pineapple juice or coconut to make a drink taste tropical. Measure equal parts lemon, pineapple sage-infused Aperol, yellow Chartreuse, and white rum, shake with ice, and let the herbs do the work.
