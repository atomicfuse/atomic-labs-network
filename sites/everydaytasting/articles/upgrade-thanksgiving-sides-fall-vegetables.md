Farmer Lee Jones of The Chef's Garden appeared on "Fox & Friends Weekend," per Fox News, to show off fresh fall vegetables and Thanksgiving side dishes. The idea behind the segment: seasonal produce can bring brighter, fresher flavors to a holiday table that often leans heavy.

The segment's details beyond that are limited, so this guide doesn't pretend to reproduce his recipes. Instead, it takes his core idea, letting fall vegetables lead your sides, and turns it into a practical plan you can use this week. You'll leave with a way to choose vegetables, a simple method that works for almost any of them, and a few plating habits that make a plain side look intentional.

## Start With the Vegetable, Not the Recipe

The easiest way to get fresher flavor is to shop first and decide second. Walk the produce section or farmers market and pick what looks best: firm, heavy for its size, with no soft spots or wilting. Then build the side around it.

This approach also keeps costs down. Seasonal vegetables are usually at their most plentiful in fall, so you're working with what's abundant rather than hunting for something out of season.

Good candidates for a Thanksgiving plate include carrots, Brussels sprouts, squash, sweet potatoes, beets, and cauliflower. These are common fall staples, and all of them respond well to the method below.

## One Method That Works for Most Fall Vegetables

If you're a beginner, memorize this and you can handle nearly any root or hardy vegetable. Roasting concentrates flavor and needs very little attention.

### The basic roast

1. Heat your oven to 425°F.
2. Cut about 2 pounds of vegetables into pieces of similar size, roughly 1 to 1½ inches, so they cook evenly.
3. Toss with 2 tablespoons of oil, ¾ teaspoon of salt, and a few grinds of black pepper.
4. Spread on a rimmed baking sheet in a single layer. Crowded pans steam instead of browning, so use two pans if needed.
5. Roast 25 to 40 minutes, flipping once halfway, until tender and browned at the edges.

Dense vegetables like carrots and beets sit at the longer end of that range. Brussels sprouts halved and cauliflower florets tend to land closer to 25 to 30 minutes. Start checking early; you can always add time.

Hands-on prep is about 15 minutes. The oven does the rest, which frees you up on a busy cooking day.

## Finish With Something Bright

Heavy holiday plates benefit from contrast. A roasted vegetable straight from the oven is good; one finished with an acidic or fresh element tastes livelier. Try one of these right before serving:

- A squeeze of lemon or a teaspoon or two of vinegar over the hot vegetables
- A handful of chopped fresh parsley or other tender herbs
- A spoonful of plain yogurt thinned with a little lemon juice, drizzled on top
- Toasted nuts or seeds for crunch

These cost very little and take under five minutes. They also make a classic side feel updated without changing the dish itself.

## Simple Swaps for Classic Sides

You don't need to replace tradition. Small swaps keep the familiar and freshen it.

- **Instead of a heavy casserole:** serve roasted vegetables with a bit of the same flavors you love, such as herbs and a little butter at the end.
- **Instead of plain boiled carrots:** roast them, which brings out sweetness without added sugar.
- **Instead of one big pile of mashed potatoes only:** add a second vegetable side so guests have a lighter option next to it.
- **Out of fresh herbs?** Use about one-third as much dried herb, and add it before roasting rather than after.

## Borrow Flavors From Other Kitchens

Fall vegetables take well to seasonings from around the world, which is an easy way to discover something new without a new recipe.

- **Warm spice:** cumin and coriander with roasted carrots, finished with the yogurt drizzle above, gives a Middle Eastern-style feel.
- **Sweet and savory:** a little soy sauce and a touch of honey on Brussels sprouts adds an East Asian-leaning glaze. Add it in the last 5 minutes so it doesn't burn.
- **Smoky heat:** smoked paprika on squash or sweet potatoes adds depth with no extra work.

Use about ½ to 1 teaspoon of any spice per 2 pounds of vegetables, then taste and adjust next time.

## Quick Plating Habits That Look Chef-Style

The Fox News segment framed the appeal as bringing fresher flavors to the table, and presentation helps sell that. You don't need special skills.

- **Use a wide, shallow platter** rather than a deep bowl. Vegetables stay crisp at the edges and look more appealing.
- **Mix colors.** Orange carrots, purple beets, and green sprouts on the same platter make the table look abundant.
- **Add a final garnish.** Fresh herbs or a sprinkle of flaky salt added at the end reads as finished.
- **Keep it warm.** A platter warmed briefly in a low oven holds heat longer at the table.

## Plan Your Timing

Oven space is the real Thanksgiving bottleneck. Here's a workable approach:

- **The day before:** wash, peel, and cut hardy vegetables, then store them in the fridge in airtight containers.
- **Day of:** roast while the turkey rests, since a resting bird frees up the oven. Turn the heat to 425°F and your vegetables will be done in under 40 minutes.
- **Last step:** add your bright finish just before serving.

If the oven is truly full, cook vegetables in a skillet on the stovetop with a lid for the first few minutes, then uncover to brown.

## The Takeaway

Pick one vegetable that looks great at the store, roast it at 425°F, and finish it with lemon or fresh herbs. That single side, built from what's in season, is the simplest way to bring the fresher fall flavor Jones was showcasing to your own table.
