Southern food has a reputation for being heavy. Texas-based chef and restaurateur Tiffany Derry is pushing back on that. As reported by Fox News, she talks about wholesome ingredients, colorful vegetables, and simple preparation as the real foundation of what's traditionally served across the South.

By the end of this article, you'll know how to cook in that spirit on a weeknight: a flexible vegetable skillet, a few easy swaps, and a simple method you can repeat with whatever produce you have.

To be clear about what's reported and what's mine: the ideas from Derry below come from the Fox News coverage. The recipe and the quantities are my own practical suggestions, not hers.

## The Idea: Fresh, Colorful, Simple

The takeaway from Derry's perspective, per Fox News, is that Southern cooking is rooted in fresh, colorful, vegetable-forward food made with simple, wholesome ingredients. The stereotype is a plate of heavy, fried everything. The pushback is that the tradition is much broader than that.

For a home cook, that's good news. Simple ingredients and simple preparation mean fewer steps, a shorter shopping list, and a lower bill.

## A Flexible Southern-Style Vegetable Skillet

This is a template, not a strict recipe. It takes about 25 minutes and serves 3 to 4 as a main with rice or cornbread, or 4 to 6 as a side.

### What You Need

- 2 tablespoons oil or bacon drippings (oil keeps it vegetarian)
- 1 medium onion, diced
- 2 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 bell pepper, chopped
- 2 cups sliced okra, fresh or frozen
- 2 cups corn kernels, fresh or frozen
- 2 cups chopped tomatoes (about 2 large, or one 14-ounce can, drained)
- 1/2 teaspoon salt, plus more to taste
- 1/4 teaspoon black pepper
- Pinch of cayenne or a few dashes of hot sauce

### Steps

1. **Heat the pan (1 minute).** Warm the oil in a large skillet over medium heat.
2. **Start the base (5 minutes).** Add the onion and bell pepper with a pinch of salt. Cook until soft, stirring now and then. Add the garlic for the last 30 seconds.
3. **Add the okra and corn (6 to 8 minutes).** Raise the heat to medium-high. Cook until the okra loses its raw look and edges start to brown. Frozen okra works fine; add it straight from the freezer.
4. **Add the tomatoes (8 to 10 minutes).** Stir in the tomatoes, remaining salt, pepper, and cayenne. Simmer until the tomatoes break down and the mixture looks saucy rather than watery.
5. **Taste and adjust.** Add salt, heat, or a splash of vinegar if it needs brightness.

## Easy Swaps That Keep It Affordable

Vegetable-forward cooking works best when you buy what's in season or on sale. Use these swaps:

- **No okra?** Use zucchini or yellow squash, cut into half-moons, and cook for 4 to 5 minutes.
- **No fresh tomatoes?** Canned diced tomatoes are a fine stand-in, drained so the skillet doesn't turn soupy.
- **No fresh corn?** Frozen kernels cost less out of season and need no thawing.
- **Want more protein?** Stir in a can of rinsed black-eyed peas or butter beans during the last 5 minutes.
- **Want smoky flavor without meat?** Add 1/2 teaspoon smoked paprika with the garlic.

## Cooking Basics That Make Simple Food Taste Good

When a dish has few ingredients, technique does the work.

### Season in layers

Salt the onions early, then adjust at the end. A little at each stage tastes better than a lot at the finish.

### Don't crowd the pan

If your skillet is overfilled, vegetables steam instead of browning. Use the largest pan you have, or cook in two batches.

### Finish with something bright

A teaspoon of vinegar, a squeeze of lemon, or a few dashes of hot sauce wakes up cooked vegetables. It's a small step that makes the dish taste intentional.

### Keep colors in mind

Green, yellow, red, and orange on one plate is an easy way to see whether you've built a varied meal. It also echoes the colorful, vegetable-forward approach Derry describes.

## Why This Rethink Matters

Per the Fox News report, Derry's perspective fits a wider interest in revisiting comfort food through health, heritage, and authenticity. In my view, the practical lesson is that tradition and lighter, fresher cooking aren't opposites. Many of the foods people associate with the South, such as vegetables, beans, and simple one-pan dishes, are budget-friendly and quick.

## Serving Ideas

- Spoon the skillet over 1 cup of cooked rice per serving.
- Serve alongside cornbread for a full meal.
- Top with a fried egg for a fast dinner.
- Reheat leftovers within 3 to 4 days and stir into scrambled eggs the next morning.

## Your Next Step

This week, pick three vegetables in different colors, chop them, and cook them in one skillet with onion, garlic, and salt. Taste, add something bright, and see how much flavor simple ingredients can carry.
