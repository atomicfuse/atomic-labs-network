Vermont's signature soft serve now comes with a stamp book. Per Afar, the Vermont Creemee Passport launched in April 2026 and covers 189 shops across the state. By the end of this article, you'll know what a creemee is, what the passport includes, and how to build a stop-by-stop plan that keeps your trip fun instead of a sugar sprint.

## What the Creemee Passport Actually Is

According to Afar, the passport is a 20-page booklet paired with a digital directory. Together they map 189 places to try a creemee. The booklet has pages for collecting stamps, so you can record each stop the way you would at a national park or a brewery trail.

Afar also reports that proceeds support Vermont nonprofits, so buying one is more than a souvenir purchase.

I can't tell you from the reporting how much the passport costs or where to buy it, so check the official Vermont Creemee Passport listing before you go.

## So What Is a Creemee?

A creemee is Vermont's version of soft serve. Per Afar, it's richer and made with higher butterfat than the soft serve you may know. That's the practical difference: a creemee tastes creamier and more full-bodied, so a small size goes further than you'd expect.

The treat is tied to maple culture and local farms in Vermont, which is part of why a passport built around it makes sense. Each shop becomes a small window into the town it's in.

## How to Plan Your Creemee Route

With 189 shops, you can't do everything. Pick a plan that fits your time.

### Step 1: Choose a home base region

Open the digital directory and zoom in on one area, such as the town where you're staying. Clustering stops close together means more time eating and less time driving.

### Step 2: Set a realistic daily goal

Three creemees in a day is plenty for most people. That's my own rule of thumb, not an official one. Order the smallest size available at each stop, or share one between two people.

### Step 3: Mix in non-dessert stops

Space your creemees between a lunch, a walk, or a farm visit. A creemee tastes better when you aren't already full of the last one.

### Step 4: Get your stamp before you leave

Bring the booklet in with you, not back in the car. Ask for the stamp when you order so you don't forget it as you head out.

## Order Like You're Curious

A passport rewards variety, so treat each stop as a chance to try something different.

- **Start with maple.** It's the flavor most tied to Vermont's food identity, and it gives you a baseline.
- **Try the shop's featured flavor next.** Ask what the staff is excited about. Their answer is usually more interesting than the standard board.
- **Compare notes.** Jot a one-line rating in the margin of your booklet or your phone. By the fifth stop you'll remember which was creamiest.

I can't point to specific shops or flavors here, since the reporting doesn't name them. The directory is the right place to find those.

## Why This Kind of Trip Works

Afar frames the passport as part of a broader move toward collectible food trails and local-pride travel. It's easy to see the appeal. A stamp book gives you a reason to turn down a side road, and many of the places on it are small businesses you might otherwise drive past.

It also keeps the stakes low. You don't need a big itinerary. One creemee, one stamp, and one new town is a perfectly good afternoon.

## Make It a Weekend Project

If you want a challenge, try for as many stamps as you can in a weekend, but aim for a number that leaves room to enjoy each stop. Quality of stops beats quantity of stamps. The passport covers 189 shops, so you'll have reasons to come back.
