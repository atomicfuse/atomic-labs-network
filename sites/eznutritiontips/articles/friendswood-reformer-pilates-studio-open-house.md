## Why a new studio is worth a look

Reformer Pilates is low-impact, which makes it a natural fit for people who want to build strength and stay active without pounding their joints. A neighborhood studio also removes a common barrier: distance. The closer a workout is, the more likely you are to keep going.

An open house is typically a low-pressure way to see the space and get a feel for the setting. Treat it as a scouting trip. Check what the room feels like, whether the equipment looks approachable, and whether you'd want to be there at 6 a.m. on a Tuesday.

## What to eat before a Pilates class

Pilates demands core control and balance, so you don't want to be running on empty or feeling heavy. A simple rule of thumb:

### If class is 2-3 hours away

Eat a small, balanced meal with carbs, protein, and a little fat. Think oatmeal with Greek yogurt and berries, or whole-grain toast with eggs and sliced avocado.

### If class is 30-60 minutes away

Keep it light and easy to digest. A banana with a spoonful of peanut butter, a small yogurt, or a few dates with a handful of almonds all work.

### What to skip

Very large, greasy, or high-fiber meals right before class. Lots of core work and lying in different positions is not the moment for a heavy burrito.

## A quick open-house snack plan

If you're heading over after work, you may be hungry by then. Pack something portable so you aren't tempted to either skip eating or arrive famished:

- An apple with a small packet of nut butter
- A hard-boiled egg and a piece of fruit
- Trail mix with nuts and dried fruit, about a small handful

These are practical suggestions, not studio requirements, and they're easy to adjust to your own tolerance.

## Hydration counts too

Sip water through the day rather than chugging right before you start. Bring a bottle to class. If you tend to get lightheaded when you work out, a little salt in your meal earlier in the day and steady fluids can help, but check with your doctor if dizziness is frequent.

## Eat after, but keep it simple

After a session, aim for protein plus carbs to help you feel recovered and satisfied. Easy options:

- Cottage cheese with pineapple
- A turkey and veggie wrap
- A smoothie with milk or yogurt, fruit, and a handful of spinach

You don't need a special recovery product. A normal, balanced meal does the job for most people doing a low-impact class.

## Make it a routine, not a one-off

A new studio is only useful if you use it. Pick a realistic goal, such as one or two classes a week, and anchor your meals around those times so you're never guessing what to eat. Consistency in both movement and food beats any single perfect session.

If you're in Friendswood, mark Nov. 6, stop by the open house, and plan your snack before you go.
