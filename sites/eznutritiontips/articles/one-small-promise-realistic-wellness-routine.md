Most wellness routines fail for the same reason: they ask for a total overhaul. Pilates instructor Bryony Basse takes the opposite view. In a "day in the life" piece told to Rose Beer for AOL, she says transformation doesn't come from reinventing yourself. It comes from "building trust with yourself through small things you say you will do and then actually do."

Below, you'll find a way to turn that idea into a routine, with food, movement, and evening habits borrowed from how Basse describes her own days.

## Start With One Promise, Not Ten

Basse's core argument is that most people don't need a life overhaul. They need "one or two promises to themselves that are small enough to keep." Her examples: 10 minutes of Pilates in your pyjamas before work, or putting your phone away for the first 20 minutes of the morning.

Here's how to use that:

1. Pick one promise you could keep on your worst day.
2. Attach it to something you already do, like the kettle boiling or brushing your teeth.
3. Keep it for a couple of weeks before you add anything.

Her logic: consistency builds evidence, and evidence builds self-belief. Once a promise feels trustworthy, build from there.

## Take Decisions Out of Food (Without Restricting It)

This is where Basse's routine gets practical for anyone who loves eating well. She says she doesn't fast or follow a restrictive diet. At home, she likes eating "quite monotonously" during the day because it removes some of the decision-making, and she's then much more flexible at dinner.

That's a useful structure to borrow:

- **Make daytime meals repeatable.** Choose a breakfast and lunch you enjoy and rotate them, so you aren't deciding from scratch while busy.
- **Save the variety for dinner.** That's where she lets herself be flexible.
- **Skip "off limits" lists.** Basse says she doesn't believe in foods being off limits, and she has dark chocolate every day.

She also starts her morning with something warm to drink, as a fan of Chinese medicine who avoids cold drinks. You don't need to share that philosophy to try the habit. A warm drink first thing is an easy, low-effort anchor for a morning promise.

## Schedule Movement Around Real Life

Basse has a nine-month-old and runs a business, so her movement is, in her words, quite scheduled. Her week includes four Pilates by Bryony classes and two weight-training sessions in the gym.

She explains why she does both:

- **Pilates** gives her strength, control, mobility, and mind-body connection.
- **Gym sessions** let her work with heavier loads.

She calls movement her number-one tool for managing stress and mental fatigue. If she's very in her head, she says, getting into her body changes her state incredibly quickly.

You don't need six sessions to borrow the idea. Put your small promise in the calendar like a meeting, and notice how you feel afterward on stressful days.

## Keep Recovery Simple

Basse enjoys saunas and cold water, and sea swimming when she can, noting that cold water makes her feel mentally clear. But she doesn't think recovery has to involve lots of expensive tools. Her foundations are walking, sleep, gentle movement, and a proper rest day.

If your budget or schedule doesn't allow for a sauna, the free items on her list still count. A rest day isn't a failure of consistency. It's part of it.

## Try Visualisation Before Something Nerve-Racking

Basse meditates daily and is particularly interested in visualisation, which she uses like a mental rehearsal. If something coming up makes her nervous, she mentally walks herself through it beforehand.

A simple way to try it: before a presentation, a tough conversation, or a first class at a new studio, spend a few minutes picturing the steps in order. It's free and takes no equipment.

## Build a Wind-Down You'll Actually Follow

Basse starts winding down around 10pm and tries to fall asleep soon after. Her evening looks like this:

- Dim the lights, light candles, and put on a calming playlist
- Mint tea and a book
- No phone for 30 to 60 minutes before bed
- Magnesium

In the morning, she keeps her phone away from the bed, uses an alarm, and never snoozes. She wakes between 6am and 7am and has learnt to embrace flexibility because of her baby. She also journals most mornings: looking back at the previous day, writing what she's visualising, plus one thing she'll do for her mind and one for her body.

That last prompt is a ready-made promise generator. Two lines, every morning.

## A Note on the Extras

Some of what Basse does goes beyond basics. She takes five grams of creatine daily, citing interest in strength and recovery and what she calls emerging research on cognitive function. She also did a DUTCH hormone test to understand how her energy and mood shift through her cycle. These are personal choices described in her routine, not recommendations for everyone. If you're considering supplements or testing, a conversation with a doctor or registered dietitian is a sensible first step.

She's candid that wellness can become complicated and commercialised. Drawing on her interest in Blue Zones research, she points to fundamentals associated with healthy ageing that are remarkably simple: movement, food, purpose, connection, and a sense of belonging.

## Your Next Step

Choose one promise small enough to keep tomorrow, whether it's a warm drink, 10 minutes of Pilates, or 20 phone-free minutes. Keep it, then keep it again. That's the whole method.
