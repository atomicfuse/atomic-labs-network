You can follow a recipe perfectly and still end up with a flat, forgettable meal. The problem often started in the produce aisle. By the end of this article, you'll have a three-sense checklist for choosing fruits, vegetables and herbs that taste better before you ever turn on the stove.

## The Idea: Cooking Starts at the Market

Carmen Perr, the Los Angeles-based author of "Cook What Grows," argues that better home cooking begins before you enter the kitchen, according to Fox News. Her advice centers on paying attention to texture, color and aroma while you shop, rather than relying only on labels or a recipe's ingredient list.

That's a useful shift. A recipe tells you what to buy. Your senses tell you which one to buy.

## Use Your Senses, Not Just the Label

Labels and price tags tell you very little about how a tomato will taste or whether a bunch of basil will perk up your pasta. Your hands, eyes and nose can tell you more. Perr's three cues give you a simple routine to run through each time you shop.

### Texture: Pick It Up

Texture is your first clue to freshness and ripeness. Handle produce gently instead of just scanning it.

- **Fruit:** Notice whether it feels heavy and slightly yielding, or hard and lifeless, or mushy.
- **Vegetables:** Look for firmness that suggests they're crisp rather than limp or rubbery.
- **Herbs:** Leaves should look and feel lively, not slimy, wilted or dried out.

A practical habit: compare two or three of the same item side by side. The difference is often obvious once you hold them together.

### Color: Look for Life

Color tells you a lot about quality and ripeness. Rich, vibrant color generally signals produce that's in good shape, while dull, faded or patchy color can be a sign it's past its best or hasn't developed fully.

The point isn't to chase perfection. Slight variation is normal in real food. Look for color that seems alive and appropriate to the item, and skip anything that looks tired.

### Aroma: Take a Sniff

Aroma is the most underused tool in the store. Fragrant produce often signals flavor to come. Herbs are the easiest place to start: crush a leaf lightly or bring the bunch to your nose. If you can barely smell it, expect it to taste just as faint in your dish.

The same goes for fruit. A good smell is a promising sign, and no smell at all is worth noting.

## A Quick Routine for Your Next Shop

You don't need to memorize rules for every item. Try this three-step pass instead:

1. **Look** at the color. Set aside anything dull or faded.
2. **Touch** what's left. Check for the right firmness and a fresh feel.
3. **Smell** your finalists. Choose the one that smells the way it should taste.

It takes maybe ten extra seconds per item, and it builds a skill you'll use every time you shop.

## Why This Matters for Your Budget and Your Plate

More people are looking to cook affordably and seasonally at home, and choosing well is part of that. When produce tastes great on its own, you need fewer extras to make a meal work. Good selection also supports low-waste cooking: ingredients you picked carefully are more likely to get eaten rather than tossed.

This approach also fits farmers markets, where you can often handle and smell what you're buying. It's a comfortable place for beginners to practice, since you can see what's in front of you and build confidence one item at a time.

## Healthy Eating Gets Easier When Food Tastes Good

If you're trying to eat more vegetables, flavor is your ally. Produce that smells fragrant, looks vibrant and feels fresh is simply more enjoyable to eat, which makes healthy recipes easier to stick with. You don't need complicated technique to make a salad, roasted vegetables or a simple herb sauce worth eating. You need good ingredients.

## Your Next Step

On your next shopping trip, pick just one item, whether it's a bunch of herbs, a piece of fruit or a vegetable you use often, and run it through the look, touch, smell test. Do that once, and you'll start shopping with your senses on every trip.
