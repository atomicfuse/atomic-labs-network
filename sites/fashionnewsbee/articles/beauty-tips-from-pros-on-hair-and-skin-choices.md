Before you grab the kitchen scissors at 11 p.m. or book a same-week filler appointment because a video told you to, InStyle has a message from the people who see the aftermath. The outlet gathered celebrity makeup artists, manicurists, estheticians and hairstylists to share the regrets they encounter most, from impulsively cut bangs to overdone injectables. The through-line, per the report: some beauty decisions are much easier to make than to undo.

The full roundup covers skin, hair, nails and makeup. Here, we're focusing on the two regrets that stand out as the clearest cautionary tales, because they share one trait: a fast decision with a slow reversal.

## Why This Conversation Is Happening Now

The timing makes sense. Beauty trends move at TikTok speed, and cosmetic treatments are becoming more mainstream. The result, as the coverage frames it, is that people are making quicker appearance decisions with longer-lasting consequences.

It's hard not to notice the mismatch. A trend can cycle out in a few weeks. A fringe takes months to grow out, and cosmetic tweaks can take their own time to settle or fade. The pace of the feed and the pace of your body are simply not in sync.

## Regret No. 1: The Impulsively Cut Bangs

Bangs are a pop-culture perennial, and the urge to cut them tends to arrive suddenly, usually with a screenshot attached. That's the pattern the professionals flag: bangs cut on impulse, rather than planned with a stylist, are among the regrets hairstylists hear about most, according to InStyle.

The appeal is obvious. A fringe feels like the cheapest, fastest reinvention available, a new face without a new wardrobe. Our take: that's exactly the trap. The speed that makes it tempting is the same thing that leaves no room to ask whether it suits your hair, your routine or your patience.

### A pause-first approach

The spirit of the expert advice is to slow the decision down. A few practical ways to do that, as our own suggestions rather than quotes from the pros:

- Sleep on it. If the urge survives a few days, it's more likely to be real.
- Book a consultation with a stylist instead of improvising at home.
- Ask what daily upkeep the look will demand before you commit.

## Regret No. 2: Injectables That Went Too Far

The second regret is heavier. Overdone injectables made the list of what beauty professionals see people wishing they could take back. Within the scope of what InStyle reported, the takeaway is restraint: this is not an area for a snap decision driven by a trend.

We'll stay in our lane here and not offer medical guidance beyond what the experts said. But the logic of the broader piece is easy to follow. Cosmetic treatments becoming more mainstream can make them feel as casual as a haircut, and they aren't. The stakes are higher and the do-overs are less simple.

### Red flags worth taking seriously

Again, this is our read rather than a direct expert checklist:

- Feeling rushed, whether by a promotion, a trend or a deadline.
- Choosing a treatment because it's viral rather than because you wanted a change before it was.
- Skipping a proper conversation with a qualified professional about what you want and what's realistic.

## The Real Lesson: Trend Speed vs. Long-Term Confidence

The shared thread is not "never change anything." Beauty is supposed to be fun, and reinvention is half the point. The point is that the best changes tend to support how you feel over time, rather than how a look performs for a week.

That's the useful reframe for anyone scrolling through a feed of transformations. The video shows the reveal. It rarely shows month three.

## Your Pre-Commit Checklist

1. Give it a waiting period. A real want outlasts a trending sound.
2. Talk to a pro first, whether that's a hairstylist, esthetician or another qualified expert.
3. Ask about the undo. If you can't answer "how do I reverse this?", you aren't ready.
4. Choose subtle over dramatic when the change is hard to reverse.

For the full list of regrets across skin, hair, nails and makeup, InStyle's original report is the place to go. The short version for today: the most flattering beauty move may be the one you took 48 hours to think about.
