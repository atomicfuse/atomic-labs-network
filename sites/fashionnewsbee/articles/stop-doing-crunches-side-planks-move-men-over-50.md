## The Pilates Move That Trumps Crunches for Men Over 50

If you're a man over 50 still cranking out crunches, it might be time for an overhaul in your core workouts. Increasingly, fitness experts recommend ditching those traditional ab exercises in favor of more functional movements, like side planks. Yes, you heard it here first! These side-lying gems not only build core strength; they also support your spine, improve posture, and enhance your daily functionality — all crucial aspects as you age.

### Why Side Planks?
According to an article from Aol, side planks offer a plethora of benefits, making them particularly valuable for men over 50. With an emphasis on functional core strength, this Pilates staple is designed to enhance your ability to perform everyday tasks — from lifting groceries to navigating stairs without losing your balance.

Aging can often bring along friends like back pain and balance challenges, but fear not! Side planks target lateral core strength, which is essential not just for athletic performance but for practical movements throughout the day. Think of it as a bridge between the gym and your real life.

### Getting Started: Form Cues
To reap the benefits of the side plank, mastering proper form is a must. Here’s a quick guide to ensure you’re doing it right:

1. **Position Yourself**: Lie on your right side with your legs extended and stacked. Your right elbow should be directly beneath your shoulder.

2. **Lift Off**: Engage your core and lift your hips, creating a straight line from head to toe. Keep your body aligned — no sagging or rounding!

3. **Breathe**: Remember to breathe while holding the position and avoid the urge to tense up.

4. **Duration**: Start with 15-30 seconds and gradually increase your hold time as you build strength.

### Modifications for Beginners
If you're new to Pilates or if side planks feel a bit daunting, there are modifications you can make to ease into this exercise:
- **Knees on the Ground**: Keep your knees on the ground as you lift your hips. This reduces the difficulty while still engaging your core.
- **Wall Support**: Start by performing side planks with your back against a wall for added stability before transitioning to a free-standing position.

### Progressing Your Side Planks
Once you’ve got the basics down, you might want to challenge yourself with advanced variations:
- **Leg Lift**: While holding the side plank, lift your top leg and lower it back down without letting your hips drop.
- **Reach Under**: Extend your top arm toward the ceiling, then reach under your body, rotating slightly. This engages more muscles and helps improve mobility.
