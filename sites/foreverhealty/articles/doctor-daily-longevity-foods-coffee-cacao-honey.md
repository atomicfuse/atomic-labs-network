Longevity advice tends to come with a price tag: testing panels, powders, gadgets. This week's version is refreshingly cheap. Per Fox News, a Florida physician says he consumes three foods every day to support longevity and healthy aging: coffee, cacao and raw honey. You probably have at least one in your kitchen right now.

Below, you'll get what each food is credited with, a sensible way to fold them into a morning routine, and a clear line between what's reported and what's still one doctor's opinion.

## What the doctor says, and what it rests on

The physician, described as a functional medicine doctor, points to antioxidants and polyphenols as the common thread. According to the report, he sees these foods as offering antioxidant, cardiovascular, immune and gut-health benefits when they're part of a sustainable diet.

Two things are worth keeping straight:

- **This is a practitioner's personal routine.** It's what he eats and why he believes it helps. It isn't a trial showing these three foods extend lifespan.
- **The claims are framed as "may."** Polyphenols and antioxidants are plausible reasons to like a food, but plausible is not the same as proven for your particular body.

That's not a reason to dismiss the list. It's a reason to treat it as a pleasant, low-risk habit rather than a longevity guarantee.

## Coffee: the easiest one to keep

Coffee is on the list for its antioxidant content. If you already drink it, the practical question isn't whether to start but how to drink it well.

- **Keep it simple.** Black or with a splash of milk lets the coffee do the work. Flavored syrups and sweetened creamers can quietly cancel the point.
- **Watch your own limits.** If coffee makes you jittery or wrecks your sleep, the benefit isn't worth it. Having it in the morning rather than late in the day is a common-sense way to protect sleep.
- **Don't add it if you don't like it.** No one needs to take up coffee for longevity.

## 100% cacao: the bitter one

The doctor specifically names 100% cacao, not milk chocolate. That distinction matters. Candy bars are mostly sugar and fat; cacao is the unsweetened source of the polyphenols the doctor is talking about.

The tradeoff is taste. Unsweetened cacao is bitter, which is why it tends to show up in drinks and mixed dishes rather than eaten straight. A few ways to make it palatable:

- Stir a spoonful of cacao powder into hot milk or a warm drink.
- Blend it into a smoothie with banana.
- Fold it into oatmeal or yogurt.

Start small and adjust. If you find yourself adding lots of sugar to make it drinkable, scale back.

## Raw honey: sweetener, not supplement

Raw honey rounds out the trio, and it's the one where moderation matters most. It's still sugar. The report ties it to immune and gut-health support, but a drizzle isn't a license to pour.

A useful rule: let honey replace the sweetener you'd use anyway rather than adding it on top. A teaspoon in tea or on yogurt is a reasonable starting point (that amount is my suggestion, not the doctor's).

## Putting them together: a simple morning routine

The appeal of this list is that the pieces fit into one routine without much effort. Here's one way to try it:

1. **Coffee first**, black or lightly milked.
2. **A cacao drink or smoothie** with unsweetened cacao powder, with a small amount of raw honey to take the edge off the bitterness.
3. **Pair them with real food.** Oats, eggs, fruit, or yogurt do more for your overall diet than any single ingredient.

Notice what this doesn't require: supplements, special equipment, or a big budget.

## Fact versus opinion

Here's the honest scorecard based on what's been reported:

- **Reported:** One Florida physician eats coffee, 100% cacao and raw honey daily and links them to longevity, citing antioxidants and polyphenols.
- **Reported as potential:** Antioxidant, cardiovascular, immune and gut-health benefits, when used as part of a sustainable diet.
- **Not established here:** That these foods make you live longer. Nothing in the report offers proof of that, and no single food can carry that weight.

The phrase worth holding onto is "part of a sustainable diet." Foods like these work best as additions to a pattern that already includes vegetables, protein, fiber and movement, not as shortcuts around it.

## Who should be cautious

If you have a health condition, take medication, or are managing blood sugar, check with your own doctor before building a daily habit around caffeine or honey. Sleep problems, reflux, or anxiety are good reasons to rethink the coffee. And because honey and cacao add up in calories and sugar when they're sweetened, the label on anything you buy matters.

## The takeaway

Pick the one of the three you'll genuinely enjoy tomorrow morning, make it the unsweetened or lightly sweetened version, and keep it up for a few weeks. A modest habit you can sustain beats an ambitious one you abandon by Friday.
