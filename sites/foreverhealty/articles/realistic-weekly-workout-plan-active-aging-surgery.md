Staying active as you age can be challenging, especially after experiencing surgeries or injuries. Jill Brown, a 59-year-old fitness trainer, not only defied those odds but increased her strength and confidence after undergoing four surgeries in five years. Her story highlights how you can adapt your workout routine to accommodate your body while still pushing for progress. Here's a detailed look at her weekly routine, designed specifically for those looking to enhance their fitness in their 50s and beyond.

## Monday: High-Intensity Interval Training (HIIT)
Kick off your week with a HIIT workout focused on functional exercises. This approach maximizes strength and endurance while minimizing impact on the body. Incorporate exercises like bodyweight squats, lunges, and short bursts of cardio to elevate your heart rate. Aim for 20-30 minutes of intervals, mixing high-intensity moves with short recovery periods.

## Tuesday: Body Sculpt Classes and Physical Therapy
Tuesday is all about targeted exercises. Participate in two Body Sculpt classes that emphasize strength training using lighter weights for higher repetitions. Alongside this, dedicate time to physical therapy aimed at shoulder and hip recovery. This balance of strength and therapeutic work will help stabilize any weaknesses and prevent future injuries.

## Wednesday: Rowing and Core Work
On Wednesdays, utilize the rowing machine for a full-body workout that is low-impact but effective. Row for 20 minutes, focusing on your form, followed by core strengthening exercises like planks, bridges, or Russian twists. This combination will enhance endurance while reinforcing core stability, crucial for daily activities.

## Thursday: Pilates and Resistance Training
Incorporate a Pilates class into your Thursday routine to improve flexibility and core strength. Pilates emphasizes functional movement which is essential for maintaining an active lifestyle as you age. Complement this with back and biceps resistance training. Think about using light dumbbells; this promotes strength while protecting your joints.

## Friday: Cardio Sculpt Step Class
Fridays should include a Cardio Sculpt Step class that combines cardiovascular work with strength training. Use a combination of dumbbells and battle ropes to create an invigorating workout while also keeping things fun and engaging. This session can focus on total body conditioning, helping you enhance both strength and stamina.

## Saturday: Body Sculpt and Leg-Focused Strength Training
Finish the week strong with a Body Sculpt class followed by leg-focused strength exercises. Again, utilize lighter weights for multiple repetitions, focusing on exercises that target your quads, hamstrings, and glutes. Remember, consistency is key, and having a robust lower body can greatly enhance your functional strength.

## Why This Matters
As more individuals over 40 seek sustainable fitness routines, Jill Brown's experience offers a compelling case for reframing how we view aging and recovery. Training smarter means prioritizing functional strength and adapting workouts, rather than letting age or injury dictate your fitness journey. 

By consistently following a routine that incorporates a mix of HIIT, functional exercises, strength training, and proper recovery protocols, you can not only rebuild your strength after setbacks but also maintain a vibrant and active lifestyle. The goal isn't perfection; it's finding what feels achievable for you. So, whether you just had surgery or are looking to reinvigorate your workouts, this plan has the flexibility you need to thrive as you age.

## Final Takeaway
Adapting your workout routine around injury or age is entirely possible. Take a cue from Jill Brown—be persistent, be flexible, and confidently embrace every decade of your life with a workout that keeps you strong and capable.
