If you run a studio, you probably know the patchwork: one tool for bookings, a spreadsheet for attendance, a messaging app for reminders. A newly announced platform called Gymsly is pitching itself as the replacement for all of it, and the idea is worth a look even if you never use this particular product.

## What was announced

Per a press release distributed through ABNewswire and published by the Gwinnett Daily Post, AK Studio LTD, a London-based software company, announced the availability of Gymsly. The company describes it as a fitness management platform built for gyms, health clubs, yoga studios, Pilates studios, CrossFit boxes, dance studios, martial arts academies and personal trainers.

According to the release, Gymsly combines membership management, class scheduling, billing, access control, staff payroll and member communication in one web dashboard. A companion member app is available on iOS, Android and the web.

This piece covers the company's own claims. I haven't tested the product, so treat the feature list as a pitch, not a verdict.

## The problem it targets: tool sprawl

The release says many fitness businesses still run operations across separate tools: one system for bookings, spreadsheets for attendance, and personal messaging apps for reminders. Gymsly is designed to replace that patchwork, with 20 modules reachable from a single login.

That is the useful lens for any owner. Every extra tool means another login, another place member data can go stale, and another thing a new staff member has to learn. Consolidation is attractive, though it also concentrates risk, so check how easily you could export your data later.

## What the feature list covers

The release lists these capabilities:

- **Membership management:** member profiles, plans and durations, check-in groups and tags.
- **Class scheduling:** timetables and bookings.
- **Billing and payments:** automated invoicing, renewals and receipts. The platform supports 16 currencies, including USD, GBP, EUR, INR, AED and AUD.
- **Access control:** QR code, RFID, Bluetooth and biometric check-in devices.
- **Staff and trainer tools:** shift scheduling and payroll, plus a trainer portal where coaches manage their clients and programmes.
- **CRM and communication:** a lead pipeline, plus automated reminders and broadcasts over WhatsApp, email and SMS.
- **Member app:** passwordless sign-in, a digital QR membership card and a class booking feed.
- **Business tools:** inventory and point of sale, analytics, financial forecasting, and a public website builder for the gym or studio.

## One notable payments decision

Gymsly says it deliberately does not take a cut of member payments. In the release, founder and CEO Zubair Ahmed said: "When a member pays, the money goes straight to the gym's bank, and the software they depend on should respect that." Whether that matters to you depends on what your current processor charges, so compare the fees line by line.

## Why one platform can suit different studio types

The company says Gymsly began as gym management software and has since been configured for a range of fitness business models. Per the release:

- Yoga and Pilates studios use it to run class timetables.
- CrossFit and martial arts academies use it for class management.
- Dance studios use it to manage memberships, attendance and grading groups.
- Personal trainers use it for one-to-one and small-group clients.
- Multi-location gym chains and corporate wellness programmes use its multi-branch controls and role-based permissions.

There are also dedicated editions for specific studio types, including Pilates Manage and Yoga Manage, both powered by Gymsly.

The practical takeaway: a studio with a fixed timetable and a gym with open-access membership have different daily needs. Before you commit to any all-in-one tool, write down your three most frequent admin tasks and check that the software handles each one well, not just on a feature checklist.

## Compliance and reach

The release says the platform supports data protection requirements including GDPR, CCPA, PIPEDA and India's DPDP Act, and follows PCI DSS standards for payments. It also supports local tax invoicing, including VAT, GST, SST and UPI payments. The company says it serves fitness businesses across 20 countries, including the United States, the United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, India, the United Arab Emirates, Germany, France, Spain, Singapore and South Africa.

If you operate across borders, that tax and privacy coverage is worth verifying directly with any vendor rather than assuming it from a press release.

## Pricing and trial

Per the release, plans start at $29 per month for up to 100 members and scale to custom enterprise plans for multi-location operators. A 14-day free trial is available with no credit card required. Details are at gymsly.fit.

## How to use this if you're shopping

A free trial with no card is a low-risk way to test any platform against your real week. Load your actual class timetable, run one billing cycle's worth of dummy invoices, and have a member try the app. If it saves you from juggling spreadsheets and message threads, it earns its place. If it doesn't, you've lost nothing but an afternoon.

Pick the pain point that costs you the most time each week, and judge every tool, Gymsly included, by whether it removes that first.
