EA FC 27's soundtrack runs to 120 songs from 36 countries, according to a feature from Goal. If you play football games for the menus as much as the matches, that's a lot of new music to dig through. Here's how to think about it, and why that playlist matters well beyond your headphones.

This is a First Look based on Goal's reporting, not a hands-on review. We haven't played the game for this piece, and the details below come from that feature. Check the official EA storefront for your platform and region before you buy, since this coverage doesn't establish release dates, prices, or editions.

## Treat the soundtrack as a discovery tool

Goal's framing is that the soundtrack is "a platform of music discovery...not just a game." That's a useful way to approach it. Instead of letting 120 tracks wash over you, you can use the game the way you'd use a streaming recommendation feed.

A few practical ways to do that:

- **Note what catches your ear.** When a track grabs you in the menus or between matches, check the in-game track info or look it up afterward so you don't lose it.
- **Sample outside your usual taste.** With 36 countries represented, per Goal, the playlist is a low-stakes way to hear genres and languages you wouldn't search for yourself.
- **Build your own list.** Pick your top five after a week of play. It's a better filter than shuffling all 120.

## Why a spot on the playlist matters to artists

Goal's piece centers on how a placement can change an artist's life. The outlet spoke to artists and to the curator behind the soundtrack about reaching new fans and turning childhood dreams into career breakthroughs.

That last part is worth sitting with. Many of the artists featured grew up playing these games, and being on the playlist means joining a tradition they remember from their own living rooms. For listeners, that means the songs you hear were chosen with real weight behind them, not as background filler.

We can't quote the artists here, since we only have Goal's summary of their conversations. If you want their own words on what the placement meant, the full Goal feature is the place to go.

## The work behind the 120

According to Goal, the playlist comes out of a meticulous, year-round curation process. It isn't assembled in a last-minute rush before launch. The curator, identified in the feature's angles as Steve Schnur, is central to that effort.

What does that mean for you? It suggests the tracklist is built as a whole, balancing different sounds and regions instead of stacking up similar songs. It's our read, not a claim from the feature, but a collection this size from this many countries looks designed to give almost any player a few songs they'll love and a few that surprise them.

## Why the nostalgia angle keeps coming up

Goal notes that nostalgia for past FIFA soundtracks drives plenty of social media conversation, and that the pressure to deliver an iconic playlist has never been higher. Anyone who has argued about which year had the best tracklist knows the feeling.

Here's a simple way to take part. Think of the songs you still associate with a specific game, then ask whether this year's playlist produces the same pull. Our take: you probably won't know on day one. The tracks that stick tend to be the ones you hear across dozens of matches, so give the soundtrack a few weeks before you judge it.

## Who this matters to most

**If you play for the football only,** you can mute the music and lose nothing, though you'd miss a free source of new songs.

**If you care about music,** the 36-country spread makes this one of the easier ways to hear artists you might not meet through a normal algorithm.

**If you remember older FIFA soundtracks fondly,** this is the playlist that will be measured against them, and it's fair to be a tough critic.

## What to do next

When you first launch EA FC 27, spend one session paying attention to the music instead of skipping it. Save three songs you didn't know before. That small habit turns a 120-track playlist into something personal, and it gives the artists behind it exactly what Goal says the placement is for: new listeners.
