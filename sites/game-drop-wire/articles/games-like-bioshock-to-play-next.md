BioShock 4 still has no confirmed release date, so if you want that mix of eerie setting, first-person exploration and choices that matter, you need to look elsewhere. Per a list from Svg, five games come closest: Prey, Atomic Heart, Dishonored, System Shock (Remake) and Alan Wake 2. Below is how to pick between them.

A note on coverage: this is a recommendation roundup, not a hands-on review. The picks come from Svg's list, and I haven't added platform, price or subscription details, so check your storefront or console store for current availability before you buy.

## Quick way to choose

Svg's list highlights what these games share with BioShock: immersive worlds, first-person gameplay, atmospheric storytelling, exploration-driven narratives, mysterious settings and player agency. Each also brings its own mechanics and themes. Use that as your filter:

- **Want the closest lineage?** Start with System Shock (Remake).
- **Want player choice front and center?** Try Prey or Dishonored.
- **Want a mysterious setting that pulls you forward?** Atomic Heart or Alan Wake 2 are your best bets.

That's my own sorting of the list, not a ranking from Svg, but it should save you some deliberating.

## System Shock (Remake): play the ancestor

If you're curious where BioShock's DNA comes from, this is the one. Svg's list includes the System Shock remake, and the fact that it's a remake matters for you: it gives newer players a way into the game that inspired BioShock without having to wrestle with an older release.

Who it's for: players who enjoy tracing a series back to its roots and want to see how the immersive sim style took shape.

## Prey: exploration and agency

Prey earns its spot by leaning on the traits the BioShock series is known for: a mysterious setting, exploration that drives the story, and room for you to decide how to handle situations. It's a sensible first pick if the thing you miss most about BioShock is poking around a strange place and piecing together what happened.

Who it's for: players who like to take their time, read the environment and make their own choices.

## Dishonored: choice-driven play

Dishonored is on the list for the same underlying reason BioShock fans love their favorites: player agency. It offers its own mechanics and themes rather than copying Rapture, so expect a different flavor of first-person play with the same sense that your decisions shape your experience.

Who it's for: anyone who wants more control over how they approach a scenario than a straight shooter gives them.

## Atomic Heart: a mysterious world to explore

Atomic Heart fits the atmospheric, mystery-forward side of the BioShock appeal. Its setting is the hook, and it's a good match if you remember BioShock mainly for the feeling of arriving somewhere unsettling and wanting to know why.

Who it's for: players who put atmosphere and world first and want an action-adventure that rewards curiosity.

## Alan Wake 2: the story-first outlier

Alan Wake 2 rounds out the list, and it's the pick that shows the breadth of what "like BioShock" can mean. Svg groups it with the others for its atmospheric storytelling and the way mystery, setting and character pull you in. If narrative matters more to you than any specific mechanic, this is where to look.

Who it's for: players who care most about story and mood.

## Why this list is useful right now

With BioShock 4 undated, Svg points to real demand from players for immersive, story-rich games in similar settings. That's the honest reason to dive into these: not because something is about to drop, but because they're games you can start today and finish well before any announcement.

## What to do next

Pick one based on what you miss most: lineage (System Shock), choice (Prey or Dishonored), setting (Atomic Heart) or story (Alan Wake 2). Check the platform and price on your own storefront, then start the one that matches. If you want a single place to begin, the System Shock remake shows you where BioShock came from.
