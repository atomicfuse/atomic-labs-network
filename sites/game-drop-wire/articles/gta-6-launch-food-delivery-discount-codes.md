GTA 6 is out, and per Mashable, DoorDash, Uber Eats and Grubhub have launched GTA 6-themed discount codes to catch the wave of players settling in for long sessions. If you're planning a marathon, here's what's reported, what it tells you, and how to set up your night so you aren't scrambling for dinner mid-mission.

## What's been reported

Mashable reports that all three major delivery apps, DoorDash, Uber Eats and Grubhub, have put out discount codes tied to the game's release. Mashable also notes that food delivery demand reportedly spiked around launch as gamers stayed up late and dug into extended play sessions.

The source material doesn't list the specific codes, discount amounts, expiration dates, or which regions and platforms they apply to. So we're not going to guess at them.

## Why the apps are doing this

This is the franchise's first new mainline game in 13 years, which makes the launch a cultural moment well beyond gaming. Brands are racing to attach themselves to that attention surge.

Our take: delivery apps know exactly when you're least likely to cook. A player who has waited more than a decade and cleared their evening is the ideal customer for a promo code. The timing makes sense even if the codes themselves are modest.

## How to find and use the codes

Since the details aren't confirmed in the reporting, verify before you rely on anything:

1. **Open each app directly.** Promotions usually appear in the app's deals or offers section. Check DoorDash, Uber Eats and Grubhub rather than trusting a code from a random post.
2. **Read the terms.** Look for minimum order amounts, restaurant restrictions, expiration dates and whether a code is limited to new customers.
3. **Check your region.** Availability can differ by location, and the reporting doesn't say where these offers apply.
4. **Compare the final total.** Fees and tips can erase a discount. Look at the checkout price across apps before you commit.
5. **Don't stack on assumptions.** If a code doesn't apply, it's better to find out before the order, not after your session starts.

## Plan the night before it starts

The reported demand spike is a useful warning. If many players are ordering late at the same time, expect the usual late-night crunch. A few practical moves:

- **Order before your session, not during it.** Placing an order before you boot up means you aren't pausing at a key moment.
- **Keep a backup.** Have snacks and drinks on hand in case delivery is slow.
- **Set a budget.** A long play session can turn into several orders. Decide your limit up front.

## The bigger picture

Mashable's report is a snapshot of how a blockbuster release now moves real-world spending, from food delivery to marketing campaigns. Brands treat a major launch like an event worth building promotions around.

For you, that means a small opportunity: the deals exist because companies want your attention. Use them if they save you money, ignore them if they don't.

## The takeaway

Before you start your first mission, open all three delivery apps, check the current offers, and place one order you've already priced out. Then put the phone down and play.
