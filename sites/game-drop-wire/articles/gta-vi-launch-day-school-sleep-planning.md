If you have school or work the morning after a huge game launch, you can still play without wrecking your week. The trick is deciding before launch night how much you're willing to trade for those first hours. A Connecticut report shows why that plan matters.

## What happened in Connecticut

Per The Hour, students in Connecticut went to school as *Grand Theft Auto VI* launched. One school official said, in the report's words: "Some students commented that they were tired after being up all night playing 'GTA6.'"

That's the extent of what we can confirm from the report here. We don't have attendance figures, district names, or details on how schools responded, so we won't guess at them. What the account does show is a familiar collision: one of the biggest entertainment releases in years landing on an ordinary school day.

This is coverage based on that news report. It isn't a review or a hands-on preview, and we're not making claims about the game itself. For launch dates, platforms, editions, and prices, check the publisher's announcements or your console's official storefront, since those details can vary by region.

## Decide your launch-night budget first

The students who felt it the next day likely made the same choice many players do: they kept going. You can avoid that by setting a limit before you press start.

- **Pick a hard stop time.** Choose it while you're rested, not at midnight when you're mid-mission.
- **Set an alarm for the stop, not just the wake-up.** A phone timer or your console's built-in play timer works.
- **Choose one goal for the night.** Finishing the opening hours, or just getting through setup and character basics, is more satisfying than vaguely playing until you crash.

## Get the boring setup done early

A surprising amount of launch-night time disappears into things that have nothing to do with playing.

- **Pre-load if your platform allows it.** Check your storefront for a pre-load option so you aren't waiting on a huge download.
- **Clear storage ahead of time.** Large games can force you to delete things at the worst moment.
- **Install updates and charge controllers.** Do it the day before.

Every hour you save on setup is an hour you can spend sleeping.

## If you're a student: protect the next day

Being tired in class is the cost the school official's comment points to. A few practical ways to limit it:

- **Split your sessions.** A big game doesn't expire at launch. Play after homework on day one, then more on the weekend.
- **Avoid spoilers on purpose.** If fear of missing out is what keeps you up, mute keywords on social apps for a few days and play at your own pace.
- **Keep a normal wake-up time.** Sleeping in after a late night often makes the next night harder too.

## If you're a parent: talk before launch, not after

A rule announced at 11 p.m. on release night rarely goes well. Sit down a few days ahead and agree on specifics: when play stops on a school night, what has to be finished first, and what the weekend looks like. Teens are more likely to stick to limits they helped set. Also check the game's age rating on the official storefront or rating board listing so you know what your household is actually agreeing to.

## Should schools expect a rough morning?

This is our take, not a reported finding: the Connecticut comment suggests teachers may want to expect some tired students after any blockbuster release. A little awareness goes a long way, whether that's a lighter first period or simply knowing why a class seems sluggish. Without more data, though, it's too early to say how widespread the effect was.

## The bottom line

Big launches are fun partly because everyone's playing at once, but the game will still be there tomorrow. Set your stop time tonight, do your downloads early, and treat the first week as a marathon rather than a sprint.
