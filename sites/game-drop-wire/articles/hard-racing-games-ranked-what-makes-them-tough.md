## Pick by the Kind of Hard You Want

CBR credits four ingredients for these games' reputations. Each points to a different kind of challenge, so use them to choose.

### Tight controls

If you like mastering a precise input scheme, look at the physics-driven entries. Trials Fusion is on the list, and the genre's appeal is that failure is quick and easy to understand: you see exactly where you crashed. That makes retrying feel less like punishment and more like practice.

### Aggressive AI and arcade pressure

Daytona USA sits at number 9. CBR's description notes that racers like it offered quite a challenge both in and out of the arcade. If you want a classic where the opposition and the clock keep you honest, this is the entry to start with.

### Realistic physics

iRacing, at number 5, represents the simulator end of the list. If your idea of difficulty is learning how a car really behaves rather than beating a scripted opponent, it's the one that fits. The rest of the list leans arcade or physics-driven, so the sim stands out.

### Unforgiving tracks

Redout 2 and Split/Second round out the lower half of the ranking. Our material doesn't spell out why each earned its spot, so check CBR's full write-up before assuming what the trouble is.

## Driver Is the One With a Reputation

Driver, at number 6, comes with a hook: CBR's angle notes call out its infamous tutorial challenge. If you've heard older players grumble about a game that was hard before it even started, this is likely the one they mean. It's a fair test of whether you're willing to learn the rules of a game that doesn't ease you in.

## Hard but Fair, or Just Frustrating?

This is the question worth asking as you play any of these. A hard-but-fair racer lets you see your mistake and fix it. A frustrating one hides the cause. The fast, visible failure of racing games helps here, since you can usually tell within a lap whether you lost to your own braking or to something the game did to you.

A practical way to test: give each game an hour. If you can name one specific thing you improved by the end, it's the good kind of hard. If you can only name what annoyed you, move on.
