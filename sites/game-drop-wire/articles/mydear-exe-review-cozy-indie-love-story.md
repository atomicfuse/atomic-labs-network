**Verdict: play it if you want a short, quiet, emotional indie.** *MyDear.exe* is a casual PC adventure that tells a 57-year love story through an old computer's interface, and it is best suited to players who prefer feeling to fighting. If you want mechanical depth or a challenge, look elsewhere. If you want a story that lands softly and then hits hard, this is your pick.

## What *MyDear.exe* actually is

The setup is simple and affecting. The game takes place inside an old PC. You play as Ellie, who explores a game made by her longtime partner, Lucien, in order to relive their 57-year love story.

That premise does a lot of work. A game inside a game, found on an aging machine, gives the story a built-in sense of memory. You are not watching a romance unfold from a distance. You are digging through something one person made for another.

Per Ladiesgamers, the result is a great mix of light game elements and excellent storytelling, delivered in a very subtle way. The reviewer's advice is blunt: keep the tissues handy.

## Why the file-explorer storytelling works

The review's strongest praise goes to the inventive use of a file-explorer-style interface as the storytelling device. My take: this is the right call, because folders and files are how most of us actually store our lives. Photos, old documents, forgotten saves. An interface people already associate with personal clutter feels more intimate than a custom menu ever could.

It also gives the game a natural structure. Rather than moving through a conventional world, you open things, find things, and piece together a relationship. The game turns the act of poking through a computer into emotional discovery, which suits a story about looking back across decades.

## Mini-games keep it from being a slideshow

Visual novels can drift into passive reading. According to the review, *MyDear.exe* avoids that with a variety of mini-games layered into the story. Those are the "light game elements" the review mentions.

Here is why that matters. Light interaction gives you small tasks between emotional beats, so the story is something you participate in rather than simply receive. The reviewer frames these as supporting the narrative, not competing with it, and that restraint is what I would want from a game this personal. A heavy mechanic would overpower a tale this gentle.

## The old-PC atmosphere

The review also credits the game's nostalgic computer ambience. The aesthetic is not just decoration. It reinforces the premise that this is a game Lucien made, on a machine that has aged along with the couple.

For anyone who remembers older desktops, that familiarity likely adds to the pull. For younger players, the setting still works as a memory box: a closed container you open slowly, learning what it holds.

## Why the subject matters

Most games centre on young heroes with a world to save. *MyDear.exe* centres on elderly characters, memory, and a love that has lasted 57 years. That is a rarer lane, and it is the main reason I would point readers toward it over another generic cozy pick.

Cozy, narrative-driven indies keep finding an audience among players who want intimate experiences rather than action-heavy releases, and this one fits that appetite while choosing a more mature emotional register than most.

## Who it's for, and who should skip it

**Best for:**
- Fans of cozy indies and visual novels who want an emotional story over a mechanical one
- Players who like experimental interface design, where the menus are the story
- Anyone looking for a short, heartfelt game that does not demand a big time commitment

**Probably skip if:**
- You want difficulty, long progression, or systems to master
- Emotional stories about aging and loss are not what you are in the mood for right now

The source describes it as a short casual indie, so expect a compact experience rather than something to sink dozens of hours into.

## The bottom line

We would put *MyDear.exe* on the list for anyone who likes small games with big feelings. Its central idea, a love story told by rummaging through an old computer, is clever because it turns something mundane into something tender. Ladiesgamers's review backs that up with praise for its storytelling, its mini-games, and its atmosphere.

It will not reinvent what a game can be, and it does not try to. It simply opens a folder and lets you find a life inside. Bring the tissues.
