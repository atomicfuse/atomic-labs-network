Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4 was the best-selling game in the US in October 2026, according to a report from Nintendo Life on the month's projected sales chart. That extends the franchise's streak to 19 consecutive years of topping the chart in its debut month. If you're deciding what to buy before the holidays, the takeaway is simple: the biggest seller isn't automatically the right pick for you, but the chart does tell you where the crowds will be.

This is a Gaming News piece based on reported chart data. We haven't played the game for this article, so there's no review verdict here.

## The headline result: Call of Duty wins again

Per Nintendo Life, Modern Warfare 4 took the top spot on the projected US best-sellers list for October. The report describes the series as dominant for 19 consecutive years in its launch month.

What that means for you in practice:

- **You'll find players.** A top-selling shooter tends to mean full lobbies, which matters if you play multiplayer with friends.
- **It's the safe social buy.** If your group chat is asking what everyone's playing, the sales numbers suggest many people are landing on the same answer.
- **It isn't a quality ranking.** Sales charts show what people bought, not what they enjoyed. If shooters aren't your thing, a 19-year streak doesn't change that.

## Switch 2 leads hardware

The same report notes that Switch 2 led US hardware sales for the month. Accessories also did well, with Nintendo's Zelda controller singled out for strong performance.

If you're shopping for a console, this suggests Nintendo's new system is moving quickly in the US. Per the report, the Zelda-themed controller is one of the accessories getting attention, which makes it worth a look if you already own a Switch 2 and want a gift-sized add-on.

A caution on interpretation: the chart is described as projected, and we don't have unit numbers from the report. Treat "led hardware" as a ranking, not a measure of how large the lead was.

## Other titles still showing strength

The report also points to year-to-date chart momentum for Black Ops II and Tomodachi Life: Living the Dream. That's a useful reminder that a game doesn't need to be the month's newest launch to keep selling. If you missed either one, they're clearly still finding buyers.

The contrast is worth noticing: one is a shooter, the other a life-sim. Together they suggest the charts are not just a one-genre story, even with Call of Duty on top.

## What to watch in November

Nintendo Life frames November as a turning point for the holiday race, with Zelda: Ocarina of Time and GTA 6 entering the sales conversation. We don't have release dates, platforms, or prices for either from the report, so check the official storefronts and publisher announcements before you plan purchases around them.

Our read, and it's only a read: with Call of Duty already banked and big names arriving next, November's chart is likely to look very different from October's. Don't be surprised if the order changes.

## How to use this before you buy

1. **Match the game to your habits.** If you play multiplayer shooters regularly, Modern Warfare 4's sales are a good sign for activity. If you don't, skip the hype.
2. **Confirm your platform.** Check the official storefront for your system and region before buying, since editions and availability can differ.
3. **Hold your budget for November.** If either Zelda: Ocarina of Time or GTA 6 is on your list, confirm their details first so you aren't buying twice in the same month.
4. **Think about gifts.** If someone you shop for has a Switch 2, the Zelda controller's reported strength makes it a reasonable accessory idea. Verify price and stock at a retailer.

Sales charts are a map of what everyone else is playing. Use them to find your crowd, then pick the game you'll still want to boot up in January.
