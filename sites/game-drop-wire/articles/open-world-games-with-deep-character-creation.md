If you pick open-world games partly by how much they let you shape your character, this list is for you. CBR recently published a ranking of the 10 best open-world games with character creation, arguing that the best creators make customization a core part of player agency, not a menu you rush through. The details we have cover four of those games: Mount & Blade II: Bannerlord, Code Vein II, Elden Ring, and Fallout 4. Here is what to look for in each, and how to decide which fits you.

This is a **What to Play** roundup based on CBR's reporting, not a hands-on review. We haven't ranked these ourselves, and the source's full list of ten isn't reproduced here. Check each game's official storefront for current platforms, editions, and prices before you buy.

## What CBR says makes a character creator great

Per CBR, customization can shape more than your looks. It can influence appearance, backstory, skills, and playstyle. That is a useful lens for choosing a game: ask whether the creator changes only how your character looks, or also how the game plays.

The piece also points to how players use these tools. Communities share builds, cosplay recreations, and roleplay concepts online, and CBR names Elden Ring and Cyberpunk 2077 as games that keep that customization culture alive.

## Mount & Blade II: Bannerlord

This is the pick for players who want their origins to matter. CBR includes Bannerlord on its list of open-world games with strong character creation, in the context of customization shaping backstory and skills alongside appearance.

**Best for:** roleplayers who like building a character's history and abilities from the start.

## Code Vein II

Code Vein II is on the list too. CBR's broader discussion mentions recreating anime characters in character creators, and that is the kind of creative use these tools invite. We can't confirm from our source material exactly which options Code Vein II offers, so look at current footage or the official store page to see how deep it goes.

**Best for:** players who enjoy detailed appearance tools and sharing their creations.

## Elden Ring

Elden Ring is one of the two games CBR specifically calls out for keeping customization culture alive. That fits what you see in the community, where players trade builds and recreate characters.

If you are new to it, your starting choices matter because creation here ties into how you play, not only how you look. One of the angles CBR's coverage highlights is ranking Elden Ring's classes for beginners, which suggests the starting class is worth a little homework before you commit.

**Best for:** players who want their build and playstyle to be part of the identity they create.

## Fallout 4

Fallout 4 rounds out the four games we can confirm from CBR's list. It fits the roleplay-focused case: a creator where you decide who your character is before the world starts reacting to you.

**Best for:** players who want to settle into a role and play it for a long stretch.

## How to choose between them

Use these quick filters:

- **You want to roleplay a backstory:** start with Bannerlord or Fallout 4.
- **You want to recreate a favorite character:** look closely at Code Vein II's creator.
- **You want your build to define how you play:** Elden Ring is the strongest fit here, and picking your starting class deserves research.

CBR's source material also floats a fun challenge worth stealing: pick a character you love and try recreating them in one of these games. It is a quick way to test how flexible a creator really is before you sink dozens of hours in.

## What we can't tell you yet

CBR ranked ten games, and we can only confirm four of them. We also can't say here how CBR ordered them, so treat this as a shortlist rather than a countdown. If a particular game's creator is your deciding factor, watch gameplay of the creation screen first.

Before you buy anything, spend ten minutes watching a creator walkthrough for your top pick. If the options excite you, you have your answer.
