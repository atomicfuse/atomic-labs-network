If the part of an RPG you enjoy most is the build screen, this list is for you. CBR recently highlighted turn-based RPGs with skill trees that leave room to build almost any kind of character. The outlet's original headline promised five games, but only four are covered here: Encased, King Arthur: Knight's Tale, Expeditions: Rome, and Fell Seal: Arbiter's Mark. Below is what each one offers and how to pick between them.

This is a **What to Play** roundup based on CBR's reporting, not hands-on review coverage. We haven't scored these games, and we're not listing release dates, prices, or platforms here. Check the official storefront for your system before you buy.

## What makes a skill tree "good" for build-crafters

Per CBR, the core argument is that the best RPG skill trees reward creative building. That can happen through class mixing, branching utility skills, or specialized party roles. Keep those three ideas in mind as you read, because each game below leans on a different one.

## Fell Seal: Arbiter's Mark: pick this for class mixing

If you want to experiment with combinations, start here. CBR groups Fell Seal: Arbiter's Mark among the games with skill trees that support wide experimentation. It's a tactics RPG, so your build choices play out on a grid, turn by turn.

**Best for:** players who like testing odd class pairings and seeing what sticks.

**How to approach it:** treat your first few characters as test subjects. Try a combination you'd normally dismiss and see how it holds up in a fight before you commit.

## Expeditions: Rome: pick this for party roles

Expeditions: Rome is on CBR's list for skill-tree depth and variety in how you shape your team. Since a turn-based party game is only as good as the roles in it, this is the one to consider if you enjoy deciding who tanks, who supports, and who finishes the fight.

**Best for:** players who think in terms of a whole squad rather than a single hero.

**How to approach it:** give each party member a clear job early. Specialization is where this style of skill tree tends to pay off.

## King Arthur: Knight's Tale: pick this for branching progression

King Arthur: Knight's Tale makes the list as a turn-based RPG with a skill tree that gives your characters room to grow in different directions. If you like branching paths where early choices shape what a character becomes, it's a strong candidate.

**Best for:** players who enjoy committing to a path and seeing it through.

**How to approach it:** before you spend points, look at where a branch leads. Planning a few steps ahead is half the fun of a tree like this.

## Encased: pick this for old-school RPG freedom

Encased rounds out the list. CBR's brief frames the group as offering real build freedom instead of rigid progression, and its angles point to comparisons with classics like Fallout and tactical RPGs. If you miss that kind of old-school progression, this is the natural place to look. That's our read on the framing, not a claim from CBR about Encased specifically.

**Best for:** players who want a character that feels like their own invention.

**How to approach it:** decide what kind of character you want to be before you start spending points, then see how far the tree lets you take that idea.

## How to choose between them

All four games are credited with strong skill trees, so the real question is what kind of building you enjoy:

- **Mixing and matching classes:** Fell Seal: Arbiter's Mark
- **Shaping a whole team:** Expeditions: Rome
- **Following a branching path:** King Arthur: Knight's Tale
- **Chasing old-school build freedom:** Encased

These pairings are our suggestions based on how CBR describes the common threads across the list. Treat them as a starting point, not a verdict.

## Why skill trees are a good way to pick your next RPG

CBR notes that RPG players are increasingly looking for meaningful build freedom over rigid progression paths, and that turn-based and tactics RPGs are getting renewed attention. A skill tree is a quick way to judge whether a game will hold up on a second playthrough. If a tree invites you to try something different next time, you've likely found a game with replay value.

## Your next step

Pick the one style of building that sounds most like you, check its store page for your platform, and start your first character with a plan you're willing to throw away. The best skill trees reward the player who experiments.
