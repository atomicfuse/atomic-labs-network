Sean Little has done what plenty of gamers have only daydreamed about. The Scottish painter and decorator has booked a week off work to play the new Grand Theft Auto game, and, per the BBC, he jokingly had his wife and daughter sign a "non-interference" contract so he can play uninterrupted.

It's a funny story, and it also holds a useful idea. If you're planning to disappear into GTA 6, a little negotiation with the people you live with beats a lot of apologizing later. Here's how to borrow the spirit of Little's contract and make it work in a real household.

## What Sean Little Actually Did

The BBC's profile frames Little as a long-time GTA fan who has waited 13 years since GTA 5. His plan has two parts: take the week off, and get the family on board in writing, even if the writing is tongue-in-cheek.

The BBC describes the contract as a joke. That matters. The point isn't a legal document, it's a playful way to say "this matters to me, and here's what I'm asking for." That's the part worth copying.

## Step 1: Book the Time Off Early

Little didn't try to squeeze the game into evenings. He cleared a full week. You don't need to match that, but the principle holds: a game this big is hard to enjoy in 45-minute bursts between chores.

If a full week isn't possible, consider:

- A long weekend instead of a week
- Taking the launch day itself off, then playing evenings
- Splitting leave into a few single days over the first couple of weeks

Pick whichever your job and family can realistically absorb, and request it before everyone else's holiday plans fill the calendar.

## Step 2: Have the Conversation Before Launch

The contract works as a joke because it opens a real conversation. Do the same, and do it days ahead rather than the night before.

Cover three things:

1. **When you'll be unavailable.** Be specific: "Saturday and Sunday afternoons," not "all week."
2. **When you will be available.** Meals, school runs, bedtime routines. Protecting those makes the rest easier to agree to.
3. **What counts as an interruption.** Emergencies obviously don't. "Where are the car keys?" probably should.

## Step 3: Write a Mock Contract (Yes, Really)

Humor lowers the stakes. A one-page, silly agreement gets a smile where a stern speech gets an eye-roll. Here's a template you can adapt:

- **The Player agrees to:** cook dinner on two nights, handle the school run, and surface for every family meal.
- **The Household agrees to:** hold non-urgent questions during agreed play blocks.
- **Both parties agree to:** a review on day three, with free renegotiation.
- **Signed:** everyone, with dramatic flourish.

Notice the trade. Little's version, as reported, asked his wife and daughter for non-interference. A fairer version gives something back. That's the difference between a joke that lands and one that quietly irritates.

## Step 4: Offer Trades, Not Just Requests

If you want uninterrupted playtime, pay for it in something people value. Ideas that cost little:

- Take over a chore someone else dislikes for the week
- Plan a family outing for the weekend after launch
- Let them choose the movie or takeaway on a set night

You could also ask the people in your life what they'd want in exchange. The answers are often cheaper than you expect.

## Step 5: Build in Real Breaks

A week of heavy play sounds great in theory. In practice, your eyes, back and patience have limits. Set a loose routine: stand up each hour, get outside once a day, eat something that isn't snacks. You'll enjoy the game more on day five if you haven't burned out on day two.

## Is Taking Leave for a Game Reasonable?

Some people will raise an eyebrow. Here's our take, not the BBC's: people book leave for festivals, sports tournaments and film marathons without anyone blinking. Gaming is leisure like any other, and a game that fans have waited 13 years for is a fair reason to carve out time. The BBC's story points to a wider conversation about adult fandom and how families negotiate hobbies and screen time, and the answer is usually the same: communicate early, share the load.

## Keep the Joke, Keep the Peace

What makes Little's story charming is that it's a gag with an honest message underneath: this game matters to me. You can say the same without a lawyer. Pick your dates, draft a goofy agreement, offer something in return, and get signatures before launch day. Then, when you finally load into Vice City, nobody has to wonder where you went.
