If you just wrapped Peacock's *Crystal Lake* and still want more doomed counselors and dread in the woods, there's a game for that. Vice recommends Supermassive Games' *The Quarry* as the next stop for *Friday the 13th* fans, and the pitch is simple: it's a slasher movie you actually control.

Below, you'll get why the two fit together, what *The Quarry* does with classic camp-horror tropes, and how to approach it if you want to play it like a fan of the genre.

## Why The Quarry Is the Natural Follow-Up

Per Vice, *Crystal Lake* has renewed interest in Pamela Voorhees, the Jason mythology, and camp-set slashers in general. If that mood hooked you, *The Quarry* delivers much of the same atmosphere.

Vice points to several shared ingredients:

- A summer-camp setting
- Camp counselors who are in serious trouble
- Romantic drama among the cast
- The feeling of being hunted in the woods

That's the core *Friday the 13th* checklist, and *The Quarry* hits it. Its setting is Hackett's Quarry, and its description from Vice is blunt: a bloody nightmare where your decisions determine who survives, and who never leaves camp.

## The Difference: You Make the Calls

Watching a slasher is a spectator sport. You yell at the screen when someone splits up from the group or opens the door they absolutely shouldn't. *The Quarry* hands you that power.

As Vice frames it, this is interactive survival horror. The choices you make shape which counselors make it through and which ones never leave. That's what makes it a good companion to a series like *Crystal Lake*: the same tropes you've been watching play out become decisions on your plate.

### Slasher tropes become player choices

The genre runs on moments where a character makes a questionable call. In a game built around choice, those moments are yours to handle. Do you play it smart, or do you let a romance or a bad instinct steer you toward trouble? That tension is where the fun lives.

## How to Play It Like a Slasher Fan

The source article doesn't lay out a step-by-step survival guide, so I won't pretend to hand you a decision-by-decision cheat sheet. But the setup suggests two good ways to approach it, and these are my take rather than anything Vice prescribes.

### Option one: try to save everyone

If you've spent a whole series watching counselors get picked off, keeping the entire group alive is a satisfying reversal. Treat every choice as if the genre's usual rules don't apply to you.

### Option two: lean into the genre

Play it the way a slasher would script it. Follow the romance, ignore the warnings, and see who survives. It's a different experience, and probably the more fun one on a first run if you love the movies' pitch-black humor.

Either way, because your decisions determine the outcomes, the game rewards a second playthrough with a different attitude.

## Crystal Lake vs. Hackett's Quarry

The two share DNA, but they scratch different itches.

- ***Crystal Lake*** is where the Voorhees legacy lives. It feeds your appetite for Pamela and Jason lore.
- ***The Quarry*** gives you the camp-counselor side of the equation: the people running through the trees, with their relationships and mistakes front and center.

One is something you watch; the other puts you inside the group. If the series left you wanting to be in the woods rather than just observe them, that's the gap *The Quarry* fills.

## Who Should Pick It Up

This is for you if:

- You enjoyed *Crystal Lake* and want more camp-set horror.
- You like seeing how a story branches based on your choices.
- You'd rather steer a horror plot than sit back and watch it.

It's less obviously for you if you want a *Friday the 13th* game starring Jason himself. Vice's recommendation is about mood, setting, and structure, not a direct adaptation of the franchise.

## Your Next Move

Don't let the *Crystal Lake* credits end the trip. Start *The Quarry* this week, pick a counselor to protect, and see how long you can keep the whole cabin alive.
