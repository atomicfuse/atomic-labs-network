I'm not going to hand you a score for Grand Theft Auto VI that I haven't earned. I can't verify the finished game's missions, performance or online systems from my own knowledge, and a review that invents them would be worth nothing. What I can do is tell you how I'll judge it, where I'm already excited, and where Rockstar has to prove itself. Think of it as the scorecard I'm taking into launch day.

## The Criterion: Does It Justify a 13-Year Wait?

GTA V arrived in 2013. Every year since, the bar has risen, and Rockstar kept feeding us GTA Online instead of a sequel. So my one criterion is simple: **does GTA VI feel like a generational leap in how a living world plays, not just how it looks?** Pretty is a given. Rockstar has never shipped an ugly game.

## What Has Me Genuinely Hyped

### Vice City and Leonida Look Alive

Per Rockstar's own trailers, the game is set in the state of Leonida, with a modern Vice City at its heart. The footage leans into crowds, phone-camera culture, beaches, swamps and traffic. That matters because the best Rockstar worlds feel like places that would exist without you. If the final game delivers that density outside the cutscenes, it wins me on atmosphere alone.

### Two Protagonists, Lucia and Jason

Rockstar has confirmed Lucia and Jason as the leads, framed in the trailers as a criminal duo. I like this because GTA V's three-hero switching was brilliant for heists but thin on emotional stakes. A tighter pair could give the story a spine. The risk is that the writing tries too hard to be a Bonnie-and-Clyde cliché, and I'll be watching that closely.

### The Rockstar Polish Factor

Red Dead Redemption 2 proved this studio will obsess over small things: how a character walks through a doorway, how weather changes a mood. That track record is the best argument that the long development was spent on craft and not just on bloat.

## What Worries Me Before I Play

### 1. The Online Shadow

GTA Online made a fortune, and it's hard not to wonder how much of the sequel's design bends around it. My test: can I finish the single-player campaign without being nudged toward a storefront or a grind? If the story feels like a tutorial for the multiplayer, that's a failure.

### 2. Platform Access

Rockstar announced the game for PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S first, with no PC version confirmed in its announcements that I'm aware of. As a site that cares about PC games, I find that frustrating. Waiting is a pattern with Rockstar, but it still stings, and it deserves to be called out.

### 3. Delays and Expectations

Rockstar has pushed the release date back, with the most recent date I know of being in November 2026. Delays often mean polish, and I'd rather have that than a broken launch. But a hype cycle this long sets expectations nobody can meet. Some of the disappointment will be the fans' fault, not the game's.

### 4. Satire That Might Have Lost Its Edge

The series built its name on mocking American excess. In an era when real social media is already a parody, the question is whether the jokes still bite or just wink. If every radio ad and billboard feels like a reference rather than a punchline, I'll notice.

## The Five Tests I'm Scoring

- **World reactivity:** Do NPCs and systems respond to my chaos in surprising ways?
- **Mission variety:** Does the campaign avoid the "drive here, shoot that" loop?
- **Story and characters:** Do Lucia and Jason earn my attention beyond the trailers?
- **Technical stability:** Is the performance smooth on standard consoles, not just the best-case setup?
- **Respect for the player:** Is the single-player free of monetization pressure?

## My Verdict Going In

I want this to be great, and the evidence from Rockstar's past work says it probably will be. But a wait of 13 years doesn't buy a free pass. It raises the price of every dull mission, every recycled gag and every pop-up reminding me to spend money. If GTA VI makes its world feel genuinely alive and keeps its single-player honest, it earns its place at the top of console gaming. If it doesn't, no amount of neon will cover it.

When I've actually played the finished game, you'll get the real review, with a real score and the real complaints. Until then, treat anyone who gives you one early with a healthy dose of suspicion.
