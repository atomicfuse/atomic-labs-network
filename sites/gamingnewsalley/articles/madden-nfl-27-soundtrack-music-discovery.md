Before you ever snap the ball in Madden NFL 27, the game is already doing something it has quietly done for decades: putting music in your ears that you weren't expecting to love. EA Sports confirmed the full 80-song soundtrack ahead of the game's August 13 worldwide launch on PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X|S, PC and Nintendo Switch — and per Billboard, who got an early look at the complete tracklist, it's the biggest in franchise history.

That number alone tells a story. Madden doesn't just license music anymore. It curates culture.

## Why Madden Became a Legitimate Music Platform

Think about the pipeline. Tens of millions of players — many of them 18-to-24-year-olds who haven't finished building their taste yet — boot up a sports game and sit in menus, ride loading screens and grind through franchise mode. Hours accumulate. Songs repeat. Hooks embed themselves. A track you'd never seek out on a streaming service becomes the sound of a specific autumn, a specific season, a specific win.

That's not an accident. The franchise has long understood that its audience and hip-hop's core audience overlap almost completely. The result is that Madden has operated as a low-friction discovery engine for Black music and culture for years, reaching young fans who might not follow the same blogs or playlists their older siblings relied on. When the game succeeds at this, it functions less like a sports title and more like a cultural time capsule you can load up a decade later and get hit with a rush of memory.

Madden NFL 27's tracklist is built with that history in mind.

## What the Madden NFL 27 Tracklist Actually Looks Like

The confirmed portion of the soundtrack reads like a deliberate cross-section of where music is in 2025, not a single-genre safe play. You've got hip-hop anchors sitting alongside rock, country and everything in between.

On the rap side, the list carries real weight. **Nas & DJ Premier** contribute "GiT Ready" — two legends whose pairing alone signals the franchise is still interested in credibility, not just chart position. **Hit-Boy & The Alchemist** bring "Business Merger," a collaboration between two of the most respected producers working today. **Isaiah Rashad** appears with "M.O.M," keeping the underground-to-mainstream pipeline open. **French Montana & Max B** submit "Ever Since U Left Me (I Went Deaf)," while **Quavo** shows up on **BNYX®**'s "HunchO STEP!"

The throwback thread is real too. **DJ Shadow & Mos Def's** "Six Days (Remix)" and **Gnarls Barkley's** "Line Dance" are the kind of selections that reward older players while introducing those artists to anyone who missed them the first time around. **Diplo** pulls double duty with both "Trippy Mane" (featuring Project Pat) and "Flashlight" (featuring Project Pat and Juicy J) — a southern rap injection that feels intentional.

Outside the hip-hop lane, **Danger Mouse, Black Thought & Rag'n'Bone Man** contribute "UP," which sounds exactly as genre-bending as that lineup suggests. **Lamb of God** bring "Into Oblivion" for the rock contingent. **Luke Combs** slots in with "My Kinda Saturday Night," and **Ella Langley** offers "Dandelion" — both pointing to a conscious effort to make the soundtrack legible to football fans who don't live in hip-hop spaces.

**Don Toliver**, **Leon Thomas**, **Armani White** and **Alex Warren** fill out a younger, more R&B-adjacent tier that tracks exactly where streaming numbers are pointing right now.

And that's only the confirmed portion of 25-plus tracks from a list that reportedly runs 80 deep.

## The Underrated Artists Worth Watching

If Madden has a consistent superpower, it's the mid-list slot — the artist who isn't headlining but gets a song placed and suddenly has a new audience of millions. A few names in this tracklist fit that profile.

**Armani White** has been building momentum with a sharp ear for hooks, but "SAY IT TO ME." in a Madden context could accelerate his timeline meaningfully. **Dee Mula's** "Made It Out Alive" is similarly positioned — the kind of track that rewards repeat listens in a way loading screens are uniquely good at delivering. **Hiruy Tirfe**, featuring DJ Aktive, Keith Phelps and Khemist on the "Ben Franklin Remix," represents exactly the kind of deeper-cut placement that turns Madden into a credibility signal for artists working outside the mainstream spotlight.

History says at least one of these names is going to land differently because of this tracklist. That's been true of nearly every Madden release for the past 20 years.

## What This Soundtrack Says About Where Gaming Sits in Culture

Madden NFL 27 arriving with 80 songs isn't just a flex — it's a statement about the role video games now play alongside streaming services, social media and sports broadcasts as places where people actually encounter new music. The franchise is old enough that players who grew up with its soundtracks are now adults with disposable income and nostalgia to spend. EA seems to understand that the emotional connection to Madden's music is a feature worth doubling down on, not a side effect to manage.

The tracklist doesn't try to be everything at once by accident. It spans Nas and Luke Combs, Lamb of God and Ella Langley, because the people buying Madden span exactly that range. Leaning into that breadth, rather than chasing one demographic, is what has kept the franchise's soundtrack culturally relevant while other sports games treat their music as an afterthought.

Madden NFL 27 hits PS5, Xbox Series X|S, PC and Nintendo Switch on August 13. Before you check the ratings or the new features, queue up the soundtrack. There's a good chance something on that list will still be in your head by September.
