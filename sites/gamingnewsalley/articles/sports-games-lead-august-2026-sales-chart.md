U.S. video game spending fell 9% in August 2026, but sports games barely noticed. According to Circana's report, as covered by GamesBeat, the overall dip came from weaker content, mobile and hardware sales, while NBA 2K27, Madden NFL 27 and EA's Kickoff Bundle dominated the charts. Read on and you'll know what the slump looked like, which games held up, and why annual sports franchises keep proving so durable.

## What the August numbers say

The headline figure is a 9% decline in total U.S. spending. Per GamesBeat's summary of the Circana data, three areas dragged it down: content, mobile and hardware.

The broader context matters here. The report points to console prices at record highs and a sharp drop in mobile spending, which together suggest a more cautious consumer. When hardware costs more, fewer people jump into a new console, and that can ripple into software and add-on spending.

That's an interpretation, not something the report proves on its own, but the pattern fits the numbers.

## The August top 10

Here is the ranking as reported, with publishers:

1. NBA 2K27 (Take-Two Interactive)
2. Madden NFL 27 (Electronic Arts)
3. Marvel Tokon: Fighting Souls (Sony)
4. Kickoff Bundle, 2026 (Electronic Arts)
5. Star Wars: Zero Company (Electronic Arts)
6. Halo: Campaign Evolved (Microsoft)
7. Elden Ring (Bandai Namco Entertainment)
8. Big Walk (Panic)
9. Mario Kart World (Nintendo)
10. Splatoon Raiders (Nintendo)

Sports took the top two slots and a bundle from EA sat at fourth. Electronic Arts shows up three times in the top five, counting the bundle and Star Wars: Zero Company.

## Why sports games hold up in a down month

The reporting doesn't spell out the reasons, so what follows is my read of the chart.

**They're a yearly habit.** Madden and NBA 2K release new editions on a schedule. Rosters, ratings and modes refresh, and players who bought last year's edition often buy the new one. Demand is predictable in a way a new franchise's never is.

**They're easy to justify.** If your budget is tight, a game you'll play for months with friends feels safer than a gamble on something unproven.

**They reach beyond core gamers.** Fans of the real sports are a built-in audience, and that audience doesn't shrink just because the broader market does.

The takeaway for you: when you read that the market is down, check which segments are down. A 9% drop overall can still coexist with a record-strength release at the top.

## The surprises further down the chart

The top two aren't shocking. The rest of the list is more interesting.

### A fighting game at number three

Marvel Tokon: Fighting Souls, from Sony, placed third. A licensed fighter landing right behind the two big sports titles says something about the pull of recognizable IP.

### Big Walk breaks into the top 10

Panic's Big Walk landed at eighth, ahead of two Nintendo releases. It's a smaller-scale game placing next to major publishers' output. The GamesBeat summary floats streamer-friendly appeal as a possible reason, though that's a suggested angle rather than a confirmed driver.

### Elden Ring still sells

Elden Ring appeared at seventh. One angle in the coverage ties its presence to a Switch bump and what that says about ports. The data supplied doesn't detail the size of that bump, so treat it as a thread worth watching: older hits arriving on new platforms can find a fresh audience.

### Nintendo holds two slots

Mario Kart World and Splatoon Raiders closed out the list at ninth and tenth. Nintendo's presence is steady even as hardware spending softens.

## Are console prices the real problem?

Circana's numbers show hardware sales weakening alongside record-high console prices. That's correlation, and the report summary doesn't say price alone caused the drop. Still, it's hard not to connect the two. A pricier console means a bigger commitment before you buy a single game.

If you're deciding whether to buy hardware now, one practical approach: list the games you'd play in the next six months and see how many you'd actually buy. If the answer is a couple of sports titles, you may already own a platform that handles them.

## What to watch next

Two things will show whether August was a blip or a trend. First, whether mobile spending keeps falling after that sharp drop. Second, whether hardware sales recover or stay depressed while prices stay high.

Sports games, meanwhile, look likely to keep doing what they do. If you're tracking the market, look past the total and watch what sits at the top of the chart. That's where the real signal lives.
