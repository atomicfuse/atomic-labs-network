Cbr recently ranked the 10 hardest '90s arcade games of all time, and the list leans on fighting games and beat 'em ups that, in the outlet's words, terrorized players with extreme difficulty. By the end of this piece, you'll understand the design logic behind that brutality and have a simple way to test it yourself without burning through a roll of quarters.

One caveat up front: the ranking covers ten games, but the portion available to me names seven of them (spots 10 through 4). So this article sticks to those seven and doesn't guess at the top three.

## The Seven Games Cbr Named

Here's the stretch of the countdown we can confirm, per Cbr:

- **10.** Alien vs. Predator
- **9.** Mortal Kombat II
- **8.** Killer Instinct
- **7.** Skull Fang
- **6.** Street Fighter II
- **5.** Total Carnage
- **4.** Dragon's Lair II

The mix is telling. You get beat 'em ups, fighters, a shooter and a game built around a famously punishing style. Different genres, same goal: keep you reaching into your pocket.

## Why Arcade Difficulty Was a Business Decision

Arcade cabinets earned money per play. A game that you could beat on one credit was a game that stopped making money for the operator. So difficulty wasn't only about challenge, it was about monetization, mastery and frustration, which is exactly the lens Cbr's list invites.

That's my read, not a statement from the developers, but it explains why the list reads the way it does. A game built to be "fair" at home would have been a bad deal in an arcade.

### What the design tends to look like

The genres on this list share a few tricks you can spot when you play:

- **Enemies that punish hesitation.** Beat 'em ups and shooters like Alien vs. Predator and Total Carnage throw crowds at you, so health drains while you're still learning.
- **Opponents that demand real execution.** In fighters such as Mortal Kombat II, Killer Instinct and Street Fighter II, the computer opponent is the gatekeeper. You either learn the game's systems or you pay again.
- **Rules that feel strict by design.** Dragon's Lair II sits on the list as a reminder that difficulty can come from demanding precision rather than from sheer enemy numbers.

I'm describing genre tendencies here, not claiming specifics about any single boss or move. Cbr's reasons for each ranking aren't something I can quote.

## How to Test One Yourself on a Credit Budget

Nostalgia for these cabinets is still strong, and collections, emulation and re-releases make many classics easy to revisit. Here's a way to turn that into a real challenge.

### Step 1: Pick one game and one budget

Choose a single title from the list. Set a hard cap, say three credits, and write it down. Without a cap, any game feels beatable because you can brute-force it.

### Step 2: Play the first session to learn, not to win

Spend your first run noting what kills you. Is it getting swarmed? A specific opponent? A timing window? Naming the problem is half the fix.

### Step 3: Change one thing per run

Swap a habit each attempt: back off earlier, stop mashing, or stay at range. If you change everything at once, you won't know what worked.

### Step 4: Score yourself honestly

Track how far you got on each credit. Progress on a budget shows mastery better than a lucky clear.

## Which Opponents Felt Most Unfair?

This is where the list becomes a fun argument. Fighting-game computer opponents and beat 'em up crowds are the usual suspects when people talk about cheap arcade difficulty. Decide your own standard: is a game hard because it demands skill, or because it cheats? Play a couple of the titles above and see where you land.

## How This Compares With Modern "Hard" Games

It's tempting to line these up against today's punishing games, such as Soulslikes. The big difference, in my view, is the cost of failure. Modern hard games generally ask for your time and patience. Arcade games asked for money each time you lost. That changes how the frustration feels: one is a challenge you chose, the other was a toll booth.

Neither is better, but it explains why a retro run with a limited credit budget feels so different from grinding a modern boss.
