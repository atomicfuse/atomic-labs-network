West Virginia hosts Houston in a Big 12 matchup, and if you're making the trip to Morgantown, you can do more than tailgate. Per Fox News, late autumn is a strong time to explore the area's Appalachian trails, river gorges and waterfalls. Below are four spots the outlet highlights, plus a way to choose among them based on how much time you have and how close you want to stay to campus.

One caveat up front: the source gives us the names and general character of these places, not trail lengths, drive times or difficulty ratings. Check current conditions and details with the managing agency before you go.

## If You Only Have Time for One: Coopers Rock State Forest

This is the headliner. Fox News points to Raven Rock Trail in Coopers Rock State Forest for views over the Cheat River Canyon, and it describes November as a particularly good moment for Morgantown hiking.

Why does a canyon view matter to a curious traveler? Because it shows you what the landscape actually is. The Cheat River has cut a gorge through the Appalachian plateau, and a lookout lets you read that geology at a glance: the river below, the steep walls, the ridgelines beyond. That's the kind of context a stadium visit can't give you.

Plan this as your main outing. Give it a morning if kickoff is later, and bring layers, since a canyon rim in late November can feel considerably colder than town.

## If You Want a Waterfall: Valley Falls State Park

Fox News also names Valley Falls State Park for its waterfalls. Falling water is a good reason to go in the cooler months, when the surrounding trees are bare or turning and the scene is less crowded with summer foliage.

A few practical habits help here:

- Wear shoes with real grip. Wet rock near falls is slick, especially with leaf litter.
- Check the park's current hours and any trail closures before driving out.
- Bring a phone or camera with a charged battery. Falls and gorge scenery are the sort of views people want to photograph.

## If You Want to Stay Close to Town: Deckers Creek Rail Trail

Not every fan wants a long day away from campus. Deckers Creek Rail Trail is on Fox News's list, and rail trails as a type are generally built on former rail corridors, which tends to make for gentler, more predictable walking than a rugged forest path. The source doesn't give specifics for this trail, so confirm access points and surface conditions locally.

This is a sensible pick if you're traveling with people who aren't avid hikers, or if you want a walk to loosen up before a long afternoon in the stands.

## If You Want Nature Without Leaving Campus: Core Arboretum

Core Arboretum rounds out the list, and Fox News frames the area's nature as campus-accessible. That's the lowest-effort option: a green space you can fit between breakfast and game-day plans without renting a car or planning a full outing.

An arboretum is also a quiet lesson in local ecology. Labeled plantings let you learn what grows in this part of Appalachia, which makes the autumn color more than a pretty backdrop.

## How to Choose

Here's a simple way to match a trail to your weekend:

- **Half a day or more, want the big view:** Coopers Rock State Forest and Raven Rock Trail.
- **A couple of hours, want something distinctive:** Valley Falls State Park.
- **Easy walk, close to town:** Deckers Creek Rail Trail.
- **Under an hour, minimal logistics:** Core Arboretum.

If you're doing two, pair the long outing with the short one on separate days so you aren't racing the clock before kickoff.

## Build the Weekend Around Daylight

Late November days are short, so put your biggest hike earliest, ideally the morning before a game or the day after. Save the arboretum or rail trail for when time is tight. Decide on your trail before you leave the hotel, check conditions, and you'll have a better Morgantown weekend than the scoreboard alone can promise.
