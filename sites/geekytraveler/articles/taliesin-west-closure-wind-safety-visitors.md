## How to stay safer at open-air and historic sites

None of this is a prediction of danger at any particular place. These are sensible habits that cost you little.

### Check conditions before you leave

Look at the forecast for wind, not just rain and temperature. Many people check only the high and the chance of showers. Wind advisories and gust forecasts matter far more at sites with roofs, canopies, scaffolding, or loose structures overhead.

### Confirm the site is open that day

Many tours operate on set schedules and may change or cancel when conditions turn. Check the official website or call the day of your visit, and again on your way if the weather is shifting.

### Listen to your guide and follow instructions immediately

If a guide asks the group to move indoors, change route, or leave an area, do it right away and without lingering for one more photo. Guides know the site and are the first line of communication.

### Notice what is above and around you

When the wind picks up, pay attention to roofing, awnings, signs, tents, trees, and anything lightweight that could be lifted. Move away from them, ideally toward solid, enclosed shelter, and not along the edge of a structure or under an overhang.

### Don't treat a tour as a reason to stay put

Groups can feel committed to finishing an itinerary. If you feel unsafe, say so to your guide and move to shelter. A shortened tour is a small cost.
