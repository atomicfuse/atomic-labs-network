AAA's holiday travel advice and its Thanksgiving forecast made the rounds this week, as reported by CBS News. Tucked into that coverage was a forward-looking detail: cruises are expected to be a major travel choice in 2027. If you're juggling holiday-week logistics now, that trend is worth understanding, because it explains a lot about how travelers are changing the way they plan.

This piece sticks to what AAA's guidance, as relayed by CBS News, supports. You'll leave with a clear sense of why bundled trips are gaining ground and how to borrow that logic for any trip, cruise or not.

## What AAA Is Signaling About 2027

Per CBS News, AAA expects cruises to be a major vacation choice in 2027. The report doesn't give a detailed breakdown here, so treat this as a directional signal rather than a hard number.

Still, the signal matters. Holiday travel is a peak stress point for families: crowded roads, crowded airports, and shifting vacation habits all shape how people plan. A trip type that reduces the number of decisions is a natural beneficiary of that stress.

## Why Bundled Trips Appeal

A cruise packages several things that normally require separate bookings: transportation between destinations, lodging, and a good share of the daily logistics. That's our analysis, not a claim from AAA, but it fits the broader shift the coverage points to: toward bundled, easier-to-plan trips.

Think about what a typical land vacation asks of you:

- Booking a flight or planning a drive
- Reserving a hotel in each location
- Figuring out how to move between stops
- Coordinating everyone's schedule on the ground

A bundled trip collapses many of those decisions into one. For a family where one person usually does all the planning, that's a real draw.

## Steal the Logic Even If You Never Cruise

You don't need a cruise ship to plan like a cruiser. The appeal is fewer moving parts, and you can build that into any trip.

### Consolidate your bookings

Look for ways to reduce the number of separate reservations. Fewer bookings means fewer places for something to go wrong during a busy travel period.

### Decide the big pieces early

Holiday weeks are crowded on roads and in airports, per the coverage. Settling transportation and lodging first leaves less to improvise when everything is at its most congested.

### Build in the budget up front

Budget-conscious travelers benefit from knowing the major costs before departure. Bundled options make that easier because more of the price is visible at booking. Compare what's actually included before assuming any package is cheaper.

## Holiday-Week Basics Worth Remembering

AAA's holiday tips and Thanksgiving forecast were the heart of the CBS News report, but the specific figures and dos and don'ts aren't something I can responsibly reproduce here. For exact numbers on expected travelers, peak driving times, and flight-day advice, check AAA directly. What the coverage does establish is the underlying problem: roads and airports are strained, and planning ahead is the best defense.

A few general habits follow from that, offered as common-sense advice rather than AAA's wording:

- Check your route and your flight status before you leave, not after.
- Give yourself more buffer than you think you need.
- Keep the family's plans simple on the busiest days.

## The Bigger Pattern Behind the Headline

The interesting part isn't cruises themselves. It's what the preference reveals: travelers are valuing ease. When roads are packed and airports are chaotic, a trip that arrives pre-organized looks very good.

If you're the detail-obsessed type, that's an invitation. Ease and depth aren't opposites. Once logistics are settled, you have more attention left for the things that make a place worth visiting: why a port exists where it does, what a harbor's architecture was built to handle, what the local history actually is.

## Your Next Step

Before you plan anything for 2027, pick the one part of your usual trip that causes the most stress, and find a way to bundle or settle it first. Then spend the attention you saved on the destination itself.
