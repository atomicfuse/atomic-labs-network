Fandom merch is easy to buy and easy to regret. With several new *A Court of Thorns and Roses* collaborations out now, you can spend a lot before you've finished the chapter. This guide shows you how to sort the collabs by how much you'll actually use them, and how to set a limit before you add anything to your cart.

## Start with a fandom budget, not a wish list

Pick a number before you browse. Merch is built to feel like a limited moment, and a cap is the simplest defense.

1. **Set a total.** Decide what you'd happily spend on ACOTAR items this month. Treat it like any other discretionary category.
2. **Split it.** Give most of it to one item you'll use often, and a small slice to something fun.
3. **Wait 48 hours.** Put your picks in a cart or saved list and come back. What still excites you is probably worth buying.

## Sort the five by cost per use

I can't give you prices, but I can give you a way to rank them. Ask how often you'd use the item, not how much you love the series.

### Everyday wear: Knix and For Love & Lemons

Knix is known for intimates, and For Love & Lemons is a fashion brand. Both are things you wear, which makes them good candidates if you want fandom in your regular rotation. Before you buy, check whether the item fits your existing wardrobe. A piece you'd only wear as a costume is a different purchase from one you'd wear weekly.

### The subtle wearable: Starfall jewelry

Jewelry is the easiest merch to wear in public without a cover-art T-shirt announcing it. Small pieces can go with almost anything, so the cost per wear can drop quickly. Read the materials and return policy first, since that tells you how long it will last.

### The low-risk picks: Target and Barnes & Noble

Mass retailers are usually the first place to look when you want a smaller purchase or a gift. Per The Cut, both carry ACOTAR merch. Compare these against what you'd pay at a specialty brand for a similar item, and note that I can't say what either store stocks beyond that.

## Ways to pay less

- **Check for retailer sales and coupon codes** before checkout. Search the brand name plus "promo code," and sign up for the email list only if you'll use the welcome offer.
- **Use store perks.** If you shop at Target or Barnes & Noble already, look at whether a loyalty program, store card, or membership discount applies. Only use a card discount if you pay it off in full each month.
- **Buy one thing at a time.** Splitting purchases across brands makes it easy to lose track of the total. Keep a running tally in your notes app.
- **Look at the return window** before you order, especially for clothing and intimates where fit matters.

## Gifting a romantasy reader

If you're buying for someone else, the same rule applies: match the item to how they live. A reader who wears jewelry daily will likely get more from a Starfall piece than from a novelty item. A cozy reading accessory suits someone who's always curled up with a book. When in doubt, ask them which item from the collabs they've already looked at, or choose something with an easy return.

## A simple decision test

Before you buy, run each item through three questions:

1. Will I use it at least a few times a month?
2. Would I still want it if it weren't tied to the series?
3. Does it fit inside the number I set earlier?

Three yeses means buy it. One or fewer means skip it, however good the packaging looks.

## The takeaway

Write down your ACOTAR spending limit before you open a single product page, then buy the one item you'd use most and let the rest wait two days.
