BuzzFeed Shopping recently published a roundup of 22 kitchen products aimed at everyday annoyances. Its own headline admits the post won't solve all your kitchen problems. Three of the problems it names are a disorganized jumble of water bottles, crumbs in your oven, and stinky garbage disposals.

The full list of 22 isn't something we can walk through here, so we'll stick with those three. For each, you'll get a practical way to decide whether a product is worth your money, and a cheaper check to run first. That's the smart shopping move: fix the problem, not the urge to buy.

## Why a "22 fixes" list is a useful shopping tool (and a risky one)

Product roundups are good at one thing: showing you a problem has a purchasable solution. They're less good at telling you whether that solution fits your kitchen or your budget.

Before you add anything to a cart, ask three questions:

- **How often does this annoy me?** A daily irritation justifies a purchase more than a once-a-month one.
- **Can I solve it with something I own?** Spend only when the answer is no.
- **Will I use it in a month?** Gadgets that need extra steps tend to end up in a drawer.

Hold on to those as we go through the three problems.

## Problem 1: The water bottle avalanche

BuzzFeed flags a disorganized jumble of water bottles as a kitchen problem, and plenty of households know it well. Open the cabinet and something rolls out.

### Try this first

Before buying an organizer, do a purge. Gather every bottle and tumbler you own, then keep the ones you actually reach for. Donate or recycle the rest. Fewer bottles may solve the problem on their own.

### If you still need a product

A water bottle organizer makes sense when you've trimmed the collection and still have a cabinet that won't stay tidy. Before you buy, measure the shelf's depth and height, and check that your tallest bottle fits. Consider your widest bottle too, since many organizers are built for slimmer shapes. Measure first and you avoid the return hassle.

## Problem 2: Crumbs and gunk in the oven

Crumbs in the oven are another pain point on BuzzFeed's list. They smoke, they smell, and they're easy to ignore until they're a bigger job.

### Try this first

The cheapest fix is habit. Once the oven has cooled, wipe out loose crumbs after messy cooking, and slide a sheet pan under dishes that tend to bubble over. Small, frequent cleanups beat one big scrub.

### If you still need a product

An oven crumb-cleaning product is worth considering if your oven is tight to reach or you cook messy foods often. Check whether it's reusable, because a tool you wash and reuse costs less over time than one you keep replacing. Read the product page for which oven types it works with, since we can't tell you from the roundup which specific item BuzzFeed picked.

## Problem 3: The stinky garbage disposal

BuzzFeed's third named problem is the smelly garbage disposal. It's the kind of annoyance that makes you hold your breath at the sink.

### Try this first

Run cold water and the disposal for a bit after use so food bits get flushed through. Avoid putting in stringy, greasy, or starchy scraps that tend to cling. Often the smell is leftover food, and a good rinse habit prevents a lot of it.

### If you still need a product

A garbage disposal deodorizing product can make sense as a periodic freshener. Compare the cost per use rather than the sticker price. A pack that looks cheap but lasts only a few uses can cost more than a bigger one. Skip anything that makes promises you can't verify in reviews.

## How to shop a roundup without overspending

Here's a simple routine for any list like this one:

1. **Pick one problem.** Choose the annoyance you deal with most.
2. **Set a cap.** Decide your maximum before you look at prices.
3. **Compare at least two options.** Look at size, reusability, and cost per use.
4. **Check return policies.** Fit issues are the top reason organizing products go back.
5. **Wait on the rest.** If something stays on your wish list for a week, you probably don't need it.

This keeps a 22-item list from turning into a 22-item cart.
