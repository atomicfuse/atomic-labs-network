ELLE Decor and Walmart are staging a holiday living room inside a Greenwich Village townhouse this week, and you can borrow the playbook without buying a plane ticket or a townhouse. By the end of this article, you'll have a spending plan for recreating a polished, festive living room on a Walmart budget, plus a few ways to avoid the impulse buys that wreck holiday budgets.

## What's actually happening

Per ELLE Decor, the magazine is hosting a free Holiday Wonderland shopping experience in Greenwich Village on November 20 and 21. The curated living room installation features festive decor and giftable finds from Walmart. Visitors can shop using QR codes and get free holiday portraits. If you can't make it, ELLE Decor says the collection is also available to shop online.

I can't tell you prices or specific products from the event, since that detail isn't in what ELLE Decor shared. So treat the installation as inspiration, then use the framework below to decide what's worth buying.

## Step 1: Set a number before you browse

Curated, shoppable rooms are designed to make everything look like a yes. Beat that by setting a cap first.

- Pick a total for decor only, separate from gifts. Even a modest figure works if it's firm.
- Split it into three buckets: one focal point, a few small accents, and consumables like candles or greenery.
- Write the number in your phone's notes and subtract as you add items to your cart.

A cart total you've promised yourself is more useful than a mental sense of "not too much."

## Step 2: Choose one focal point

Editorial rooms look expensive because they commit to a single hero. In a living room, that's usually the fireplace, a mantel, the tree, or a sofa wall. Spend the largest share of your decor bucket there, and keep everything else quiet.

No fireplace? A console table, bookshelf, or window works. The goal is one spot your eye lands on first.

## Step 3: Shop your house first

Before opening a single tab, walk through your home and pull anything that could work seasonally:

- Throw blankets in warm or deep tones
- Candles and holders you already own
- Bowls, trays, or baskets for displaying ornaments
- Spare lamps for softer evening light

Every item you reuse is money that stays in your account. Then buy only what fills the gaps.

## Step 4: Use the online collection as a shortlist, not a mandate

Browsing ELLE Decor's online picks is useful as a editor-vetted starting point. Here's how to keep it from becoming a spree:

1. Open the collection and add your favorites to a list rather than the cart.
2. Wait a day. Anything you've forgotten about wasn't essential.
3. Compare the survivors against your focal-point and accent buckets.
4. Check Walmart's current listings for price changes before you buy, since I can't confirm the event's pricing.

## Step 5: Time purchases around Black Friday

Holiday hosting, gifting, and Black Friday shopping all land in the same stretch, which is exactly when budgets slip. A few ways to stay in control:

- Buy decor you need for hosting first, and hold optional extras until the sales begin.
- Don't assume every "deal" is a deal. Compare against the price you'd have paid last week.
- Keep gift spending in its own budget so a discounted mantel garland doesn't eat into it.

## Step 6: Recreate the photo backdrop for free

The in-person event includes free holiday portraits, and that's a trick you can copy at home. Pick the focal point you styled, clear the clutter around it, turn off harsh overhead lights, and use lamps or string lights for warmth. A phone on a stack of books and a timer is all you need for a family card photo, no studio fee required.

## What to skip

- Matching sets of everything. Mixing a few textures looks more collected, and costs less.
- Decor you'll only use one season and can't store. Be honest about closet space.
- Anything you're buying only because a QR code made it easy.

## The takeaway

Use the ELLE Decor and Walmart living room as your mood board, then set a spending cap, pick one focal point, and shop your own home before you buy. A room that feels festive doesn't need a full cart, just one decision you stick to.
