Every year, some people lose real money because they never spent it. If you have a flexible spending account (FSA), the balance may not follow you into the new year. By the end of this guide, you'll know how to check what you have, sort your options by actual need, and avoid buying something just because it's eligible.

The prompt for this piece is a Fox News roundup of 18 FSA/HSA-eligible wellness and self-care products, from health trackers and blood pressure monitors to massage tools, skincare, and glasses. The pitch is simple: turn remaining healthcare dollars into practical health upgrades before funds expire. Let's turn that list into a plan.

## Step 1: Check Your Balance and Your Deadline

Before you shop, find out two things: how much is left, and when you lose it. FSA and HSA rules differ, and employer plans vary, so confirm the details with your plan administrator or benefits portal. Don't assume.

Also confirm eligibility for the specific item before you check out. Your plan's eligible-expense list or customer service line will settle it faster than guessing. Keep your receipts either way.

## Step 2: Cover Needs First, Wants Second

The cheapest purchase is one you'd have made anyway. Look at what you'd otherwise pay for out of pocket in the next few months: new glasses, sunscreen, hand sanitizer, a blood pressure cuff your doctor suggested. Spending pre-tax dollars on those is a real saving. Spending them on a gadget you'll abandon is just spending.

### Everyday essentials from the roundup

Fox News listed several low-drama items that most households could use:

- **EltaMD UV face sunscreen**
- **Touchland hydrating hand sanitizer spray three-pack**
- **Breathe Right nasal strips**
- **Miracle Patch**
- **Navage nose cleaner starter bundle**, a fit for sick-season prep

These are the safest bets for leftover funds because you'll likely use them up.

### Vision

The list also includes **GlassesUSA glasses** and **Ray-Ban Meta AI Glasses**. If your prescription is due for an update, glasses are the kind of planned expense that makes year-end FSA spending sensible. Check your plan's rules on the smart-glasses option before assuming it qualifies.

## Step 3: Consider Health Monitoring Tools You'll Actually Use

The roundup leans heavily into wellness tech:

- **Omron Evolv wireless upper arm blood pressure monitor**
- **Axis full body scale**
- **WHOOP health tracker**
- **Oura Ring 5**
- **The Hume Pod 2.0**
- **The Wellness Company membership**

The Fox News piece doesn't spell out prices or eligibility details in the material I have, so check current pricing and your plan's rules for each. My take: a blood pressure monitor is the easiest to justify if it's something you or a family member will check regularly. Wearables and memberships deserve more scrutiny. Ask whether you'll wear it daily, and whether a subscription or ongoing cost applies. A tracker in a drawer is money wasted, even if it was pre-tax.

## Step 4: Recovery and Pain Relief for Active Lifestyles

If you're an athlete, a desk worker with a sore neck, or both, the roundup offers several options:

- **TheraGun Prime**
- **TheraCup**
- **Hyperice X 2 Knee**
- **Eye massager with heat and cooling**
- **Back and neck chair massager**

Match the tool to a problem you actually have. Achy knees point to the Hyperice. Long hours at a desk point to the chair massager. Buying one targeted item beats buying three vaguely relaxing ones.

## A Quick Decision Filter

Before you click buy, run each item through three questions:

1. **Would I buy this with after-tax money?** If yes, use FSA money. If no, skip it.
2. **Will I use it within the next month?** Gear that sits unused defeats the purpose.
3. **Does my plan cover it?** Verify first, so you aren't stuck with a non-reimbursable purchase.

## Don't Forget Timing

Holiday shopping season makes health purchases feel giftable as well as practical, which Fox News pointed to as part of the appeal. Just remember the funds belong to your plan. Check your plan's rules before buying items for someone else, since eligibility generally depends on who the expense covers.

Also allow time for shipping. A purchase placed too close to your deadline may not count if it ships late, depending on your plan's rules. Order early and confirm with your administrator.

## The Bottom Line

Open your benefits portal today, find your balance and deadline, then pick one or two items you'd genuinely use. Spend the money on what you need, keep the receipts, and let the rest of the gadget list wait.
