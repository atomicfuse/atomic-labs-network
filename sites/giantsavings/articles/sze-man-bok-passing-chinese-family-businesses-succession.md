His death not only marks a pivotal moment for Hengan but also emphasizes the broader challenges facing many aging Chinese family businesses as they confront the realities of leadership transition.

## A Moment of Transition for Chinese Family Businesses

Hengan International has established itself as a key player in the hygiene products sector in China. As one of the oldest family-run businesses in the country, Sze's passing underscores a crucial turning point not just for the company but also for the wider landscape of Chinese capitalism. Many founders from China’s first wave of self-made billionaires are reaching retirement age, and this situation is prompting urgent reflections on how the wealth, control, and innovation held by these business moguls will be passed to the next generation.

The succession issues these companies face reflect broader themes of legacy and generational change. The smooth transition of power can make or break family businesses, especially as they try to maintain competitiveness in rapidly evolving markets.

## The Unique Landscape of Chinese Family Business Governance

Chinese family-run enterprises often differ significantly from Western businesses in terms of governance and succession planning. While Western models may adopt more corporate cultural practices with formal boards and external stakeholders, many Chinese family businesses still rely heavily on familial ties and internal dynamics. Succession is frequently concentrated within the family, noted for its ambition but often lacking the structured frameworks seen in Western counterparts.

This discrepancy can lead to challenges—including power struggles and difficulties when the next generation, who may have different visions for the company, takes the reins. The deaths of influential founders like Sze Man Bok may leave a vacuum that is hard to fill, particularly if successors are unprepared or if there’s a lack of clarity about leadership roles.

## Lessons from Other Family Businesses

Hengan is not alone in its challenges; many other Chinese family businesses have navigated similar waters successfully or otherwise. Some companies, like the Wanda Group, have been able to adapt and preserve their legacies through strategic external partnerships and modern governance systems. Others, however, have struggled; leadership transitions have led to declines in market position and brand value.

The story of succession in China’s family business landscape is as varied as it is crucial. What happens next at Hengan will be a strong indicator of how effectively older family-run enterprises can adjust to new realities while honoring their legacies. The focus will be on whether successors can maintain the innovative spirit of their founders while modernizing their business practices to thrive in an exceptionally competitive environment.

## Looking Ahead

As the era of China’s first-generation billionaires appears to be winding down, the future of family-controlled conglomerates rests on the strength and preparedness of the next generation.
