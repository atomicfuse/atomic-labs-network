## The Proposed Wealth Tax: A Game Changer?
California's proposed wealth tax is designed to target individuals with substantial unrealized gains, which could significantly affect tech founders and investors. This tax, if enacted, would tax individuals based on their total wealth, not just income, impacting those with substantial holdings in startup equity and cryptocurrencies. For tech CEOs like Armstrong, who have considerable investments in their own companies, this proposal could result in hefty liabilities on assets that are not yet liquid.

## Why Are CEOs Threatening to Leave?
The conversation around high taxes in California isn't new, but the latest proposal intensifies concerns about an exodus of talent. With some of the most successful innovation in technology emerging from California, the potential departure of leaders to states with more favorable tax environments raises alarms. Founders and CEOs are weighing the financial implications of staying compared to relocating to states with low or no income taxes, such as Texas or Florida.

Moreover, the tech sector has been steadily gaining political and economic clout. If high-profile figures like Armstrong leave, it could signify a broader trend of ‘tax flight,’ where capital and innovation migrate to friendlier jurisdictions. This scenario may have ripple effects on California’s economy, affecting not just employers but also employees and support industries.

## The Cost of Staying vs. Leaving
