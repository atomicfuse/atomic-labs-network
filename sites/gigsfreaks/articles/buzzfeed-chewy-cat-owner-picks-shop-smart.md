## The Pitch: Easier, Cleaner, More Fun

Per BuzzFeed, the picks are meant to make cat ownership easier, cleaner, and more fun. That's a neat three-part scorecard, and it's a good one. It's hard not to notice that those three categories map onto what cat owners actually complain about (and brag about) online: the mess, the hassle, and the sheer entertainment value of a creature who treats a cardboard box as a luxury suite.

## A Three-Question Filter for Any Cat Purchase

Since the list's own scorecard is easier, cleaner, and more fun, let's turn it into a quick test you can run on anything in your cart. This is our framework, not BuzzFeed's.

### Does it reduce mess?

If a product claims to make your home cleaner, ask what specific mess it tackles. Litter tracking, scratching damage, and hair are the usual suspects. A product that names its target is more convincing than one promising vague freshness.

### Does it make your life simpler?

Convenience is a legitimate reason to spend. If a purchase saves you a daily chore, the cost spreads out over every day you use it. If it adds a new step to your routine, it's probably a gadget you'll resent by February.

### Will the cat actually care?

This is the wild card, and any cat owner knows it. Enrichment items are only worth it if your particular cat engages with them. Anyone who has watched a feline ignore a brand-new toy in favor of the box it shipped in understands the risk. Our suggestion: start with one enrichment item, not five, and let your cat cast the deciding vote.

## Worth It vs. Skip It

The smartest way to read any "worth every penny" roundup is to sort the picks into two piles. Daily-use items that solve a recurring annoyance go in the worth-it pile. Novelty items with a one-week lifespan go in the skip-it pile, unless the joy of watching your cat investigate them is itself the point, which is a valid entertainment budget line.

If you're a first-time cat parent, the same logic applies in a different order: nail the basics that keep your home livable, then add the fun stuff. If you're running a multi-cat household, duplicates of the essentials may matter more than variety.
