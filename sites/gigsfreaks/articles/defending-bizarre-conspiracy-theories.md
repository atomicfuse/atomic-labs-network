## 1. The Moon Landing Was Staged  
First up is the classic: did we really land on the moon in 1969? Despite photos, moon rocks, and the accounts of astronauts, some folks firmly believe it’s all a Hollywood production—Orson Welles, eat your heart out!

The flat Earth theory is firmly planted in the conspiracy universe. With AV Club levels of passion, proponents claim that everything we’ve been taught is wrong. Flat Earthers gather to question the curvature of the Earth, and the notion that photos from space might just be some really convincing CGI. Sorry, guys, but reality just doesn’t bend that way. 

## 4. The 5G Connection to COVID-19  
In the weirdest crossover event of the year, some theorists connected the launch of 5G networks to the COVID-19 pandemic. For many, this connection is more about digital paranoia than physics, leading to complaints about phone towers being the real villains while science fights to help humanity. 

## 5. The Area 51 Raid  
Remember when a Facebook event went viral about storming Area 51 to "see them aliens?" Though created humorously, it revealed a societal undercurrent: a thirst for truth behind the veil of secrecy. ## 6. The Mandela Effect 
Ever misremember a movie quote or a product's name? That’s the Mandela Effect at work. Conspiracy theorists are convinced these slips of memory indicate alternate realities. Forget your Tesseracts; this theory invites endless rambles on collective memory phenomena.
