Alison Hammond has a message for anyone with opinions about her love life: she didn't ask for them. The Great British Baking Show host has responded to criticism over her relationship with her younger boyfriend, David Putnam, arguing that the backlash reflects a sexist double standard around age-gap dating, according to The Cut. Her position, as the outlet frames it, is simple: if two consenting adults are happy, everyone else can mind their business.

The Cut's description of the piece captures the specific irritation. Hammond reportedly doesn't love being "given a wildlife classification" for dating a younger man. That's a polite way of pointing at a certain four-letter animal nickname we'll get to in a moment.

## What Hammond Is Actually Pushing Back On

To be clear about what's confirmed: Hammond is dating David Putnam, he is younger than she is, and she has publicly objected to the criticism and commentary that has followed. Beyond that, we're working from The Cut's summary of her comments, so we won't put words in her mouth. The through-line is her "mind your business" philosophy, and the argument that the reaction would look very different if the genders were flipped.

That last part is the real story. Hammond isn't just defending one relationship. She's poking at a pattern.

## The 'Cougar' Problem

Here's the take, and it's ours rather than hers: the wildlife classification is the tell. There is no widely used, equally cheeky animal nickname for an older man dating a younger woman. Hollywood has spent decades treating that pairing as background scenery, a casting-department default. Meanwhile, a woman does the same thing and gets a predator metaphor attached.

It's hard not to notice what the word actually does. "Cougar" frames a woman's attraction as hunting and her partner as prey, which conveniently recasts a happy adult couple as a transaction or a conquest. It's sold as a joke, but jokes that only ever land on one gender are usually doing some work.

## Why It's Landing Now

Celebrity age-gap relationships remain a reliable flashpoint, and The Cut notes the scrutiny tends to intensify when the older partner is a woman. Hammond's comments plug into a broader conversation about misogyny, dating norms, and how women over 50 get policed for simply enjoying their lives.

There's also the matter of who is talking. Hammond's whole public persona is warmth, big laughs, and a refusal to take herself too seriously. When someone with that reputation says she doesn't love the commentary, it reads less like a publicist's statement and more like a person politely running out of patience. ## The Double Standard, in Plain Terms

The reasoning goes like this:

- Older man, younger woman: usually treated as ordinary, sometimes even as a flex.
- Older woman, younger man: treated as a topic, a punchline, or a cautionary tale.

Nothing about either arrangement changes if both people are consenting adults who like each other. The only variable that shifts the public reaction is whose birth certificate is on which side. That's the point Hammond's defenders are making, and it's a tough one to wave away.

## Women Over 50 and Being Desired in Public

The subtext of a lot of this chatter is that women past a certain age should quietly retire from romance, or at least do it discreetly. Hammond, hosting one of the coziest shows on television and dating openly, isn't playing along. The Cut's framing suggests her stance is less about proving anything and more about refusing to justify herself.

That might be the most effective move available. Every explanation, every defensive clarification, treats the criticism as a legitimate question that deserves an answer. "Mind your business" doesn't.

## The Bottom Line

Nobody is owed a vote on Hammond's relationship, and she isn't asking for one. If anything, the whole saga is a neat demonstration of her point: the interest is intense, the framing is gendered, and the person at the center is just trying to date someone she likes. The only thing left to bake here is a nice, quiet exit from the conversation, and she seems perfectly happy to hold the door open.
