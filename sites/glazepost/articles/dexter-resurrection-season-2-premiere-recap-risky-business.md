*Dexter: Resurrection* is back for Season 2, and per Vulture's recap of the premiere, "Risky Business," the show's central question is delightfully petty: after years of murdering for the greater good, doesn't Dexter deserve a little recognition?

## The Vigilante Who Wants a Plaque

The angle worth chewing on is Dexter's ego. Vulture frames the premiere around a man who has spent years doing secret, illegal, deeply unpaid work and has started to feel unappreciated. It's a midlife crisis, serial-killer edition. Other men buy a sports car. Dexter, apparently, wants a little validation.

It's hard not to see the joke the premiere is playing on its own premise. The whole point of Dexter Morgan has always been that nobody can know. His code, his careful rituals and his double life all depend on anonymity. A Dexter who craves applause is a Dexter working against his own survival instincts, and that tension is where the season's "risky business" lives. There's also something quietly funny about a long-running antihero aging into worry about relevance. Vulture's takeaway is that the franchise itself is in a creative resurgence, so the show seems well aware that its main character and its brand have both been through the wringer.

## Harrison Gets a Badge-Adjacent Milestone

The other big emotional beat is Harrison's police-academy milestone. Per Vulture, it sets up strong emotional and procedural stakes, and it's easy to see why. Dexter's dark passenger always came with a rule about staying one step ahead of the cops. Now his son is training to become one.

That shifts the father-son dynamic in a way the series can mine for tension. Dexter wants recognition from the world while his kid earns it the legitimate way. Whether Harrison ever gets close to his father's secrets is a question the recap's framing invites, though we'll leave the answers to the show.

Here's the context for why this premiere is getting attention. *Dexter* has been in rare comeback mode after years of uneven follow-ups. Fans of the original run know the franchise has a bumpy history, and revivals are notoriously hard to land. Vulture's read is that the premiere suggests the momentum is still there.

## The Bottom Line

The sharpest joke in "Risky Business" may be its simplest. For years, Dexter's greatest skill was never being noticed. Now the man who perfected invisibility wants a round of applause, and in a series built on secrets, that might be the most dangerous urge he has ever had.
