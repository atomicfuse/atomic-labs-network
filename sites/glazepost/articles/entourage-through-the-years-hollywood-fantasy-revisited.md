That's a useful description, because the series sold something specific. It wasn't just "what's it like to be a star." It was "what if your friends got to come along."

That's the hook worth remembering. Plenty of Hollywood shows are about ambition. This one was about proximity: the velvet-rope fantasy, with a seat for everyone you grew up with. ## What Aged Well, and What Didn't

Here's where I'll plant my flag, clearly as opinion. The part of *Entourage* that holds up best is the friendship premise. A group of pals trying to stay close while one of them gets pulled into a bigger orbit is a story that never really expires.

The part that feels most dated is the glossy, aspirational tone itself. Hollywood's public image has changed a lot since the 2000s. Celebrity now comes with constant access through social media, which makes the old mystique of the exclusive party look quaint. TMZ's retrospective frames the show as a lens for exactly that shift, and it's a fair one: *Entourage* depicted a world where access was scarce and prized, and today's audiences watch it knowing how much of that scarcity has evaporated.
