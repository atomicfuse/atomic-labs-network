### Ambiguous symbolism

The film is full of cryptic imagery, and that is the fuel. When a movie shows you something striking and never explains it, viewers fill the silence. The more elaborate the explanation, the more satisfying it feels, which is not the same as being true.

### Kubrick's perfectionism

Kubrick is widely known as a meticulous filmmaker. That reputation creates a particular kind of reading habit: if he was this careful, then every detail must be deliberate, and every deliberate detail must be a clue. It's hard not to see the leap. Perfectionism explains precision. It doesn't automatically explain a hidden code.

The film's difficult release history is also listed among the reasons it attracts this kind of thinking. Our read: when a project's backstory feels messy or shrouded, audiences tend to assume the mess is covering something. Often it's just the ordinary chaos of making a big, expensive movie.
