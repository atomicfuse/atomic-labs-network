Buckingham Palace was reportedly "blindsided" by disturbing allegations involving Thomas Markle, Meghan Markle's estranged father, according to a Fox News report citing an expert. The allegations are now under investigation, and police reviews are continuing, per the outlet. Fox frames the story as a fresh flashpoint for Prince Harry and Meghan's already strained relationship with the royal family.

We don't have details of the allegations from the material available to us, and we're not going to guess at them. Serious claims are involved, they are under investigation, and nothing here should be read as a finding of fact. What we can do is look at the part of the story that's actually about institutions: what a palace does when it is, reportedly, caught flat-footed.

## What "Blindsided" Actually Signals

In royal-watching shorthand, "blindsided" is a loaded word. It suggests the institution learned about something at the same moment as everyone else, which is the nightmare scenario for a communications operation built on advance warning, careful phrasing and carefully timed statements.

To be clear, the "blindsided" characterization comes from an expert quoted by Fox News, not from a confirmed palace statement. That distinction matters. It's an outside read on the palace's position, and the palace may see it differently.

## The Playbook When You Didn't Choose the Timing

This is our take, not reporting: when an institution is surprised by a story involving someone it has no formal relationship with, the options are narrow. Thomas Markle is not a working royal, and he isn't part of the palace's household. Meghan and Harry no longer carry out royal duties. That leaves the palace with a very short list of moves.

### Option one: say nothing

Silence is the oldest tool in the royal kit. If the matter is under police review, there's a strong procedural reason to avoid commenting on anything that could touch an investigation. Silence also avoids the risk of saying something that has to be walked back later.

### Option two: keep distance

One of the angles floated around this story is that King Charles and Prince William would likely adopt a "keep distance" strategy. That's a prediction rather than a confirmed plan, but it fits the broader logic. The allegations concern a man outside the institution, and any public engagement risks pulling the Crown into a family dispute it has long tried to keep at arm's length.

### Option three: let others speak

In the vacuum, experts fill the airwaves. The Fox piece is a good example: a story in which the loudest voice is a commentator interpreting what the palace must be feeling. That's how "palace sources" and "royal experts" become the narrative when no one official is talking.

## Why the Sussexes Keep Getting Pulled In

Harry and Meghan have become something of a lightning rod. Any royal-adjacent controversy, even one centered on a relative, tends to land on their doorstep because their story with the institution has been public, contested and heavily covered. A new development involving Meghan's father reopens a chapter many readers thought they had already read.

Some will see that decision through a new lens, and it's reasonable to say that a family estrangement can look very different once serious allegations enter the picture. But that's a reading, not a conclusion anyone should draw before the investigations run their course.

## The Media Ethics Problem Nobody Likes to Mention

This is where the entertainment machine has to check itself. Celebrity families make for irresistible headlines, and royal stories are a genre unto themselves, complete with expert panels, anonymous insiders and a lot of adjectives. Words like "disturbing" and "blindsided" do heavy lifting in a headline.

But when the underlying subject is serious allegations under police review, the standard has to change:

- **Attribution is everything.** Claims belong to whoever made them, and "reportedly" is doing real work.
- **Expert opinion is not evidence.** An expert's view of how the palace feels is interpretation, not a statement from the palace.
- **Investigation isn't conviction.** Being under review is not a finding of anything.

The temptation is to treat this as another chapter in the Sussex-versus-Windsors saga, with sides and scorecards. The more honest framing is that it's a story about people and an unresolved process, wrapped in a franchise audiences already follow obsessively.

## What to Watch Next

The story's short shelf life is built into its structure. Either the investigations produce something concrete, or the news cycle moves on. In the meantime, watch for three things: whether the palace issues any formal comment, whether Harry and Meghan's representatives respond, and whether outlets keep their language careful as details emerge.

For now, the most telling detail may be the simplest one: the headline is about an institution being surprised, not about anything the institution has done. When a palace built on control is reportedly the last to know, the real story is how little control anyone has once the cameras swing around.
