A new essay circulating in alternative media says a hidden clock is ticking. Published by Thetruthseeker and written by Mike Adams, it argues that global elites are in a "depopulation race" and must thin the human herd before a financial collapse sparks a revolt. By the end of this article, you will know exactly what the essay claims, what it does not prove, and a simple method for judging stories built like this one.

## The Story as Adams Tells It

The essay, titled "The Depopulation Race Before the Financial Collapse: Why I Believe the Globalists Are Killing Us Before We Can Revolt," lays out a plan in four steps. Per the piece's own description: depopulate, reset, enslave and automate.

According to Adams, the machinery includes mRNA vaccines, which he frames as biological weapons, along with engineered famines and bioweapons. He predicts an economic collapse and seizure of assets. The twist is the timing. The plan, he suggests, works flawlessly on paper, but a growing number of people are "awakening," and the plan may not roll out before revolution erupts.

Then comes the prescription. Adams recommends precious metals, natural health, and decentralized communities.

That is the whole architecture: a hidden enemy, a deadline, and a way out.

## What the Essay Asserts Versus What It Shows

The key word in the title is "believe." The piece is an opinion and a prediction, not an investigation. Based on the summary of the essay, it offers no documents, no named insiders, and no verified evidence of a coordinated plan. Those are the things a claim this large would need.

Here is the gap, step by step:

- **"mRNA injections are bioweapons."** This is a claim, not a finding. Clinical trial data and immunology are the right places to test it, and the essay's framing does not engage them as far as the available summary shows.
- **"Engineered famines."** Food shortages are real events with documented causes like weather, war, and supply chains. Calling them engineered requires proof of intent, which is not offered.
- **"Collapse is coming."** Predictions of collapse are unfalsifiable until the day they are wrong. They can be repeated indefinitely.

This is my analysis, not Adams's: when every event, from a vaccine to a bad harvest, gets slotted into one plot, the theory stops explaining anything. It simply absorbs everything.

## Why a Story Like This Feels True

The narrative lands in a specific moment. Economic uncertainty is high, and worries about AI automation replacing jobs are growing. Those are real anxieties, and they are not imaginary.

The depopulation story takes diffuse fears and gives them a face. Wealth inequality, job loss, and institutional distrust are hard to fight. A villain with a schedule is easier to hold in your mind. It also flatters the believer: you are among the few who see clearly.

The structure is familiar from many hidden-hand tales. It borrows pieces from several existing frameworks, including the "great reset" and vaccine-harm narratives, and stitches them into one cohesive story. Each piece supports the others, so doubting one feels like doubting the whole. That interlocking quality is what makes it feel solid.

## Follow the Prescription

Notice what the essay ends with. Survival advice centered on precious metals, natural health, and decentralized communities.

I am not claiming Adams is insincere, and nothing in the material says so. But it is a fair question to ask of any fear narrative: who benefits if you accept it? Fear-based survival content often sits next to things for sale, such as courses, metals, and supplements. When the solution is a purchase, the story deserves extra scrutiny. Ask whether the author sells what the story tells you to buy.

## Where the Real Concerns Live

Strip away the plot and some legitimate questions remain:

- How should societies handle wealth concentrated in few hands?
- What happens to workers as automation spreads?
- Why has trust in public institutions eroded, and how can it be rebuilt?

These deserve serious attention. They do not require a secret extermination program to be urgent.

## A Five-Question Test for Any Hidden-Plot Claim

1. **Is there a document, a named source, or a verifiable record?** Or only inference?
2. **Could the claim be proven wrong?** If no event could disprove it, it is not evidence-based.
3. **Does it need a vast number of silent conspirators?** Secrets that large rarely hold.
4. **Is there a simpler explanation?** Economics, politics, and error explain much.
5. **Who profits from your belief?**

Run the Adams essay through that list and you will see why it works better as a window into modern anxiety than as a map of reality.

## The Takeaway

When a story hands you an enemy, a deadline, and a product, slow down. Check the evidence first, then decide how afraid to be.
