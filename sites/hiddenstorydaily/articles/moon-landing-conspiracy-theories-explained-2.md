More than half a century after Apollo 11, a significant portion of the global population still suspects the whole thing was staged. Polls conducted in various countries have found anywhere from 6% to over 20% of respondents expressing doubt about whether humans ever walked on the Moon. That number has not collapsed with time — if anything, the internet has given it new oxygen. So what exactly do skeptics believe happened, where did the theory start, and why does it keep spreading?

By the end of this piece, you'll know the strongest versions of the conspiracy claims, the documented evidence that counters them, and the one uncomfortable truth that keeps the doubt alive regardless.

## Where the Theory Was Born

The modern Moon landing conspiracy didn't spring fully formed from the public. It was largely crystallized by Bill Kaysing, a former technical writer at Rocketdyne — a company that built rocket engines for NASA. In 1976, Kaysing self-published a pamphlet titled *We Never Went to the Moon*, arguing that NASA lacked the technical capability to land humans safely on the lunar surface and fabricated the entire program. His background gave the claims a veneer of insider credibility, even though he had left Rocketdyne before the Apollo program reached its critical phases.

From that seed, the theory grew. Director Peter Hyams' 1978 film *Capricorn One* — which depicted a faked Mars landing — further embedded the idea in popular culture as a plausible government operation.

## The Core Claims, Taken Seriously

The conspiracy argument isn't one claim — it's a cluster. Understanding each one individually matters.

### The Waving Flag

Skeptics point to footage of the American flag appearing to flutter on the airless Moon. If there's no atmosphere, how does it wave? The actual explanation, supported by NASA mission documentation, is that the flag had a horizontal rod sewn along its top edge to keep it extended. When astronauts planted the pole into the ground with a twisting motion, the flag swung and oscillated — and because there's no air resistance in a vacuum, it kept moving longer than it would on Earth before settling. Footage shows it eventually going completely still.

### Stars Missing from Photographs

This is one of the most cited pieces of evidence. Apollo photographs show pitch-black skies with no visible stars. Skeptics argue this proves the backdrop was a studio set. The photographic explanation is straightforward: cameras operate on exposure settings. The lunar surface in daylight is brilliantly bright. Setting a camera to correctly expose a sunlit astronaut in a white suit means the exposure time is far too short to capture faint starlight. Any photographer shooting a bright subject against a dark background will reproduce the same effect.

### The Van Allen Radiation Belts

This is arguably the most technically serious claim. The Van Allen belts are zones of intense radiation surrounding Earth, and astronauts passing through them would absorb a significant radiation dose. Skeptics argue the dose would be lethal. According to NASA's own records and analyses published by independent researchers, the Apollo trajectory was designed to pass through the thinner parts of the belts quickly — transit time was roughly 30 minutes through the most intense zones. Estimated radiation exposure for the mission fell within survivable limits, though not trivial ones. This claim remains one where the technical debate is most substantive, even if mainstream science accepts the mission's safety margins.

### Stanley Kubrick Directed It

Perhaps the most cinematic theory: that NASA hired Stanley Kubrick, fresh off *2001: A Space Odyssey*, to direct footage of a fake Moon landing in a film studio. This theory gained renewed traction after a 2015 documentary featured an interview with a man claiming to be Kubrick making this admission — the film was later widely identified as a hoax, and the man in the interview was an actor. There is no documented evidence connecting Kubrick to any NASA deception operation.

## The Evidence That Doesn't Fit the Conspiracy

Faking the Moon landing would have required the silence of an estimated 400,000 engineers, scientists, and contractors who worked on the Apollo program. No credible whistleblower has ever come forward with verifiable documentation. The Soviet Union — which had every geopolitical incentive to expose a fraud during the Space Race — tracked the missions independently and never challenged their authenticity. Retroreflectors placed on the lunar surface by Apollo astronauts are still used by observatories around the world today: laser beams fired at the Moon bounce back from those exact locations.

## Why the Doubt Refuses to Die

Here's the uncomfortable part. The conspiracy persists not purely because of bad evidence — it persists because trust in government institutions genuinely eroded after verified deceptions like COINTELPRO, the Gulf of Tonkin incident, and later the Iraq weapons program. When governments are caught lying about major events, the psychological cost of trusting them on *any* major event rises. That's not irrational. It's pattern recognition applied too broadly.

The Moon landing conspiracy thrives in that gap between what institutions say and what people feel they can safely believe. The technical claims largely don't survive scrutiny. The cultural conditions that created them? Those are very much still with us.

If you want to stress-test the theory yourself, start with the retroreflectors — they're the one piece of physical evidence you can independently verify without trusting NASA at all.
