Here's the viewing info first, then some practical lessons for a display that works on a real budget.

- Go early in the window if you have little ones. Evening crowds can build.

## Five lessons you can use at your own house

These are general decorating principles, not a description of the winning display. They work in a small yard, on an apartment balcony, or on a single front step.

### 1. Pick one theme and stick with it

Monsters, pumpkins, spiders, ghosts. Choose one. A single theme makes a modest collection of decorations look planned instead of scattered.

If you already own a mix of items, group them by type. Put all the pumpkins together and all the cobwebs together.

### 2. Make it friendly for small visitors

If trick-or-treaters of all ages will walk up, aim for fun over frightening. Goofy faces, bright colors, and silly expressions invite kids closer.

Save the creepier effects for a spot that's easy to skip.

### 3. Use lighting before buying more stuff

Light changes everything after dark. A few inexpensive moves go a long way:

- String lights along the porch rail or around a doorway.
- Use orange or purple bulbs in one fixture instead of everywhere.
- Add battery-powered candles or LED tea lights in jars along the walkway.
- Aim one spotlight at your focal piece.

One well-lit feature looks better than ten unlit ones.

### 4. Build a focal point

Every good display has one thing your eye lands on first. It might be a big figure, a wreath-style door cover, or a cluster of pumpkins of different heights.

Put it near the front, and keep the smaller items around it as supporting players.

### 5. Think about the walkway

A path guides people through the display. Line it with lights or pumpkins, and clear anything they could trip over. It also makes your house feel welcoming instead of cluttered.

## A realistic low-budget plan

Want to pull this off in an evening or two? Try this order:

1. **Choose your theme.** Ten minutes, free.
2. **Shop your house first.** Old sheets, cardboard, paper bags, and spare lights can all become decorations.
3. **Make one focal piece.** Cardboard and paint can become a friendly monster face for a window or door.
4. **Add lighting.** This is where a small spend pays off most.
5. **Edit.** Step back at dusk and remove anything that looks like clutter.

Thrift stores and dollar stores often have seasonal items right now, but check prices and safety labels on any electrical products before you buy.

## Take the idea, not the budget

Contest winners can feel out of reach, but the lesson is simple: a clear theme, good lighting, and one memorable centerpiece. If you can visit 1318 Sunny Ln on October 30 or 31, look at how the pieces work together. Then pick one idea and try it on your own porch tonight.
