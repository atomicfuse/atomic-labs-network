Holiday season is the time of year most of us spend a little more than we planned. Jeff Stein, in his Iowa Politics column for Civicmedia on Nov. 23, pointed to Thanksgiving travel, Halloween spending, and Christmas retail forecasts as signs that households keep making choices about where their money goes, even when prices feel high.

I'm not here to argue politics. But that observation is useful for your home. Most of us will spend something on the season. The real question is whether the money lands on things that make a room feel good, or disappears into stuff you'll box up and forget.

Here's a simple way to decide, with steps you can finish this week.

## Start With a Number, Not a Shopping List

Pick a total before you browse. It can be $30 or $200. What matters is that you chose it on purpose.

Then split it into three rough buckets:

- **Lighting:** about half
- **Greenery and natural pieces:** about a third
- **Small extras (ribbon, candles, ornaments):** whatever's left

This keeps you from buying ten cute items that don't work together.

## Spend on Lighting First

Light changes a room more than any object does. Warm, low light makes a plain living room feel cozy in minutes.

Try this:

1. Turn off the overhead light in the evening.
2. Use two or three lamps at different heights instead.
3. Swap in warm-white bulbs if your lamps look harsh or bluish.
4. Add one string of warm-white lights along a shelf, mantel, or window frame.

In a small space, string lights are especially handy. They add glow without taking up any floor or table room.

## Keep Your Color Palette to Three

A tight palette makes budget decor look planned. Choose three colors and stick with them.

A few combinations that work in real homes:

- **Cream, evergreen, and brass:** classic and calm
- **Navy, white, and silver:** crisp, and good for winter more than just December
- **Rust, cream, and natural wood:** warm, and it carries from fall into the new year

Before you buy anything, look at what you already own. Throw pillows, a blanket, or a candle holder may already match your palette.

## Use Greenery Instead of Many Small Decorations

One good bunch of greenery beats a dozen small trinkets. It fills space, smells nice, and looks fresh.

Where to use it:

- A bundle in a pitcher on the dining table
- A few branches tucked behind picture frames
- A simple strand along a shelf

While you're at it, check thrift stores, yard clippings from your own trees (with permission if they're not yours), and the end-of-day discount at grocery stores. Many people find that pine trimmings and branches cost little or nothing.

## Shop Secondhand for the Things That Last

Thrift stores are a good bet for items that don't need to be new:

- Glass jars and vases
- Candlesticks and holders
- Baskets for blankets or wrapping paper
- Plain trays for grouping items on a coffee table

A tray is a small-space trick worth knowing. Grouping three items on a tray makes them look intentional rather than scattered.

## Decorate Fewer Spots, Better

You don't need every surface to be festive. Choose two or three "focus zones" where people actually look:

1. **The entry:** a wreath or a small bowl of ornaments
2. **The main seating area:** a throw blanket and a lamp
3. **The table where you eat:** a simple centerpiece

Leave the rest alone. Empty space makes the decorated spots stand out.

## Make Your Decor Work Past December

If your budget is tight, look for pieces that still make sense in January. Candles, wood, greenery, and warm lighting all do. A glittery reindeer probably doesn't.

That way, your spending stretches across the whole winter instead of ending on Dec. 26.

## A Realistic Example

Say you have $60 and a small living room.

- $25: two warm-white string lights and warm bulbs for your lamps
- $20: a bunch of greenery and a thrifted pitcher
- $15: a few candles and a ribbon in one of your three colors

That covers lighting, greenery, and a few finishing touches. It won't look like a magazine spread, but it will feel like a home that's ready for guests.

## Your Next Step

Before you buy a single thing, write down your number, pick your three colors, and choose your two focus zones. Then spend where people will actually notice.
