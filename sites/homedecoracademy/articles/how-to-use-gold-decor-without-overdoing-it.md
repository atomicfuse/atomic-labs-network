This week, Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney made jokes about Donald Trump's gold-heavy Oval Office makeover at a Parliamentary Press Gallery dinner, per Yahoo. Impersonators joined in, poking fun at the gold decor along with Trump's jabs at Canada.

We'll leave the politics to the political sites. But the punchline carries a decorating lesson: gold is a fun, warm finish, and it's easy to use too much of it. When every surface shines, the room starts to feel like a joke instead of a home.

Here's how to use gold in your own space, on a normal budget, so it looks intentional.

## Start With the One-Third Rule

A good starting point is to let metallics make up a small slice of the room. Think roughly a tenth to a third of what you see, not most of it. That's a rule of thumb, not a law, but it keeps you honest.

Try this quick check:

1. Stand in the doorway and look at the room.
2. Count the gold or brass items you can see.
3. If you count more than five or six in a small room, remove a few.

The goal is for gold to read as a highlight, like jewelry on an outfit.

## Pick Three Spots, Not Ten

Choose a few places for gold to show up and leave the rest alone. Good beginner options:

- **Lighting:** a lamp base, pendant, or sconce.
- **Hardware:** cabinet pulls or drawer handles.
- **Small accents:** a picture frame, tray, or candleholder.

Repeating the same finish in three places across the room ties it together. Scattering it everywhere makes it feel busy.

## Balance Shine With Matte

Shiny surfaces need quiet neighbors. Pair gold with textures that soak up light:

- Linen or cotton curtains
- A wool or jute rug
- Matte painted walls
- Wood furniture with a natural finish

This contrast makes the gold look richer, even if the pieces are inexpensive.

## Choose Your Wall Color Carefully

Gold looks different depending on what's behind it.

- **Warm whites and creams:** soft and classic. This is the safest choice.
- **Deep green, navy, or charcoal:** dramatic. Gold pops, so you need even fewer pieces.
- **Cool gray:** can work, but pick a softer, brushed gold so it doesn't clash.

Before you commit, tape a paint swatch to the wall and hold your gold item against it in daylight and at night. Metallics change a lot with the light.

## Think About Lighting Before You Buy

Gold reflects whatever light hits it. Warm bulbs (often labeled around 2700K) make it glow softly. Very bright, cool bulbs can make it look harsh or yellow-green.

In a small space, one or two lamps at different heights usually flatter metallics more than a single overhead fixture.

## Get the Look on a Budget

You don't need pricey pieces. A few realistic ideas:

- **Spray paint:** an old frame, lamp base, or vase can get a gold finish for the cost of a can. Use light coats and let each dry fully.
- **Thrift stores:** look for brass candlesticks, trays, and picture frames. Brass can often be polished clean at home.
- **Swap hardware:** new cabinet pulls are a quick change and easy to do with a screwdriver.
- **Gold-toned trays:** a tray on a coffee table or dresser groups small items and adds shine in one move.

### Mix finishes gently

You can combine gold with black, wood, or even a bit of silver. Just let one finish lead. If gold is the star, keep the others to supporting roles.

## A Realistic Example: A Small Living Room

Say you have a 10-by-12-foot living room with white walls and a gray sofa. A simple plan:

1. Add a thrifted brass table lamp.
2. Hang one large mirror with a thin gold frame.
3. Set a small gold tray on the coffee table.
4. Add a chunky wool throw and a jute rug for texture.

That's three gold touches, plenty of matte, and a room that feels warm instead of overloaded.

## When to Stop

If you're unsure, take one item out and look again. Most rooms look better with one less shiny thing than one more.

Your next step: pick the three spots where you want gold, shop or paint for those only, and leave everything else simple.
