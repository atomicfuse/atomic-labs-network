BuzzFeed Shopping recently published a roundup of 27 kitchen products pitched as so handy you might start calling them your BFFs. The idea is appealing: small tools that make daily cooking less annoying.

The published roundup doesn't give us product details we can vouch for here, so this article won't pretend to review those 27 picks. Instead, it takes the idea behind the list and turns it into something you can use today: a simple way to decide which tools deserve a spot on your counter, and which ones should go in a drawer or out the door.

## Start With What You Already Reach For

Before you buy anything, spend one week paying attention. Each time you grab a tool, jot it down on a sticky note or in your phone.

At the end of the week, you'll likely see a pattern. A few items show up every day. Many show up never.

That short list is your real kitchen. Everything else is a candidate for storage.

## Use the Three-Question Counter Test

Counter space is the most valuable real estate in a kitchen, especially a small one. Before any item gets a permanent spot, ask:

1. **Do I use it at least several times a week?** If it only comes out for holidays, it belongs in a cabinet.
2. **Does it save me a step, or just move one?** A good tool makes prep faster, cleanup easier, or storage simpler.
3. **Can it look good where it sits?** If it's going to be on display daily, you should like seeing it.

If an item fails two of three, store it.

## Look for Tools That Cut Mess, Prep Time, or Clutter

The BuzzFeed roundup leans on the theme of small products with a big daily payoff. That's a useful filter, whatever you shop for. Good everyday tools tend to fall into three groups:

- **Less mess:** things that catch drips, splatters, or crumbs before they reach the counter.
- **Faster prep:** tools that shorten the chopping, measuring, or stirring you do most.
- **Easier storage:** items that stack, nest, or hang so your drawers stop jamming.

When you're considering a purchase, name which group it fits. If you can't, it may be an impulse buy.

## Give Your Counter a Simple Layout

A few styling habits make a working kitchen look calm, even when it's full of tools.

### Group by task

Keep cooking tools near the stove and coffee or tea supplies together in one corner. When things sit where you use them, you move less and clutter less.

### Contain the small stuff

A crock, a tray, or a basket turns a scatter of items into one tidy cluster. Put your most-used utensils in a single container within arm's reach of the stove.

### Leave some bare surface

Aim to keep a stretch of counter clear for chopping and setting down groceries. If you can't, move the least-used item off first.

### Go vertical when you can

In a small kitchen, a wall rail, hooks, or a shelf above the counter can hold the things that crowd your work surface.

## Shop Smart on a Budget

Useful doesn't have to mean expensive. A few habits keep spending in check:

- **Replace before you add.** If a new tool does the job of an old one, let the old one go.
- **Check thrift stores first** for sturdy basics like mixing bowls, wooden spoons, and baking pans.
- **Wait a few days.** If you still want it after a weekend, it's more likely to be a real need.
- **Read the reviews that mention daily use**, not just the ones that praise how it looks.

## Do a Quick Reset Every Season

Your cooking habits change. Soup season brings out different tools than grilling season does.

Once in a while, pull everything off your counters and put back only what you used recently. It takes about 15 minutes and often frees up more room than any new organizer would.
