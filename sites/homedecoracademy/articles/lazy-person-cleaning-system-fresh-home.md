BuzzFeed Shopping recently rounded up 25 cleaning products for people who hate cleaning but still want a tidy home. The list's pitch is simple: less scrubbing, wiping, and organizing. Its description even jokes that there's no excuse for a stinky toilet when flower-style toilet gels exist.

The full list of 25 isn't something I can walk you through here, so I won't pretend to. What I can give you is the idea underneath it, turned into a system you can start today: a small kit, a few habits, and a 20-minute weekly reset that makes a room look cared for.

## Start With the Idea, Not the Shopping Cart

A product roundup is tempting, but buying 25 things is its own chore. Storage, clutter, and half-used bottles make a home feel messier.

A better plan is to pick products that remove a task, not add one. Ask of anything you consider: does this save me a step I currently dread?

The toilet gel from BuzzFeed's pitch is a good example. It's a set-it-and-forget-it kind of product aimed at keeping a bathroom fresh between real cleanings. That's the category worth looking for.

## Build a Tiny Cleaning Kit

Keep supplies where you use them. If the spray is under the kitchen sink and you need it in the bathroom, you won't go get it.

A realistic starter setup:

- **Kitchen:** an all-purpose spray and a few cloths on a hook or in a small bin
- **Bathroom:** a toilet cleaning option that works between scrubs, plus a cloth for the sink and mirror
- **Living areas:** a duster or microfiber cloth in a basket you don't mind seeing

Three small stations beat one big caddy you have to haul around.

## The 20-Minute Weekly Reset

Set a timer. When it rings, stop. That rule is what makes this bearable.

### Minutes 1-5: Clear the surfaces

Carry stray items back to their homes. A room looks cleaner the moment the counters, coffee table, and nightstands are clear, even before anything is wiped.

### Minutes 6-12: Wipe the high-impact spots

Hit the kitchen counters, the bathroom sink, and the mirror. These are the spots your eye lands on first.

### Minutes 13-17: Floors where it shows

Focus on entryways and the kitchen. You don't need to do the whole house every week.

### Minutes 18-20: Trash and a fresh touch

Take out the trash and replace the liner. Then do one small sensory thing: open a window, or swap a towel.

## Make the Room Do Some of the Work

Some decorating choices make a home easier to keep tidy. These are cheap and beginner-friendly:

- **Add closed storage.** A lidded basket or a bin under the bed hides clutter faster than you can sort it.
- **Give things a landing spot.** A tray by the door catches keys and mail. A hook catches the bag.
- **Use fewer, bigger items.** One large tray of candles looks calmer than six scattered ones, and it's one thing to dust around.
- **Pick forgiving colors.** Mid-tone rugs and medium-colored throws hide everyday wear better than very light or very dark ones.

In a small space, these choices matter even more because every item is visible.

## Daily Habits That Take Under Two Minutes

The weekly reset goes faster when you do a few tiny things as you go:

1. Wipe the kitchen counter after dinner.
2. Put dishes straight into the dishwasher or the sink to soak.
3. Hang or fold the towel and throw after use.
4. Do a one-minute scan of the living room before bed and return anything that wandered.

None of this feels like cleaning. It just keeps the mess from piling up.

## How to Judge Any Cleaning Product

When you browse a roundup like BuzzFeed's, filter with a few questions:

- Does it replace a task or add one?
- Will I keep it where I use it?
- Is it something I'd actually reach for on a tired day?
- Can I try one before buying several?

If the answer to the first question is no, skip it, however clever it looks.

## Start With One Swap

Don't overhaul your routine this weekend. Pick the one chore you dread most, find one product or habit that shortens it, and set a 20-minute timer for the reset. Once that sticks, add the next small thing.
