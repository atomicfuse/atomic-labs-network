Most of us hang art in the same places: above the sofa, over the bed, along the hallway. It works, but it can leave the rest of your home feeling a little flat.

Good Housekeeping rounded up seven overlooked spots for art, and the idea behind them is charming: it's like hiding little treasures around your home. Below are those seven places, plus simple ways to try each one without spending much or putting a hole in every wall.

The common thread is surprise. A piece of art in an unexpected spot makes a space feel chosen rather than filled in.

## 1. On a Kitchen Shelf

Kitchens often get skipped because they're busy and practical. A shelf is an easy way in.

Lean a small framed piece against the wall between your everyday dishes or cookbooks. No nails needed, and you can swap it out whenever you like.

**Try this:**
- Pick a piece small enough to sit beside a stack of plates.
- Lean it rather than hang it, so it feels relaxed.
- Keep it away from the stove, where grease and steam are a risk.

## 2. Conceal Your TV

A dark television can dominate a room. Good Housekeeping suggests using art to hide it.

I can't tell you from the source exactly how to build this, so choose a method that suits your setup. Some people use a framed piece that slides or hangs over the screen. Others simply surround the TV with art so the screen stops being the only focus.

Either way, the goal is the same: give your eye something else to land on when the TV is off.

## 3. Above a Door Frame

The space over a doorway is wasted wall in most homes. A piece up there draws the eye upward and makes the room feel taller and more finished.

**Try this:**
- Choose something lightweight, since it will be overhead.
- Use a long, narrow piece that echoes the shape of the door frame.
- Check that it's secured well and sits clear of the door's swing.

## 4. Tucked Into the Bathroom

Bathrooms are small, which makes them forgiving. One good piece can turn a purely functional room into something that feels like a little retreat.

If you rent, a leaning frame on a shelf or the back of the toilet tank avoids drilling. Because bathrooms get damp, consider a frame with glass and a print you wouldn't mind replacing. A thrifted find is perfect here, since it's low-stakes.

## 5. Beneath a Staircase

The wall beneath or alongside a staircase is an awkward angle that most people leave bare. Art can make that odd shape feel intentional.

A few smaller pieces grouped together can follow the slant of the stairs. Lay them out on the floor first, then trace each frame on paper and tape the paper to the wall to check your arrangement before you hammer anything.

## 6. In a Cupboard

This is the most playful idea on the list. Good Housekeeping suggests placing art inside a cupboard, so it's a little surprise you find when you open the door.

It's also a gentle, low-risk way to experiment. Nobody sees it unless the door is open, so you can try a bolder print or a quirky thrift-store piece you weren't sure about.

## 7. On the Face of a Bookshelf

Bookshelves can look crowded with spines and clutter. Hanging or propping art on the face of the shelf breaks up that busy look.

**Try this:**
- Lean a small frame in front of a row of books.
- Leave some gaps so the shelf doesn't feel packed.
- Mix art with a few objects you love, like a bowl or a small plant.

## Why this works on a budget

These ideas suit thrifted art, collected objects, and a more lived-in style of decorating. As Good Housekeeping frames it, small design choices are a low-cost way to make a home feel curated without a full renovation.

You don't need big, expensive pieces. Small prints, postcards, and secondhand frames do the job, and the more unexpected the location, the less the art needs to be perfect.

### A simple way to start

1. Walk through your home and pick three overlooked spots from the list.
2. Choose one small piece of art for each, from what you already own or from a thrift store.
3. Lean it first, then commit to hanging it only if you still like it after a week.

## Start with the spot nobody expects

Pick one cupboard, shelf, or doorway today and put a single piece of art there. Small surprises are what make a home feel like yours.
