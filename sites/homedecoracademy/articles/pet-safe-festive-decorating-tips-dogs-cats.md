You don't have to skip the string lights, flowers, or rangoli to keep your dog or cat safe. You just have to decorate with a little forethought.

Per Abplive's Pet First series, veterinarians recommend six simple habits for festive homes with pets. Here they are, translated into room-by-room steps you can do this weekend, plus a few budget-friendly ideas of my own.

The core idea is simple: festive seasons bring extra lights, diyas, flowers, guests, noise, and floor décor, and each one can be a hidden risk for a curious pet. Fix the risks, keep the sparkle.

## 1. Secure electrical wires and lights

Dangling cords look like toys to many pets. Chewing on them is the risk.

Try this:

- Run light strings up high, along walls or window frames, rather than draping them low or across the floor.
- Tuck extra cord length behind furniture.
- Use inexpensive cord covers or tape to hold wires flat against the wall or baseboard.
- Unplug lights when you leave the house or when your pet is alone in the room.

**Budget tip:** Adhesive clips cost very little and get cords out of paw range in minutes.

## 2. Choose sturdier, pet-safe décor

Fragile ornaments that sit low are an accident waiting to happen. A wagging tail or a cat's jump can send them flying, and broken pieces are dangerous.

A few swaps help:

- Put breakable pieces on high shelves or mantels.
- Use sturdier items like fabric, wood, or shatter-resistant decorations at pet level.
- Skip tiny decorations that could be swallowed.

If you're styling a small space, think vertically. Hanging décor and wall pieces add festive color without taking up floor or table space where pets roam.

## 3. Keep diyas and candles out of reach

Open flames and curious pets don't mix. A wagging tail can knock over a lit diya, and a cat may jump onto a surface to investigate.

Safer habits:

- Place diyas and candles on high, stable surfaces your pet can't reach.
- Never leave a lit flame unattended in a room with a pet.
- Consider flameless LED candles for spots your pet can get to. They give you the glow for a few dollars each.

Lighting matters for mood, and LED options let you layer warm light at low levels without any risk to a swishing tail.

## 4. Be careful with flowers and garlands

Fresh flowers and garlands brighten a room, but some can harm cats and dogs, and pets often chew on what hangs or sits within reach.

What to do:

- Check whether a flower is toxic to pets before bringing it home. Your vet can help if you're unsure.
- Hang garlands high, away from nibbling mouths.
- Place flower arrangements on tall surfaces, not low tables or the floor.
- Sweep up fallen petals and leaves promptly.

If you're unsure about a particular bloom, ask your vet or choose a different one. Fabric or paper flowers are a reusable, budget-friendly stand-in.

## 5. Prevent access to rangoli powders and glitter

Floor décor sits right where pets walk, sniff, and lick their paws. Rangoli powders and glitter can be a problem if they get ingested or tracked around.

To keep floors festive and safe:

- Create your rangoli in a spot your pet can't enter, such as behind a closed door or a baby gate.
- Keep powders and glitter stored in sealed containers, up high.
- Clean up spills right away.

If your pet needs to be near the entryway, consider making the design on a board or mat you can lift and move out of reach, or choose a version that doesn't involve loose powder.

## 6. Create a designated pet-friendly space

Festive time also means guests and noise, which can stress some pets. A calm corner gives them somewhere to retreat.

Set one up in about 15 minutes:

1. Pick a quiet room or corner away from the main action and the door.
2. Add their bed or blanket, water, and a favorite toy.
3. Keep decorations in that space minimal, with no loose cords, flames, or flowers.
4. Let your pet use it before the party starts, so it feels familiar.

This is also the easiest fix of all: a pet who is comfortable in their own space is less likely to wander toward the hazards.

## A quick walk-through before guests arrive

Get down to your pet's eye level and look around the room. You'll spot trailing cords, low ornaments, and reachable petals fast.

Then run through this list:

- Cords tucked or lifted
- Fragile items moved up
- Flames and flowers out of reach
- Powders and glitter sealed or blocked off
- Pet space ready

## The takeaway

Before you hang a single light this season, crouch down to your pet's level and scan the room. Fix whatever you can reach from there, and your festive décor will look just as good with a lot less worry.
