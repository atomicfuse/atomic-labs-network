BuzzFeed Shopping recently published a roundup of 23 Walmart products meant to give your home a glow-up. The full list isn't reproduced here, so this article won't guess at what's on it. What it can do is take the idea behind a list like that and make it useful: a way to refresh your home one room at a time without a renovation or a big spending spree.

By the end, you'll have a simple plan for deciding what to buy, what to skip, and where a small spend makes the biggest visual difference.

## Start With One Annoyance, Not a Shopping List

Roundups are fun to scroll, but they can lead to a cart full of items that don't work together. Before you buy anything, walk through your home and note one thing that bugs you in each room.

For example:

- The living room feels dim in the evening.
- The bedroom looks cluttered.
- The entryway has nowhere to drop keys.
- The bathroom feels bare.

That list is your real shopping list. Each product you consider should fix one of those problems.

## Living Room: Fix the Lighting First

Lighting changes how a room feels faster than almost any other swap. A single overhead light tends to flatten a space.

Try this:

1. Add at least two lamps at different heights, such as a table lamp and a floor lamp.
2. Use warm-toned bulbs for a cozier look.
3. Turn off the overhead light in the evening and see how it feels.

If you have budget left, add a throw blanket and one or two pillow covers. Covers are cheaper than full pillows, and they're easy to switch out when you want a new look.

## Bedroom: Layer Instead of Replace

You don't need new furniture to make a bedroom feel calmer. Layering does most of the work.

- **Bedding:** Choose two or three colors that go together. Neutral sheets with one accent color is a safe start.
- **A throw:** Fold it across the foot of the bed for texture.
- **Clear surfaces:** A small tray on the nightstand corrals clutter so it looks intentional.

If your room is small, keep colors light and limit what sits on the floor. Open floor space makes a tight room feel larger.

## Entryway and Small Spaces: Make Every Inch Work

Small spaces look better when they're organized. A few wall hooks, a slim shelf, or a basket for shoes can turn a messy corner into a tidy landing spot.

Two quick rules help:

- Go up, not out. Use wall space before floor space.
- Hide the clutter. Baskets and lidded boxes look cleaner than open piles.

A mirror is also worth considering. It bounces light around and makes a narrow area feel a bit bigger.

## Bathroom: Small Swaps Add Up

Bathrooms are usually the cheapest room to refresh. Matching towels, a new shower curtain, and a simple soap dispenser can make the space feel pulled together.

Pick one color family and stick with it. Mixed colors and mismatched bottles are what make a bathroom look busy.

## Renters: Choose Changes You Can Undo

If you rent, focus on things you can take with you or remove easily:

- Lamps, curtains, and rugs
- Removable hooks and peel-and-stick options (check the package for what they're safe on)
- Bedding, pillow covers, and throws
- Art you can lean on a shelf instead of hanging

Curtains are a particularly good swap. Hanging them closer to the ceiling can make walls look taller.

## How to Shop a Big Roundup Wisely

A long list of home finds is a good place to get ideas, but a few habits will keep your budget in check.

### Set a cap per room

Decide how much you'll spend in each room before you browse. Even a small number, like a set amount for the bedroom, keeps you from overbuying.

### Check the details

Look at the dimensions, materials, and recent reviews before you buy. A rug that's too small or a lamp that's too tall can undo the whole effort.

### Buy in stages

Start with lighting and textiles, live with them for a week, then add more. You'll notice what's still missing.

### Keep a return plan

Save your receipts and check the return policy, so a wrong color or size is an easy fix.

## What Makes a Room Look More Expensive

You don't need pricey items to get a polished look. These habits do more than any single product:

- **Stick to a palette.** Three main colors is plenty.
- **Mix textures.** A woven basket, a soft throw, and a smooth ceramic piece feel richer together.
- **Leave some empty space.** Not every surface needs decor.
- **Group items in odd numbers.** Three objects on a shelf usually look better than two or four.

## Your First Step This Weekend

Pick the one room that bothers you most, name the single problem, and fix only that. One lamp, one set of curtains, or one tidy basket is enough to start. When that room feels better, move on to the next.
