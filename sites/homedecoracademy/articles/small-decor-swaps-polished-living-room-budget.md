You don't need a big budget or a weekend of work to make a living room feel more pulled together. Sometimes it's a handful of small pieces in the right places.

Real Simple recently rounded up 11 living room decor picks from an interior designer, all available at Amazon, with prices starting at just $9. The published summary names coffee table books, coasters, area rugs, side tables and picture frames, among other pieces. It doesn't spell out the full list of 11 or each price, so I won't guess at them here.

What I can do is show you how to use those categories. Think of this as a plan for putting small, affordable swaps to work.

## Start with the pieces people touch and see most

The categories named in the roundup have something in common: they're all easy to add without renovating anything. They also work for renters, since none of them require paint, drilling into walls (frames aside, and those can be leaned), or changing fixtures.

Here's a simple order of operations:

1. Ground the room with a rug.
2. Make sure every seat has a surface nearby.
3. Style the surfaces you have.
4. Add personal touches on the walls or shelves.

## Area rugs: define the space

A rug is often the piece that makes a living room look finished. It marks out where the seating area begins and ends, and it adds color or texture underfoot.

A few beginner-friendly tips:

- Choose a rug large enough that at least the front legs of your sofa and chairs sit on it.
- If your furniture is patterned, go calmer on the rug. If your room is mostly solid colors, a pattern can add interest.
- Measure your floor with painter's tape before you order. Seeing the outline in real life prevents the "too small" surprise.

## Side tables: give every seat a landing spot

A sofa with nowhere to set a drink feels unfinished, and it's less comfortable to use. A side table fixes both problems.

When shopping, check these three things:

- **Height:** Aim for something close to the arm of your sofa or chair.
- **Size:** In a small room, a slim or round table is easier to walk around.
- **Finish:** Match the wood tone or metal to something already in the room, or deliberately contrast it.

Once it's in place, keep it simple: a lamp or a small plant, plus room for a mug.

## Coffee table books: instant styling with a purpose

Coffee table books are on the designer's list, and it's easy to see why. They add height, color and a bit of personality to a flat surface, and they give guests something to flip through.

Try this arrangement on your coffee table:

- Stack two or three books, largest on the bottom.
- Set a small object on top, like a candle, a bowl or a little plant.
- Choose subjects you actually like, such as travel, food, art or gardening, so the stack feels like yours.

If you already own books with nice spines or covers, use those first. Buying new ones only makes sense if you're filling a gap.

## Coasters: a small buy that protects your furniture

Coasters are the least glamorous item on the list, and probably the most practical. They protect your table from rings, and a good set looks like a deliberate part of the setup rather than an afterthought.

Keep them in a small tray or bowl on the coffee table so they're easy to grab. Pick a material and color that echoes something else in the room, like the wood of your side table or the tone of your rug.

## Picture frames: make your walls feel personal

Frames are a low-cost way to turn blank space into something that feels lived in. You can fill them with family photos, postcards, kids' art or prints you like.

A few easy ways to make them look intentional:

- Stick with one or two frame finishes, such as black and wood, so a mixed group feels cohesive.
- Lay frames on the floor and arrange them before you hang anything.
- Renters can lean larger frames on a shelf or console instead of making holes.

## Why small swaps work

A living room tends to look expensive when it looks considered. That usually means texture on the floor, surfaces that are styled but not cluttered, and a few personal details that make the space feel like yours. None of that requires one big purchase.

Per Real Simple, shoppers are looking for budget-friendly ways to refresh their homes, and the designer's picks reflect that, with items starting at $9. That price point means you can try one or two ideas, see how the room feels, and add more later.

## A simple shopping plan

Before you buy anything, take a quick look at your room and answer three questions:

- What feels bare? Add a rug, frames or books.
- What feels inconvenient? Add a side table or coasters.
- What feels impersonal? Swap in your own photos and favorite books.

Compare sizes and read the dimensions carefully on any online listing. Returns are easier when you catch a mismatch early, but it's even easier to measure first.

Pick the one thing in your living room that bugs you most, fix just that this week, and let the rest follow.
