A living room can look polished and still be a place where people actually put their feet up. You can borrow that idea at home without a luxury budget, and this article shows you how.

The inspiration comes from Modern Luxury's Design 9 Awards, which named a Mandeville Canyon residence by Marmol Radziner as Southern California's best living room. Per Modern Luxury, the room makes space for softer silhouettes and a whole lot of family time.

That is all the project detail we have, so we won't guess at the furniture list or the finishes. What we can do is take the two ideas the room is recognized for, softness and family use, and turn them into steps you can try this weekend.

## Why this idea is worth borrowing

High-end design, per the Modern Luxury coverage, is leaning warmer, more livable and less rigid. Homeowners want comfort and gathering space, not a showpiece room nobody sits in.

That shift is good news if you're working with a modest budget. Comfort and ease don't cost extra. Often they cost less than a formal look.

## Step 1: Soften the hard edges

"Softer silhouettes" simply means furniture and accessories with gentler lines: rounded corners, curved backs, and fewer sharp angles. Here's how to get there without replacing your whole room.

- **Swap the coffee table.** A round or oval table is often the quickest change. Secondhand shops and online marketplaces are good places to look.
- **Add one curved piece.** A rounded accent chair or a curved-back stool is plenty. You don't need everything to curve.
- **Use round accessories.** A round mirror, a round tray or a rounded lamp base breaks up a boxy layout.
- **Choose fabrics that drape.** Linen-look curtains and a soft throw ease up straight lines.

If you're not ready to buy anything, try rounding the room's "feel" by removing one square item, such as a stiff console, and see how the space changes.

## Step 2: Plan the room around how your family really uses it

A family-centered living room starts with questions, not shopping. Spend a few days noticing:

1. Where does everyone actually sit?
2. What do you set down when you walk in: bags, remotes, mugs?
3. Which spot always ends up empty?

Then adjust. If nobody uses the formal chair in the corner, move it, or replace it with floor cushions or a pouf for flexible seating. If drinks land on the floor, add a side table within arm's reach of each seat.

### Create easy gathering spots

Pull seating closer together. Seats that sit about conversation distance apart make a room feel welcoming. A sofa pushed against the far wall can leave a gap that feels cold. Try floating it a little toward the middle, then see if talking is easier.

### Keep paths clear

Leave a comfortable walkway through the room. If you're turning sideways to get past furniture, something is too big or too crowded. Removing one piece often helps more than adding one.

## Step 3: Choose finishes that can handle daily life

Relaxed doesn't mean careless. A few practical choices make a family room easier to live with:

- **Washable slipcovers or removable cushion covers** are far cheaper to replace than a whole sofa.
- **A low-pile or flat-weave rug** is easier to vacuum and clean than a plush one.
- **Baskets with lids** hide toys, blankets and chargers in seconds.
- **Wood tables with a little character** hide scratches better than glossy surfaces.

## Step 4: Keep it warm, not fussy

Warmth is a mood you can build cheaply. Use lighting first: swap a harsh overhead light for a couple of lamps with warm bulbs at different heights. A lamp on a side table and another in a corner makes evenings feel calmer immediately.

Then choose a limited color palette. Pick two or three tones you love, such as soft cream, a mid-tone wood and one deeper accent, and repeat them. A restrained palette lets the room feel polished even when the pieces are thrifted or mismatched.

## Step 5: Add polish in small doses

The Marmol Radziner room is described as balancing architectural polish with family life. You can echo that balance on a smaller scale:

- Hang curtains high and wide so windows look larger.
- Frame a few pieces of art or family photos in matching frames.
- Edit your surfaces. Three well-chosen objects on a shelf look better than ten scattered ones.
- Fold throws and line up cushions once a day. It takes a minute and the room still feels lived in.

## A realistic weekend plan

If you want to try this without spending much, here's one way to split it up:

**Saturday morning:** Watch how the room is used and move furniture closer for conversation.

**Saturday afternoon:** Hunt for one round piece, such as a coffee table or mirror, secondhand.

**Sunday:** Add warm lamps, a basket for clutter and a washable throw. Edit surfaces down.

That is a handful of changes, and none of them require a renovation.

## The takeaway

Don't decorate a living room to be admired from the doorway. Pick one hard-edged piece to replace with a rounded one, then arrange your seating so your family wants to sit down together.
