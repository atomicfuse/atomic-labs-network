Your room might be one or two small tweaks away from looking finished. A recent HomeDecor piece, originally reported by Yahoo, lists five common decorating mistakes that designers reportedly notice right away, from limp throw pillows to backwards books. The good news: each one is cheap and quick to fix.

The common thread is that a home looks better when your choices feel intentional, personal, and properly scaled. Here are the five, with practical ways to fix each one.

## 1. Too Many Small Pieces of Art

A wall dotted with lots of little frames can read as scattered, especially over a sofa or bed. The scale feels off, and the wall never settles into one clear look.

**Try this:**

- Swap several tiny frames for one or two larger pieces.
- If you love your small pieces, group them tightly so they read as one bigger shape.
- Lay everything on the floor first and rearrange before you hammer a single nail.

A good test: stand back. If the wall looks like confetti, go bigger or group closer.

## 2. Relying on So-So Pillows

The source calls out pillows that are just okay. Think flat, limp inserts or covers you picked because they were fine. Pillows are one of the easiest ways to make a sofa look considered, so "fine" is a missed chance.

**Try this:**

- Replace tired inserts with fuller ones so pillows hold their shape.
- Pick covers you actually like, rather than whatever matched in the store.
- Start with two pillows you love instead of a pile of five you don't.

New covers cost far less than new pillows, so that's a budget-friendly place to start.

## 3. Choosing Mass-Market Art Only

There's nothing wrong with affordable prints. The issue, per the piece, is when every piece on your walls is mass-market. The room starts to look like it could belong to anyone.

This ties into a wider move toward personality-driven interiors, vintage finds, and meaningful decor over copy-paste trends.

**Try this:**

- Mix in one thrifted or vintage piece among the store-bought ones.
- Frame something personal, like a postcard from a trip or a child's drawing.
- Look at flea markets and secondhand shops for art with a bit of history.

One unexpected piece can make the whole wall feel chosen.

## 4. Turning Books Backwards

The page-edges-out look is a styling trick some people use for a neutral palette. The source flags it as a mistake designers notice. It hides the titles, which are part of what makes a shelf feel like yours.

**Try this:**

- Turn the spines back out. Let your real books show.
- Stack a few horizontally to vary the rhythm, then rest a small object on top.
- If color bugs you, group books by tone rather than hiding them.

## 5. Treating Every Shelf the Same with Trendy Items

When each shelf gets the same formula of trendy accessories, the whole unit looks like a catalog page. Shelves feel better when they carry a mix.

**Try this:**

- Combine books, a plant, and one object with meaning to you on each shelf.
- Vary heights and leave some empty space so things can breathe.
- Skip the trend piece if you don't love it. A shelf of things you care about beats a shelf of things you saw online.

## Do a Quick Room Audit

You can check all five in about 15 minutes. Walk into your living room as if you were a guest and ask:

1. Does the art feel the right size for the wall?
2. Do the pillows look full and chosen?
3. Is there at least one piece that's personal or vintage?
4. Are the book spines facing out?
5. Does each shelf have its own mix, not a copied formula?

Any "no" is your starting point. Fix just one this weekend and see how the room feels.

## Start With the Thing You Love

Pick one personal object, whether it's a thrifted frame, a favorite book, or a souvenir, and give it a visible spot today. Intentional style usually begins with something that's actually yours.
