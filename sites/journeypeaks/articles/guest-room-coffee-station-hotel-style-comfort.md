## Why a guest-room coffee station works

Homes & Gardens argues that in-room coffee adds boutique-hotel comfort, convenience, and a touch of everyday luxury for holiday visitors. That matches what travelers already know. Think about the last hotel room you loved. It probably wasn't the thread count that stuck with you. It was not having to hunt for a kitchen, or worry about waking the house, to get your first cup.

A station in the room solves that for your guests. They can wake up at their own pace, skip the awkward early-morning kitchen small talk if they'd rather, and feel looked after.

## Why a compact single-serve machine fits

Guest rooms are rarely big, and a dresser or console table only has so much room. A single-serve machine suits that for a few practical reasons:

- **Small footprint.** The Keurig in the deal is described as compact and space-saving, so it won't swallow a surface.
- **One cup at a time.** Guests make exactly what they want, when they want it, with no pot going stale.
- **No learning curve.** Pop in a pod, press a button. Nobody needs a tutorial at 6 a.m.

Homes & Gardens also floats the same idea for bedrooms, offices, and small kitchens, so if the guest room is empty most of the year, the machine can earn its keep elsewhere.

## How to set it up like a boutique hotel

The machine is only the start. Hotels make a station feel intentional through arrangement. Here's a simple doing-order.

### 1. Pick a sturdy, accessible surface

A dresser, narrow console, or small cart all work. Keep it near an outlet so no cord stretches across the floor, and keep it away from the edge of the surface.

### 2. Use a tray

A tray visually groups everything and makes the corner look styled rather than cluttered. It also catches drips, which protects your furniture.

### 3. Stock a small, tidy selection

Offer a handful of pod varieties, including a decaf option, plus tea if you have it. Put them in a small basket or dish so guests can browse. Add sugar, a stirrer or two, and creamer or a milk alternative if you can keep it fresh.

### 4. Add real mugs

Two good mugs feel far more welcoming than disposable cups. Set them next to the machine.

### 5. Fill the water reservoir before guests arrive

This is the detail that makes it feel ready. Top it off and leave a pod nearby so the first cup takes under a minute.

### 6. Leave a small note

A short line about where extra pods and spare water live saves guests from guessing. Hotels do the same thing with a card by the minibar.

## Other small touches that feel like a hotel

Homes & Gardens frames the coffee maker as one of several small luxuries that make overnight guests feel welcome. Pair it with things travelers appreciate when they check in: a water carafe or bottles on the nightstand, a phone charger within reach of the bed, and a spot to set down a suitcase. None of these are expensive, and together they say you planned for the visit.
