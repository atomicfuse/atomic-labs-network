Multi-day food-and-wellness programs at luxury resorts are easy to book and hard to evaluate from a brochure. Forbes recently covered one in detail: a three-day luxury dining series from Marriott International at Mandapa, a Ritz-Carlton Reserve in Bali. This piece uses that report to show what such a program typically contains, and how to decide whether one is worth your money.

We only have the outline of the Forbes story, so details like prices, dates and exact menus aren't covered here. Check the resort directly for those.

## What the Mandapa program is

Per Forbes, the series pairs elaborate meals with culinary activities and wellness rituals over three days at Mandapa. It is aimed at affluent travelers who want more than a nice room and a spa menu. The three pillars are:

- **Dining:** multi-course, high-end meals as the centerpiece.
- **Hands-on culinary workshops:** you cook or learn, rather than only eat.
- **Traditional wellness rituals:** practices tied to Bali, folded into the stay.

The title of the Forbes piece, "Eat, Pray, Forage," hints at the tone: food, spiritual calm and ingredient-hunting rolled into one itinerary.

## Why resorts are building programs like this

In our read, this reflects a broader shift. Luxury travel is leaning toward experiences and wellness instead of pure indulgence, and a curated multi-day program is one way hospitality brands compete for high-net-worth guests. Bali is already a major wellness destination, so a resort there has a natural story to tell about culture, food and self-care together.

For you, that means the amenity race (thread count, pool size) matters less than what the resort will actually have you do.

## How to judge a program like this before you book

These are our suggestions, not claims from the Forbes report. Ask the resort:

### 1. How hands-on is it?
A workshop where you chop, cook and taste is different from a demonstration you watch. Ask how many guests per session and what you personally do.

### 2. Where do the ingredients come from?
Foraging and local sourcing are selling points only if the resort can tell you specifics: which farms, which markets, who guides you.

### 3. Are the wellness rituals Balinese in substance?
Ask who leads them and what they involve. Traditional practices should have real context, not just a rebranded massage.

### 4. What's the pacing?
Three days of elaborate meals plus activities can be a lot. Look for free time built in, so the schedule doesn't become a checklist.

### 5. What's included?
Clarify whether meals, workshops and rituals are bundled, and what costs extra. At this tier, "experience" pricing can hide add-ons.

## Who it suits

It fits you if you travel for the table and want to come home with a skill or a story, and if you like having an itinerary designed for you. If you prefer to wander Bali's markets on your own schedule, a tightly programmed stay may feel like too much structure, and you could take the same interests in a looser direction.

## Plan it like an insider

Whether you choose Mandapa or another resort, treat a program like this as a package to interrogate, not just admire. Read the Forbes piece for the full firsthand account, then send the resort the five questions above before you commit a single night.
