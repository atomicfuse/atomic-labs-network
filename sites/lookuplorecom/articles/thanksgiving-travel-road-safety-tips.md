Thanksgiving storms battered much of the U.S. this week, and the disruption was hard to miss. Per HuffPost, heavy rain, high winds, coastal flooding, snow and power outages hit from the East Coast to California, during one of the busiest travel periods of the year. ## What Happened on Thanksgiving

HuffPost reports that the extreme weather forced the postponement of Macy's 100th Thanksgiving Day Parade. Other parts of the country dealt with power outages and heavy snow. The coverage also points to flight delays and infrastructure damage, with impacts stretching coast to coast.

The details of individual outages and closures vary by region, and conditions change quickly. For current information near you, check your local National Weather Service office and your utility's outage map.

## Why Coastal Flooding Can Catch You Off Guard

The coverage highlights coastal flooding and king tides as part of the story. A king tide is an especially high tide that happens when the sun, moon and Earth line up in ways that boost the normal tidal range. On its own, it can push water into low-lying streets. When a storm arrives at the same time, wind and rain can add to the water already pushed ashore.

That's why a street can flood on a day that doesn't look dramatic from your window. If you live near the coast or are driving through a shoreline town, check the tide schedule alongside the forecast.

## What to Do If Roads Flood While You're Driving

This is general safety guidance from standard National Weather Service advice, not something drawn from the HuffPost report.

### Turn around instead of pushing through

The National Weather Service's well-known slogan is "Turn Around, Don't Drown." You can't judge water depth by looking at it, and the road beneath may be washed out. A small amount of moving water can move a vehicle.

### Plan a different route before you need one

- Check your route for closures before you leave, then again at your last fuel or rest stop.
- Keep a second route in mind, ideally on higher ground.
- Don't follow a GPS reroute onto a road that looks submerged.

### Stay put if you're stuck

If your car stalls in rising water, get to higher ground if you can do so safely. If you're surrounded by water, call 911 and tell them where you are.

## Planning Around Delays and Outages

With flight delays and power outages in the mix, a few habits help when the weather turns:

- **Check flight status before heading to the airport.** Your airline's app will usually tell you about delays sooner than the departures board.
- **Charge devices ahead of a storm.** Outages are a risk when winds are high, so top off phones and a backup battery.
- **Keep a basic kit in the car.** Water, a flashlight, a blanket and a phone charger cover most surprises on a long drive.
- **Build in slack.** A flexible arrival time makes a delay an annoyance rather than a crisis.

## The Bigger Picture

HuffPost's coverage frames the storms as part of a pattern in which coastal flooding, king tides and extreme weather increasingly disrupt major public events, transportation and local infrastructure. A postponed parade is a visible example, but the same forces affect roads, power lines and airports that most people rely on without thinking about it.

The practical takeaway: before your next holiday trip, check the forecast, the tide table if you're heading to the coast, and a backup route. Then, if you meet water over the road, turn around.
