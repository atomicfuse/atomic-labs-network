## Content Guidelines
Content guidelines
Category	What to publish	Example headline
Scenic Day Hikes	Short loops, waterfall walks, lakeside trails, and hikes with a memorable viewpoint.	7 Short Hikes With Big Views in the Smokies
National Parks	First-visit itineraries, scenic drives, and easy walks organized by how much time readers have.	One Day in Acadia? Here’s How to Spend It
Weekend Getaways	Mountain towns and road trips anchored by a hike, with ideas for where to stay, eat, or stop along the way.	5 Mountain Towns Where You Can Walk From Main Street to the Trail
Trail Tips	Practical advice on choosing a trail, packing for a day hike, and understanding trail ratings.	What “Moderate” Actually Feels Like on a Day Hike


- Lead with the reason to go. Tell readers what they’ll see, where it is, and who will enjoy it. Use clear, conversational language and specific details.
- Give every trail guide an at-a-glance box. Include distance, route type, elevation gain, estimated time, trailhead, and parking. Explain what makes the hike feel easy or challenging, such as steep stairs, uneven footing, or limited shade.
- Help readers plan the rest of the day. Add a nearby lunch stop, scenic drive, or second short walk when it fits. For roundups, give readers a reason to choose each trail, such as a shaded route, a lake view, or a quick walk from the trailhead.
- Verify trip details before publishing. Check closures, permits, fees, and seasonal access with the official park or land manager. Link to that source and show when the guide was last checked.
