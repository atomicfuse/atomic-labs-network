Airport food and drink are expensive, and a gate area with no free seats is nobody's idea of a good start to a trip. A lounge can fix both. Per Yahoo's rundown aimed at UK travellers, there are five main ways to get in for less than walking up and paying the door price. The right one depends on how often you fly and what you're willing to commit to.

Here's how to pick, starting with the questions that narrow it down fastest.

## Start With How Often You Fly

Yahoo's core argument is that lounge access is good value only when it matches your travel habits. Before comparing options, answer three questions:

- How many times a year do you fly?
- Do you fly from the same airport or a handful of different ones?
- Do you already pay for a credit card, a bank account with perks, or a loyalty scheme?

If you fly once or twice a year, you probably don't want an annual commitment. If you fly constantly, a recurring route will almost always beat paying per visit. The details of pricing and lounge networks vary, so check current terms for any product before you sign up.

## 1. Credit Card Rewards

Some credit cards include lounge access as a perk. Yahoo lists this first, and it's the one to check if you already hold a card. Look at your existing card's benefits page before buying anything new, since you may be paying for access you've never used.

If you're considering a new card, weigh the annual fee and any interest costs against the lounge visits you'd realistically make. A perk you use twice a year rarely justifies a pricey card on its own. Lounge perks are increasingly a selling point in card marketing, so read the conditions closely: how many visits are included, whether guests are covered, and which lounges count.

## 2. Packaged Bank Account Perks

Packaged bank accounts, the kind with a monthly fee that bundles in extras, can include lounge access. Yahoo flags this as a route many people overlook.

The maths is simple but easy to skip. Add up the monthly fee across the year, then check what else the package gives you, such as travel insurance or breakdown cover, that you'd otherwise buy separately. If you'd use those extras anyway, lounge access becomes a bonus rather than the whole reason to pay. If you wouldn't, it's an expensive way to get a sandwich and a sofa.

## 3. Get a Membership

A dedicated lounge membership suits people who fly often enough to use it repeatedly but don't have the status or ticket class to get in otherwise. You pay up front or annually, then use the membership across participating lounges.

The catch is coverage. A membership is only worth it if the lounges it includes are at the airports you actually use. Before paying, look up your usual departure airports and confirm they're covered. Also check whether guests cost extra, which matters if you travel as a couple or family.

## 4. Fly Business or First Class

This is the most expensive route in headline terms, but lounge access comes bundled with a premium ticket. Yahoo includes it because, for some trips, the upgrade fare is already on the table, and the lounge is part of what you're paying for.

Don't buy a premium seat just for the lounge. But if you're weighing an upgrade for the seat, the lounge adds real value to that decision, particularly on a long journey or a tight connection where a quiet place to sit, eat and charge your phone makes a difference.

## 5. Have Airline Elite Status

Frequent flyers who reach elite tiers with an airline can often use lounges even on standard tickets. If you already concentrate your flying with one carrier or alliance, it's worth checking where you stand and what the next tier would unlock.

The practical move: if you're close to a threshold, consider consolidating your bookings with one airline. If you're nowhere near, don't chase status just for lounge entry.

## What About a One-Off Pass?

If none of the five routes fits, a single-visit pass is the low-commitment option. Yahoo's framing suggests it can be worthwhile when it replaces what you'd spend anyway. The comparison is straightforward: if a pass costs around £20 and you'd otherwise buy a meal and a couple of drinks at the airport, the lounge can come out ahead, especially on a long wait.

To test it, price your usual airport spend before you go. Count a meal, two drinks and a snack, then compare. If the pass wins on a delayed or early-morning flight, it's a smart one-off. If you'd only grab a coffee, skip it.

## Which Route Fits Which Traveller

- **Occasional holidaymaker:** Check the card or bank account you already have, then consider a one-off pass for long waits.
- **Frequent flyer:** Compare a membership against elite status on your most-used airline.
- **Business traveller:** Premium tickets and elite status are likely already in play, so confirm what your fares include.
- **Points collector:** Look closely at card perks and how they stack with airline status.

## Mistakes That Waste Money

A few common traps follow from the above:

- Paying for a card or account mainly for lounge access you'll rarely use.
- Buying a membership without checking it covers your home airport.
- Forgetting that guests may cost extra.
- Paying for a pass without comparing it to what you'd really spend on food and drink.

## The Takeaway

Before your next trip, check what you already own. Open your card and bank account benefits, look up your airline status, and only then decide whether a membership or one-off pass makes sense. The cheapest lounge access is often the kind you're already paying for.
