Airport parking is one of those trip costs that can quietly grow. The biggest culprit, per travel expert Ricky Durrance's advice as reported by Yahoo, isn't the daily rate. It's a timing mistake that tips you into an extra day of charges.

This checklist walks you through the checks to make before you book, how to work out the time you'll actually leave the car park, and how to add a buffer without overdoing it. It takes about ten minutes and suits anyone driving to an airport, especially families on a school break when airports are busy and every pound or dollar counts.

## Why timing matters more than the rate

The main takeaway from Durrance's advice is simple: understand how your car park charges, plan beyond your flight times, and add only a sensible buffer.

That order matters. A cheap headline rate can end up costing more than a pricier one if your return time slips past a charging threshold. Small timing errors turn into whole extra days on the bill.

## Step 1: Find out how your car park counts a day

Before you compare prices, check how the car park defines a day. There are two common approaches, and they can lead to very different totals:

- **Calendar-day charging:** each calendar date you're parked counts as a day, so a late-evening return can add a full extra day once the clock passes midnight.
- **24-hour charging:** you're billed in 24-hour blocks from the moment you enter, so what matters is how many hours pass, not which dates you touch.

The booking terms should say which applies. If they don't, check the operator's website or contact them before you pay. Everything else in this checklist depends on that answer.

## Step 2: Plan around your real exit time, not your landing time

Your flight's scheduled arrival isn't when you leave the car park. Between landing and driving out, you'll typically need to cover:

1. Getting off the plane and through the terminal
2. Passport control and baggage reclaim
3. Walking to the car park, or waiting for and riding a shuttle bus
4. Reaching your car and driving to the exit

Work forward from your scheduled landing time and add a realistic amount for each step. If your car park uses a shuttle, factor in the wait and the ride as well.

## Step 3: Watch the midnight line

This is where the extra-day charge most often sneaks in. If your flight is due to land late in the evening, a delay or a slow baggage belt can push your exit past midnight.

Under calendar-day charging, that can mean paying for another full day for the sake of a few minutes. Look at your landing time with this in mind. If you're close to midnight, either book the extra day deliberately (so you're covered) or choose a car park with 24-hour charging.

## Step 4: Add a sensible buffer, not a huge one

Durrance's advice is to add a buffer, but a sensible one. Too little leaves you exposed to delays. Too much means paying for hours you never use, and that can itself tip you into another day.

A practical approach:

- Start with your scheduled landing time.
- Add your estimated time for the terminal, bags, and shuttle.
- Add a modest cushion for a late flight.
- Compare that final exit time against the car park's day boundary.

If the buffered time lands just over a boundary, the answer isn't to shave the buffer to nothing. Decide whether the cushion or the extra day is worth more to you, and book accordingly.

## Step 5: Check the grace period and the terms

Some car parks may allow a short grace period after your booked exit time, while others charge the moment you overrun. Don't assume. Read the terms for what happens if you leave late, and note any fees that apply. Knowing this in advance tells you how much risk you're really carrying.

## A quick pre-booking checklist

Run through these before you pay:

- Does the car park charge by calendar day or by 24 hours?
- What is my realistic exit time, including baggage, walking, and any shuttle?
- Does that time cross midnight or another charging boundary?
- Is my buffer sensible, neither skimpy nor excessive?
- What are the late-exit terms and any grace period?

## When this matters most

Per Yahoo's report, the advice lands as families head to airports for October half-term and look to trim holiday costs. Busy travel periods bring a greater chance of queues and delays, so your return timing is less predictable. That's exactly when a small miscalculation can get expensive.

## The one thing to do

Before you book, find out whether your car park charges by calendar day or by 24 hours, then calculate your return exit time against that rule. That single check does more to protect your budget than any rate comparison.
