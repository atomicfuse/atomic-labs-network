Active travel is booming, and the usual picks for a biking or hiking trip are well known. If you want something less obvious, Yahoo's recent look at Backroads highlights two newer places: a route from Krakow to Budapest, and Egypt. Here is what is known about them, what isn't, and how to decide whether either fits your next trip.

## What the report says

Per Yahoo, active travel has surged since the pandemic as people look for immersive, healthier, experience-led vacations that go beyond traditional sightseeing. The piece positions Backroads as a leader in finding emerging destinations for guided cycling, hiking, trekking and multi-sport trips. Its edge, according to the report, is building original itineraries in places travelers may not yet associate with active vacations.

Two destinations anchor the story:

1. Krakow to Budapest
2. Egypt

The coverage I have doesn't include day-by-day routes, mileage, pricing, seasons or trip lengths for either one. So I won't invent them. What follows is how to use the information that does exist to plan well.

## Krakow to Budapest: a trip that moves

The name tells you the format. This is a point-to-point journey between two cities, not a single-base vacation. That shapes how you plan.

**Who it suits:** Travelers who like seeing multiple places without repacking daily logistics themselves. A guided trip handles that, which is a big part of the appeal of an operator like Backroads.

**What to confirm before booking:**
- Total trip length and how many days are spent actively moving
- Daily distances and the difficulty level for each activity
- Whether it's cycling, hiking or a mix
- Which season the trip runs, since weather will affect both comfort and scenery
- What's included, such as lodging, meals and luggage transfer

**Add days at the ends.** Both cities are worth time on their own. If you can, arrive a day early in Krakow and stay a day after in Budapest. Check with the operator on whether those nights can be added.

## Egypt: an active trip in a place people rarely pair with one

Egypt is the more surprising item. Most visitors associate it with sightseeing, and the premise of the Yahoo piece is that Backroads builds active itineraries in places people don't usually connect with hiking or cycling.

That makes it a good test case for the guided model. If you're curious about a destination but unsure how to move around it on your own, a guided trip handles the planning that can feel daunting.

**Ask the operator:**
- What the activity actually is on each day (walking, hiking, cycling)
- How heat is managed, including start times and rest stops
- Which sights are included and how much time you get at each
- What fitness level is expected

## Why a guided active trip, and who it's for

The pitch in the report is luxury active travel: movement and sightseeing in the same itinerary. That works best for people who:

- Want exercise built into the day, not squeezed in before breakfast
- Prefer someone else to plan routes, lodging and logistics
- Like the idea of seeing a place at human speed

It works less well if you want a lazy beach week or total control over your schedule.

## How to decide between them

Use a few simple filters:

- **You want a classic European mix of cities and landscape:** Start with Krakow to Budapest.
- **You want a destination that feels less expected for an active trip:** Look at Egypt.
- **You're new to active travel:** Choose whichever offers the easier activity level and ask about a less strenuous option.
- **You're short on time:** Ask the operator whether shorter versions exist. Don't assume they do.

## A quick pre-booking checklist

1. Get the full itinerary in writing, with daily activity details.
2. Check the season, since hours, closures and weather can change what's possible.
3. Confirm the fitness level required, and be honest about yours.
4. Ask what's included and what costs extra.
5. Verify entry and visa requirements for your passport well before departure.
6. Pack for the activity: broken-in shoes, layers, and sun protection.

## The takeaway

The fresh part of this trend isn't a single hot spot; it's the idea that active trips can happen in places you haven't pictured yourself hiking or biking. Pick one of these two, request the full itinerary from the operator, and compare it against your own fitness, time and budget before you commit.
