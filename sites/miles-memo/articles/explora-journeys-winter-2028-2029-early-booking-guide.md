Explora Journeys has opened sales for its Winter 2028-2029 sailings, and the line is dangling a real incentive for planners who act early: savings of up to 40% on bookings made by December 2026, per Travelpulse. The collection spans 230 destinations. If you're weighing whether to commit to a cruise more than two years out, this guide walks through how to decide, what to check first, and how to avoid locking in something that doesn't fit.

## The Planning Details First

Here is what the announcement confirms, and what it doesn't.

- **What's on offer:** Winter 2028-2029 sailings across 230 destinations.
- **The deal:** Up to 40% savings for bookings made by December 2026, as reported by Travelpulse.
- **Who it's aimed at:** The push is directed at travel advisors and B2B partners, so working with an advisor is the natural route in.
- **What's not in the reporting:** Specific ship names, itineraries, departure dates, sailing lengths, and fares. Treat those as questions to ask before you pay anything.

That last point matters. "Up to 40%" is a ceiling, not a promise. The discount you actually get will depend on the sailing and the cabin, so ask for the exact figure on the exact voyage you want.

## Why Booking This Far Ahead Can Make Sense

Early-booking offers exist because cruise lines want to fill premium inventory well before departure. For you, the upside tends to come in three forms:

1. **Price.** The headline discount is the obvious draw, and the December 2026 cutoff gives you a clear deadline rather than a vague "limited time."
2. **Choice.** When sales just opened, more itineraries and cabin categories are typically available than will be closer to sailing. If you care about a particular route or room, early is when you have the most options.
3. **Time to plan around it.** A winter trip two years out lets you line up time off, flights, and school or work schedules without scrambling.

The trade-off is flexibility. You're committing before you know what your calendar, budget, or health will look like in winter 2028.

## What 230 Destinations Means for You

A big destination count is useful, but it can also be paralyzing. Two hundred and thirty ports and stops across a winter collection means the real work is narrowing down, not finding options.

Start with three filters:

- **How long can you be away?** Decide your maximum number of days before you look at routes. Ask the line or your advisor for sailing lengths, since the announcement doesn't specify them.
- **What's the point of the trip?** Warm-weather relaxation, a bucket-list port, and a food-focused route are different trips. Pick one priority.
- **How much time do you want on land versus at sea?** Itineraries heavy on port days suit active travelers. Lighter ones suit people who want to enjoy the ship.

Write down your top two priorities, then ask for sailings that match both. That's faster than browsing the full collection.

## A Pre-Booking Checklist

Before you put down a deposit on a sailing this far out, get answers to these:

- **What exactly is the discount?** Get the percentage, the cabin category it applies to, and whether it covers the full fare or part of it.
- **What's the deadline?** The reported window is bookings by December 2026. Confirm the precise date and time zone.
- **What are the cancellation and change terms?** Two-plus years is a long runway. Ask how much you can change or get back, and by when.
- **What's the deposit schedule?** Know when each payment is due so nothing surprises you.
- **What's included in the fare?** Compare the discounted price against what you'd actually pay, not just the sticker number.
- **Is travel insurance advisable?** For a long-lead booking, it's worth asking what coverage options exist and what they cover.

## Who This Suits, and Who Should Wait

**It suits you if** you already know you want a premium winter cruise, you have a flexible-enough life to plan two years out, and you'd rather lock in savings and selection than wait for last-minute deals.

**Consider waiting if** your plans are uncertain, you need maximum flexibility, or you can't yet say which part of the world you'd want to visit. A discount doesn't help if you end up cancelling.

## If You're a Travel Advisor

The announcement is clearly pitched at your side of the business. Per Travelpulse's report, the offer pairs the early-booking savings with a push toward advisors and B2B partners. The practical takeaway: your clients who are already planning ahead are the natural audience, and a hard December 2026 deadline gives you a concrete reason to start the conversation now rather than later. Lead with the deadline, the destination breadth, and the questions your client needs answered, then fill in itinerary details once you have them from the line.

## The Bottom Line

Before the December 2026 cutoff, pick your two trip priorities, get the exact discount and cancellation terms in writing for the sailing you want, and only then decide. An early-booking deal is worth taking when you've done that homework, and worth skipping when you haven't.
