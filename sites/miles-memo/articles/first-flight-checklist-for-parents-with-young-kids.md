A child's first flight is mostly a planning problem. Get the few things below sorted ahead of time, and the day goes from something to dread to something manageable. This checklist takes about an hour to work through the week before you go, and it suits parents of babies, toddlers and preschoolers taking a first trip by air.

## Before you book: pick a trip that fits a small traveler

- **Keep the first trip short.** A flight of a couple of hours is a gentler introduction than a long-haul. If you can choose, that's the easy win.
- **Think about timing.** Many parents plan around nap and bedtime. A departure that lines up with a usual sleep window can help, though every child is different.
- **Check the airline's family rules.** Policies on strollers, car seats, baby milk and boarding differ between carriers. Read yours before you pack, not at the check-in desk.

## One week out: the prep that pays off

### Talk it through
Tell your child what will happen in simple steps: we go to the airport, we show our tickets, we sit on the plane, it goes up in the air. Picture books and shows about holidays and planes help. A familiar character like Peppa Pig making the trip sounds like exactly the idea behind the easyJet tie-in, and it's a decent tactic for any family.

### Sort documents early
Check passports, booking references and any travel authorizations for every traveler, children included. Put them in one easy-to-reach pouch.

### Pack two bags in your head
Split your packing into what you need during travel and what can sit in the hold. The first list is the one that matters on the day.

## The carry-on kit

Pack this bag as if you might be delayed by two hours, because you might be.

- A full change of clothes for your child, plus a spare top for you
- More snacks than seems sensible, in easy-to-open packaging
- Nappies or diapers and wipes, plus a few bags for the used ones
- Any milk, formula or food you need (check liquid rules for your departure airport)
- A comfort item, such as a blanket or a favorite soft toy
- A few small, new-to-them toys or a downloaded show for the quiet stretches
- Any medication, kept in your hand luggage

## At the airport: build in slack

The biggest stress reducer is time. Arrive earlier than you would on your own, because every step takes longer with a small person.

1. **Check in online if you can**, so the desk isn't a bottleneck.
2. **Let them move before you board.** Toddlers who have run laps of the terminal sit better afterward.
3. **Find the family-friendly spots.** Many airports have play areas or quieter corners. A quick look at the airport website beforehand can save wandering.
4. **Ask about boarding.** Some airlines offer family boarding; others don't. Decide with your partner who handles the bags and who handles the child.

## On the plane: small moves that help

- **Plan for takeoff and landing.** Changing air pressure can bother little ears. Many parents offer a feed, a bottle or a drink at those moments. Check with your pediatrician if you have specific concerns.
- **Spread out the entertainment.** Don't unveil every toy in the first ten minutes. Bring new things out one at a time.
- **Keep the mood light.** Other passengers are generally more understanding than parents fear, and a calm adult makes a calmer child.
- **Walk the aisle when the seatbelt sign is off.** It breaks up the flight for everyone.

## If you have less time to prepare

If the trip is tomorrow, do three things: pack the change of clothes and snacks, check your airline's rules on strollers and car seats, and give yourself an extra hour at the airport. That covers most of the avoidable trouble.

## After landing: go easy on day one

The holiday doesn't have to start at full speed. Aim for a simple first day: unpack the essentials, get a decent meal, and let your child explore the room or a nearby park. Overtired kids and long transfers are a bad mix, so if your package includes airport transfers, such as those bundled in some easyJet holidays deals, confirm the pickup details before you leave home.

## The takeaway

You can't control how your child behaves at 30,000 feet, but you can control the buffer around it. Write your carry-on list tonight, add an hour to your airport arrival, and watch the Captain Kate and Mummy Pig video with your child before you go. A first flight goes better when everyone knows what's coming.
