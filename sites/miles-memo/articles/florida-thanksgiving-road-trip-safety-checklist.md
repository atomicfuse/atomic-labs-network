Thanksgiving week is one of the busiest stretches on Florida roads, and the numbers show why it pays to plan rather than "wing it." Per the Florida Highway Patrol (FHP), as reported by the News-Journal, there were 7,331 crashes and 45 fatalities across Florida during the five-day Thanksgiving travel period in 2025. This checklist turns that guidance into steps you can finish before you leave the driveway.

## Plan the Basics Before You Go

A holiday drive in Florida suits families, college students heading home, and anyone crossing the state to see relatives. A car is the default for most of these trips, which means your biggest planning job is picking when you leave.

The timing matters. According to the reporting, the Wednesday before Thanksgiving sees the highest crash volume of the holiday period. If your schedule has any flexibility, that is the day to avoid, or at least the day to build in extra time and patience. Leaving earlier in the week or traveling on the holiday itself are options worth weighing.

Also check the forecast. Holiday traffic, rain chances, and impaired-driving risks all rise at this time of year, per the report, so a route that looks easy on a map can turn slow quickly.

## Map Your Route and Build In Slack

Don't improvise your way down I-95 or I-75. A few minutes of planning helps:

- Pick your route and a backup before you leave.
- Add buffer time for heavy traffic and weather instead of assuming a clean run.
- Decide in advance where you will stop for fuel, food, and breaks, so you aren't making those choices while tired.
- Share your arrival window with whoever is hosting you.

If you are short on time, the simplest fix is to shorten the drive: cut one stop you don't need, but keep the breaks that keep the driver alert.

## Get the Car Ready

Vehicle preparation is one of the core recommendations in the safety guidance. Do a quick check a few days ahead rather than the morning you leave, so you have time to fix anything you find.

- Look over your tires and make sure they are in good shape.
- Confirm your lights, wipers, and brakes work, especially if rain is in the forecast.
- Fill the tank before you hit the road.
- Pack a basic emergency kit. Sensible items include water, a phone charger, a flashlight, and a first-aid kit.

The kit contents above are common-sense suggestions rather than an FHP list, so adjust them to your family and trip length.

## Drive Sober, Every Time

Impaired driving is a leading concern during the holidays, and it is entirely preventable. If you will be drinking at a Thanksgiving gathering or a night out the evening before, arrange a sober driver, a rideshare, or a place to stay before the first drink, not after.

## Put the Phone Away

Distraction is the other habit the safety guidance targets. Set your navigation, music, and podcast before you shift out of park. If you are traveling with others, hand navigation duty to a passenger. Messages can wait until you stop for a break.

## Slow Down and Leave Space

Speed turns a minor mistake into a serious crash, and holiday traffic gives you less room for error. Slow down, especially in rain or heavy congestion, and keep a generous gap between you and the vehicle ahead. Arriving ten minutes later is a small price.

## Know Florida's Move Over Law

Florida's Move Over Law is part of the guidance for holiday drivers. In short, when you see emergency vehicles or other responders stopped along the roadside, you should move over a lane if you can do so safely, and slow down if you cannot. Heavy holiday traffic means more stopped vehicles and more people working beside fast-moving lanes, so know the rule before you need it. Check the FHP or the state's official site for the exact requirements.

## If You Break Down

Planning ahead pays off most here. Pull as far off the road as you can, turn on your hazard lights, and call for help. Staying alert to passing traffic matters, and so does giving other drivers plenty of notice that you are stopped. The preparation above, a full tank, a charged phone, and a kit in the trunk, makes a breakdown a nuisance instead of an emergency.

## The One Thing to Do Today

Pick your departure day and time now, and make it anything but the Wednesday rush if you can. Everything else on this list gets easier once you have given yourself room to drive calmly.
