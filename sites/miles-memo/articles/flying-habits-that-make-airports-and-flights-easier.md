Most flying frustration comes from small things: a cramped seat, a dead phone at the gate, a card that gets blocked abroad. A video on YouTube pulls together a dozen habits experienced flyers use to avoid those problems. The source material only covers ten of them in detail, so here are those ten, grouped by when you'll use them: before you book, before you leave, and at the airport and on board.

Plan on about 30 minutes of prep total. None of this requires a car, a special budget, or a particular kind of trip. It suits anyone who flies, from first-timers to frequent business travelers.

## Before You Book

### Pick your seat based on the plane, not just the price

The video's advice is to choose a seat based on aircraft type and row location, not only on what it costs. Researching the aircraft before you book can prevent hours of discomfort. Look up which plane your flight uses, then check where your row sits on it before you pay for an upgrade or accept an assigned seat.

### Choose your flight time on purpose

The video recommends picking flight times to avoid turbulence and delays. One of its key points is that early morning flights tend to have smoother air than midday flights because temperatures are cooler. If you have a choice between a 7 a.m. departure and a 2 p.m. one, that's a reason to favor the early flight, especially if you dislike bumpy air.

## Before You Leave Home

### Back up your documents in two ways

Take photos of your important documents, store them in a cloud service, and print backup copies. The video says this takes about 10 minutes and can prevent a real travel emergency. Do it the night before you go, and keep the paper copies in a different bag from the originals.

### Tell your bank you're traveling

Notify your bank before an international trip so your card isn't blocked when you make purchases abroad. A quick note in the bank's app or a call before you leave is enough. A frozen card in another country is far harder to fix than it is to prevent.

### Pack the carry-on with a plan

Roll your clothes and use the space inside your shoes for small items. According to the video, rolling saves space, cuts down on wrinkles, and lets you see everything at a glance. Try it with a test pack a day early so you know what fits.

### Get your liquids sorted

Transfer liquids into properly sized containers and keep them together in a clear bag. Doing this at home means you aren't repacking at the security line with a line of people behind you. Check your airline and airport rules for the exact container limits before you go.

### Pack a power strip or multi-port adapter

Outlets at gates are scarce. A power strip or multi-port adapter lets you charge several devices from a single outlet, and it makes you popular with the people sitting nearby. It's also handy in hotel rooms with only one plug near the bed.

### Add a tracker to checked luggage

If you check a bag, the video suggests putting a small tracking device inside. Then if the bag doesn't show up on the carousel, you know where it is rather than guessing.

## At the Airport and On Board

### Eat light and drink water before you fly

Prepare your body ahead of time by eating light foods and staying hydrated. It's a simple habit, and it's easy to skip when you're rushing to the gate. Fill a reusable bottle after security if you can.

### Stay seated until your group is called

Skip the boarding scrum. The video says staying seated until your group is called reduces unnecessary standing and stress. Your seat is assigned either way, so there's little to gain from crowding the gate.

## A Note on the Other Two Tips

The video is billed as 12 tips, but the details for the last two weren't available in the material I worked from. Rather than guess at them, I've kept this list to the ten that are clearly supported. If you watch the video, you can pick up the rest there.

## If You Only Have 10 Minutes

Do these three: back up your documents, call or message your bank, and look up the aircraft type for your flight. They cost nothing, take almost no time, and cover the problems that are hardest to fix once you're at the airport.

Pick one habit from this list for your next trip and do it the night before you leave.
