Holiday flights are some of the most expensive and stressful tickets you will book all year, and prices can shift quickly as demand climbs. You do not need hours of comparison shopping to deal with that. Per Yahoo's reporting on Google Flights, three small tweaks can surface lower fares that standard booking flows often hide: the calendar view, the "Cheapest" tab, and price tracking alerts.

This guide walks through each one in the order you would use them. Plan on about 15 minutes for the first search, then let alerts do the rest. It suits anyone heading home for the holidays who has at least a little flexibility on dates.

## Step 1: Use the calendar to pick your dates

Start here, before you commit to a departure day. Instead of typing in exact dates and accepting whatever comes up, open the calendar in Google Flights and look at how fares change across the days around your trip.

The point is to let prices help choose your dates, not the other way around. If your plan is flexible by even a day, the calendar shows you whether shifting your departure or return makes a difference. Yahoo's piece presents this as one of the main ways to slice hundreds off a ticket.

### How to run the calendar check

1. Enter your departure and destination airports.
2. Open the date picker and look at the prices displayed across the calendar.
3. Test your ideal dates first, then compare the days just before and after.
4. Check both the outbound and return legs. A cheaper day on one end can offset a pricier day on the other.

If you only have a few minutes, do just this step. It is the one most likely to change what you pay.

## Step 2: Check the "Cheapest" tab

Once you have a date range, look at how Google sorts your results. Google Flights has a dedicated "Cheapest" tab, and Yahoo recommends checking it rather than relying only on the default view.

The default results tend to highlight a mix of price and convenience. The Cheapest tab puts price first. That does not mean you should automatically book the lowest number on the page, but it shows you how far apart the options really are.

### What to look for

- **The price gap.** Compare the top Cheapest result against the flights in the default view. If the difference is small, take the more convenient flight. If it is large, you have a real decision to make.
- **The trade-offs.** Cheaper itineraries may come with longer travel time or less convenient timing. Open the details and read them before you decide.
- **Your own limits.** Decide in advance what you will accept, such as an early departure or a longer day, so a low price does not talk you into a trip you will dread.

That last point is my own advice rather than something from Yahoo's article, but it is the difference between a deal and a regret on a holiday travel day.

## Step 3: Set price tracker alerts

If you are not ready to book, do not keep refreshing the page. Set up Google Flights price tracking and let it watch the fare for you. Yahoo points to real-time price tracker alerts as the third tactic.

Alerts are most useful when you have already done steps one and two. You know your dates, you know roughly what a good fare looks like, and now you are waiting for a drop to land.

### How to use alerts well

- Turn on tracking for the exact route and dates you want.
- Consider tracking a second set of dates if you found a cheaper alternative on the calendar.
- Decide on a price you would happily pay. When an alert arrives at or below that number, book it instead of waiting for something better.

Because holiday prices can move fast as demand spikes, tracking gives you a calmer way to decide than panic-buying after a scary price jump.

## A simple order of operations

Put the three together and you have a repeatable routine:

1. **Calendar first.** Find the cheapest dates that still work for your family and schedule.
2. **Cheapest tab second.** Compare the lowest fares with the default results and judge the trade-offs.
3. **Alerts last.** Track your best option and book when the price meets your target.

### If you have less time

Skip straight to the calendar and set one alert on the best date combination you find. That takes only a few minutes and covers the two biggest levers.

## What these tricks will not do

These are search tweaks, not guarantees. Yahoo's article does not promise a specific dollar savings for every route, and holiday fares can still be high no matter how you search. The aim is to make sure you are not paying more than you have to simply because you accepted the first results you saw.

## The takeaway

Before you book a single holiday flight, open the Google Flights calendar, check the Cheapest tab, and set an alert. Do it today, while you still have choices, and let the price decide when to buy.
