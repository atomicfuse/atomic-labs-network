Holiday flights come with crowded terminals, weather disruptions, long lines, and baggage headaches. You can't control most of that. You can control how ready you are when it happens. This checklist, built on advice from Flyacv, the site covering Arcata-Eureka Airport (ACV), walks you through what to do before you head to the airport so a bad day stays a minor one.

Plan on about 15 minutes the day before you fly, plus a few minutes at each step below. No car needed for this one; it all happens on your phone and at your suitcase. It suits families, casual travelers, and anyone flying out of a smaller local airport.

## Download your airline's app first

Flyacv's first tip is simple: get the airline app before travel day. Do it at home on reliable Wi-Fi, not in a security line with a dying signal.

Once it's installed:

- Sign in and confirm your reservation shows up.
- Turn on notifications so schedule changes reach you directly.
- Keep your boarding pass accessible in the app.

If you're juggling kids and bags, having everything on your phone beats digging through a purse or backpack.

## Check your flight status the day before and the morning of

Holiday weather and congestion can shift schedules. Flyacv recommends checking flight status ahead of time rather than assuming everything is on schedule.

Make it a routine with two checks:

1. **The day before:** Look for changes to departure time or gate, and decide whether you need to adjust your plans for getting to the airport.
2. **Before you leave the house:** Confirm nothing has changed overnight.

If a delay shows up early, you gain time to rebook, rearrange a ride, or simply stay home a little longer instead of sitting at the gate.

## Pack light, and pack with security in mind

Baggage is one of the most common holiday headaches, and Flyacv's advice is to travel light. Fewer bags mean fewer things to check, carry, lose, or wait for.

### A quick packing pass

- Set out everything you plan to bring, then remove the extras.
- Keep essentials you'd hate to lose (medications, chargers, documents) in your carry-on.
- Pack gifts so they're easy to inspect, or consider shipping them ahead. That's my suggestion rather than something from Flyacv, but it takes the guesswork out of security.

Packing mistakes that slow people down tend to be the ones that force a repack at the checkpoint. Which brings us to the next step.

## Know the TSA rules before you get in line

Flyacv advises following TSA rules, and the time to read them is at home, not at the conveyor belt. Check the TSA's guidance on what can go in a carry-on and what needs to be checked, especially if you're bringing food, drinks, or wrapped presents.

A few minutes of review means you won't be the person holding up a line during the busiest travel stretch of the year.

## Arrive early

Holiday crowds mean longer lines at check-in and security. Flyacv's advice is to arrive early, and it's the one tip that protects you from almost every other problem on this list. A cushion of time absorbs a slow bag drop, a long security line, or a last-minute gate change.

Exactly how early depends on your airport and airline, so check your carrier's recommended arrival time and then add a buffer for the holidays. That buffer is my recommendation; confirm the airline's own guidance for your flight.

## Why a smaller airport can make this easier

Flyacv's piece focuses on travelers using ACV, a smaller local airport, and the case for small airports is straightforward: less sprawl, usually fewer crowds, and a simpler path from curb to gate. The planning tips still matter there. A smaller airport reduces stress, but it doesn't remove weather delays or schedule changes, so the app, the status checks, and the early arrival all still apply.

If you have a choice between airports and the fares are comparable, it's worth weighing how much hassle each one will add. Flyacv also frames preparation as a way to save money, so compare options before you book rather than after.

## Flying with kids

Families feel every holiday travel problem more sharply. A few adaptations to the checklist:

- Do the app setup and flight status checks the night before, when you're not also wrangling snacks.
- Pack light on purpose. Every extra bag is one more thing to keep track of with children in tow.
- Arrive early enough that nobody is rushing. Kids handle waiting better than they handle being hurried.

These are my adaptations of the general advice, not specifics from Flyacv.

## Your 24-hour countdown

**Day before:**
- Install and sign in to the airline app, then turn on notifications.
- Check flight status.
- Review TSA rules and finish packing light.

**Travel day:**
- Check flight status again before you leave.
- Have your boarding pass ready on your phone.
- Get to the airport early.

## The one thing to do tonight

Open your airline's app, confirm your reservation, and switch on notifications. Everything else on this list gets easier once your flight information is in your pocket and updating in real time.
