Holiday travel is crowded, expensive and prone to disruption. Time Out's editors have put together 11 tips for making it calmer and cheaper, and this guide sorts them into a plan you can act on: what to do before you book, what to do on the way, and when to rethink the whole trip.

Time Out's list is short on detail in places, so where a tip needs more explanation below, that's my own take and I've said so. The tips themselves are the editors'.

## Before You Book

### Plan a buffer day (or two) around travel

Time Out's first tip is to build a day or two of cushion on either side of your trip. My take: a buffer means a delayed flight or a slow drive doesn't wreck the holiday itself. It also helps you return rested instead of heading straight to work.

### Travel on the actual holiday day if you can

Time Out suggests traveling on the holiday itself when possible. My take: the logic is that most people crowd into the days before and after, so the day itself can feel calmer. This works best if your hosts don't mind a later arrival, so confirm the meal time first.

### Celebrate the holiday on another day

Another Time Out tip: move the celebration. If everyone can agree to gather a few days earlier or later, you can dodge peak travel and possibly peak prices. Families with flexible schedules have the most to gain.

### Just don't travel

Time Out's final tip is the blunt one. Staying home is a legitimate choice, especially if the trip is costly or stressful. If you're on the fence, price out the full trip, including getting to the airport, and decide whether it's worth it.

## Getting Through the Airport

### Leave at least two hours between connections

Time Out advises at least two hours between connecting flights. Holiday crowds and delays can eat a tight layover fast. A longer gap costs you some waiting time, but it protects you from a missed connection.

### Schedule your ride

Time Out recommends booking your ride in advance. Whether that's a car service, a shuttle or a lift from a friend, settling it ahead of time means you aren't competing for transport at a busy curb after a long day.

### Bring your own airport food

Time Out's editors suggest packing your own food rather than relying on terminal prices and lines. Two related tips on their list are worth pairing with it:

- **Airport charcuterie:** Time Out is a fan of assembling your own snack spread for the terminal. Think of it as a picnic with a gate number.
- **BYO...W:** The editors list this one as "BYO…W." I'd read it as a nudge to bring your own drink. If that's what it means, an empty reusable bottle you fill after security is the easy version.

Security rules on liquids and foods vary, so check current TSA guidance before you pack.

## Packing Smarter

### Don't pack what you can get delivered instead

Time Out suggests having bulky or easily replaced items delivered to your destination rather than hauling them. My take: this is most useful for families, where diapers, snacks or gifts can take up half a suitcase. Coordinate with your hosts so packages arrive when someone's home.

### Moisturize

Time Out's editors list moisturizer as its own tip. Dry cabin air is a familiar annoyance, so tuck a small tube of lotion into your carry-on and use it during the flight.

## A Simple Holiday Travel Checklist

Here's how I'd turn the list into steps:

1. **Weeks out:** Decide whether to go, and consider moving the celebration or skipping the trip.
2. **When booking:** Add a buffer day, look at the holiday itself for travel, and leave two hours between connections.
3. **The week before:** Arrange your ride and send anything bulky ahead.
4. **Packing day:** Add food, a water bottle and moisturizer to your carry-on.

## The Takeaway

You can't control crowds, but you can control how much slack your plan has. Pick the two tips above that fit your trip, whether that's an extra day on each end or a longer connection, and build them in before you book.
