A new survey from corporate travel platform Navan, reported by TravelPulse, looks at the biggest in-flight pet peeves among business travelers and how everyday airplane annoyances shape the flying experience. The full results aren't something we can reproduce here, so this piece doesn't claim to rank them. Instead, it takes the survey's central idea, that small behaviors add up to a better or worse flight, and turns it into a checklist you can actually use before your next trip.

The payoff: a short set of habits, grouped by when you do them, that makes you the easy seatmate and protects your own comfort too.

## Why this is worth a few minutes of planning

Per TravelPulse, the Navan findings land as business travel continues to rebound. More people are back in airport lounges and middle seats, and comfort and etiquette are getting more attention. That makes sense: a flight is a small, shared space, and a few minutes of forethought costs nothing.

This checklist suits anyone flying in the next week or two, including holiday travelers who may not have flown in a while. It takes about ten minutes to run through before you leave, and you don't need any special gear.

## Before you leave home

Most in-flight friction starts on the ground, with decisions made at the packing stage.

- **Pack to what fits under the seat and overhead.** If your bag needs a shove to close the bin, it's too big for the trip. A bag you can lift easily is also one you won't block the aisle wrestling with.
- **Keep essentials in one small bag.** Headphones, charger, medication, snacks and a pen should live in a single pouch so you aren't digging through the overhead bin mid-flight.
- **Charge your devices and download your content.** Wi-Fi is never guaranteed, and this keeps you from needing to speak up about it.
- **Choose your seat deliberately.** An aisle seat suits people who get up often. A window suits people who want to sleep undisturbed. Picking the one that matches your habits prevents awkward climbing over strangers.

## At the gate and while boarding

Boarding is where aisle traffic jams form, so a little awareness goes a long way.

1. **Have your bag organized before you step on.** Take out your headphones and laptop at the gate so you aren't unpacking in the aisle.
2. **Stow your bag and sit down.** Don't reorganize in the aisle while others queue behind you.
3. **Put your bag in the bin over your own row or ahead of it.** Placing it behind your seat makes it harder to reach and can leave the people nearby with no space.
4. **Be ready to stand up and let people in.** If you have the aisle seat, a quick step out is simpler than a squeeze-by.

## Once you're in the air

This is where most pet peeves live, and the fixes are small.

### Share the armrest and the space

With a shared armrest, the simplest approach is to keep your elbows in and be flexible. Give a little, and you'll usually get a little back.

### Use headphones for everything with sound

Videos, calls, games and music should all go through headphones. If you must take a call on a plane with Wi-Fi, keep it brief and quiet, and check the airline's rules first.

### Be thoughtful with the seat recline

Reclining is allowed on most flights, but a slow, gentle recline is kinder than a sudden drop into someone's laptop. During meal service, consider keeping your seat upright.

### Mind your food choices

Strong-smelling meals in a pressurized cabin travel far. Save the pungent leftovers for the gate.

### Keep the aisle and bathroom line clear

If you stand to stretch, step out of the flow of traffic. Don't linger by the lavatory doors if you can help it.

## Before you land

The final ten minutes matter more than most people think.

- **Stay seated until it's your turn.** Standing as soon as the plane stops just creates a crowd in the aisle.
- **Grab your bag without blocking others.** Step into your row, pull it down, and move forward.
- **Check your seat area.** Phones, chargers and earbuds often slip into seat pockets and between cushions.

## If you're the one being annoyed

Etiquette goes both ways. If someone's behavior is bothering you, a polite, specific request usually works best, such as asking if they'd mind lowering their volume. If that doesn't help, flag a flight attendant rather than escalating. Having your own noise-canceling headphones and an eye mask also gives you some control that doesn't depend on anyone else.

## A quick version if you're short on time

When you only have two minutes before heading out, check three things: your bag fits easily, your headphones are in your pocket, and you know your seat preference. Those cover the most common sources of friction.

## The one habit to take with you

Before you board, picture the person in the seat next to you and ask what would make their flight easier. Then do that one thing.
