Fox News highlighted four options in and around town, and the best choice depends on how much time you have and how far from campus you want to be. This guide sorts them by that, so you can pick one and get on with your weekend.

## The Planning Basics

**Time needed:** Plan on a half day for anything outside town, and an hour or two for the options close to campus.

**Do you need a car?** For Coopers Rock State Forest and Valley Falls State Park, expect to drive. Core Arboretum is the campus-accessible option, and Deckers Creek Rail Trail is the one to think of as a town-based outing. Confirm trailhead access points locally.

**Who it suits:** Fans who want more than a tailgate. Hikers can go rugged at Coopers Rock, while anyone with limited time or energy can stay near WVU.

## If You Have Half a Day: Coopers Rock State Forest

This is the headliner. Fox News points to Raven Rock Trail at Coopers Rock State Forest for views over the Cheat River Canyon. It's the pick if you want the big scenic payoff and photos that look like fall in West Virginia.

Since it's the farthest-flung of the group, treat it as the main event of a morning. Hike early, then head back toward town with time to eat before the game. Build in a cushion for game-weekend traffic, and check the forest's current conditions and any seasonal closures before you set out.

## If You Want Waterfalls: Valley Falls State Park

Fox News lists Valley Falls State Park for its waterfalls. It's the right call if you'd rather listen to moving water than look out over a canyon. Like Coopers Rock, it works best as a dedicated outing rather than a quick stop, so pair it with a lunch plan rather than trying to squeeze it in right before kickoff.

If you're deciding between the two out-of-town options, here's a simple rule: choose Coopers Rock for sweeping overlooks, and Valley Falls for water. Don't try to do both on a game day.

## If You Want to Stay Close: Deckers Creek Rail Trail

The Deckers Creek Rail Trail is the flexible one. A rail trail is generally a gentler, easier walk than a rugged ridge route, which makes this a reasonable option for mixed groups, kids, or anyone nursing a long night before. That's my read on rail trails in general rather than a detail from the Fox News piece, so check the specific stretch you plan to use.

Use it for a morning walk to loosen up before a long day of sitting in a stadium.

## If You Have an Hour: Core Arboretum

Core Arboretum is the nature fix that doesn't require leaving the university orbit. Fox News frames it as part of the area's campus-accessible nature, which makes it the best fit when your schedule is tight. It's a good choice for the afternoon before kickoff, or on a travel day when you've arrived late and want to stretch your legs.

## A Sample Game Weekend

Here's one way to put it together. Treat the sequence as a suggestion, and verify hours and kickoff logistics yourself.

### Option A: The Full Outdoors Day
- **Morning:** Raven Rock Trail at Coopers Rock State Forest, starting early.
- **Midday:** Drive back toward Morgantown and eat.
- **Afternoon:** Tailgate or head to the stadium for the Houston game.

### Option B: The Short-on-Time Version
- **Morning:** A walk on Deckers Creek Rail Trail.
- **Early afternoon:** Core Arboretum for a quiet loop.
- **Later:** Game time.

### Option C: The Day After
If you're staying through Sunday, save Valley Falls State Park for the morning after. Waterfalls are a good way to wind down once the game is over and the crowds thin.

## Why Late Autumn Works

Fox News argues that November is a strong time to hike around Morgantown, and it's easy to see the appeal: the trip gets you out of the parking lot and into the landscape that surrounds the university. It also reflects a growing habit among sports travelers, who want a local experience beyond the stadium.

The practical caveat is that late-season daylight is short and the weather can turn. Pack layers, bring a light for early-evening finishes, and plan to be off the trail well before dark.

## The Takeaway

Pick one hike, not four. Match it to your clock: Coopers Rock or Valley Falls if you have a half day, Deckers Creek or Core Arboretum if you don't. Then check the current conditions and hours before you leave, and enjoy the game.
