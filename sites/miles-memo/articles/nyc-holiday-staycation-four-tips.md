If you live in New York City, you already have a holiday destination: your own. A staycation costs less than a flight, skips the airport crowds, and can feel like a real break if you plan it the way you'd plan any other trip. Here are four tips, drawn from a JPMorganChase travel piece republished by the Haitian Times, plus ways to turn each one into a plan you can follow.

## Before You Start: The Planning Basics

**Time needed:** One night is the sweet spot, which means two days. A single day works if you're short on time or budget.

**Do you need a car?** No. Staying in the city means you can skip the rental and the holiday traffic entirely. Subways, buses, and your own feet cover most of it.

**Who it suits:** Locals who want a break without holiday travel hassles, families trying to keep costs down, and anyone who hasn't done the "tourist stuff" in years.

The case for staying put is simple. Holiday travel often means weather delays, packed airports, and high prices. Meanwhile, the city is well suited to a staycation this time of year, with seasonal markets, lights, performances, and hotel deals all in play.

## 1. Treat Your Hometown Like a Destination

This is the mindset shift that makes everything else work. Most New Yorkers know their own neighborhood and their commute, and not much else. Plan your staycation the way you'd plan a trip somewhere unfamiliar.

Try this:

- **Pick a theme or an area.** Choose one part of the city you rarely visit instead of zigzagging across five boroughs.
- **Make a short list.** Three or four stops is plenty: one thing to see, one meal you've been meaning to try, one seasonal activity.
- **Group nearby stops.** Fewer subway transfers means more time enjoying yourself, and less feeling like you're running errands.

If you have less time, shrink the plan to a half day in a single neighborhood with a market or light display, and one sit-down meal.

## 2. Spend the Night Away

The single biggest thing that makes a staycation feel like a trip is not sleeping in your own bed. Book a hotel.

A night in a hotel gives you a reason to pack a small bag, check in, and be "on vacation" in a way a day out doesn't. Hotel deals are part of what makes the city attractive for a staycation in the holiday season, so compare rates before you commit.

### Choosing a base

Since you don't need to be near an airport, pick a neighborhood based on what you want to do. Staying near the activities you've planned cuts down on transit and lets you head back to the room to rest between outings. If you're traveling on a budget, check whether you have rewards points or discounts you can put toward the stay.

I'd suggest picking the hotel first and building the day around it, rather than the other way around. That way your neighborhood choice shapes the itinerary.

## 3. Be Flexible

Staycations tend to fall apart when you over-schedule them. The point is to feel refreshed, not to run a checklist.

A few ways to build in give:

- **Leave gaps.** Plan one anchor activity per half day and let the rest be open.
- **Have a backup.** If a market is packed or a show is sold out, know a second option within walking distance.
- **Go off-peak when you can.** Weekday daytime visits to popular spots are usually calmer than weekend evenings. That's general advice rather than a guarantee, so check each place's hours before you go.

Flexibility also protects your budget. Without a flight or fixed itinerary, you can change plans for weather or energy without losing money.

## 4. Try Something New

The fourth tip is the one that makes a staycation memorable. Do at least one thing you've never done in your own city.

Ideas to get you started:

- A seasonal market you've walked past but never browsed
- A holiday performance you haven't seen
- A restaurant in a neighborhood you rarely visit
- A landmark or attraction you've always assumed was only for visitors

The goal is simple: come home with a story, not just a rested weekend.

## A Sample 24-Hour Holiday Staycation

Here's one way to put the four tips together. Swap in your own neighborhoods and favorites.

**Day one**

- **Late morning:** Pack a small bag and head to your hotel. Drop your things, even if your room isn't ready yet.
- **Afternoon:** Explore the neighborhood around your hotel. Visit a seasonal market and take your time.
- **Evening:** Have dinner at a place you haven't tried, then catch a performance or see the holiday lights.

**Day two**

- **Morning:** Sleep a little later, then have a relaxed breakfast near the hotel.
- **Late morning:** Do your "something new" activity before checkout.
- **Afternoon:** Head home and keep the rest of the day free.

This fits a single night, and you can cut it down to a half day by choosing just the market and dinner.

## Before You Go: Verify the Details

Seasonal events change from year to year. Check opening dates, hours, and whether tickets or reservations are required before you lock in your plan. Popular holiday attractions and restaurants can fill up, and hotel rates vary by date.

## Your Next Step

Open your calendar, pick one night in the next few weeks, and book the hotel first. Once the room is reserved, the rest of the staycation falls into place.
