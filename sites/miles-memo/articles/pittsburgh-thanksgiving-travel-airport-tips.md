## Who this is for and how much planning you need

This suits anyone flying out of Pittsburgh International (PIT) around Thanksgiving, especially families and anyone with a tight connection. Set aside about 15 minutes this week to do the checks below. Doing them now beats discovering a problem in the security line.

If you're driving to the airport, a car helps, but heavier-than-usual crowds on the roads are likely too, according to USA TODAY's coverage. Build in extra time for the drive as well as the terminal.

## Pick your travel day with the crowds in mind

The core advice for any Thanksgiving trip is simple: avoid the peak congestion when you can. The busiest days are the ones everyone else is also choosing, so the more flexible you are, the better your odds.

Here's how to use that:

- **Look at your calendar first.** If you can shift your departure by even a day, compare options before you book or rebook.
- **Choose off-peak times within your day.** Early flights and less popular hours tend to mean thinner crowds, though you should check your airline's schedule and the airport's current guidance.
- **Have a fallback.** If your flight is canceled or delayed, knowing your next-best option saves you from scrambling at the gate.

For exact peak days and times at PIT, check USA TODAY's full Pittsburgh guide and the airport's own channels, since those figures can change.

## Build an airport timeline you can follow

A holiday timeline should be generous. Here's a framework you can adjust once you've confirmed current guidance from your airline and the airport:

1. **The night before:** Check in online, download your boarding pass, and confirm your flight status.
2. **Leave home early:** Add a buffer for heavier road traffic and parking.
3. **Arrive at the terminal with room to spare:** Holiday crowds mean longer lines at bag drop and security. Your airline's recommended arrival time is the minimum, not the target.
4. **Head to security promptly:** Don't wait until after you've grabbed coffee.
5. **Go to your gate and settle in:** Being early is a lot more comfortable than being rushed.

If you're short on time, skip the sit-down meal before the airport and grab food after you clear security.

## TSA do's and don'ts for holiday flyers

USA TODAY's coverage highlights TSA tips for avoiding delays. The specifics weren't detailed in the information available here, so confirm the current rules at TSA.gov. These habits are widely useful for any busy day at the checkpoint:

### Do

- Keep your ID and boarding pass easy to reach.
- Check TSA's current rules on carry-on liquids before you pack.
- Wear shoes and layers that are simple to remove, and empty your pockets before you reach the bin.
- Pack your carry-on so items that need separate screening are easy to pull out.

### Don't

- Don't wrap gifts before flying. Screeners may need to open them.
- Don't pack questionable items in your carry-on without checking TSA's guidance. Thanksgiving foods like pies, sauces, and gravy can raise questions, so look up how TSA classifies them before you go.
- Don't wait until you're in line to sort out your bag.

## What to pack before heading to PIT

Packing well is the easiest way to move faster through the terminal. Before you zip the bag:

- **Documents:** ID, boarding pass, and any confirmation numbers on your phone and on paper if you like a backup.
- **Carry-on essentials:** Medications, chargers, a refillable water bottle (empty it before security), and snacks for kids.
- **Gifts and food:** Check TSA rules for anything you're bringing for the holiday table, and consider shipping gifts ahead.
- **A light layer:** Terminals and delayed planes can run cold.

If you're traveling with kids, keep their documents, snacks, and entertainment in one easy-to-reach pocket so you're not digging during the screening process.

## Keep the stress down

Crowds are a given. Your stress level isn't. A few habits help:

- Check flight status the morning of travel and again before you leave for the airport.
- Keep a charged phone and a portable battery handy for delays.
- Decide in advance who handles what, especially with families: who has the tickets, who has the bags, who watches the kids.
- Be patient with staff and other travelers. Everyone in that line is trying to get home.
