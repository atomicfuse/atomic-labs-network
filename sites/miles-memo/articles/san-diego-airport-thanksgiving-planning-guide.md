San Diego International Airport (SAN) expects up to 950,000 passengers over the 11-day Thanksgiving period, November 20 to November 30, according to a report originally from Yahoo. That is roughly 8% to 10% more than last year. If you're flying out, or driving someone else to the terminal, a little planning now can keep a holiday headache from turning into a missed flight.

This guide covers when to expect the worst crowds, how to choose between parking, rideshare, and the San Diego Flyer, and how to handle pickups.

## Know the Calendar: The Busiest Day Is Sunday, Nov. 29

Airport officials project Sunday, Nov. 29, as the single busiest day of the period. That's the return-home crunch, and it matters whether you're flying out that day or picking up relatives.

Thanksgiving travel is squeezed into a short window, so congestion tends to feel sharper than during other holidays. Per the report, officials are urging travelers to plan for:

- Longer security and check-in lines
- Heavier curbside traffic
- Construction-related detours
- Limited parking

If your dates are flexible, avoiding Nov. 29 is the simplest move. If they aren't, build in extra time and treat everything below as mandatory rather than optional.

## Give Yourself Extra Time

The report doesn't spell out an exact arrival time, so use your own judgment: the busier the day, the earlier you should go. A reasonable approach is to add a cushion beyond whatever you normally allow, especially on Nov. 29. Check your airline's recommended arrival window before you leave, and have your boarding pass on your phone and bags packed to the airline's size and weight rules so you aren't repacking in a long line.

## Expect Construction Detours

Officials have flagged construction-related detours around the airport. Don't rely on a route you've driven a dozen times. Look at a live map before you leave, and have a second route in mind. Check the airport's official website for current access changes, since signage and lane configurations can shift during construction.

## Parking vs. Rideshare vs. the San Diego Flyer

Parking is limited during this period, which changes the math on how to get there. Here's how the options compare.

### Reserve parking if you're driving

Parking reservations are among the alternatives officials are pointing travelers toward. Walking up and hoping for a space is a gamble when lots are expected to be tight. If you drive, reserve ahead and confirm your lot's details before you go.

### Use rideshare zones

Curbside traffic will be heavy, so don't expect a quick drop-off right at the door. Follow the airport's designated rideshare zones rather than trying to stop wherever looks convenient. For a drop-off, tell your driver the terminal and airline in advance so they head to the right place the first time.

### Consider the San Diego Flyer

The San Diego Flyer is another alternative officials highlighted. If you'd rather skip both parking stress and curbside gridlock, it's worth checking whether its pickup points and schedule fit your trip. Confirm current details on the airport's website before relying on it.

## Picking Up Family? Plan the Pickup, Not Just the Drive

Locals are often the ones caught off guard, because the airport is familiar until a holiday makes it unrecognizable. A few ways to make arrivals easier:

- **Track the flight** so you don't circle the terminal for 30 minutes waiting on a delayed plane.
- **Use the rideshare zones or a parking spot** rather than idling at a crowded curb.
- **Agree on a meeting spot** with your arriving passenger before they land, since phone calls in a crowded terminal can be frustrating.
- **Expect Nov. 29 to be the hardest day** for any pickup.

## Meet Them at the Gate With a SAN Pass

If you want to greet a loved one at the gate instead of at the curb, the SAN Pass Program is the tool officials are promoting. It's designed for non-travelers who want to go through security to meet arriving passengers or see off departing ones. Details such as how to request a pass and what ID is needed are best confirmed directly with the airport before you go. It's a nice option for grandparents, kids traveling alone, or anyone who needs a hand with luggage.

## A Simple Game Plan

Here's a quick sequence to follow in the days before you travel:

1. **Check your date.** Is it Nov. 29? Add extra buffer.
2. **Pick your transport.** Reserve parking, schedule a rideshare, or look into the San Diego Flyer.
3. **Check the route.** Look up construction detours the morning you leave.
4. **Decide on pickups.** Choose a rideshare zone, a parking spot, or a SAN Pass for gate greetings.
5. **Leave early.** Longer lines and heavier curbside traffic are the baseline this year, not the exception.

## The Bottom Line

With up to 950,000 passengers expected, the airport isn't going to feel quiet. Decide how you're getting there today, book what needs booking, and leave earlier than feels necessary. The extra 30 minutes cost you nothing if the lines are short, and save your trip if they aren't.
