Thanksgiving is one of the busiest travel periods of the year, and per Foxrochester's report on advice from AAA of Western and Central New York, 2026 holiday travel is already seeing an uptick. Crowds are building at airports and traffic is heavier on the roads. If you're still getting ready to go, this checklist covers what to settle before you leave, plus how to handle the road and the airport once you're moving.

The advice comes down to a few simple habits: leave earlier than you think you need to, have a backup plan, pack smart, and expect some delays. None of it is complicated. It just works best when you decide on it before you're sitting in a backed-up lane or a long security line.

## Before You Leave: The Five-Minute Plan

A little prep up front saves the most stress later. Do these before you walk out the door:

- **Pick your departure time on purpose.** With heavier holiday traffic and airport crowds, a flexible "we'll leave after lunch" approach tends to put you in the thick of it. Choose a time, and build in a cushion.
- **Choose an alternate route.** Don't wait for a navigation app to reroute you mid-jam. Look at a second option now so you can switch calmly if the main route stalls.
- **Pack with the delay in mind.** Assume the trip may take longer than planned and stock your bag or car accordingly (more on that below).
- **Tell the people waiting for you what to expect.** A loose arrival window is easier on everyone than a hard time you'll likely miss.

## If You're Driving: Plan Around the Congestion

Holiday road trips go wrong when everyone is on the same road at the same time. AAA's guidance, as reported by Foxrochester, centers on planning alternate routes and preparing for delays rather than hoping for a clear run.

### Know your Plan B before you need it

Pick one backup route and glance at it before you leave. If the main road slows to a crawl, you'll already know whether the detour is worth it. A familiar backup beats a panicked guess.

### Treat the drive time as a range

Whatever your map says on a quiet Tuesday, give yourself extra room on Thanksgiving weekend. Plan meal and fuel stops with that in mind, and don't schedule anything tight at the other end, like a dinner reservation 20 minutes after your estimated arrival.

### Stock the car for a slow day

Keep water, snacks, phone chargers, and anything your passengers need within reach rather than buried in the trunk. If traffic stops, you don't want to pull over and unpack the whole car.

## If You're Flying: Beat the Crowds Where You Can

Airports are where holiday crowds feel most intense. The core advice is to arrive early, and to travel as if a delay is possible.

- **Give yourself extra time at the airport.** Longer lines at check-in, security, and the gate are normal this weekend. Arriving early turns a stressful sprint into a slow walk.
- **Pack smart.** Keep what you need at the airport (ID, boarding pass, charger, snacks) in an easy-to-reach spot so you aren't digging through a bag at security.
- **Prepare for delays.** Download or screenshot what you'll need, and think through what you'd do if your flight runs late. Knowing your options ahead of time keeps a setback from becoming a scramble.

I can't give you airline-specific rebooking rules or exact arrival times here, since those vary by airline and airport. Check your airline's own guidance for the specifics.

## Traveling With Kids: Lower the Stress Ahead of Time

Kids make delays feel longer, so the preparation matters more. The same principles apply, with a few family-friendly twists.

- **Build in extra time.** Arriving early at the airport or leaving early for a drive gives you room for bathroom stops and the unexpected without anyone feeling rushed.
- **Keep essentials within reach.** Snacks, drinks, entertainment, and chargers should be in a carry-on or on the car's back seat, not packed away.
- **Tell kids what to expect.** A heads-up that the airport will be crowded, or that the drive might be slow, sets expectations before frustration sets in.
- **Hold the schedule loosely.** If you've planned the day down to the minute, one delay ruins it. Leave slack.

## A Last-Minute Checklist to Run Through

If you're reading this the day you leave, work through these in order:

1. Decide your departure time and add a buffer.
2. Look at your alternate route (drivers) or confirm your airport arrival plan (flyers).
3. Pack snacks, water, and chargers where you can reach them.
4. Pack for delays, not just for the destination.
5. Let your hosts know you may arrive later than planned.

## The Takeaway

You can't control how crowded the roads and terminals get, but you can control how prepared you are. Leave earlier than feels necessary, keep a backup route or plan in your pocket, and treat any delay as something you've already allowed for. Do that, and the travel part of Thanksgiving gets a lot less stressful.
