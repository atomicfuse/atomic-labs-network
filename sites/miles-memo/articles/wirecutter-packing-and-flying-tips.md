Wirecutter's journalists pulled together 44 travel tips from more than a decade of gear testing and reporting, published by The New York Times. The version we have in front of us covers 30 of them, and they fall into a handy order: pack, choose luggage, prep your phone and documents, then make the flight itself less miserable. Here they are, grouped the way you'll actually use them.

**Planning details:** Set aside about 30 minutes to work through the packing section a few days before you leave, and a bit more if you're going abroad and need the phone and document steps. No car required. These tips suit anyone flying, especially weekend travelers and budget-minded packers.

## Pack Lighter With a System

Start here, because every other decision gets easier when you bring less.

- **Try the 5,4,3,2,1 method for a weeklong trip.** Wirecutter recommends it as a framework for keeping a week's worth of clothing under control.
- **Stick to one color palette.** Versatile pieces that all match mean fewer outfits to plan and more combinations from fewer items.
- **Fold formal clothes, roll casual ones.** Different fabrics pack better different ways.
- **Use compression bags** to shrink bulky clothing.
- **Pack merino wool pieces.** You can rewear them without odor, which cuts down what you need.
- **Bring a packable daypack or tote** for day trips and souvenirs.
- **Skip the garment bag** unless you truly need it.

## Sort Out Toiletries and Small Items

This is where leaks, mushy soap, and security-line fumbling happen.

- **Use a Ziploc freezer bag** for TSA-compliant liquids.
- **Don't decant sensitive skin-care products** into travel containers.
- **Transfer full-size toothpaste into a refillable tube.**
- **Keep bar soap in a soap bag** so it doesn't turn mushy.
- **Use travel containers for non-toiletry items,** such as hot sauce.
- **Use a pill case as a jewelry organizer.**

## Choose Luggage That Fits Your Trip

The luggage advice is more relaxed than you might expect.

- **Don't be afraid to check a bag.** If it keeps you from cramming a carry-on, it can be the smarter move.
- **Put an analog luggage tag on it,** even if you use a Bluetooth tracker.
- **Know that many carry-ons exceed listed airline dimensions.** Check the specs before you assume a bag will fit.
- **Go smaller for international trips.** Choose a smaller carry-on when flying abroad.
- **Don't assume the lightest suitcase is best.** Weight isn't the only thing that matters.
- **Pick a medium checked bag over a large one.**
- **Buy a luggage scale** so you aren't stressing about overweight fees at the airport.

## Prep Your Phone Before International Trips

These steps take minutes at home and save real hassle on arrival.

- **Install an inexpensive eSIM** before you go.
- **Download maps, translation languages, and phone updates** before you arrive, while you still have reliable Wi-Fi.
- **Protect your phone's privacy when crossing borders.**

## Carry the Paperwork

- **Keep paper copies of key travel details.** A dead phone shouldn't strand you.
- **Photograph or photocopy your passport.**
- **Pack personal items in an unsuspecting small bag.**

## Make the Flight More Comfortable

- **Wear polished but comfortable clothes.** You can look put-together without sacrificing comfort.
- **Choose broken-in, versatile shoes** over cute ones.
- **Pick a travel pillow that matches how you sleep.** A pillow that suits a side sleeper may not work for someone who dozes upright.
- **Upgrade your eye mask.** The free airline version isn't the one to rely on.

## If You Only Have 10 Minutes

Pick three: the eSIM, downloaded maps and translations, and a photo of your passport. Together they cover the most common headaches of arriving somewhere new. If you're not leaving the country, swap in the luggage scale and an analog luggage tag.

The larger set of 44 tips includes more than we've covered here, so check the original Wirecutter piece for the rest.

## Your Next Step

Open your suitcase this week and test one idea: pick a color palette and see how many outfits you can build from it. If you can fill the bag with less, you're already traveling better.
