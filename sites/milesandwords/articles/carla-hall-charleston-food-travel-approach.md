## Why a Chef's List Is Worth Trusting

A celebrity chef's recommendation carries weight for a reason. Someone who eats professionally tends to notice things most visitors miss, and a favorite is usually a place they return to, not just a place they've heard of. That's the credibility angle: a chef's list is a filter.

Still, treat it as a starting point. Any single list reflects one person's taste, schedule and budget.

## How to Turn Her List Into a Plan

Once you have the restaurants from the original article, do these things:

1. **Sort by meal.** Pick one breakfast or lunch spot and one dinner spot per day. Charleston is a city you want to walk and explore, so don't overstack reservations.
2. **Check hours and booking rules.** Popular restaurants can fill up. Look at each restaurant's own website for current hours and reservation details before you commit.
3. **Cluster by neighborhood.** Map every pick and group them. Fewer cross-town trips means more time eating and wandering.
4. **Leave two slots open.** Keep a meal or two unplanned so you can follow your own curiosity, which is exactly the spirit of her "trick" idea.

## Eat Like a Local, Whatever the City

While we can't tell you Hall's exact trick, you can build your own habit of finding good food. These are our suggestions, not hers:

- **Ask a person, not an app.** When you check in, ask your host or a shop owner where they ate last week, not where you should go.
- **Ask a specific question.** "Where do you go for a weeknight dinner?" gets a better answer than "What's good?"
- **Compare answers.** When two unconnected people name the same place, put it on your list.
- **Mix planned and unplanned.** Book one anchor meal, then let the rest of the day decide.
