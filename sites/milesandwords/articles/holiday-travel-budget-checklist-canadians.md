CP24 recently ran a sponsored segment with personal finance and travel expert Barry Choi, aimed at Canadians planning fall getaways, holiday trips and winter escapes. It was tied to the Blue Rewards program, so treat it as sponsored content. The segment's full details aren't something I can reproduce here, so I won't put words in Choi's mouth. What the segment's framing does point to is a real problem: year-end travel planning is a tight window, and prices, availability and reward options can shift quickly.

So here's a practical checklist you can use today to plan seasonal travel without overspending. It's built on the themes CP24 highlighted: smarter planning, rewards optimization and avoiding common holiday costs. The steps are general planning advice, my own take rather than Choi's.

## Start with a number, not a destination

Most overspending begins when you fall in love with a place before you know what you can afford. Before you open a booking site:

1. Set a total trip ceiling, including flights, lodging, food, local transport and gifts or extras.
2. Split it into fixed costs (flights, hotel) and flexible ones (meals, activities).
3. Add a buffer of roughly 10 percent for surprises. That percentage is a rule of thumb, not a figure from CP24.

Once you have a ceiling, destinations that fit it become obvious, and the ones that don't drop off the list quickly.

## Book the big pieces early

CP24's framing is that peak planning season is when prices and availability can move fast. Flights and lodging for holiday weeks are the items most likely to tighten up. A sensible approach:

- Decide your travel dates first, and check whether shifting by a day or two changes the fare.
- Compare a few options, then commit to the flights and accommodation before worrying about activities.
- Leave tours and restaurant plans for later, since those are easier to adjust.

If your dates are fixed, that's the strongest reason to book sooner rather than later.

## Get your rewards in order before you pay

Rewards are where a lot of travelers quietly lose value. Before you book anything, check these:

### Know your balance and its rules
Log in to your rewards account and look at your current balance, any expiry dates, and what the program lets you redeem points for. Doing this before booking means you choose the payment method deliberately, not as an afterthought.

### Don't let rewards drive the trip
A discount or bonus is only a saving if you would have taken the trip anyway. If a rewards offer pulls you toward a pricier destination or a longer stay, you've spent more, not less.

### Check the fine print on blackout dates and fees
Holiday periods are when reward redemptions can be most restricted. Confirm what applies to your dates before you count on points covering the cost.

For details specific to Blue Rewards, go to the program itself. This article can't confirm its terms or benefits.

## Watch the costs that sneak in around the holidays

The base fare is rarely the full price. Build these into your budget up front:

- **Baggage and seat fees:** Holiday trips usually mean more luggage. Check what your fare includes.
- **Airport transport and parking:** Peak-season rates can add up on a longer trip.
- **Food in transit:** Airports and stations are expensive. Pack snacks.
- **Currency costs:** If you're heading south or abroad, consider how you'll pay and what fees apply.
- **Gifts and extras:** Holiday trips often come with spending that isn't on the itinerary.

Write each of these next to your ceiling number. Seeing them listed makes it harder to be surprised later.

## Consider a fall or early-winter shorter trip

CP24's piece covers fall getaways alongside holiday and winter travel, a useful reminder that you don't need to spend your whole budget in one December trip. A shorter fall escape can give you a break at lower stakes, and it spreads your spending across the season. If your holiday plans are already expensive, this may be the smarter place to put your flexible money.

## A quick booking checklist

Use this before you hit confirm:

1. Total trip budget set, with a buffer.
2. Dates chosen, with any flexibility noted.
3. Flights and lodging compared across a few options.
4. Rewards balance, expiry and redemption rules checked.
5. Extra fees (bags, transport, currency) added to the budget.
6. Cancellation and change policies read.
7. Payment method chosen on purpose.

## The takeaway

The best holiday travel savings come from decisions you make before you book: set your ceiling, lock in the big costs early, and know exactly what your rewards can and can't do. Spend ten minutes on that checklist this week, and you'll book with a plan instead of a hope.
