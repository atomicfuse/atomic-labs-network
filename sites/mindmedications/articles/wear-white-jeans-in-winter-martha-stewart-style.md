You can wear white jeans in winter. Martha Stewart just did it on a global stage, and by the end of this piece you'll know how to pull it off and where to find pairs starting at $24.

## The Moment That Reopened the Debate

Per InStyle, Martha Stewart, 84, wore white jeans at the 2026 Winter Olympics, breaking one of fashion's oldest unwritten rules: no white pants after summer. The outlet calls the pants divisive but flattering, and its editor says the look was convincing enough to follow.

My take: the rule was always more habit than law. A pair that fits well and looks polished doesn't care what month it is.

## Why This Rule Is Worth Breaking

Thinking about your wardrobe this way is a small piece of personal growth. Dropping a rule you inherited, and asking whether it actually serves you, is a good habit well beyond denim.

Practically, white jeans also give you a lighter base for the heavy, dark layers that dominate cold months. Dressing with more confidence tends to make getting out the door easier, too.

## How to Style White Jeans in Cold Weather

InStyle's piece doesn't spell out a styling formula, so treat these as my suggestions, not Stewart's:

- **Pair them with winter textures.** Chunky knits, wool coats and boots read seasonal and keep white from looking like a leftover summer piece.
- **Go for deeper colors on top.** Navy, charcoal, camel or forest green make the white look intentional.
- **Choose a fit you can move in.** Flattering matters, but so does comfort when you're standing outside or walking between errands.
- **Mind the hem.** Winter slush is real. Ankle-length or slightly cropped cuts keep the bottoms cleaner.
- **Check the fabric in daylight.** If a pair looks sheer under bright light, layer a longer top or keep looking.

## A Simple Way to Test the Trend

You don't need to rebuild your closet. Buy one pair, wear it three ways in the first week (with a sweater and boots, with a coat, with a casual layered top), and see how you feel. If you reach for them again, they've earned a spot. If not, you've spent very little to find out.
