A digital concept called the Audi Concept Q imagines the original Audi Quattro rebuilt for the electric age. It is not an Audi project and it won't be in a showroom. But it raises a fair question for anyone who follows the brand: if Audi did make an electric Quattro, what would it need to get right?

This piece uses the concept as a springboard. It covers what the design borrows from the original, and what a modern electric performance coupe would have to deliver to earn the name.

## What the Audi Concept Q actually is

The Concept Q is a CGI design study, per Autoevolution. It was created by Angelo Di Lorenzi, who is a Junior Exterior Designer and posts as "angelodilor_design" on social media. He is also a former Exterior Design intern at Alfa Romeo.

To be clear about the basis here: this is a digital rendering, not a car anyone has driven. There are no confirmed specifications, range figures, pricing or production plans, and none are covered in the reporting. Everything below about the design comes from how Autoevolution describes it. Everything about what a real car would need is my own analysis.

## The design cues it borrows from the original Quattro

According to Autoevolution, the concept blends several elements:

- **Quattro rally heritage.** The original Ur-Quattro built its reputation in rallying, and the concept leans on that identity.
- **Classic fastback proportions.** The long, sloping roofline is a coupe shape that reads as Quattro at a glance.
- **Square-light cues.** Boxy lighting is one of the quickest ways to signal the original car's look.
- **Widebody styling.** Flared, muscular bodywork gives it a more aggressive stance than a standard road car.
- **Slim LED details.** This is the modern layer, a way to update the lighting without losing the squared-off character.

The formula is simple. Keep the silhouette and the attitude, and let the lighting technology tell you it's current.

## What this means in daily terms

A retro-inspired design is not just a styling exercise. When you look at a car like this, you are judging three things quickly: does it look like the car I remember, does it look like it belongs on today's roads, and does it look like it would be fun?

The Concept Q aims at all three. The nostalgia is in the shape. The modernity is in the slim LEDs. The fun is in the wide stance.

## Why the idea is landing now

Per the Autoevolution report, Audi is under pressure from declining sales, especially in key markets such as North America. Heritage-inspired designs could, in theory, help rebuild excitement around the brand.

That logic is not unique to Audi. Retro-inspired EV concepts keep appearing because automakers want an emotional link between famous past models and an electric future. Electric cars can feel similar to one another, and a recognizable shape gives a brand something to stand out with.

That is the report's framing. My own read is that a halo car works best when it gives buyers a reason to look at the rest of the lineup. A striking coupe can do that even if few people buy one.

## What a real electric Quattro would need to deliver

This section is my analysis, not reported fact. If Audi ever built something like the Concept Q, the shape would only be the start. The name Quattro carries a specific promise, so I would judge it on these points.

### It has to feel like a driver's car

The original earned its name through rally success. An electric version would need to make sense of the badge through how it handles, not just how it looks. A widebody and a fastback roof suggest sportiness. The driving would have to confirm it.

### It has to be more than a styling exercise

Retro cars can be charming and still disappoint if the substance doesn't match. When any brand shows a heritage-flavored EV, ask what is actually new underneath, such as the drivetrain, the traction and the charging. A concept render can't answer that, and neither can I.

### It has to help the rest of the range

Audi's sales challenge is brand-wide. A single coupe won't fix that alone. Its value would be in bringing attention and goodwill back to the showroom.

## Original Ur-Quattro vs. Concept Q: what to compare

If you want to judge the two side by side, use the cues the reporting highlights:

1. **Silhouette:** both use a classic fastback-style profile.
2. **Lighting:** square-light cues on the concept echo the original, while slim LEDs bring it up to date.
3. **Stance:** the widebody styling gives the concept a more aggressive look.
4. **Purpose:** the original is tied to rally heritage. The concept is tied to the idea of an electric future.

What you can't compare is performance, range, size or price. The concept has no published numbers, so anyone who quotes them is guessing.

## Should Audi build it?

That is a question for Audi, and nothing in the reporting suggests the company is planning it. As a design exercise, though, the Concept Q makes a clear case: the Quattro name still carries enough emotional weight that a modern reinterpretation looks instantly appealing.

So here is the practical takeaway for your own car shopping. When a brand leans on its past to sell its future, look past the shape and ask what the car does that the old one couldn't. A good heritage design earns your attention. The drive, the range and the price are what earn your money.
