**Short answer:** No, Volkswagen has not announced an electric Beetle. What exists is an unofficial CGI concept called the VW ID. Beetle Beast, and it's a useful way to think about what an electric hot hatch could be, and where a Beetle revival would fit among the cars you can actually shop for.

By the end of this piece, you'll know what the concept is, what it can't tell you, and how to compare the electric performance hatchbacks that are real.

## What the ID. Beetle Beast actually is

Per Autoevolution, the concept comes from Augusto Fuchs, who posts as 'augusto_fuchsdesign' on social media. Autoevolution describes him as a Transportation Design Master at IED Torino and a Surface Modeler & Designer for Airbus Helicopter.

The render imagines the Beetle reborn as an electric ID. Series hot hatch. It blends the Beetle's recognizable shape with aggressive performance-EV styling, framed as an enthusiast-focused revival.

Two things to keep straight:

- **It's a digital rendering, not a prototype.** It is an independent designer's idea, not a Volkswagen project.
- **There are no specs to report.** The coverage we have doesn't give a battery size, range, power output, price, or release date, so any numbers you see attached to it elsewhere are guesswork.

## Why a Beetle EV idea keeps coming up

The timing makes sense. Retro-flavored electric cars and electric hot hatches are gaining momentum. Autoevolution's framing points to a group of real models:

- Volkswagen ID. Polo GTI
- Hyundai Ioniq 5 N
- MINI JCW Electric
- Alpine A290

That list shows carmakers are chasing something beyond efficiency: personality and driving fun. A Beetle carries instant recognition, and a performance version would aim at the same emotional pull.

That's our read of the trend, not a Volkswagen statement. Nothing here suggests the company is planning a Beetle.

## How to judge a hot hatch EV like this one

If a concept like this ever became a real car, here is what you'd want to check before getting excited. These are the same criteria worth applying to the electric hot hatches you can already look at.

### Daily usability

The original Beetle shape is famously round. A domed roofline looks great but can limit rear headroom and cargo opening. Ask whether the back seat fits adults and whether a stroller or suitcase fits with the seats up.

### Real-world range and charging

Performance tuning usually costs range. Look for official range figures for your market and the maximum DC fast-charging speed, since that determines how long road-trip stops take.

### Price against rivals

Hot hatches live or die on value. Compare any newcomer's price with the Ioniq 5 N, MINI JCW Electric, and Alpine A290, and check which trim gets the performance hardware you're paying for.

### Whether the driving matches the looks

Aggressive styling is a promise. Steering feel, braking, and how the car behaves when pushed are what deliver it, and you can only judge that on a test drive.

## Beetle nostalgia versus electric performance

The appeal of this concept is the tension in it. The Beetle is remembered as friendly and simple. Hot hatches are loud, fast, and a bit cheeky. Putting them together is a design challenge: keep the charm, add the attitude, and avoid a car that looks like a costume.

That's why the render gets attention, but a concept can ignore crash structures, battery packaging, and cost. Production cars can't.

## What to do with this if you're shopping

Enjoy the render, but shop the cars that exist. If you want an electric hot hatch now, list the real ones named above, check the specs and pricing for your market and model year, and book test drives back to back. If a Beetle ever returns officially, you'll have a clear checklist ready.
