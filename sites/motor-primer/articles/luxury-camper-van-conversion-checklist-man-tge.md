**Short answer:** a luxury camper van conversion isn't about looks. It's about whether you can live in it for days without constantly rearranging it, and whether it can run without a campsite hookup. Autoevolution recently profiled a MAN TGE conversion from Ashben Conversions that makes a handy case study. Below is what that build gets right, and the questions to ask when you're reading a conversion listing.

## What Ashben Built

Per Autoevolution, the van is a MAN TGE with a bold, head-turning exterior and a more refined interior than you'd guess from the outside. Inside, it packs a full bathroom, a fixed bed, garage storage and power systems described as ready for off-grid use. Autoevolution's takeaway is that clever design can deliver an apartment-like travel setup in a compact footprint.

That list is a good template. Each item answers a question you should ask about any conversion you're considering.

## 1. Does the Bed Stay a Bed?

A fixed bed means you don't convert a dinette into a mattress every night and back every morning. If you're traveling for more than a weekend, that matters more than almost any gadget. Folding beds are fine for occasional use, but they turn daily routines into chores.

The Ashben coverage also points to east-west sleeping pods. In plain terms, that means sleeping across the width of the van rather than along its length. The pods are also described as a way to change how the van's bedroom works. Crosswise layouts can free up length for other things, such as a bathroom or storage. The trade-off is that you need to be comfortable with the width available, so lie down on the actual mattress before you commit.

**What to check:** Lie down in the bed, ideally in the way you'd really sleep. Check head and foot room, and see if you can sit up without hitting a ceiling or cabinet.

## 2. Is There a Real Bathroom, and What Did It Cost You?

A full bathroom is the headline feature of this build, and it's the biggest layout decision in any van. Every square foot given to a bathroom is a square foot not given to storage, seating or kitchen counter.

If you camp mostly at sites with facilities, a bathroom may be dead weight. If you want to stay somewhere remote or hate the idea of a midnight walk, it's worth a lot. Decide that before you shop, because it determines which vans you should be looking at.

**What to check:** Ask how the bathroom is used in practice. Is it a dedicated space? Does it double as anything else? Then ask what you lose elsewhere because it's there.

## 3. Where Does the Gear Go?

The Ashben van includes garage storage. A garage in a camper is a dedicated space for bulky gear, which keeps it out of the living area. That's often the difference between a van that feels spacious on day one and one that feels cluttered by day three.

Be skeptical of listings that say "plenty of storage" without showing it. Bring your own gear, or at least measure it, and see if it fits.

**What to check:** Take your bulkiest items (bikes, camp chairs, luggage) and see whether they fit without blocking the bed or bathroom.

## 4. Can It Run Without Hookups?

Off-grid-ready is the phrase that separates serious conversions from weekend builds. According to the coverage, this van has power systems built for that purpose. A true off-grid setup generally involves four pieces working together: a battery bank, solar panels, an inverter and a way to charge from the vehicle or the grid.

The coverage we reviewed doesn't list capacities for the Ashben van, so we won't guess. That's an instructive point in itself: "off-grid ready" is a claim, not a spec. When you're looking at any conversion, ask for numbers.

**Questions to ask the builder or seller:**

- How big is the battery bank, and what chemistry is it?
- How much solar is on the roof?
- What does the inverter power, and what can't you run on it?
- How does the system charge while driving and when plugged in?
- What does a typical day's usage look like, and how long will the system last without sun?

A good builder can answer all of these quickly. A vague answer is information.

## 5. Does the Exterior Matter?

The Ashben MAN TGE is described as a head-turner. Looks are a legitimate reason to buy; you'll see this thing every day. But treat styling as the tiebreaker, not the decision. Two vans with equal beds, bathrooms and power can differ wildly in livability, and the exterior won't tell you which is better.

Also keep the base vehicle in mind. The TGE is the foundation, so the usual used-vehicle questions still apply: service history, mileage and the condition of anything the conversion touches, such as wiring and body panels where fixtures were added.

## A Simple Way to Compare Two Conversions

Put the vans side by side and score them on five things:

1. **Sleeping:** fixed or convertible, and how it feels to lie in.
2. **Bathroom:** included or not, and what space it costs.
3. **Storage:** where the bulky gear goes.
4. **Power:** documented capacities, not adjectives.
5. **Base vehicle:** history and condition, not just the interior.

This is our framework, drawn from what Autoevolution highlighted in the Ashben build. It isn't a test of that van, and we haven't driven or stayed in it.

## Before You Shop

Write down which of those five matter most for how you travel, then ask every seller for hard numbers on each. A conversion that looks like a boutique apartment but can't tell you its battery size is a showroom, not a camper.
