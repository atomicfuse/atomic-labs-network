The reason is simple: the buildout is reportedly not going to slow down based on who wins in November, and the same electricity system that charges your car is where that investment lands. ## What this means for your routine

You can't control national politics, but you can control how exposed your car budget is to electricity price swings. A few practical moves:

### Know your current rate

Find your price per kilowatt-hour on your latest bill. Multiply it by your car's consumption to get a real cost per mile. Your owner's manual or the window sticker lists efficiency for your specific model year and market, so use those rather than a generic figure.

### Check for time-of-use plans

Many utilities offer cheaper overnight rates. If yours does, charging at home off-peak is usually the easiest way to keep costs down. Ask your utility what's available and read the fine print on peak windows.

### Compare home charging with public charging

Public fast charging typically costs more per kWh than home charging. If most of your driving fits a home-charging routine, you're less exposed to price changes at public stations. If you rely on public chargers, track what you actually pay for a month before deciding whether an EV or a hybrid fits you better.

### Don't make a purchase decision on election news

The expert's message is that the buildout is steady. That argues against waiting for the election to resolve before choosing a car. Base the decision on your commute, parking situation, local rates and incentives that apply to your market and model year. Incentive rules change, so confirm current eligibility with an official source before you count on a credit.

## What we don't know

The report doesn't say how fast data center demand will grow, which regions will be affected most, or whether electricity prices will rise because of it. Those are open questions. Be wary of anyone who answers them confidently from a single interview.

## The takeaway

Pull out your latest electric bill this week, find your rate per kWh, and work out your cost per mile. That one number will tell you more about your EV budget than any election forecast.
