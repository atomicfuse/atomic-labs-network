The headline, per Fox Business, comes from an expert discussion on 'The Claman Countdown,' where Sanders Morris Chairman George Ball and SlateStone Wealth chief market strategist Kenny Polcari talked about the fallout from a red day in the markets.

## Why an EV owner should care anyway

If energy is a limiting resource for one fast-growing industry, it is reasonable to ask how that shows up for other big electricity users, including the cars plugged in on your street. The connection is analysis, not reported fact: the same grid serves both. What that means locally depends on your utility, region, and rate plan.

That makes the useful question not "will AI starve my car of power?" but "how exposed is my charging routine to changes in local electricity costs and availability?" You can answer that with a few checks.

## Five checks that make your charging routine resilient

### 1. Read your rate plan

Log in to your utility account and see whether you are on a flat rate or a time-of-use plan. Many utilities offer cheaper overnight rates, but plans vary by market, so confirm the actual off-peak hours and prices on your own bill rather than assuming.

### 2. Schedule charging instead of plugging in and forgetting

Most EVs and many home chargers let you set a charge start time. Moving charging to off-peak hours is usually the easiest way to cut cost and reduce strain on the grid. Check your vehicle's app or infotainment menu, since the options differ by model year and manufacturer.

### 3. Know your real daily need

Most people drive less than they think. Look at your last month of trips and find your typical daily mileage. If a normal day uses a fraction of your battery, you have flexibility to skip a charge on an expensive day or wait for cheaper hours.

### 4. Check your home electrical capacity

If you are considering a Level 2 home charger, have a licensed electrician assess your panel. Capacity, wiring, and local permit rules vary, and this is a job for a professional, not a guess.

### 5. Keep a backup charging plan

Identify two public chargers near home and work, and note their networks and payment methods. Having an alternative means a local outage or a busy station is an inconvenience, not a problem.

## How to read AI-and-energy headlines

Stories linking AI to power demand will keep coming. A few habits help:

- **Separate the argument from the data.** An expert saying energy limits AI is a viewpoint. Numbers on consumption need their own source.
- **Look for your local angle.** National headlines rarely translate directly to your utility bill. Your utility's rate announcements do.
- **Distrust certainty about the future.** Nobody in this segment, as summarized, promised specific effects on EV owners, and neither should anyone selling you a fix.

## The bottom line

The energy-as-buffer idea is an interesting lens on AI, but your action item is closer to home. Pull up your utility rate plan this week, find your off-peak hours, and schedule your next charge for them. It costs nothing, works regardless of how the AI debate plays out, and puts you in control of the one slice of the grid you actually use.
