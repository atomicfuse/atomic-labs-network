If you are shopping for a 2026 model, seat comfort deserves a place next to price and fuel economy. You sit in it for every mile. A new J.D. Power ranking offers a useful data point, and a few surprises, about which vehicles owners say have the fewest seat problems.

## The Headline: BMW Z4 Leads, Not the Mustang

According to J.D. Power's 2026 U.S. Seat Quality and Satisfaction Study, as reported by Slashgear, the BMW Z4 has the best-performing seats of any vehicle tested. It scored 3.1 problems per 100 vehicles (PP100). Lower is better.

The study draws on responses from more than 78,000 owners, per the report. The Ram 1500 came in second and the Porsche Cayenne third.

## The Top Six, Ranked

Here are the scores as reported:

1. **BMW Z4:** 3.1 PP100 (best overall)
2. **Ram 1500:** 3.5 PP100
3. **Porsche Cayenne:** 3.9 PP100
4. **Mercedes-Benz S-Class:** 4.0 PP100
5. **Volvo V60:** 4.0 PP100
6. **Ford Mustang:** 6.1 PP100 (best mass-market mid-size/large model)

Note that the Mustang's 6.1 is listed as the best in its mass-market category, not as a top-five finisher overall. The headline's "not the Mustang" framing makes sense in that light: the Mustang has a reputation to defend, but the Z4 beat it by a wide margin on this measure.

## What PP100 Actually Means for You

PP100 counts owner-reported problems, not how plush a seat feels on a test drive. A score of 3.1 means owners reported roughly three seat problems for every 100 vehicles. The gap between first and second place (3.1 vs. 3.5) is small. The gap between the Z4 and the Mustang (3.1 vs. 6.1) is about double.

In practice, treat the top few as a cluster. Differences of a few tenths are unlikely to change your daily experience. Differences of two or three points are worth noticing.

This is a research-based ranking from owner surveys. It is not a firsthand test, and we have not sat in these cars for this piece.

## Luxury Does Not Guarantee Better Seats

Per the report, luxury brands only marginally outperform mass-market brands in seat quality. The results back that up. The Ram 1500, a full-size pickup, placed ahead of the Porsche Cayenne, the Mercedes S-Class and the Volvo V60.

That matters if you are weighing a premium trim or a luxury badge mainly for comfort. A higher price tag does not automatically buy fewer seat complaints. Our read: if seats are your priority, spend your effort on testing, not on assuming a badge will deliver.

## How to Test Seats Before You Buy

Survey data gets you a shortlist. Your own body makes the final call. Try this at the dealership:

- **Sit for at least 20 to 30 minutes.** Many seat issues only show up after a while, so ask for an extended test drive.
- **Adjust everything.** Check seat height, lumbar support, cushion length and backrest angle. A seat that cannot fit you is a problem no matter its score.
- **Drive your usual route.** Highway stretches and rough pavement reveal support and vibration issues.
- **Check the controls.** If the car offers heating, ventilation or memory settings, make sure they work and are easy to reach.
- **Bring your regular passengers.** Rear seats are scored by the same owners who ride in them.

## One Caveat on the Z4

The Z4 is a two-seat roadster, which makes a direct comparison with a pickup or a family SUV imperfect. If you need space for passengers or cargo, the Ram 1500, Cayenne or Volvo V60 on this list may suit you better. Also check the current availability of any model you are considering, since model lineups change by year and market, and this ranking covers U.S. owners.

## The Takeaway

Use the J.D. Power numbers to build a shortlist, then let a long test sit make the call. If a mass-market model fits you better than a luxury one, the data says you are not giving up much, if anything, on seat quality.
