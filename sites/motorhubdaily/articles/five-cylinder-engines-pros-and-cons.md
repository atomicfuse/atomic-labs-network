If you've wondered why a handful of cars sound unlike anything else on the road, or whether a five-cylinder is worth seeking out, here's the short version: you get character and a sweet spot of performance, and you give up efficiency, choice, and practicality. Here's how that trade plays out.

## Where the five-cylinder stands today

Per Slashgear, Audi's RS3 is the only new five-cylinder car sold in America. That makes the layout a rare enthusiast curiosity rather than a mainstream option. Turbocharged four-cylinders dominate the market, and Slashgear's assessment is that the five-cylinder's future looks increasingly niche.

That context matters if you're shopping. If you want a five-cylinder new, your choices are essentially one car. If you want one used, you're looking at the older models from the era when automakers such as Audi, Volvo, and Volkswagen offered the layout.

## What you gain: sound and character

The main appeal, according to Slashgear, is that five-cylinder engines have a distinctive sound and feel. They aren't the smooth hum of a four or the familiar growl of a six. If you care about how a car makes you feel at part throttle on a back road, this is the argument in the five-cylinder's favor.

That's subjective, of course. Some drivers hear a charming warble; others won't notice a difference from a good four. Our advice: drive one before you decide you need one.

## What you gain: a middle ground in performance

Slashgear describes the five-cylinder as a sweet spot between four- and six-cylinder engines. In practical terms, the idea is more power and presence than a typical four, without the size of a six.

The catch is that this sweet spot has shifted. As Slashgear puts it, five-cylinders are now outperformed by modern four-cylinders. Turbocharging and engineering improvements have let smaller engines do the job the five once handled.

## What you give up: efficiency

Efficiency is where the five-cylinder loses most clearly. Slashgear says modern four-cylinders beat it, and that's the heart of why automakers moved on. A turbo four can deliver strong performance while asking less of the fuel tank than a five.

If your driving is mostly commuting and errands, that matters more than exhaust note. Fuel costs add up over years of ownership, and an engine that's more efficient at the same performance level is hard to argue against.

## What you give up: availability and practicality

Slashgear also points to availability and practicality as drawbacks. With one new five-cylinder car on sale in the US, you can't cross-shop much. You take the car that comes with the engine, including its price, size, and equipment.

We can't tell you from this reporting what a five-cylinder costs to maintain or repair compared with a four, so we won't guess. If you're considering an older used example, that's a question to put to a specialist mechanic and to owners' forums before you buy. A pre-purchase inspection is worth the money on any used performance car.

## Five-cylinder versus turbo four: a quick way to decide

Use these questions to sort yourself out:

- **Do you want the sound and personality above all?** A five-cylinder is the more distinctive choice.
- **Do you want the best efficiency for the performance?** Slashgear says modern fours now come out ahead.
- **Do you want options?** Fours and sixes are everywhere. Fives are not.
- **Do you want it new?** In the US, that means the Audi RS3.

## Is the RS3 the last of its kind?

It's the only new one on sale in America right now, per Slashgear, and the trend toward turbo fours suggests it won't have much company. Whether it's the last is a prediction nobody can confirm. What we can say is that it's the easiest way to experience the layout on a new-car warranty.

## The takeaway

Treat a five-cylinder as a choice you make with your ears, not your spreadsheet. If a test drive of the RS3, or a well-inspected used Audi, Volvo, or Volkswagen, makes you grin, that's a legitimate reason to buy. If you're mostly after efficiency and value, a modern turbo four will serve you better. Book the test drive and let the engine make its own case.
