If you're weighing a luxury car, the badge on the hood tells you less than you'd think about how long it will last. A 2026 longevity study from iSeeCars, as reported by SlashGear, puts numbers behind that idea, and the top of the ranking comes with a twist: Lexus finished first, with Acura second.

Here's what the study covers, what its results can and can't tell you, and how to turn them into a smarter purchase.

## What the study looked at

Per SlashGear's report, iSeeCars examined which luxury car brands are most likely to last beyond 250,000 miles. That's a useful yardstick. It's roughly double what many people expect from a car, and it speaks directly to ownership cost: a vehicle that keeps running for 250,000 miles spreads its purchase price over far more years of use.

The headline results:

- **Lexus** ranked as the longest-lasting luxury brand.
- **Acura** came in second.
- **Jaguar and Maserati** scored at the bottom of the longevity rankings.

The summary I worked from doesn't include the full ranking, percentages, or the model-level breakdown. So I'm not going to guess at where other brands landed or by how much. If you want the complete list, go to the original iSeeCars study.

## Lexus vs. Acura: what a close podium tells you

The two brands at the top share a reputation for dependable, low-drama ownership, and the study backs that up. But the ranking is a statement about brands, not about every car they sell. A brand-level result is an average across many models and years.

That matters when you shop. Before you commit to a specific car:

1. **Check the exact model year.** Engines, transmissions, and infotainment systems change between generations, and reliability can shift with them.
2. **Confirm your market.** Specifications and powertrain options differ by country, so a study built on US data may not map neatly onto a car sold elsewhere.
3. **Look at the individual car's history,** not just the badge. A neglected Lexus can still be a bad buy.

In practice, the study gives you a sensible starting shortlist. It doesn't replace checking the car in front of you.

## Why the bottom of the list matters too

Jaguar and Maserati landing at the bottom is the other half of the story. Both carry strong prestige and, typically, steep new prices. The study's result suggests that prestige and durability are separate qualities, and that you're not guaranteed the second by paying for the first.

That's my read, not iSeeCars' claim, but it's a practical one. If a brand scores poorly on lasting past 250,000 miles, a used example with high mileage deserves extra scrutiny. A low asking price can be tempting, but repair bills on a car with a weaker longevity record can erase the savings.

## How to use this when buying used

Longevity data is most valuable on the used market, where you're buying a car partway through its life. A brand with a strong record gives you more confidence that the remaining miles are still ahead of you.

### Run a short checklist

- **Ask for service records.** Regular oil changes and documented maintenance matter more than any brand ranking.
- **Get a pre-purchase inspection** from an independent mechanic before you sign anything.
- **Run a vehicle history report** to check for accidents and title problems.
- **Test drive for more than ten minutes.** Listen for odd noises, feel for hesitation in shifts, and test every electronic feature.

### Price the repairs, not just the car

Luxury parts and labor cost more than mainstream equivalents. Before you buy, get a rough idea of what common repairs run for that specific model. A cheaper car that costs more to keep on the road isn't a bargain.

## Is the premium worth paying?

The study speaks to a real tension for luxury shoppers: higher prices demand justification. Longevity is one way to measure whether you're getting it. If a car lasts well past 250,000 miles, the higher upfront cost can make sense over a decade or more of ownership. If it doesn't, you've paid extra for a badge.

This is a research-based comparison drawn from a published study, not a firsthand test of these cars. Treat it as one input alongside a test drive, an inspection, and your own budget.

## The takeaway

Before you fall for a luxury badge, look up how that brand holds up past 250,000 miles. Start your shortlist with the brands that earn it, then verify the exact model, year, and service history before you pay.
