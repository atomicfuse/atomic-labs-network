If you've moved to Maryland and your car still wears out-of-state plates, the clock matters more than it used to. Per Slashgear, Maryland is stepping up enforcement of its 60-day registration requirement for new residents starting October 1, 2026, with escalating penalties that include daily fines of up to $420 and possible vehicle impoundment.

This guide covers what the reported penalty path looks like, why the rule is getting attention, and what to do so you never end up on the wrong side of it.

## The Rule: 60 Days to Register

New Maryland residents are required to register their vehicles within 60 days. That requirement isn't new. What's changing, according to Slashgear's reporting, is how firmly it will be enforced and how quickly the consequences stack up.

The practical takeaway: treat the 60 days as a hard deadline from the day you establish residency, not a suggestion. Put the date on your calendar the week you move.

## How the Penalties Escalate

As described in the report, noncompliance follows a ladder rather than a single penalty:

1. **Notice.** You're alerted that your vehicle isn't properly registered.
2. **Daily fines.** The reporting cites a $7 per day fine, which can build to as much as $420.
3. **Impoundment.** If the problem continues, your vehicle could be impounded.

Do the math on that middle step. At $7 a day, the $420 ceiling works out to 60 days of fines. That is a lot of money to pay for the privilege of not finishing paperwork, and it comes before any impound costs.

I'd treat the notice as the moment to act, not the moment to start researching. Every day after it costs you.

## Why Virginia Registration Became a Problem

The enforcement push has a financial backdrop. Per the report, tens of thousands of Maryland residents currently register their vehicles in Virginia to avoid higher fees. The numbers cited are roughly $30 for Virginia registration versus $200 for Maryland.

That's a $170 difference, and it's easy to see the temptation. But the report says the practice costs Maryland significant revenue, and the new enforcement is meant to recapture it.

Here's our read: a $170 saving looks smaller once you compare it with a possible $420 in fines plus the hassle of impoundment. The gamble only pays if you're never caught, and the state is now signaling it intends to catch more people.

## Where the Money Goes and the Safety Argument

The report ties registration revenue to public services, including emergency services such as medevac helicopters. It also raises road safety, noting concerns that unregistered vehicles can compromise safety standards, and references Baltimore's ranking as a high-risk driving city.

We can't independently verify those statistics from the reporting available, so check the specifics with Maryland's official sources. What's clear is the state's framing: registration isn't only a fee, it's a funding stream and a compliance check.

## A Practical Compliance Checklist

The reporting points to compliance deadlines and exemption options, though it doesn't spell out the details of those exemptions here. So treat this as a process, and confirm the specifics with the Maryland Motor Vehicle Administration (MVA) before you act.

### Step 1: Count your days
Identify the date you became a Maryland resident and add 60 days. Mark it, then set a reminder two weeks earlier.

### Step 2: Check whether you qualify for an exemption
Exemption options exist, per the report. Whether one applies to you (for example, based on your circumstances) is something to confirm directly with the MVA rather than assume.

### Step 3: Gather your paperwork early
Registration generally requires proof of ownership and insurance, but requirements vary, so verify the current list on the MVA's website for your situation and model year before you go.

### Step 4: Budget for the real cost
Plan around the reported $200 Maryland registration figure rather than the $30 Virginia one. Fees can vary by vehicle, so use the MVA's fee information for your exact car.

### Step 5: Don't ignore a notice
If you've already received one, register immediately. The $7 daily fine is the part you control by acting fast.

## If You're Currently Registered in Virginia

If you live in Maryland full time but your car is registered in Virginia, you're exactly who this enforcement targets, per the report. The simplest path is to register in Maryland now, before a notice arrives. Paying $200 once is cheaper than racking up daily fines toward $420 and risking impoundment.

If you genuinely live in Virginia and only visit Maryland, this rule is about residency, so your situation is different. When in doubt, ask the MVA how it defines residency in your case.

## The Bottom Line

Mark your 60-day deadline, confirm any exemption with the Maryland MVA, and register before a notice shows up. Saving $170 isn't worth fines that can climb to $420 and a car at risk of impoundment.
