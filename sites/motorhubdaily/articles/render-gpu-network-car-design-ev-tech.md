## How Render Works

According to that same source, Render is a decentralized GPU computing marketplace. Creators who need graphics horsepower pay in RENDER tokens to use idle GPUs owned by node operators around the world. The token lives on Solana.

Think of it like a ride-share model for graphics cards. Instead of one company owning every machine, individual owners with spare capacity rent it out, and the people who need the work done pay for it.

## Why Cars Are Part of the Conversation

The source ties demand for GPU computing to AI and 3D rendering workloads, and says film, design, and AI creators actively use Render. Those are the same kinds of workloads that sit behind the car world's visuals and software:

- **Design visualization:** Photorealistic images and animations of new models are 3D rendering work.
- **AI development:** Driver-assist and in-car software depend on AI training, which is GPU-hungry.

That is my analysis, not a claim from the source. The source describes the network's general users, and it does not name any automotive customers. Still, it helps explain why GPU availability and cost are a live topic for anyone following where vehicle tech is headed.

## The Token Model in Plain English

The source describes a burn-and-mint model for RENDER. In general terms, that means tokens are removed from circulation (burned) as creators pay for work, and new tokens are minted to reward the node operators who supply GPU power. The idea is that usage and supply stay linked rather than supply growing without any connection to demand.

The page frames this as a core part of how the token is meant to stay sustainable. Whether it works that way in practice is a separate question the page can't answer for you.

## Who Supplies the Power

Node operators are the owners of the idle GPUs. They earn RENDER for letting creators use their hardware. The source summary doesn't detail specific hardware requirements or earnings, so if you were considering becoming an operator, you'd need to read the project's own documentation first.
