A van that looks like a tradie's work vehicle from the outside, but sleeps, cooks and runs off-grid inside. That's the idea behind a Toyota Hiace conversion from Australian builder Free Roam Fitouts, recently covered by Autoevolution. If you're weighing a van build or buying a converted rig, it's a useful case study in what makes a small space livable.

This is a researched look based on Autoevolution's report, not a firsthand test drive. Specifications vary by model year and market, so confirm details for the van you're considering.

## What this build is

Per Autoevolution, the conversion is built on a super long wheelbase Hiace. Outside, it doesn't say much. Inside, the report describes a bright, comfortable, well-equipped living space that makes smart use of limited room.

That contrast is the whole appeal.

## Why a stealth exterior is worth considering

A plain exterior means the van doesn't advertise itself as a camper. There are no obvious decals, awnings or roof gear announcing that someone sleeps inside. Autoevolution frames this as part of a growing interest in "stealth campers" that work like full mobile homes without showing it.

There are practical upsides to think about:

- **Everyday usability.** A van that looks ordinary is easier to live with as a daily driver or park in town.
- **Low profile.** A discreet rig draws less attention when parked.
- **Simple styling.** Fewer add-ons on the outside can mean fewer things to maintain.

Those are my takeaways rather than claims from the builder, but they're the reasons to weigh a stealth approach against a flashier conversion.

## Why the super long wheelbase matters

The length of the van is the biggest lever on interior space. A longer body gives a builder room to separate sleeping, cooking and lounging zones instead of cramming them together. If you're shopping for a Hiace, the wheelbase you pick largely decides how roomy the finished layout can be. Check which lengths are sold in your market, since availability differs by region.

## Layout tricks to borrow

Autoevolution highlights a few features that make this small van feel bigger than it is.

### A convertible lounge and bed

The lounge converts into a bed, so one area does two jobs. In a compact van, any surface that serves double duty frees up floor space you'd otherwise lose to a permanent bed.

### A real kitchen

The build includes a kitchen, which is what separates a van you can live in from one you can only sleep in. When you view a conversion, ask where you'd prep food, where you'd store it and whether you can cook without rearranging the whole interior.

### A bright interior

The report describes the cabin as bright. Light finishes and good lighting make a narrow space feel open. It costs little and changes how a van feels on day one.

## The off-grid basics: power and insulation

The van is set up for off-grid use, and Autoevolution points to its power setup and insulation as key parts of the build. For a beginner, these are the two systems to scrutinize before anything cosmetic.

### Power

Off-grid power determines how long you can stay away from campsites with hookups. When you inspect a van, ask the builder what runs on the system, how it recharges and what you can't run. Get the answers in writing, with the actual component specs.

### Insulation

Insulation affects comfort in both heat and cold, which matters in a country like Australia where long-distance trips cross very different climates. It's also hidden behind the panels, so ask what was used and where, because you can't check it by eye once the build is finished.

## A quick checklist for judging any camper conversion

1. **Wheelbase and length.** Does it match your needs and fit where you plan to park?
2. **Dual-purpose spaces.** Does the bed convert to seating, or does it eat the living area?
3. **Kitchen.** Can you cook and store food without clearing the van?
4. **Power.** What does the system run, and for how long off-grid?
5. **Insulation.** What was installed, and is it documented?
6. **Base vehicle.** Check the model year, service history and market-specific specs of the underlying van, since the conversion is only as sound as the vehicle beneath it.

## Where the Hiace fits

Autoevolution's coverage focuses on the Hiace as a practical, reliable platform for Australian travel, where long distances and off-grid exploring are common. It doesn't compare the Hiace against rivals like the Sprinter or Transit, so I won't claim it wins that contest. If you're choosing a base van, compare payload, interior dimensions, running costs and local service support for the specific model years you can actually buy.

## The takeaway

Before you fall for a conversion's looks, spend your time on the parts you can't see: layout logic, power and insulation. A plain-looking van with those right will serve you better than a striking one with them wrong. Ask for the specs, then walk through the layout as if you already live there.
