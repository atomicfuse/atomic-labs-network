If you own a pickup, the lower corners of the cab are probably where rust will show up first. This guide covers why it happens, how to check yours in about ten minutes, and how to decide between a driveway repair and a body shop visit. Do it before winter and you may save yourself a much bigger bill.

The topic comes from a Slashgear piece on why truck cab corners rust and how to fix them. Below is what that reporting says, plus practical steps for putting it to use. I haven't tested these methods on a specific truck, so treat product choices and timing as general guidance, and check your owner's manual and local conditions.

## Why cab corners rust first

Per Slashgear, cab corners collect moisture, salt, dirt, and debris in hidden lower-body areas. That combination is the problem. The corners sit low, close to the spray from your tires, and they contain seams and cavities where water can enter but doesn't easily leave.

Once debris packs into those spots, it holds moisture against the metal for long stretches. Road salt makes this worse, which is why owners in salt-heavy regions tend to see trouble sooner. According to the report, corrosion can spread from the inside out. That matters because the damage you can see is often only part of what's happening behind the panel.

## How to inspect your cab corners

Pick a dry day and give yourself good light. Wash the lower cab first if it's dirty, since mud hides early problems.

1. **Look at the paint.** Check the bottom edges and corners for bubbling, flaking, or a rough, blistered texture. Bubbling paint is a sign rust may already be advanced, because the corrosion has built up under the surface and is pushing the paint outward.
2. **Check the drains.** Look for small drain holes along the lower cab and rocker area. If they're clogged with dirt or debris, water has nowhere to go. Clear them gently with a plastic tool or a blast of compressed air.
3. **Feel the surface.** Run your fingers along the corner seams. Rough spots or flaking edges are early warning signs.
4. **Look from underneath.** With a flashlight, check the inner side of the corners where you can reach. Because rust spreads from the inside out, a clean-looking outer panel doesn't guarantee a healthy one.

Take photos each time you inspect. Comparing them season to season shows you whether a spot is spreading.

## DIY fix or body shop?

The repair options Slashgear outlines run from treating surface rust to replacing panels, then protecting the repaired area with coatings, sealers, and cavity wax. Here's a practical way to sort which end you're on.

### When a driveway repair is reasonable

If the rust is on the surface, with no bubbling and no holes, treating it yourself is a sensible first move. The general process is to remove the rust, clean the area, and then seal it so moisture can't return. Skipping that last step is how rust comes back.

### When to call a body shop

Bubbling paint, soft or flaking metal, or rust you can see has eaten through the panel points toward more serious work. At that stage the fix can involve replacing panels, which is a job for a shop. A cosmetic patch over advanced corrosion tends to hide the problem rather than stop it, since the rust keeps working underneath.

A useful rule of thumb that's mine, not Slashgear's: if you're unsure how deep it goes, get a shop's opinion before you spend money on materials. An estimate is usually cheaper than redoing a failed repair.

## Protect the repair, or it won't last

Fixing the rust is only half the job. The same conditions that caused it will return, so the finish matters. The report points to three layers of protection:

- **Coatings** to protect the repaired surface.
- **Sealers** to keep moisture out of seams.
- **Cavity wax** to coat the inside of hidden areas, which is where corrosion can spread from.

The cavity wax step is the one most owners skip, and it addresses the exact inside-out pattern that makes cab corner rust so stubborn. Follow the product's directions, and make sure drains stay open after you apply anything so you don't create a new water trap.

## A pre-winter checklist for truck owners

Road salt speeds up corrosion, so the weeks before winter are the best time to act. A simple routine:

- Wash the truck, paying attention to the lower cab and corners.
- Clear drain holes and any packed debris.
- Inspect for bubbling paint, flaking, and rough seams.
- Treat any surface rust and seal the area.
- Add protection to hidden cavities where you can.
- Rinse off salt during winter when conditions allow, since salt left sitting keeps working on the metal.

## Why it's worth the effort now

Slashgear notes that used truck prices and repair costs remain high. That cuts two ways. A truck with clean corners holds its value better, and bodywork on a neglected one costs more than it used to. Catching a small rust spot early is almost always cheaper than replacing a panel later.

If you're shopping for a used pickup, apply the same inspection to the truck you're considering. Bubbling paint at the cab corners is a good reason to ask questions or negotiate.

## The takeaway

This weekend, wash your truck, clear the drain holes, and look closely at each cab corner. If the paint is intact, seal and protect it. If it's bubbling, get it looked at before the salt arrives.
