Some road signs make you slow down because of the hazard. Others make you slow down because you can't work out what they mean. Slashgear recently rounded up some of the strangest signs from around the world, and five of them are worth a closer look, because behind the oddness sits a practical lesson about staying alert at the wheel.

A caveat up front: the details below come from Slashgear's reporting, and I haven't driven these roads or seen the signs in person. Treat this as a researched roundup, not a test drive.

## 1. Trivia questions in Queensland, Australia

Per Slashgear, signs in Queensland pose trivia questions to drivers, and the purpose is to keep people awake on long stretches of road.

That's the most useful idea on the list. Fatigue is a hazard on any long drive, and a sign that gives your brain something to chew on is a simple, low-cost attempt to fight it. The signs themselves are a reminder of what you can do on your own:

- Plan breaks before you feel tired, not after.
- Swap drivers if you can.
- Treat any mental nudge, a sign or a conversation with a passenger, as a cue to check how alert you really are.

If you're driving somewhere remote and a quiz pops up beside the road, take it as a prompt to check in with yourself.

## 2. A sign that is not in use, United Kingdom

Slashgear lists a UK sign about a sign not being in use. It sounds like a joke, but it points at a real problem: signs that exist yet give you nothing actionable.

The takeaway for everyday driving is to rely on what the road is actually telling you, not on a sign alone. If a sign is unclear, outdated or contradicts what you see ahead, slow down and use your judgment. Confusing signage is a good reason to leave extra space and attention, not to guess.

## 3. Beware of crabs, Christmas Island

Per Slashgear, Christmas Island has a sign warning drivers to beware of crabs.

It's a charming entry, but it's also a good example of a sign tied to a specific local hazard. Wildlife warnings are among the most location-specific signs you'll meet. If you're driving in an unfamiliar place, especially while traveling, read the animal signs as local knowledge. They tell you what the road is used for besides cars.

A few habits that apply anywhere wildlife signs appear:

- Reduce speed in the marked zone, not just when you see an animal.
- Scan the shoulders as well as the lane ahead.
- Expect surprises to be more likely at certain times or seasons.

## 4. Snow drifts in the road, Quebec, Canada

Slashgear includes a Quebec sign warning of snow drifts in the road. Weather hazards are the other category of sign that rewards attention, because conditions can change the road surface within a short distance.

If you're heading into winter driving, a sign like this is your cue to adjust before you reach the problem: lower your speed, increase following distance, and keep your inputs smooth. It's also a decent reminder to check your tires, wipers and washer fluid before the season turns, since a warning sign can't help a car that isn't ready.

## 5. Toad crossing, United Kingdom

The last entry in Slashgear's list is a toad crossing sign in the UK. Like the crab warning, it reflects the fact that roads cut through places animals still need to move across.

This is the sign most likely to be easy to dismiss because the hazard seems small. But it signals that a particular stretch of road matters to local wildlife, and that drivers are being asked to take a little extra care. Slowing down costs you seconds, and it's the courteous and safe thing to do.

## What the five signs have in common

These signs look very different, but they sort into three jobs: keeping you awake (Queensland), warning you about animals (Christmas Island and the UK toads) and flagging weather (Quebec). The UK's unused sign is the odd one out, and it's the one that shows what happens when messaging misses its mark.

It's my own read, not Slashgear's, but the pattern seems clear: the best signs are specific, tied to a real hazard, and give you something you can act on. The weakest ones leave you puzzled.

## Driving somewhere new? A quick routine

Unusual signs tend to turn up when you're traveling, which is also when you're least familiar with the road. Before a trip:

1. Look up local hazards for your route, such as wildlife and seasonal weather.
2. Schedule rest stops at set intervals so fatigue doesn't make the decision for you.
3. Check that tires, fluids and wipers suit the conditions you expect.
4. Treat any sign you don't understand as a reason to ease off the throttle.

The next time a strange sign shows up, don't just smile at it. Ask what it's trying to tell you, and adjust your driving before you've figured out the joke.
