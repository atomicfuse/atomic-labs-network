The Hyundai Tucson loses value faster than its biggest rivals. That's bad news if you buy new and sell in a few years. It can be good news if you shop used. Here's how to tell which side of that trade you're on.

## What the depreciation numbers say

Per a Slashgear report that draws on data from iSeeCars, CarEdge, and Kelley Blue Book, the Tucson loses roughly 40% to 47% of its value after five years. A related estimate cited in the piece puts the range at 40% to 50%.

In plain terms: a Tucson that cost $35,000 new would be worth somewhere around $18,500 to $21,000 after five years if it follows those percentages. That example is our own arithmetic using a round number, not a figure from the report, and actual prices vary by trim, mileage, condition, and market.

Slashgear's conclusion is that the Tucson is a solid compact SUV, but it trails the Toyota RAV4, Honda CR-V, and Mazda CX-5 on resale value. The report specifically says it fares worse than the CR-V.

## What depreciation costs you if you buy new

Depreciation is usually the biggest cost of owning a new car, and you don't see it on any monthly statement. You feel it when you trade in or sell.

If you plan to keep a new Tucson for ten years or more, resale value matters much less. You'll drive it until the value is mostly gone anyway. If you swap vehicles every three to five years, a weaker resale figure means a bigger gap between what you paid and what you get back.

That gap also affects your next purchase. A smaller trade-in value means less down payment toward the next vehicle, or a larger loan.

## Why a used Tucson can be the bargain

Someone else has already absorbed the steepest part of the drop. When a model depreciates faster than its rivals, used examples tend to be priced lower relative to comparable RAV4s or CR-Vs. That's our analysis of how the market generally works, not a claim from the Slashgear report, so check local listings to confirm it holds for the years you're considering.

The practical upside: you may get more SUV for your money, or the same SUV for less, than you would with a used competitor that holds its value better.

## How to compare before you commit

Don't take the percentages on faith for your situation. Spend 30 minutes on this:

1. **Pull listings for three models.** Search the Tucson, RAV4, and CR-V in the same model year range, similar mileage, and within your driving distance.
2. **Compare the price gap.** If the Tucson is meaningfully cheaper, the weaker resale value is already priced in. If the gap is small, the rivals may be the better buy.
3. **Check the specs by model year.** Features, powertrains, and hybrid availability change from one year to the next and between markets. Confirm the exact year and trim you're looking at rather than assuming.
4. **Look up values on more than one tool.** The report used three data sources, and it's smart for you to do the same. Kelley Blue Book is one of them and is free to check.

## Costs beyond the sticker price

Resale value is one piece of ownership cost. Before choosing, add up the others:

- **Insurance quotes.** Get quotes for each model you're considering, since they can differ.
- **Fuel costs.** Compare the fuel economy of the specific powertrain and year, including any hybrid versions.
- **Maintenance and repairs.** Review the service history on any used vehicle, and ask whether any warranty coverage remains. Warranty terms can depend on market and on whether you're the original owner, so confirm them with a dealer or the manufacturer.
- **Financing.** A lower purchase price with a high interest rate can cost more than a pricier car with a better loan.

## Quick used-SUV checks

Whichever compact SUV you choose, the basics still apply:

- Ask for the maintenance records and a vehicle history report.
- Have an independent mechanic inspect it before you pay.
- Test drive on both city streets and a highway.
- Check that all electronics, climate controls, and driver-assist features work.

## Who should buy which

If you buy new and sell within a few years, the RAV4, CR-V, or CX-5 should leave you with more money back, according to the data Slashgear cites. If you plan to buy used or keep a vehicle long term, the Tucson's faster depreciation works in your favor.

This article is a researched comparison based on reported data, not a firsthand test drive. Use it to narrow your list, then drive the candidates yourself.

Before you visit a dealer, pull prices for a used Tucson and a used CR-V of the same age and see how big the gap really is. That number tells you whether depreciation is working for you or against you.
