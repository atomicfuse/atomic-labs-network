Your credit score is steady, your monitoring app is quiet, and you feel safe. That feeling can be wrong. Per a Fox News report, a clean credit report doesn't mean your identity is secure, because several kinds of fraud never touch your credit file. Below are six of them, followed by a monthly routine that catches what alerts miss.

The core problem, as the report frames it: identity theft is expanding beyond credit cards and loan fraud into phones, benefits, healthcare, and data broker exposure. If you only watch your score, you may miss faster-moving scams that drain accounts or compromise your access.

## Six kinds of fraud credit monitoring can miss

### 1. Existing bank account takeover
No new credit account is opened here. A criminal gets into an account you already have and takes money from it. Because nothing is borrowed, your credit report stays untouched.

### 2. Government benefits and tax fraud
Someone uses your identity to file for benefits or submit a tax return. These records live with government agencies, not credit bureaus.

### 3. Medical identity theft
Your identity can be used to get healthcare services, leaving a trail in your medical bills and insurance statements rather than your credit file.

### 4. SIM swap and phone account fraud
Criminals target your mobile phone account to take over your number or compromise your access. Since your phone often receives your security codes, this one can open doors to everything else.

### 5. Scams involving direct payments
If you're tricked into sending money directly, there's no credit event to flag. The loss shows up in your accounts, not your report.

### 6. Personal information exposed through data brokers
Data brokers hold personal details about you. That exposure doesn't change your credit, but it can give scammers material to work with.

## Your monthly checklist

Set a recurring calendar reminder for the first weekend of each month. Block about 30 minutes and work through these six habits.

### Step 1: Review your financial accounts regularly
Open your bank and card accounts (in the bank's app or website) and scroll through recent transactions. Look for anything you don't recognize, even small amounts. This is your main defense against account takeover and direct-payment scams.

### Step 2: Watch for suspicious tax activity
Pay attention to anything odd involving your taxes, such as notices about returns or income you didn't file or earn. Unexpected tax correspondence is a signal worth investigating rather than tossing.

### Step 3: Check your Social Security earnings record
Log in to your Social Security account and review your earnings record. Income or work history you don't recognize can point to your identity being used by someone else.

### Step 4: Review medical bills and insurance statements
Read them, don't just file them. Look for services, providers, or dates you don't recognize. Medical identity theft shows up here first.

### Step 5: Protect your mobile phone account
Log in to your carrier account and review its security settings. Check that your contact details are correct and that no changes you didn't make have been applied. Settings names vary by carrier and phone, so look in the account or security section of your carrier's app. If your phone suddenly loses service unexpectedly, treat it as a possible warning sign and contact your carrier right away.

### Step 6: Check where your personal information appears online
Search your own name and see where your details turn up, including on data broker sites. Knowing what's exposed helps you spot scams that use those details to sound convincing.

## What credit monitoring still does well

Credit monitoring isn't useless. It's built to catch new accounts and loans opened in your name. The point is that it covers one lane out of several. Keep it, but don't treat a quiet alert inbox as proof that everything is fine.

## Make the routine stick

- Put the checklist in a notes app or a shared calendar event so it repeats automatically.
- Do it on the same device each time so you know where to log in.
- When something looks off, act the same day: contact the bank, carrier, insurer, or agency involved.

The habit that matters most is simple: don't let a calm credit score be your only check. Block 30 minutes this month and go through all six steps once.
