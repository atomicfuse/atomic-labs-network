Motorola's Android 17 rollout is reaching more phones, and some owners are searching for a "January 2027 update" they see on their devices. This guide shows you how to check what your Razr or Edge is actually offering, how to get the update installed, and what to do if nothing appears.

A quick note on what is and isn't confirmed. PhoneArena reports that the rollout is reaching Razr Ultra (2025) and Edge 70 Fusion users, though slowly. Android Headlines says the Razr Ultra (2025) has finally received Android 17, with TikTok arriving alongside it. Talk Android reports Motorola is expanding Android 17 to more Razr and Edge phones. None of that coverage, as summarized here, explains the "January 2027" label, so treat any explanation of it as an educated guess, not fact.

## What "January 2027" might mean on your phone

The most likely place you're seeing a date like this is in Settings, under the Android security patch level or in the update details. That's a reasonable reading, not something the reports confirm. If it's the security patch date, it simply tells you how current the software's security fixes are, not when the update arrives for you.

The practical point: don't judge by the date. Judge by what your phone says when you check for updates, and by the Android version number listed in Settings.

## Step 1: Check which Android version you're on

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Scroll down and tap **About phone**.
3. Tap **Android version**.

If it says Android 17, you already have the update. If it shows an earlier version, move on to the next step.

While you're there, note the **Android security update** date and the **Build number**. If you ask Motorola support or post in a forum, those details help people identify exactly what you're running.

## Step 2: Manually check for the update

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Tap **System**, then **System update** (on some builds it's under About phone).
3. Tap **Check for update**.

If an update appears, tap download. Plug your phone in first, and connect to Wi-Fi so a large file doesn't eat your mobile data.

## Step 3: Is your model on the list?

Based on the coverage, here is what's supported by reporting:

- **Razr Ultra (2025):** Android Headlines says it has received Android 17.
- **Edge 70 Fusion:** PhoneArena says the rollout is reaching its users, slowly.
- **Other Razr and Edge phones:** Talk Android says Motorola is expanding the update to more models, but I can't list which ones from the material available. Check the Talk Android article for its full list, or Motorola's official support page for your exact model.

If your phone isn't named, that doesn't mean it's excluded. It may just not be covered in those reports yet.

## Step 4: What to do if nothing shows up

The word "slowly" matters here. Staged rollouts typically reach users in waves, so two people with the same phone can see the update days or weeks apart. Before you worry, try these in order:

1. **Restart your phone,** then check again.
2. **Switch networks.** Try a different Wi-Fi connection.
3. **Free up storage.** Updates need room to download and install. Clearing a few gigabytes of old videos and unused apps is a safe start.
4. **Check again in a day or two.** Don't repeatedly factory reset; that rarely speeds up a staged rollout and can cost you data.
5. **Contact Motorola support** if your model is confirmed for the update but it still doesn't appear after a couple of weeks.

## Step 5: Prepare before you install

A major Android version change is bigger than a routine patch. A few minutes of prep protects you:

- **Back up your phone.** Go to Settings, then Google, then Backup, and run a backup now.
- **Charge to at least 50%,** ideally more.
- **Update your apps** in the Play Store first, so they're compatible.
- **Set aside 20 to 30 minutes.** Don't start the install right before you need your phone.

## After the update: what to look for

Android Headlines notes that TikTok came along with the Razr Ultra's update. If you see new apps you didn't ask for, you can usually review them under Settings, then Apps. Disable or uninstall anything you don't want, if the option is available for that app.

Then spend five minutes checking the basics: battery behavior over the first day, your lock screen and notification settings, and whether your most-used apps open normally. Settings sometimes reset after a big update, so confirm your privacy and notification choices are still the way you left them.

## The one thing to do today

Open Settings, tap System update, and check manually. Ignore the date label and trust what the phone tells you. If it says you're up to date on an older Android version, be patient: the rollout is reportedly gradual, and your turn may simply not have come yet.
