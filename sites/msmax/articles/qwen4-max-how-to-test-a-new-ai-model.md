Alibaba's Qwen team has announced Qwen4-Max, which it positions as a major upgrade in capability in the race among frontier AI models. The post was on the Hacker News front page with 369 points and 123 comments when we looked, so people are clearly paying attention.

Here is the practical question for you: should it change which AI tool you use? I can't answer that from a launch post, and neither can anyone else who hasn't run your tasks through it. What I can give you is a simple way to find out in about 30 minutes, and it works for any new model that shows up.

## What we know, and what we don't

Per Qwen's announcement, Qwen4-Max is Alibaba's latest large language model release, framed as a significant step up and a serious competitor among top-tier models. The wider context, as the Hacker News discussion reflects, is that AI labs keep releasing models quickly, and strong alternatives from China are changing the options available to developers and the pricing pressure around them.

What I'm not going to do is quote benchmark scores, prices, or context limits. I only have the headline claim, and a lab's own launch post is the lab's own account. Read the original post at qwen.ai/blog for the specifics, and treat comparisons with GPT, Claude, and Gemini as unproven until you've tried them.

## Step 1: Write down your five real tasks

Benchmarks measure someone else's problems. Open a notes app on your phone or computer and list five things you actually ask AI to do. For example:

- Summarize a long email thread into three action items
- Rewrite a rough paragraph so it sounds friendlier
- Explain a confusing error message on your computer
- Plan a week of meals around a budget
- Draft a polite reply to a difficult message

Pick tasks where you can tell a good answer from a bad one. That's the whole test.

## Step 2: Use the exact same prompts everywhere

Copy each prompt into a text file and paste it unchanged into each assistant you want to compare: the one you use now, plus Qwen4-Max if you can access it. Changing wording between tools ruins the comparison.

If you can't find Qwen4-Max in an app or website yet, check the announcement post for how to access it rather than assuming it's available in your region or on your plan. Availability is something to verify, not guess.

## Step 3: Score the answers blind

Paste the responses into a document and label them A, B, and C without naming the tools. Then rate each from 1 to 5 on three things:

1. **Accuracy:** Did it get facts and steps right?
2. **Usefulness:** Could you use it as-is?
3. **Tone:** Does it sound like something you'd send?

Scoring blind keeps brand loyalty out of it. You may be surprised which one you prefer.

## Step 4: Check for confident mistakes

Add one question where you already know the answer, such as a detail about your own city, job, or a product you own. Every AI model can state wrong things with total confidence. A model that admits uncertainty or asks a clarifying question is often more useful than one that sounds sure.

## Step 5: Think about privacy before you paste

Before sharing anything personal or work-related with a model from any company, read its privacy and data-use settings. Look for whether your conversations may be used for training and whether you can turn that off. Until you've checked, test with made-up or non-sensitive material. This applies equally to every provider, not just this one.

## Step 6: Decide with a simple rule

Total the scores. If a new model beats your current tool by a clear margin on tasks you do weekly, try it for a week. If it's a near tie, stay put. Switching tools has a cost: you lose saved chats, habits, and integrations with the apps you already use.

## Why the competition matters even if you never switch

The Hacker News discussion reflects a broader trend: more strong competitors tend to mean more choice for developers and pressure on pricing. That's my read of the situation, not a guarantee. Even if you stay with your current assistant, it's a reason to avoid locking yourself in. Keep your prompts in a plain text file and your important chats exported, so moving later takes minutes rather than days.

## Your next move

Save your five test prompts today. The next time a model launch fills your feed, you'll be able to judge it on your own work instead of the hype.
