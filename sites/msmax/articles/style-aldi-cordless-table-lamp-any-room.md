A lamp that needs no outlet can go wherever a room feels dim or flat. ALDI is selling one for $9.99, and by the end of this guide you'll know where to put a cordless lamp, how to choose a finish, and what to check before you buy.

## What ALDI is selling

Per Homes & Gardens, the Casalux Touch Battery Table Lamp is part of ALDI Finds and costs $9.99. It has a metallic finish, comes in gold and silver, and runs on batteries, so no outlet is required. You operate it by touch.

That's the full set of details I can confirm. Before buying, check the box or the ALDI listing for battery type, brightness, and run time, since those weren't included in the coverage. ALDI Finds are also typically limited-time items, so availability can vary by store.

## Why a cordless lamp is worth trying

Placement is usually limited by where the outlets are. A battery lamp removes that limit. It also suits renters, since there's nothing to install, drill, or hide behind furniture. Homes & Gardens frames portable, touch-operated lighting as an affordable way to add warmth and ambiance to a room.

At this price, you can also test a spot before committing to anything bigger.

## Five spots where a small lamp works hard

### 1. The entry table
A lamp by the door gives you a soft welcome without running a cord across the floor. Touch it on as you walk in.

### 2. A bookshelf or open shelf
Shelves often look flat. Set the lamp on one at eye level and let it glow among books or decor. Without a cord trailing down the back, the shelf stays tidy.

### 3. The nightstand
Touch operation is handy here: tap to turn it on or off without fumbling for a switch. If your bedside outlet is taken by chargers, a battery lamp frees it up.

### 4. A hallway console or window ledge
Hallways tend to be the darkest part of a home. A small lamp on a narrow table adds light without needing wiring.

### 5. The dining table or a corner
Because there's no cord, you can move it to the table for dinner and then to a dark corner afterward. That portability suits cozy fall evenings.

## Gold or silver?

Homes & Gardens notes both finishes. Here's my own take on choosing, not ALDI's:

- **Gold** tends to read warmer. It pairs well with wood, cream, rust, and other fall tones.
- **Silver** tends to read cooler and cleaner. It suits gray, white, black, or glass-heavy rooms.

If you can't decide, look at the hardware already in the room, such as cabinet pulls, picture frames, or faucets, and match the dominant metal.

## A quick setup routine

1. **Check the lamp in the store.** Confirm it's the touch battery model and see what batteries it takes.
2. **Buy spare batteries** if they aren't included, so it never goes dark mid-evening.
3. **Test two or three spots** at night, when you can see what the lamp actually does.
4. **Group it.** One small lamp reads best next to something: a stack of books, a plant, or a tray.
5. **Keep it off a heat source** and away from spills, as with any lamp.

## Set your expectations

A $10 battery lamp is accent lighting. Homes & Gardens pitches it for ambiance, and that's the right job for it. It won't replace a reading light or a room's main fixtures. Use it to add a glow, not to light a workspace.
