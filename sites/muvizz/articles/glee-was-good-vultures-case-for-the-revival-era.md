A Glee revival is officially in development at Disney+/Hulu, and the internet has done what it does best: reopened a decade-old argument. Was the show brilliant, embarrassing, or both? Vulture has weighed in with a pointed essay titled "Let's Never Forget That Glee Was Good," and its description sums up the mood: the writer has had enough of the "cringe" and "problematic" libel.

That's the news. Here's my take: Vulture is onto something, and the revival is the perfect excuse to say it out loud.

## The Meme Version vs. the Show That Existed

Per Vulture, Glee's reputation has been flattened into two words, "cringe" and "problematic," and those words now travel as memes more easily than the show itself ever did. The essay's argument is that the early seasons were critically acclaimed, culturally massive, musically influential, and intentionally satirical.

It's hard not to see how that gap opened up. Memes are compressed, context-free, and built for a scroll. A single out-of-context clip can stand in for an entire series, and in the TikTok era that clip becomes the show's whole résumé. Nobody posts "actually, the tone was a deliberate heightened-reality comedy" over a 6-second loop.

## Satire Is Not the Same as Accident

The most interesting claim in Vulture's piece is that Glee's excess was the point. The show was a musical comedy that leaned into melodrama, absurdity, and over-the-top sincerity on purpose. If you read it as straight realism, nearly every scene looks like a miscalculation. If you read it as satire, a lot of those same scenes start to look like the joke landing.

That distinction matters because "cringe" is a judgment about intent. It assumes the people making something were embarrassingly earnest and unaware. Vulture's case is that Glee knew exactly what it was doing. You can still dislike the result, and that's fair, but it's a different complaint than "they didn't realize."

## "They Did This on Glee"

One of the angles floated in the coverage is the cultural footprint of the phrase "They did this on Glee." It's a handy barometer. People don't say it about shows nobody watched. The line works as shorthand because the series was so omnipresent that its musical-number-as-emotional-climax formula became a reference point other pop culture keeps bumping into.

That's a form of influence that doesn't show up in a Rotten Tomatoes score. Vulture notes the show was musically influential, and anyone who remembers when a mashup or a cover could dominate conversation for a week knows the show helped set that template.

## Why the Revival Changes the Conversation

Nostalgia cycles, reboot culture, and meme-driven reassessment have all converged here. A revival forces the question of what Glee actually was, because a new version has to choose what to keep. Keep the camp, and you're defending the satire. Sand it down, and you've admitted the critics had a point.

There's also the practical puzzle. One of the open questions in the online debate is whether a Glee revival can work without key original cast members. I can't tell you the answer, and nothing in Vulture's framing suggests anyone can yet. But it's the question that will decide whether this is a reunion or a reboot, and fans will treat those very differently.

## What a Good Glee Revival Would Need

Here's my wish list, clearly labeled as opinion:

- **Commit to the heightened tone.** If the original was satire, the follow-up shouldn't apologize for being theatrical.
- **Treat the music as the engine.** The show's influence ran through its songs, so the revival lives or dies on whether the numbers feel like events.
- **Acknowledge the memes.** A show this thoroughly rewritten by the internet might do well to wink at its own reputation.

## The Verdict

You don't have to love Glee to agree with Vulture's underlying point: a show can be funny to mock and still be good, and a legacy shouldn't be decided by whichever clip travels furthest. The revival is arriving into an era that remembers Glee mostly as a punchline. If it's smart, it will make the punchline work for it.

And if the reassessment is already underway, maybe the funniest twist is that the show everyone called cringe turns out to be the one people couldn't stop talking about.
