## How a persona does the shielding

The documentary's framing points at a pattern fans of celebrity culture will recognize, even without naming names:

### The brand becomes the excuse

When a star's whole appeal is being unpredictable or boundary-pushing, almost anything can be absorbed into the act. The more outrageous the persona, the harder it is for viewers, and sometimes colleagues and media, to say where the character ends.

### Audiences are accomplices by default

It's easy to point at the famous person. It's harder to admit we clicked, laughed, and shared. A good accountability documentary doesn't just indict its subject. It implicates the ecosystem of bookers, outlets, and audiences that rewarded the act. That's a reasonable reading of what "hiding in plain sight" implies, though we'll leave it to viewers to judge how far the film goes.

### Hindsight is unfair and also useful

There's a fair counterargument: rewatching old clips with today's knowledge can feel like cheating. Context shifts, norms change. But hindsight is also how culture corrects itself. Looking again isn't the same as inventing a past that didn't happen. Sometimes it's just paying attention late.
