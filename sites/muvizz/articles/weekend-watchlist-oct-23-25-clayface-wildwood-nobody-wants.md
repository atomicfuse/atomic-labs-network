## If You Want to Be Properly Unsettled: *Clayface*

Vulture files *Clayface* under DC body horror, and that phrase alone makes it the odd duck of the weekend. Superhero-adjacent movies usually arrive polished and PG-13-shaped. Body horror is the opposite impulse: squishy, uncomfortable, and not interested in your snack choices.

It's hard not to read the pairing of "DC" and "body horror" as a franchise experiment, which is exactly what makes it the pick for anyone who likes watching a big brand try something weird. Whether it works is a different question, and one you'll have to settle yourself. But a shape-shifting villain is a natural fit for the genre, and a weekend in late October is the right time to find out.

## If You Want Something Handmade: *Wildwood*

Vulture tags *Wildwood* as stop-motion fantasy. In a streaming era of glossy, algorithm-smoothed visuals, stop-motion is a nice reminder that someone moved every one of those figures by hand, frame by frame. That's craft you can see on screen.

It also makes a decent palate cleanser if you're double-billing. Pair it with *Clayface* and you've got a weekend that goes from nightmare fuel to storybook, with only a short trip between them.

## If You Want Comfort Food: *Nobody Wants This*

The returning streaming comedy on the list is *Nobody Wants This*, which is Vulture's cue that the season-to-season comfort-watch crowd has something new waiting. A returning series is the lowest-effort, highest-reward choice on any weekend list: you already know the characters, you don't need to relearn a premise, and you can half-watch while someone else figures out dinner.

That's the quiet advantage of a returning comedy in the middle of fall's prestige pile-up. Not everything has to be an event. Some things just have to be good company.

## If You Want a Dinner Party (Onscreen, That Is): *The Invite*

Vulture calls *The Invite* a starry dinner-party drama, and it gets top billing in the roundup's tagline for a reason. The dinner-party drama is a reliable genre: one room, a few people, and a long evening in which polite conversation slowly stops being polite. Stars in a confined space tend to be a good bet, since the whole thing runs on performance.

It also pairs neatly with the comfort pick above, if your household wants two very different kinds of talky. One is warm and returning. The other, going by the genre's track record, probably ends with someone staring into a wine glass.

## How to Actually Pick

A simple cheat sheet, based on Vulture's own descriptions:

- **Want to be spooked:** *Clayface*
- **Want something crafted and strange:** *Wildwood*
- **Want something easy:** *Nobody Wants This*
- **Want a slow-burn ensemble:** *The Invite*
- **Want a doc fix:** the roundup flags buzzy documentaries, including the Maria Bamford one

This is also a good moment to remember that the late-October calendar is packed. Fall viewing season is in full swing, and prestige streaming drops, franchise experiments and awards-adjacent indies are all landing at once. The weekend list isn't really about finding the one right answer. It's triage.

My suggestion: choose one thing you'd be embarrassed to admit you skipped, and one thing you'd happily watch in sweatpants. *Clayface* and *Nobody Wants This* are an odd couple, but that's the point. If your weekend can hold a body-horror villain and a returning comedy, you've done fall right.
