The post-summer streaming hangover is real. You've burned through your beach reads, the blockbuster season is quietly packing up its toys, and fall TV is still doing its pre-game stretches. Which means this weekend — September 11 through 13 — is prime territory for actually watching the things you bookmarked and forgot about. Conveniently, the slate obliges.

Vulture's weekend watch guide lands with a mix of nostalgia sequels, music-doc events, prestige festival titles, and a few pure curiosity-watches. The angle here isn't completionism — it's sorting signal from noise. Here's what actually deserves your couch time, organized by the mood you're bringing to it.

## If You're Feeling Nostalgic (and a Little Witchy)

### Practical Magic 2

The one everyone's been clocking since it was confirmed: Sandra Bullock and Nicole Kidman are back as the Owens sisters, more than two decades after the 1998 original became the cult sleepover staple it is today. Whether the sequel earns its existence or simply coasts on goodwill is the central question of the weekend — and frankly, it's the kind of curiosity-watch where even a middling result makes for good group-text content. Gather your coven accordingly.

### Oasis: Don't Look Back in Anger

Speak of nostalgia — the Gallagher brothers' reunion tour may be the most-discussed music event of the year, and this documentary drops at peak cultural saturation for all things Oasis. The title, lifted from one of their most enduring anthems, does the tonal heavy lifting before a single frame plays. If you want to understand why the internet collectively lost its mind when Liam and Noel announced they were back in the same room, this is your primer and your payoff in one.

## If You Want Something With Actual Weight

### Hope

A festival title making its way to wider audiences this weekend, *Hope* arrives with the kind of prestige-adjacent energy that earns the description without overselling it. It's the sort of film that tends to reward patience — the type of pick that feels like homework until about twenty minutes in, when it quietly has you.

### Palestine 36

Among the most pointed picks on this weekend's slate, *Palestine 36* is documentary viewing with real historical stakes. Its presence here reflects a broader moment in which documentary filmmaking is carrying cultural and political weight that fictional narratives often sidestep. Not light weekend fare, but sometimes the weekend asks something of you.

## If You Want to Laugh Without Committing to a Plot

### Stavros Halkias: New Comedy Special

Halkias has built a genuinely devoted following through his podcast work and stand-up circuit, and a new special is the kind of low-stakes, high-reward stream that the weekend was invented for. Comedy specials remain one of streaming's best arguments for the couch: no lore, no cliffhangers, just seventy-odd minutes of either it lands or it doesn't. Per Vulture's pick, this one's worth finding out.

## If You Watch TV Like It's a Part-Time Job

### Saturday Night Live UK — Season 2

The transatlantic experiment returns for a second run, which in itself is something — plenty of international SNL adaptations don't make it past their first season. Season 2 means the show has presumably figured out some of the things Season 1 was still workshopping. For fans of the format, or anyone curious whether the British iteration has found its groove, this is the check-in worth making.

### Supergirl

DC's expanded universe keeps expanding, and *Supergirl* lands this weekend for the corner of the audience that has been tracking the franchise's post-*Gunn era* evolution with the intensity of a fantasy football manager. Curiosity-watch energy is high — the DC faithful want to see how the pieces are fitting together, and the casually interested want to know if this is the entry point worth taking.

### The Paper

A series with its own gravitational pull for fans of workplace ensemble drama. *The Paper* is the kind of show that tends to find its audience through word-of-mouth more than marketing, which makes a weekend premiere ideal territory — you'll either hear about it at brunch or be the one texting people about it by Sunday night.

## If You Want Something You Can Half-Watch

### The Drop

Sometimes you want a film that asks just enough of you to feel like participation without demanding full emotional investment. *The Drop* fits that slot — a streaming pick designed for the viewer who wants something on while also, let's be honest, doing other things. No shame in it. That's a legitimate viewing mode.

## If You're Already Watching the U.S. Open Anyway

### Tennis-Adjacent Viewing

The U.S. Open is in full swing, and Vulture's guide nods to the tennis-themed viewing options that become suddenly relevant when you're already in the sport's orbit. Whether that's documentary content, dramatic tennis films, or just leaving the Open on in the background while you scroll through the rest of this list — the weekend has you covered.
