Texas has gone from No. 1 and national title contender to a team close to College Football Playoff elimination, and the blame is landing on quarterback Arch Manning. Brian Kelly thinks that's aimed at the wrong person. Per Fox News, Kelly says head coach Steve Sarkisian, not Manning, bears responsibility for the Longhorns' offensive struggles in Austin this season.

Here's what Kelly's argument amounts to, why it matters, and what to watch if Texas is going to turn this around.

## What Kelly Is Saying

According to the Fox News report, Kelly places the offensive slump and Texas' back-to-back losses more on Sarkisian's play-calling than on Manning's performance. The suggested fix has three parts:

- Simplify the offense.
- Lean more on the run game.
- Build the game plan around what Manning can execute.

Notice what that does and doesn't claim. Kelly isn't saying Manning has played perfectly. The point, as reported, is that the play-caller's job is to shape the offense around the quarterback's strengths, and that's where Kelly sees the breakdown.

## Why the Coach-First Argument Holds Up

This part is my analysis, not Kelly's. Offensive struggles rarely come from one source, but the play-caller controls the variable that's easiest to change: the menu of plays. A young quarterback can only execute what he's asked to do, and if the calls ask for too much, the result looks like a quarterback problem even when it's a design problem.

Sarkisian carries a reputation as an offensive mind, which is part of why this storyline stings. When a coach with that reputation sees his unit stall, the natural question is whether the scheme is fitting the player or the player is being forced to fit the scheme. Kelly's answer, per the report, is the latter.

## Why the Run Game Is the Key

Kelly's recommendation to lean on the run is the most practical piece of his case. A stronger ground game does several things for a quarterback who is struggling:

- **Shortens the field.** Manageable third downs are easier than third-and-long.
- **Simplifies reads.** Play-action and defined throws replace high-stakes decisions.
- **Controls the clock.** Fewer possessions for opponents means fewer chances for the offense to dig out of a hole.

None of that requires Manning to become someone else. It asks the offense to hand him a smaller, cleaner job and let him succeed at it.

## The Playoff Math

The stakes are what make this more than a debate topic. Per the report, one more loss could effectively end Texas' playoff hopes. The remaining schedule includes Ole Miss, Missouri and LSU, which is not a stretch where a team gets time to experiment.

That's why the Manning-Sarkisian dynamic has become one of college football's biggest storylines. Every drive now carries weight, and every play call will be judged as evidence for one side of the argument.

## What to Watch Against Ole Miss

Ole Miss is the next test, and it's a clear way to see whether Kelly's critique is being heard in Austin. Keep an eye on these things:

1. **Early-down play selection.** Do the Longhorns commit to the run, or do they keep throwing on first and second down?
2. **Quarterback comfort.** Are throws coming off simple reads and defined routes, or is Manning being asked to create?
3. **Third-down distances.** Better early-down success shows up here first.
4. **In-game adjustments.** If something isn't working, does Sarkisian change course quickly?

If the offense looks cleaner and more run-centered, that would line up with what Kelly is recommending. If it looks the same, the pressure on both Sarkisian and Manning will only grow.

## Fair Caveats

Kelly's view is an outside opinion, and it's one voice in a larger debate. Manning's performance still matters, and reasonable people can weigh the quarterback's share of the blame differently than Kelly does. What's clear is that the slump has too many moving parts to pin on one person, and the fastest lever for improvement sits with the coaching staff.

## The Takeaway

When you judge Texas against Ole Miss, watch the play sheet before you watch the box score. If the Longhorns simplify, run the ball and build around what Manning does well, you'll have your best evidence yet about where the problem has really been.
