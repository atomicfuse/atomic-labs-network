Colorado coach Deion Sanders receives "about five" death threats every day, according to a report from The Athletic, as relayed by Fox News. This piece looks at what that reporting says, what it doesn't, and why the security side of Coach Prime's celebrity deserves a serious look.

This is a sober subject, so the focus here is on what has been reported and what it suggests about the cost of running one of college football's most visible programs.

## What the report says

Per Fox News, The Athletic obtained 15 closed harassment cases connected to Sanders since he took over Colorado football. The reporting describes police records, threatening voicemails and letters, and heightened security measures around the coach.

The "about five a day" figure is the headline number. Based on the summary available to us, it is attributed to The Athletic's reporting, and we can't independently verify how those threats were counted or classified. Treat it as a reported figure, not an audited one.

The 15 closed cases are a more concrete data point. They indicate that at least some of the harassment reached the level of formal police documentation, which is different from angry social media posts.

## Why Sanders draws this kind of attention

The reporting frames the scrutiny as a mix of factors: Sanders' celebrity, his race, and the high-profile rebuild of Colorado football. Those are the report's framing, and each piece is worth separating out.

### Celebrity

Sanders was a famous figure long before Colorado. Bringing that profile to a program that had been out of the national conversation turned the team into a nationwide spectacle. More attention means more people with strong feelings, and a small fraction of them act on those feelings in ugly ways.

### Race

The coverage lists race as part of the context around the harassment. We can't say from the available material how much of the content in the threats was racially motivated, so it's best not to guess. What the report does establish is that race is part of the conversation around his experience.

### A polarizing rebuild

Sanders is described as one of college football's most visible and polarizing figures. Polarizing figures attract both devoted fans and intense critics. That's normal in sports. Threats aren't, and they sit well outside the realm of criticism.

## The timing: a bowl push

The story arrives as Colorado fights for bowl eligibility late in the season. That matters because the pressure around the team is already high at this point in the calendar. Losses and close finishes tend to raise the temperature among fans, and a coach with Sanders' profile absorbs a lot of it.

It's hard not to wonder how much of the daily volume tracks with results, but that's our speculation, not something the reporting establishes.

## Fan criticism versus threats

If you follow college football, you've probably seen the line get blurry online. Here's a simple way to tell the difference:

- **Criticism** targets decisions: play-calling, recruiting, roster management, a fourth-down choice.
- **Harassment** targets the person, repeatedly and with intent to intimidate.
- **Threats** involve harm to the person or those around them. These are matters for police, not message boards.

Coaches are paid to take heat for results. Nobody signs up for voicemails and letters threatening their life.

## What schools owe high-profile coaches

One angle raised in the coverage is what programs owe their most visible figures in personal protection. The report describes heightened security measures around Sanders, which suggests Colorado and law enforcement have treated the threats as real enough to act on.

That raises a practical question for every program that hires a name-brand coach: security planning is part of the cost of that hire. A big personality brings revenue and attention, and it also brings risk that has to be budgeted and managed. That's our analysis, not a claim from the report.

## What to take from this

The Colorado story under Sanders is often told as a brand-building success. This reporting adds the other side of that ledger: the daily reality of a person who has become a lightning rod. When you see a coach's name trend this week, remember that criticism of a game plan is fair, and threats are not. If you see credible threats online, report them to the platform and to law enforcement rather than sharing or amplifying them.
