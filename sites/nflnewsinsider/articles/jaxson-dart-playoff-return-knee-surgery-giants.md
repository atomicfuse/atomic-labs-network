Giants quarterback Jaxson Dart had knee surgery on Sept. 25, and a surgeon now says a return in the playoffs is possible if New York gets there. That's the headline from a Fox News report that cites an OutKick interview with Dr. Drew Burdi. Here's what that means, what we don't know, and why the smart question isn't "can he?" but "should he?"

## What the Surgeon Actually Said

Per the Fox News report, Burdi told OutKick that a playoff return for Dart is possible, but risky. That's the extent of what we can responsibly say about his view. Possible is not probable, and it's certainly not a recovery timeline from the team.

A few things this coverage does not tell us:

- Whether Burdi has treated Dart. Nothing in the report indicates he did, so treat his comments as an outside medical perspective, not an official update.
- Which procedure Dart had or how it's progressing.
- What the Giants' medical staff expects.

Until the team says more, any specific return date you see online is speculation.

## Why a Playoff Comeback Is Even a Conversation

The reason this matters is the standings. According to the report, the Giants sit atop a struggling NFC East. A weak division makes a postseason berth more realistic than it would be in a stronger one, which means Dart's recovery window could overlap with January football.

That's a big "if." The Giants still have to get in. But if they do, the question shifts from hypothetical to practical.

## The Risk Side of the Ledger

Burdi's own word was risky, and it's worth taking seriously. Fans are weighing a short-term shot at a playoff run against the long-term health of a franchise quarterback. Rushing a young passer back from knee surgery can cost more than one season.

The comparison that keeps coming up is Robert Griffin III, whose name is often invoked as a warning about rushing a quarterback's recovery. That's a comparison, not a prediction: every injury and every surgery is different, and nothing here suggests Dart's situation will play out the same way.

## My Take: Ask "Should," Not "Can"

This part is opinion, not reporting. Clearing medical hurdles is one bar. Playing at a high level in a playoff game, with a knee that is still relatively new, is another. A quarterback who is available but limited can hurt a team in two ways: by playing worse than his backup would, and by risking a setback that lingers into next season.

A sensible approach would weigh a few things:

1. **Medical clearance without pressure.** The decision should rest with doctors and Dart, not the bracket.
2. **Functional readiness.** Mobility and confidence matter for a quarterback, and being cleared isn't the same as being sharp.
3. **The state of the team.** If the Giants are winning without him, there's less reason to gamble.
4. **The long view.** A franchise quarterback has many seasons of value. One playoff game, even a big one, is a small slice of that.

## What to Watch Next

Skip the hot takes and watch for real signals:

- Official injury updates from the Giants.
- Whether Dart returns to practice in any capacity.
- The NFC East standings, which determine whether this debate matters at all.

The Giants' playoff odds and Dart's recovery are tied together, but they move on separate clocks. Track the team's own updates, treat outside estimates as informed opinion, and remember that a possible return is only worth chasing if it's a safe one.
