LSU is 4-2 under Lane Kiffin, and according to a Fox News column, the bigger concern is happening off the field: the Tigers are reportedly losing top Louisiana recruits. This piece breaks down that argument, what it claims about Kiffin and Brian Kelly, and how to judge it yourself as the 2027 class develops.

A note on what this is. The argument below comes from Fox News, and it is an opinion-driven take, not a verdict. Where I add my own read, I'll say so.

## The claim: LSU isn't locking down "The Boot"

Per Fox News, Kiffin's early LSU tenure shows a pattern that echoes Brian Kelly's: shaky results paired with missed opportunities in Louisiana recruiting. The column's central worry is that LSU is failing to "lock down The Boot" during a historically strong in-state 2027 class.

The outlet reports that top Louisiana five-star recruits are being lost to Florida, Alabama, and even Nebraska. That last name is the one that stings in the argument. Losing a local star to a traditional power is one thing; losing one to a program without LSU's regional pull is another.

## Why the pressure is rising so quickly

Fox News points to the investment. LSU gave Kiffin a massive contract and entered the era with elite roster expectations. A 4-2 start would be fine for many programs, but at that price tag, the column argues, every recruiting miss gets amplified.

My take: when a school spends heavily on a coach, fans stop grading on a curve. Early recruiting news becomes a proxy for whether the hire is working.

## Why in-state recruiting still matters in the portal era

The obvious pushback is that the NIL and transfer portal era has changed roster-building. Kiffin is known for a portal-heavy, national-brand approach, and a coach can patch holes quickly that way.

Fox News argues that local high school loyalty still matters deeply to LSU's identity and long-term success. Think of it as two different ways to build a roster:

- **Portal-first:** fast, flexible, and good for filling immediate needs.
- **State-first:** slower, but it ties the program to its home talent and fanbase.

The column frames the tension as whether Kiffin's national approach fits LSU culture. That is a fair question, though not a settled one. A team can lose some in-state battles and still build a strong roster.

## The Brian Kelly comparison

The column's sharpest point is the parallel to Kelly: uneven results plus Louisiana misses. The headline asks whether Kiffin will suffer the same fate as his predecessor.

Treat that as a question, not a prediction. The report evidence offered is a 4-2 record and several high-profile recruiting losses. That is a worrying early signal for critics, but it is far from the full picture of a tenure that is only just underway. Recruiting classes take time to resolve, and commitments can change.

## How to judge this yourself

You don't have to accept the column's framing. Here is a simple way to watch it play out:

1. **Track the 2027 Louisiana class.** The argument rests on how many of the state's top prospects end up elsewhere versus in Baton Rouge.
2. **Watch who wins those battles.** Losses to Florida or Alabama are different from losses to a program like Nebraska.
3. **Compare results to recruiting.** If wins pile up, recruiting concerns usually fade. If losses mount, they sharpen.
4. **Look at the mix.** See whether the portal is filling gaps or replacing local talent altogether.

## The bottom line

Fox News makes a pointed case that Kiffin's LSU is repeating Kelly's Louisiana recruiting problem. The evidence is early, and the outcome is open. Your best move: follow where the state's top 2027 prospects sign, because that will tell you more than any column can.
