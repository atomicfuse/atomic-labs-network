A story about political unrest in France, built around comments from Louis Sarkozy, landed in our queue this week. It was reported by Fox News. It doesn't belong on a football site, so we're not going to cover it here.

This note explains why, and what you can expect from us instead.

## What We Cover

NFL News Insider exists for football readers. That means:

- Game recaps and game day analysis
- Team updates and season previews
- Trades and roster moves
- Player profiles and spotlight stories
- Draft prospect watch
- Football explainers for newer fans

A political story about France, immigration, and protests sits outside all of that. Forcing a football angle onto it would mislead you, and we'd rather be useful than loud.

## Why We're Not Stretching It

The material we have on the French story is thin. It tells us Louis Sarkozy argues that violent protests in France could intensify, and ties the unrest to far-left influence, immigration tensions, and economic strain. That is one person's argument, as reported by Fox News. We have no football connection to it, and we can't verify the broader claims ourselves. Presenting it as analysis on a football site would be padding.

## How We Handle Reporting Here

The standards we apply to football apply everywhere on the site:

### We attribute what we report
When a trade, injury, or contract figure comes from another outlet, we name that outlet. If we haven't confirmed something, we say so.

### We label rumor and opinion
Draft-night buzz and a coach's job security are classic rumor territory. You'll see words like "reportedly" on unconfirmed items, and our own takes will be marked as takes.

### We don't imply league ties
We're an independent site. We have no official NFL affiliation and no exclusive insider access, and we won't suggest otherwise.

### We keep headlines honest
The headline should promise what the article delivers. If we write about a quarterback's recovery timeline, you get the timeline, not a bait-and-switch.

## Where to Go for Non-Football News

If you want the French story, go to the original reporting from Fox News or to outlets that cover international politics full time. They can give it the context and sourcing it needs.

## What to Do Next

Browse our team previews, player spotlights, and game day breakdowns, and tell us which football topics you want explained next. That's the coverage we can do well.
