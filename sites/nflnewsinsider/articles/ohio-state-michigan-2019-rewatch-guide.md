## What you're actually watching

Per FOX, the package revisits the 2019 edition of "The Game," featuring No. 1 Ohio State against No. 13 Michigan, two ranked Big Ten powers. That ranking gap is your first lens. A No. 1 team against a No. 13 team sets up a clear question: did the underdog's talent show up, or did the favorite's advantage define the afternoon?

That's the frame to carry into the highlights. Don't just wait for the big plays; ask what each one says about the gap between the teams.

## Step 1: Sort the plays into two piles

As you watch, mentally sort each highlight into one of two categories:

- **Plays that reflect the rankings:** the moments where the top-ranked team's strength looks like what you'd expect from No. 1.
- **Plays that fight the rankings:** the moments where Michigan, ranked 13th, pushed back.

At the end, count the piles. A highlight reel flatters whoever has more highlights, so remember it's a curated cut, not the whole game. A package can't show you the quiet drives that decided things. Treat the pile count as a hint, not a verdict.

## Step 2: Watch for what the rivalry's history suggests

One angle worth testing is how the 2019 game reflected Ohio State's dominance in the rivalry. That's a framing to evaluate rather than a settled fact from the clip, so test it yourself. Ask:

- Does Ohio State look in control, or merely ahead?
- When Michigan makes a play, does it change the feel of the game, or is it answered?
- Which team looks more comfortable in the biggest moments?

Your answers will tell you more than the final score alone.

## Step 3: Run the then-vs.-now comparison

FOX's clip is resurfacing in 2026, which gives you a natural comparison point: the 2019 teams against today's Buckeyes and Wolverines. A simple way to do this without inventing stats:

1. Pick one unit, such as the offense, the defensive front or the quarterback play.
2. Note what stands out in the 2019 highlights.
3. Look at how that same unit has performed for each team this season.
4. Decide which program has changed more since 2019.

Stick to what you can see and verify. Rosters turn over completely in a few years, so the comparison is really about program identity rather than individual players.

## Step 4: Use it to settle the real debate

Per the framing around the clip, "The Game" remains one of college football's biggest annual cultural events, and rivalry-week nostalgia fuels plenty of argument. Here are three useful questions to bring to that argument, each one an opinion rather than a fact:

- Did the 2019 game shape how each program was viewed afterward?
- Is a ranked-vs-ranked rivalry game more revealing than a lopsided one?
- Does the rivalry feel different now than it did then?

Pick one, take a side and defend it with a specific play from the highlights. That's the difference between a hot take and an argument.

## Make it a watch party

If you're doing this with friends, assign each person a role: one tracks Ohio State's best plays, one tracks Michigan's, one is the skeptic who reminds everyone it's a highlight package. Pause after each big play and ask the "reflects or fights the rankings" question. It turns passive viewing into a game of its own.

## The takeaway

Before you press play, write down one prediction: which team's highlights will look better, and why. Then check it against what you see. If you were wrong, you just learned something about how this rivalry really plays out.
