## Three Takeaways for Your Own Stand Time

These are practical suggestions drawn from the nature of the story, not advice attributed to McCollum.

### 1. Sort your gear before you climb

If something can click, snap, rattle, or crinkle, deal with it on the ground. Open what you'll need, and keep small items where you can reach them without fumbling. A tin that snaps shut is a good example of gear you can handle before the hunt starts.

### 2. Decide your routine ahead of time

The safest answer to a noisy habit in the stand is a plan. If you use pouches or anything else with packaging, decide when you'll use them, and avoid doing it when a deer could be approaching. Boredom is when small habits sneak in.

### 3. Practice the draw when you're nervous, not just when you're calm

Fox News describes McCollum's draw as tense, and that tracks with anyone who has felt buck fever. Rehearse your steps: breathe, settle, pick a spot, and release. Repetition is what lets you act cleanly when your heart is pounding.
