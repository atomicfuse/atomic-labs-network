Texas beat No. 7 Ole Miss 34-27, but the way it got there should leave Longhorns fans with mixed feelings. Texas built a 24-3 lead, watched it vanish, then did enough late to hold off Trinidad Chambliss and the Rebels in a game that came down to the final play, per Fox News.

If you want one takeaway before the next two games, it's this: the second-half drop-off is starting to look like a pattern, not a one-off. Here's how to read the win.

## What Happened Against Ole Miss

The first half looked like the Texas that preseason talk promised. Arch Manning flashed, the Longhorns jumped out to a 24-3 advantage, and the game appeared to be heading toward a comfortable result.

It didn't stay that way. Per Fox News, Texas suffered another second-half meltdown, and Ole Miss climbed back into a game that ended with a final-play thriller. Fox described Steve Sarkisian's team as flipping a switch in the fourth quarter to close it out.

That last phrase is the heart of the story. A team that has to flip a switch is a team that, for stretches, had it turned off.

## Why the Pattern Matters More Than the Score

A win over a top-10 opponent is a win. Nobody in Austin is going to hand it back. But Fox's framing is that this was not an isolated slip: the Longhorns blew another big lead, with the word "another" doing a lot of work.

One collapse can be explained away by a bad drive, a turnover or a tough bounce. Repeated collapses suggest something structural, such as how the offense manages a lead or how the team responds when an opponent gets momentum. Our read, not a reported fact: when a team keeps needing a fourth-quarter rescue, the margin for error against better-prepared opponents gets very thin.

## The Arch Manning Question

Manning showed flashes against Ole Miss, according to Fox. The broader question hanging over him is whether he is progressing or simply becoming more volatile. This game doesn't settle it.

Here's a practical way to watch him over the next two weeks:

- **Early vs. late:** Does the offense look the same in the third quarter as it did in the first?
- **With a lead:** Does Texas keep attacking, or does it tighten up?
- **Under pressure:** When the opponent surges, does the offense answer or stall?

Those three checks tell you more than a single stat line. A quarterback can have highlight plays and still be part of an inconsistent offense, and Fox's piece points to offensive inconsistency as the central concern for Texas.

## Sarkisian and the Consistency Puzzle

The Longhorns have talent. Fox says as much, while calling the team dangerously inconsistent, especially on offense. That puts the spotlight on Sarkisian, who has not yet solved how to keep the offense humming for four quarters.

It's fair to ask whether the issue is play-calling, execution or both. The available reporting doesn't say which, so treat any firm answer with caution. What the scoreboard does show is that 24 points in a stretch followed by a much tighter game is not a recipe you want to rely on in the SEC.

## Ole Miss Showed a Blueprint

Even in defeat, Ole Miss may have given future opponents a template. Chambliss and the Rebels climbed out of a 21-point hole against a team Fox calls talented. The lesson for opponents: stay patient, because Texas may give you openings later.

That's our analysis rather than anything Ole Miss said, but the comeback itself is the evidence. Missouri and LSU will have watched the film.

## What Texas Needs to Fix Before Missouri and LSU

Texas now enters a tougher SEC stretch with Missouri and LSU ahead. Based on what happened Saturday, here is what to look for:

1. **A real second-half plan.** Protecting a lead shouldn't mean going passive. Watch the play-calling after halftime.
2. **Steadier offensive rhythm.** Fewer stalled drives when Texas is ahead.
3. **Fewer swings from Manning.** Flashes are good; repeatable performance is better.
4. **Finishing earlier.** Winning in the fourth quarter is fine once. Doing it every week invites an upset.

## Should Fans Feel Calm or Nervous?

Both, honestly. Beating a ranked opponent and surviving a late push shows resilience. Needing that resilience after building a 24-3 lead shows a vulnerability that better-organized teams can exploit.

The most useful move for Longhorns fans this week is to stop grading Texas by the final score and start grading it by the third quarter. If that quarter looks different against Missouri, the Ole Miss game will read as a wake-up call. If it doesn't, it will look like a warning that was ignored.
