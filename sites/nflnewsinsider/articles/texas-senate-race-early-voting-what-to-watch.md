Halfway through early voting, the contest between Democrat James Talarico and Republican Ken Paxton is a toss-up. The latest CBS News poll showed Talarico leading 51% to 49%, which is well within the margin of error. That means neither candidate can claim a real lead on polling alone.

## The Poll Is a Coin Flip, So Look at Turnout

A two-point gap inside the margin of error tells you very little. That's why the political experts featured by CBS Texas pointed to other factors: turnout, party loyalty, Latino voters, Black voter support, and heavy Republican ad spending.

The practical takeaway is to treat the poll as a snapshot of a tied race and to watch who actually shows up. Early voting is already underway, and both campaigns are working to shape turnout before Election Day.

## Party Loyalty Is the Question for Paxton

One angle CBS raised is whether Republicans will fully back their nominee. Our read, and it's an interpretation rather than a reported finding, is that this is the central tension for Paxton. If Republican voters are lukewarm, the party's traditional statewide strength in Texas may not translate into a win. If they come home, the fundamentals could still favor him.

The race may test whether Texas is becoming more competitive statewide or whether Republican fundamentals still dominate. Which of those is true will show up in how loyal each party's base proves to be.

## Latino Voters Could Reshape the Map

Experts cited by CBS Texas highlighted Latino voters as a decisive group. In a race this close, even small shifts in how that bloc votes, or whether it votes, can matter more than any persuasion effort aimed at the middle.

When you see early-voting numbers reported, look for turnout breakdowns in heavily Latino areas. That is a more useful signal than another poll.

## Black Voter Support Is a Risk for Talarico

The analysis also flagged Black voter support. The concern is for Talarico: if that support softens, a two-point polling edge could disappear quickly. This is the flip side of the Latino question. Democrats need their coalition to turn out in full, and a weak spot with one group is hard to make up elsewhere.

## Can GOP Spending Change the Race?

Republicans have put massive money into the race, with the CBS analysis citing about $200M in GOP ad spending. Spending of that size is meant to move undecided voters and motivate wavering Republicans. Whether it works is an open question, and the experts treated it as one factor among several rather than a guaranteed edge.

## How to Follow the Rest of Early Voting

- Ignore small poll swings. A 51% to 49% result is within the margin for error.
- Watch turnout reports for Latino and Black voters, plus any sign of Republican enthusiasm gaps.
- Judge the ad money by results, not by size.

The race may come down to who gets their own voters to the polls. Pay attention to turnout numbers over the next few days, because they will tell you more than the next poll.
