Fifa is expected to confirm on Monday that the United Kingdom will host the 2035 Women's World Cup, according to the BBC. It would be the first football World Cup staged in the UK since 1966, and the joint bid brings together England, Northern Ireland, Scotland and Wales.

## What is actually happening

The BBC reports that Fifa is set to confirm the UK bid, with a single tournament shared across all four home nations. The report describes a 48-team Women's World Cup, which would make it a very large event by the standards of the women's game.

Note the wording: this is an expected confirmation, not a completed one. Until Fifa makes it official, treat details as reported rather than final.

## Why the four-nation approach stands out

UK football is organized around four separate national associations, and the 1966 World Cup was an England-only affair. A joint bid means Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland are part of the story too, not just England. The BBC says the proposal covers venues and the wider impact on women's football across all four nations.

For fans in smaller nations, that could mean a World Cup within reach of home rather than a distant spectacle. How matches are distributed will matter, and you should check the confirmed venue list once Fifa publishes it rather than rely on early speculation.

## The unresolved issues

- **Tax:** Governing bodies like Fifa typically seek financial guarantees from hosts. How the UK handles those requests affects the cost to the public and the terms of the tournament.
- **Security:** Staging matches in multiple cities and nations requires coordination between different authorities.
