Week 8 of college football follows what The New York Times called one of the wildest upset Saturdays in the sport's history. That makes this a good week to ask one question about every ranked matchup: which favorite is most vulnerable? Here are all 10 games on the Times' Week 8 list, ranked from No. 10 to No. 1, with a chaos-watch read on each.

A note on sourcing: the ranking comes from The New York Times. The notes on what to watch are my own take, built on the matchups and rankings listed there.

## The Short Version: Where Chaos Is Most Likely

The Times argues that even a seemingly weaker slate can deliver surprises after a weekend like the last one. Teams with the most to lose are the ones ranked and traveling, or ranked and facing a hungry opponent. Texas, Alabama and USC are the names the Times flags as having the most to lose, since a loss could reshape the national conversation as conference races and College Football Playoff positioning tighten.

## The Bottom Half: 10 Through 6

### 10. North Dakota State at New Mexico
The list's outlier, an FCS power visiting an FBS team. Games like this are rarely on a top-10 list, which tells you how the Times views the week's depth.

### 9. Texas A&M at No. 13 Alabama
The Times frames this as a must-win type of game for Alabama. My read: a ranked home team with its playoff case in play is exactly the kind of favorite worth watching after a week of upsets.

### 8. No. 8 Texas Tech at Cincinnati
A top-10 team on the road. Texas Tech is the highest-ranked team in this half of the list outside of BYU, which makes it a classic trap-game candidate.

### 7. No. 18 LSU at Auburn
A ranked visitor in a rivalry-style SEC matchup. Rankings tend to matter less in this kind of game, so expect it to stay close.

### 6. No. 2 BYU at UCF
The highest-ranked team in action this week. When the No. 2 team travels, the upset potential is the story. Any slip would send ripples through the whole top of the poll.

## The Top Five

### 5. No. 11 Tennessee at South Carolina
Another ranked SEC team on the road. Tennessee's playoff hopes leave little room for a stumble.

### 4. No. 16 USC at Wisconsin
USC is one of the teams the Times singles out as facing real consequences from a loss. A cross-country trip to Wisconsin gives it a full test.

### 3. No. 6 Indiana at Michigan
The Times' description for the week asks how Indiana will respond after a rare loss. A trip to the Big House is a demanding way to find out. This is a bounce-back test, and what the Hoosiers show here will say a lot about their season.

### 2. No. 14 Houston at No. 10 Utah
The Times says this one may provide late-night drama. I see it as a Big 12 litmus test: two ranked teams, one conference race, and a result that will clarify who belongs in the playoff conversation.

### 1. No. 7 Ole Miss at No. 19 Texas
The Times' top game asks whether Texas can stop its slide. Arch Manning, coach Steve Sarkisian and Texas' playoff hopes are all under the microscope. Ole Miss arrives ranked higher than the home team, which is rare for a game in Austin.

If Texas loses, the pressure on Sarkisian and Manning will grow quickly. If Texas wins, it stabilizes a season that has been drifting.

## Which Favorite Is Most Vulnerable?

This is my opinion, not the Times' ranking. Based only on the matchups listed:

- **Ranked team on the road:** BYU at UCF, Texas Tech at Cincinnati, Tennessee at South Carolina and LSU at Auburn all fit. Road games after a chaotic week are where upsets tend to arrive.
- **Team with a lot to prove:** Texas, as the Times describes it, needs to stop a slide.
- **Team coming off a loss:** Indiana, in an environment as tough as Michigan's.

If you can only watch two games, pick Ole Miss at Texas and Houston at Utah. Both involve ranked teams, both carry playoff implications, and both are the games the Times leaned on most.

## How to Watch the Week

Start with the headline games, then keep the scoreboard open for BYU at UCF. A No. 2 team losing would be the biggest shock of the week. Check kickoff times and TV listings, since the Times notes Houston-Utah could run late.

Finally, treat rankings as a snapshot, not a promise. After last week, the safest assumption is that no favorite is safe.
