## Why a Quiet Carousel Doesn't Stay Quiet

Per the Times, the carousel has been slow so far. That's common this time of year, and it matters for one reason: the pool of candidates gets set by what happens on the field right now. Early-season success can quickly turn a rising coach into a major market candidate, as the Times frames it.

If you follow a team that might need a coach, this is the window when names start to form. Programs don't hire off one win, but a strong start puts a coach on the radar before the real discussions begin.

## What Makes a Coach "On the Rise"

The Times piece centers on strong starts to 2026. Our read, which is analysis and not reporting, is that a start only means something when you check it against a few questions.

### Did the team beat expectations?

A winning record is good. A winning record from a team projected to struggle is better. Early overachievers change the hiring market because they show what a coach did with the same roster others were writing off.

### Is the success believable?

Look at who the team played and how it won. A lopsided win over a weak opponent tells you less than a close, well-managed game against a strong one. Ask whether the results look repeatable or lucky.

### Does the team look different from last year?

An improved offense, a rebuilt defense, or a younger roster playing well all suggest the coach is the reason. That's the kind of story athletic directors and search committees tend to want to tell.

## How to Judge Coach-Program Fit

A rising coach and a job opening aren't automatically a match. When a vacancy appears, run through these checks yourself:

- **Resources:** Can the new program offer what the coach had? A coach who thrived with modest means may need more, or may do fine with less.
- **Style:** Does the coach's scheme and recruiting approach suit the roster and region?
- **Level of the jump:** Moving up a step is usually easier than skipping several.
- **Timing:** A coach in the middle of a great year may not want to leave, and a program may not want to wait.

These are our framing tools, not claims about any specific hire.

## Which Jobs May Move First

The Times says the biggest moves are still ahead, but we don't have a list of which programs will go first, and we won't invent one. What you can watch for yourself is simple: teams underperforming against expectations, fan and booster frustration reported by credible outlets, and any official announcements. Treat anonymous speculation as exactly that until a reliable source confirms it.

## How to Read Carousel Rumors

This is the part that saves you the most frustration.

1. **Check the source.** Is the report from a named, established outlet, or from social media chatter?
2. **Look for confirmation.** One report is a data point. Several independent ones are a trend.
3. **Separate interest from offers.** A coach being mentioned for a job doesn't mean he's been contacted.
4. **Wait for the season to play out.** A coach who's hot in October can cool by November.

## The Takeaway

When you see a list of rising coaches, ask what each one beat, who they played, and what changed from last year. Then wait for confirmed reports before treating any name as a real candidate. That habit will serve you better than any single ranking, including the Times' 20, which is worth reading in full for the names themselves.
