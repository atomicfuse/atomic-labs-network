Indiana's first Bodybar Pilates studio is set to open in Evansville on Dec. 14, per Inside Indiana Business. If you're thinking about trying reformer classes, here's what's known about the studio, and how to eat around a Pilates session so you feel good during it and after it.

The food advice below is general nutrition common sense and my own take, not information from the studio or the report.

## Why Reformer Pilates Pairs Well With a Food-First Mindset

The broader coverage frames Pilates as part of rising demand for low-impact strength, mobility, and recovery-focused workouts. That matters if you care about nutrition, because the fueling needs differ from a heavy lifting day or a long run. Reformer work is controlled and core-focused. You usually don't need a huge meal, but you do want steady energy and a calm stomach.

That's my read, not a claim from the report. Individual needs vary, so adjust to how you feel.

## Before Class: Keep It Light and Simple

Lots of Pilates movements involve rolling, twisting, and lying flat. A heavy meal right beforehand can make that uncomfortable. A practical approach:

### If class is 1 to 2 hours away

Have a small, balanced snack. Paleo-friendly options include:

- A banana with a spoonful of almond butter
- A hard-boiled egg and a handful of grapes
- Apple slices with a few walnuts

### If class is 3 or more hours away

A normal meal works. Think grilled chicken or salmon, roasted sweet potato, and a side of sautéed greens. Go easy on very large portions and extra-greasy sauces.

### If you're going first thing in the morning

Some people do fine on water alone. Others feel better with something tiny, like half a banana or a few dates. Try it on a day with no plans before you test it on class day.

## After Class: Protein, Produce, and Water

After a session, aim for a meal or snack with protein plus some carbohydrate from whole foods. Easy ideas:

- Scrambled eggs with spinach and roasted potatoes
- A big salad with sliced turkey, avocado, and olive oil
- Leftover roasted chicken with fruit
- Coconut yogurt (if you tolerate it) topped with berries and seeds

Drink water throughout the day. Studios are often warm once you get moving, and it's easy to underestimate how much you sweat.

## A Quick Grab-and-Go Snack Kit

If you're heading to class straight from work, keep a small bag stocked:

1. Single-serve nut or seed packs
2. A piece of whole fruit
3. Jerky made without added sugar (check the label)
4. Dates, which pack easily and travel well

Prep on Sunday and you won't be tempted by whatever's in the vending machine.

## What to Do Before Your First Class

Since the studio opens Dec. 14, you have time to prepare:

1. Check the studio's website or social channels for class schedules and sign-up details.
2. Ask about a beginner session, since reformer equipment can feel unfamiliar at first.
3. Test your pre-class snack on a normal day this week.
4. Plan your post-class meal in advance so you're not choosing while hungry.

## The Takeaway

A new studio is a good reason to build a simple fueling routine: a light whole-food snack before, a protein-rich meal after, and water all day. Pick your snack now, try it this week, and walk into that first reformer class feeling steady instead of starving.
