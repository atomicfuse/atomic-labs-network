## What coins can tell researchers

Researchers believe the coins could shed light on ancient Celtic trade networks and communities across Europe, according to the report. Here is my own analysis of why even 11 coins matter, and it is a general point rather than a claim about this specific hoard.

Coins are portable evidence of economic activity. Where a coin was made, where it was buried and what else lies near it can help map who was dealing with whom. A single hoard is one data point. Several hoards in a region begin to form a picture.

That is why a find described as the largest of its kind locally matters. It adds to ongoing efforts, noted by Fox News, to map Europe's pre-Roman Celtic economy and cultural connections.

## The food and drink angle

Trade networks are, in practice, supply chains. Anything people exchanged, including grain, salt, livestock, honey or drink, depended on systems of exchange like the ones coins hint at. The reporting does not say what this community traded, so treat this as context, not a finding. What it does show is that people in this region were part of a wider economy before Rome's influence arrived.
