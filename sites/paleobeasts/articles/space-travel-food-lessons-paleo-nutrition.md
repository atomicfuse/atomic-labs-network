## Start with the common ground, not the label

Diet debates get tribal fast. Paleo, vegetarian, keto and the rest often become identity badges. Here's the move that cuts through it: ignore the label and look at what nearly every camp agrees on.

- Eat more vegetables.
- Choose whole foods over heavily processed ones.
- Get enough protein.
- Cut back on sugary drinks.

A paleo approach, which centers on meat, fish, eggs, vegetables, fruit, nuts, and seeds while skipping grains, dairy, and legumes, already fits inside that list. You can use it as a framework without treating it as a creed.

## A simple paleo meal plan template

Planning is what makes a diet survivable. Use a repeatable structure instead of inventing every meal.

### Pick your building blocks

Choose one item from each column for lunch and dinner:

1. **Protein:** chicken thighs, salmon, ground beef, eggs, shrimp.
2. **Vegetable:** broccoli, zucchini, spinach, peppers, sweet potato.
3. **Fat:** olive oil, avocado, nuts, coconut oil.
4. **Flavor:** lemon, garlic, herbs, salsa, mustard.

### Cook in batches

On one afternoon, roast two trays of vegetables, cook a big batch of protein, and boil a half dozen eggs. Mix and match through the week. That gives you around three to four days of lunches with minimal effort, and you can cook a second smaller batch midweek.

### Keep breakfast boring

Eggs with sautéed greens and half an avocado is a fine default. Decision fatigue is the enemy of consistency, so repeat what works.

## A healthy snack system that actually holds up

Snacks are where plans fall apart. Build a small kit so the right choice is the easy one.

- **Portable protein:** hard-boiled eggs, jerky with no added sugar (check the label), or leftover chicken.
- **Crunch:** raw almonds, walnuts, or pumpkin seeds in small containers.
- **Fresh:** apple slices, berries, carrot sticks, cucumber.
- **Pair them:** an apple with almond butter or celery with guacamole balances sweet, fat, and fiber.

Pre-portion nuts into small containers. They are calorie-dense, and a handful is easier to manage than a bag.

## Nutrition checks worth making

Cutting grains and dairy removes some usual sources of certain nutrients, so it's worth thinking about what replaces them.

- **Calcium:** leafy greens, canned fish with bones, and almonds can help fill the gap.
- **Fiber:** lean on vegetables, fruit, nuts, and seeds.
- **Variety:** rotate proteins and colors of produce rather than eating the same three meals forever.

If you have a medical condition, are pregnant, or take medication, talk to a doctor or registered dietitian before making major changes.

## Keep the shared-goal mindset

The editorial's larger message is that some achievements are better when people stop sorting them into camps. Food works the same way. You don't need to win an argument about the perfect diet to eat well, and you don't need to convert anyone at the table.

This week, do one thing: pick three building-block combinations, batch-cook them on a single afternoon, and pack a snack kit for your bag. Then see how the week feels.
