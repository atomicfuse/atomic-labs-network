A new boutique fitness studio is headed to Anne Arundel County, and if you plan to sign up, what you eat around class will shape how good you feel in it. Here's the news, then a practical way to fuel for a Pilates, strength and cardio hybrid workout.

## The News: STRONG Pilates Opens Near Waugh Chapel

Per Eyeonannapolis, STRONG Pilates is opening a new 3,000-square-foot studio in Gambrills, near Waugh Chapel. It's planned for Hopkins Business Center and marks the brand's expansion into Anne Arundel County. The concept combines Pilates, strength training and cardio. Everything below is my take on general fueling, not guidance from STRONG Pilates. It's a starting point, and a doctor or registered dietitian can tailor it to your needs.

## Why a Hybrid Workout Changes How You Eat

A class that blends strength work with cardio asks for more than a pure stretch session would. That makes it reasonable to think about two things: having enough energy to get through class, and giving your body protein afterward. You don't need a complicated plan. You need a few reliable go-tos.

## Before Class: Keep It Light and Familiar

Most people feel best with something small, eaten with enough lead time that it's settled by the time they're on the machine. Core-heavy movement and a full stomach don't mix well.

### If class is within 2 to 3 hours
A small, balanced meal works: eggs with sweet potato, or chicken with roasted vegetables and a little avocado.

### If class is within an hour
Go small and simple. Good options include:

- A banana with a spoonful of almond butter
- A couple of dates with a handful of nuts
- Apple slices with a little sunflower seed butter

Try new foods on a non-class day first. Class morning is a bad time for experiments.

## After Class: Protein Plus Something Real

After a strength-focused session, aim for a meal or snack with protein and some carbohydrates from whole foods. A few easy ideas:

- Hard-boiled eggs and fruit
- Sliced turkey rolled around avocado, with berries on the side
- A salmon and sweet potato plate
- Chicken salad in lettuce cups with grapes

If you follow a paleo-style approach, all of these fit without any workarounds.

## Healthy Snacks to Keep in Your Gym Bag

Opening a bag and finding something sensible beats grabbing whatever's near the drive home. Pack shelf-stable items:

- Mixed nuts and dried fruit (portion them into small containers)
- Unsweetened meat sticks, if you like them
- Individually packed nut butter
- A whole piece of fruit that travels well, like a banana or an orange

Check labels if you're avoiding added sugars or particular ingredients, since packaged snacks vary widely.

## Don't Forget Water

Cardio and strength intervals mean sweat. Drink water through the day, not just in the minutes before class. Bring a bottle and sip after.

## A Simple Weekly Template

If you're planning meals around a few classes a week, keep it easy:

1. **Batch-cook one protein** on Sunday, such as shredded chicken or baked salmon.
2. **Roast a tray of vegetables and sweet potatoes** to reuse across several meals.
3. **Prep two snack options** you can grab on class days.
4. **Decide your post-class meal in advance**, so it doesn't turn into a decision on a tired evening.

## Your Next Step

When the Gambrills studio opens, don't overhaul your whole diet for it. Pick one pre-class snack and one post-class meal from the lists above, try them over your first few sessions, and keep whatever leaves you feeling steady.
