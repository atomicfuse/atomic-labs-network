## The Idea: Flavor Comes From Technique, Not Price

Cheap ingredients taste cheap when they're boiled, dumped, and served. They taste good when you add three things: browning, acid, and texture. A can of white beans and a box of pasta can deliver all three.

## Creamy White Bean Pasta

**Serves:** 4 | **Prep:** 10 minutes | **Cook:** 20 minutes

### What you need

- 8 oz short pasta (shells, rotini, or penne)
- 1 can (15 oz) white beans, such as cannellini or great northern, drained and rinsed
- 3 cloves garlic, sliced (or 1 teaspoon garlic powder)
- 2 tablespoons olive oil
- 1/2 teaspoon salt, plus more to taste
- Black pepper
- 1 lemon, or 1 tablespoon vinegar
- Optional: red pepper flakes, a handful of spinach, grated hard cheese

### Steps

1. **Boil the pasta** in well-salted water. Before draining, scoop out a mug of the starchy cooking water. This is your sauce base.
2. **Warm the oil** in a large skillet over medium heat. Add the garlic and cook 1 to 2 minutes, until it smells sweet and turns pale gold. If it goes dark brown, it's bitter, so start over or pull it sooner next time.
3. **Add the beans** and mash about half of them with a fork or the back of a spoon. Leave the rest whole.
4. **Pour in 1/2 cup of the pasta water** and stir. Simmer 3 to 4 minutes until the liquid turns creamy and slightly thick.
5. **Add the drained pasta** and toss for a minute, adding splashes of pasta water until the sauce coats the noodles without pooling.
6. **Finish with acid.** Squeeze in lemon juice, taste, and add salt and pepper. This last step is what keeps the dish from tasting flat.

**Doneness cue:** The sauce should cling to the pasta like a loose cream sauce. If it looks tight or gluey, add more water.

### Substitutions

- No white beans? Chickpeas or any mild canned bean works.
- No fresh garlic? Garlic powder goes in with the beans instead.
- No lemon? Use a teaspoon or two of any vinegar.
- Stir in frozen spinach or leftover vegetables to stretch it further.

## The Crispy Breadcrumb Topping

This is the cheapest upgrade in the kitchen, and it turns a soft, creamy dish into one with contrast.

**Makes:** enough for 4 servings | **Time:** 5 minutes

1. Heat 2 tablespoons olive oil in a small skillet over medium heat.
2. Add 1/2 cup breadcrumbs (or crumbled stale bread, pulsed or torn fine) and a pinch of salt.
3. Stir constantly for 3 to 4 minutes, until deep golden and fragrant.
4. Pour onto a plate right away, since the pan's residual heat will keep cooking them.

**The step that makes the difference:** Keep stirring. Breadcrumbs go from pale to burnt fast, and uneven browning is the usual reason they come out half-soggy. Toast them separately and add them at the table so they stay crunchy.

## Three Moves That Make Pantry Staples Taste Better

### Brown something

Garlic, onion, tomato paste, even the edges of canned beans in a hot pan all add depth. Give ingredients a minute or two without stirring so they actually color.

### Add acid at the end

Lemon, vinegar, or a spoonful of pickle brine wakes up a dish that tastes dull. Add a little, taste, add more if needed.

### Keep the cooking water

Starchy pasta or rice water thickens sauces and stretches them without cream. It's free, and it's usually poured down the drain.

## Make It Stretch Across the Week

- **Night one:** the pasta as written.
- **Night two:** reheat leftovers with a splash of water and top with a fried egg or a can of drained tinned fish.
- **Snack:** roast any extra beans on a sheet pan at 425°F for 20 to 25 minutes after patting them completely dry. Dry beans crisp; wet ones steam.

Buy dried or canned beans, pasta, and bread ends in quantity, since they keep well and cover several different meals.
