Canned food has an undeserved reputation as the lesser option. Health magazine recently rounded up six canned foods it calls surprisingly good for you, pointing to them as shelf-stable, cost-effective, and convenient. The four the write-up names are lentils, beans, sardines, and fruit. This article sticks to those four, because they're the ones I can back up, and shows you how to turn each into dinner or a snack with what's already on your shelf.

## Why canned is worth a second look

Per Health, canned foods are shelf-stable, cost-effective, and convenient, and the piece argues that lentils, beans, sardines, and fruit can support healthy eating without costing you ease or money. That matters when you want a real meal in 20 minutes and don't want to waste fresh produce that wilts before you get to it.

A can in the cupboard doesn't spoil on Wednesday while you order takeout. That's most of the appeal.

## Read the label before you buy

The one habit that makes canned food better is checking the label for two things: sodium and added sugar. This is the main caveat on canned goods generally.

- **Beans and lentils:** Look for versions with a short ingredient list. If the can is salty, drain and rinse in a colander for 30 seconds. Rinsing is a common kitchen move to wash off some of the salty canning liquid.
- **Fruit:** Choose cans packed in water or juice rather than heavy syrup, so you aren't adding sugar you didn't plan on.
- **Sardines:** Pick whichever packing you'll actually eat, and taste the first can before you buy a case.

## Canned beans: crisp them up

Beans are the most flexible can on the shelf, and they're the base of our house specialty: crispy chickpeas.

### Crispy Smoked Paprika Chickpeas

**Serves:** 2 as a snack | **Prep:** 5 minutes | **Cook:** 25 to 30 minutes at 425°F

- 1 can (15 oz) chickpeas
- 1 tablespoon olive oil
- 1/2 teaspoon smoked paprika
- 1/4 teaspoon garlic powder
- Salt to taste

1. Heat the oven to 425°F.
2. Drain and rinse the chickpeas, then spread them on a clean towel and pat very dry. Let them sit 5 minutes. **This is the step that makes the difference.** Wet chickpeas steam instead of crisp.
3. Toss with oil and spices on a sheet pan, in a single layer.
4. Roast 25 to 30 minutes, shaking the pan halfway, until they're deep golden and rattle when you shake the pan.
5. Salt while hot. They crisp further as they cool.

**Swaps:** Any canned white bean works, though smaller beans cook up a bit denser. No smoked paprika? Use chili powder or cumin. For an air fryer, cook at 390°F for 12 to 15 minutes, shaking once.

## Canned lentils: dinner in one pan

Lentils skip the simmering time of dried ones, so they're a good fit for a fast weeknight skillet.

### Quick Lentil Rice Skillet

**Serves:** 3 to 4 | **Prep:** 5 minutes | **Cook:** 15 minutes

1. Soften a chopped onion in a tablespoon of oil, 4 to 5 minutes, until translucent.
2. Add a teaspoon of cumin and a clove of minced garlic for 30 seconds.
3. Stir in a drained, rinsed can of lentils and about 2 cups cooked rice, with a splash of water.
4. Cook until heated through and the edges of the rice start to brown in places.
5. Finish with lemon juice or a spoonful of yogurt.

**Doneness cue:** the pan should look glossy, not soupy. If it's wet, keep cooking a minute or two uncovered.

**Crunch topping:** toast a handful of breadcrumbs in a dry skillet with a little oil until golden, about 3 minutes, and scatter over the top.

## Canned sardines: the 10-minute option

Sardines are the item most people skip, and the one most worth trying. Health includes them on its list of surprisingly good canned foods.

### Sardine Toast with Lemon

**Serves:** 1 to 2 | **Prep:** 5 minutes | **Cook:** 3 minutes

1. Toast two thick slices of bread until properly crisp.
2. Mash a few sardines with a squeeze of lemon, black pepper, and a little mustard.
3. Spread on the toast and top with thin-sliced onion or chopped parsley.

**Swaps:** Stir the mash into hot pasta with olive oil and garlic for a faster dinner, or into rice for a bowl in the style of a tuna rice bowl. If the flavor is too strong at first, more lemon and mustard soften it.

## Canned fruit: simple baking and snacks

Fruit in water or juice keeps for months and is ready when fresh fruit isn't.

- Spoon drained fruit over plain yogurt or oatmeal.
- Chop it into the batter for oat cookies or muffins, patting it dry first so it doesn't make the dough wet.
- Warm it in a small pan with cinnamon and spoon it over toast or pancakes.

Always drain well. Extra liquid is the usual reason baked goods turn out gummy.

## What to keep on the shelf

A workable starter stock is two cans of beans, one or two of lentils, a few sardines, and a couple of fruit cans in water or juice. Add rice, pasta, and a bottle of olive oil and you have several dinners and a snack without a store run.

This week, pick one can you've been ignoring, check the label, and cook it using one of the recipes above.
