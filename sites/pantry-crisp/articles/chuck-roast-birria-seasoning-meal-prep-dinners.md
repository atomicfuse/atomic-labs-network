## Why one big batch beats five small dinners

Birria-style meat is a good meal-prep candidate because it's a single cooking job that feeds many formats. You season and cook the beef once, then change the vehicle: a tortilla, a bowl of noodles, a pile of chips. Each meal feels different even though the work was done on Sunday.

That's the real appeal of a seasoning bomb. Per BuzzFeed's roundup, the draw is skipping the long spice list. Follow the package directions for how much to use per pound of meat, since I can't verify the amounts.

## The batch: one chuck roast, cooked once

**Serves:** 6 to 8 across several meals
**Prep:** about 15 minutes
**Cook time:** depends on method; check the package and your appliance

1. **Cut and season.** Trim the chuck roast of any large hard fat and cut it into big chunks, roughly 2 to 3 inches. Pat them dry and season with the birria bomb as directed.
2. **Cook low and wet.** Use a slow cooker, pressure cooker, or a covered Dutch oven in the oven. Chuck is a tough, collagen-rich cut, so it needs long, moist cooking.
3. **Check doneness by feel.** The meat is ready when a fork twists into it with almost no resistance and it shreds easily. If it fights back, it needs more time, not more heat.
4. **Shred and save the liquid.** Return the shredded beef to its cooking juices. That liquid keeps leftovers moist and doubles as a dipping broth.

Substitution: no chuck roast? Beef short ribs or brisket work on the same principle, though timing will differ.

## Four ways to use it through the week

### Crispy tacos (the pantry-crisp move)

Dip tortillas in the surface fat or juices from the pot, then pan-fry them in a hot skillet with a handful of shredded beef and cheese folded inside. Cook about 2 minutes per side, until the tortilla is browned and crackly at the edges. The step that makes the difference: let the tortilla drain for a few seconds before it hits the pan, so it crisps instead of steaming.

### Loaded nachos on a sheet pan

Spread tortilla chips on a sheet pan, scatter beef, canned black beans (drained and rinsed), and shredded cheese. Bake at 400°F for 5 to 8 minutes, until the cheese is fully melted and bubbling at the edges. Layer in two rounds rather than piling everything on top, so the chips underneath get some topping too.

### Quick ramen upgrade

Warm a few spoonfuls of shredded beef in a splash of the cooking juices, then spoon it over cooked instant ramen noodles. Thin the juices with a little water or broth if they taste too intense. A squeeze of lime and chopped onion help if you have them.

### Quesadillas for the busiest night

Fill a tortilla with beef and cheese, then cook in a dry skillet over medium heat for 2 to 3 minutes per side. It's done when the cheese is melted through and the outside is golden and firm.

## Storing and reheating

- Cool the beef quickly and refrigerate it in its juices in an airtight container.
- Freeze portions in flat bags if you won't eat it within a few days.
- Reheat gently in a covered pan with a splash of liquid so the shreds don't dry out.

## Is a seasoning bomb worth buying?

It depends on how you cook. If you already keep dried chiles, cumin, oregano, and garlic powder on hand, you may not need it. If you don't, a single product that stands in for the whole list can be a reasonable trade, especially for a dish you'd only make occasionally. Check the ingredient list for sodium and any allergens, and compare the price per batch with what you'd spend on individual spices.

## The takeaway

Cook one big batch of something well-seasoned on the weekend, and let tortillas, chips, noodles, and canned beans do the variety work during the week.
