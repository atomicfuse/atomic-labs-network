Longevity advice usually comes with a price tag: powders, supplements, gadgets. A recent Fox News report offers something cheaper. A Florida functional medicine doctor says he consumes three foods daily to support healthy aging, and you may already own two of them: coffee, 100% cacao and raw honey.

Below, you'll get what the doctor reportedly says about each one, where the evidence stops and opinion starts, and a simple morning mug that uses all three without turning into a dessert.

## What the doctor says about each food

Per Fox News, the physician points to antioxidants and polyphenols as the common thread. The report says these foods may offer antioxidant, cardiovascular, immune and gut-health benefits when they're part of a sustainable diet. Here is the trio:

### Coffee

The first pick is the easiest to fit into a routine, since many people already drink it. The doctor's case rests on coffee's antioxidant and polyphenol content. Nothing in the report suggests that more is better, so treat this as a reason to keep your usual cup, not to add a fourth.

### 100% cacao

The key word is "100%." That means unsweetened cacao, not a sugary hot chocolate mix or a milk chocolate bar. It has a bitter, deep flavor, which is why it pairs well with a little sweetener. Look for it in the baking aisle as cacao or unsweetened cocoa powder, and check the label to be sure nothing else is in the tin.

### Raw honey

The third pick is raw honey, which the report ties to immune and gut-health support. Honey is still sugar, so this is where portions matter. A teaspoon in a mug is a very different thing from a drizzle that never ends.

## What's research and what's opinion

This is a single doctor describing his own daily habits, reported by Fox News. That makes it a useful prompt, not proof. Antioxidants and polyphenols are real compounds found in plant foods, but a doctor saying he eats something daily doesn't show that those foods will make you live longer. The word "may" in the report's claims is doing real work.

A sensible reading: these are enjoyable foods with plausible upsides, best treated as part of an overall diet that's heavy on beans, lentils, whole grains and vegetables. If you have a health condition, are pregnant, or are sensitive to caffeine, check with your own doctor before changing your routine. Honey also isn't appropriate for babies under one year old.

## A simple morning mocha with all three

This is my own pantry-style way to use the trio, not a recipe from the report.

**Serves:** 1  
**Prep:** 3 minutes  
**Cook:** 2 minutes

**You'll need:**
- 1 cup hot brewed coffee
- 1 teaspoon unsweetened 100% cacao or cocoa powder
- 1 teaspoon raw honey, plus more to taste
- 2 tablespoons milk or a dairy-free alternative (optional)

**Steps:**
1. Put the cacao and honey in your mug first.
2. Pour in about 2 tablespoons of the hot coffee and stir hard into a thick paste. This is the step that matters: cacao clumps if you dump it into a full mug, and the paste dissolves it fully.
3. Add the rest of the coffee and stir again.
4. Add milk if you like. Taste, then add honey a quarter teaspoon at a time.

**Doneness cue:** no dry specks floating on top and no gritty layer at the bottom when you reach the last sip.

**Substitutions:** Use decaf if you're limiting caffeine. Maple syrup works if you don't have raw honey. A pinch of cinnamon softens the bitterness of the cacao.

## Keeping sugar and caffeine in check

- **Measure the honey.** Use a measuring spoon for the first week so you know what a teaspoon looks like.
- **Start with less cacao sweetener than you think.** Your palate adjusts quickly to bitter flavors.
- **Mind the clock.** If coffee affects your sleep, make this a morning drink and switch to decaf after lunch.
- **Don't let the mug replace breakfast.** Pair it with oatmeal, toast with peanut butter, or eggs so the day starts with real food.

## Make it part of a bigger pantry habit

The most useful idea in this story isn't any single food. It's that a small daily habit built from ordinary ingredients is easier to keep than a complicated routine. Make the mocha for a week, measure the honey, and see whether it earns a permanent place in your mornings.
