Boiling is the way many of us learned to cook broccoli, and it may be the worst way to treat it. Per Fox News, experts warn that boiling drains vitamin C and glucoraphanin, a compound linked to broccoli's much-discussed health benefits. The fixes are small: a different cooking method, a short wait after chopping, and a pantry spice you may already own.

Here is how to put that into practice tonight, with a 10-minute side dish at the end.

## Why boiling is the method to skip

Fox News reports that boiling broccoli drains vitamin C and glucoraphanin. Prolonged high heat can also reduce the benefits of these compounds. Glucoraphanin matters because it relates to sulforaphane, the compound often credited with broccoli's antioxidant and anti-inflammatory reputation.

The practical takeaway: keep broccoli's cooking time short and skip the big pot of water. If you do boil it for a soup, the nutrients that leach out are not entirely lost when you drink the broth, though that is my reading rather than a point from the report.

## Try the "chop and wait" trick

The report's most useful tip costs nothing. Chopping the florets 30 to 40 minutes before cooking may help preserve the cancer-fighting compounds, according to the experts cited.

To use it:

1. Cut the broccoli into florets before you do anything else for dinner.
2. Leave them on the cutting board while you boil pasta, rinse rice, or open a can of beans.
3. Cook after 30 to 40 minutes.

It is easy to fit in. Most pantry dinners have a stretch of waiting built in, so the broccoli can sit while the rest comes together.

## Steam or microwave, briefly

The experts quoted say steaming and quick microwaving may help preserve broccoli's benefits. Both use little water and short times.

- **Steaming:** Put florets in a steamer basket over simmering water, cover, and cook until bright green and just tender when pierced with a fork. Start checking at about 4 minutes.
- **Microwaving:** Put florets in a covered bowl with a spoonful or two of water and heat in short bursts. Stop when they are bright green and a fork slides in with a little resistance.

The doneness cue is the same for both: color and bite. Vivid green with a slight snap is the target. Olive-drab and soft means you went too long.

## Add a pinch of mustard powder

Mustard powder is listed among the simple ways to help preserve broccoli's benefits. It is a cheap pantry staple, and it tastes good on broccoli, which is a bonus.

Sprinkle a small pinch over the florets after cooking, or whisk it into a dressing. Start with about 1/4 teaspoon for a head of broccoli and adjust to taste. It adds a gentle tang, so it works well with lemon, olive oil, or a little garlic.

## Don't toss the stalk

The stalk is part of the broccoli, and it is easy to cook. Peel the tough outer skin, slice the stalk into thin coins or matchsticks, and cook it with the florets. Because it is denser, give it a minute or two of head start in the steamer.

## A 10-minute broccoli side with mustard

**Serves:** 2 to 3 | **Prep:** 10 minutes, plus 30 to 40 minutes resting | **Cook:** 4 to 6 minutes

**You need:**
- 1 medium head of broccoli, florets and peeled stalk
- 1 tablespoon olive oil
- 1 tablespoon lemon juice
- 1/4 teaspoon mustard powder
- Salt and pepper

**Steps:**

1. Chop the florets and slice the peeled stalk. Let them rest 30 to 40 minutes.
2. Steam the stalk slices for 1 to 2 minutes, then add the florets and steam until bright green and fork-tender, about 3 to 4 more minutes.
3. Drain well. Wet broccoli dilutes the dressing, so shake off the water.
4. Whisk the oil, lemon juice, mustard powder, salt, and pepper. Toss with the broccoli and serve warm.

**Substitutions:** Swap lemon juice for any vinegar you have. Use a spoonful of prepared mustard if you have no powder, though the report specifically mentions mustard powder, so treat that as a flavor swap only. Toss in canned white beans or serve over rice to make it a meal.

## Want it crispy instead?

Since crunch is what we do here, know that roasting or air-frying is a tasty option. The report's cautions are about boiling and prolonged high heat, so keep roasting short, pull the pan when the edges are browned but the stems still have bite, and dry the florets well first so they crisp instead of steam. If nutrient retention is your priority, steaming is the safer choice per the experts quoted.

## The one habit to start tonight

Chop your broccoli before you start anything else for dinner, and let it sit while you cook. It takes no extra effort, and it is the simplest step in the whole list.
