Country singer and author Jessie James Decker has a new cookbook, "The Secret Sauce," and a kitchen lesson she says she learned the hard way: the emotions you bring into the kitchen shape the food you make. That's per Fox News, which reports the book centers on food as a reflection of care, mood, and connection.

The outlet's coverage doesn't include her recipes, so I won't pretend to share them. What it does give us is a useful idea to test on a regular Tuesday: cook with intention. Below is a pantry dinner built to make that easy, with a crisp payoff that rewards slowing down for a few specific minutes.

## What "cook with intention" can mean on a weeknight

Decker's point, as Fox News reports it, is about mood and care. Here's my practical read on it: rushed, stressed cooking tends to skip the small steps that make food good. Calm cooking doesn't need more time. It needs a few deliberate pauses.

Try these before you start:

- **Read the whole recipe first.** Surprises are what make a kitchen feel frantic.
- **Set out everything you need.** Cans opened, spices measured, pan on the stove.
- **Pick one step to do carefully.** In the recipe below, it's drying the chickpeas.

That's it. No ritual, no candles. Just fewer reasons to be annoyed at your dinner.

## Crispy Chickpea and Rice Bowls

**Serves:** 4 
**Prep:** 10 minutes 
**Cook:** 25 minutes

### Ingredients

- 2 cans (15 oz each) chickpeas
- 2 tablespoons olive oil, divided
- 1 teaspoon smoked paprika
- 1/2 teaspoon garlic powder
- 3/4 teaspoon salt, divided
- 1 1/2 cups long-grain white rice (or about 4 cups cooked leftover rice)
- 1 can (15 oz) black beans, drained and warmed
- Lime or lemon wedges
- Optional toppings: yogurt, hot sauce, sliced onion, chopped cilantro

### Step 1: Dry the chickpeas (this is the step that matters)

Heat the oven to 425°F. Drain and rinse the chickpeas, then spread them on a clean kitchen towel. Pat them dry, then let them sit for a few minutes while you start the rice. Rub off any loose skins if they slip off easily; don't hunt for every one.

Wet chickpeas steam instead of roast. Dry ones crisp. Skipping this is the most common reason roasted chickpeas turn out chewy.

### Step 2: Season and roast

Toss the dry chickpeas on a sheet pan with 1 tablespoon oil, the paprika, garlic powder, and 1/2 teaspoon salt. Spread them in a single layer with space between them. Crowding brings the steam back.

Roast 20 to 25 minutes, shaking the pan once at about the 12-minute mark.

**Doneness cues:** they're deeply golden, they rattle slightly when you shake the pan, and a cooled one has a crunchy shell with a tender center. They crisp further as they cool, so don't overbake them chasing crunch straight out of the oven.

### Step 3: Cook the rice

While the chickpeas roast, cook the rice according to package directions, adding the remaining 1/4 teaspoon salt and 1 tablespoon oil to the pot. Fluff with a fork when done. If you're using leftover rice, warm it in a skillet with a splash of water.

### Step 4: Build the bowls

Divide the rice among four bowls. Add warm black beans and a pile of crispy chickpeas. Finish with citrus and whatever toppings you have. Add the chickpeas last so they stay crunchy.

## Easy substitutions

- **No smoked paprika:** use regular paprika plus a pinch of cumin, or chili powder.
- **No chickpeas:** drained, dried white beans also roast, though they crisp less dramatically.
- **Different grain:** pasta, quinoa, or couscous all work under the same toppings.
- **Air fryer:** cook the dried, seasoned chickpeas at 390°F for 10 to 14 minutes, shaking the basket halfway. Work in batches if they don't fit in one layer.
- **Stovetop:** pan-fry dried chickpeas in 1 to 2 tablespoons of oil over medium-high heat for 8 to 10 minutes, stirring often, and season at the end.

## Make leftovers work for you

Store the crispy chickpeas separately from the rice, loosely covered at room temperature, and eat them within a day. Airtight containers trap moisture and soften them. If they lose their snap, a few minutes in a hot oven brings most of it back.
