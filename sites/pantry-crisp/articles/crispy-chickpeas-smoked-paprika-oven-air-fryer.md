One can of chickpeas can become a crunchy, salty snack in about 30 minutes. The difference between crispy and chewy comes down to one step: getting the chickpeas dry before they see any heat. Here's the full method for the oven and the air fryer, plus how to tell when they're done and what to do when they go soft.

**Makes:** about 1½ cups (2 to 4 snack servings)
**Prep:** 10 minutes
**Cook:** 25 to 30 minutes in the oven, 12 to 15 in the air fryer

## What You Need

- 1 can (15 oz) chickpeas
- 1 tablespoon olive oil or any neutral oil
- ½ teaspoon salt
- 1 teaspoon smoked paprika
- ½ teaspoon garlic powder
- Pinch of black pepper or cayenne, optional

That's it. If your pantry has chickpeas and a jar of paprika, you can make this tonight.

## The Step That Makes the Difference: Dry Them Properly

Water is the enemy of crunch. Chickpeas straight from the can are wet inside and out, and surface moisture turns to steam in the oven, which softens them instead of crisping them.

1. Drain the can and rinse the chickpeas in a colander. Rinsing gets rid of the sticky canning liquid.
2. Spread them on a clean kitchen towel or a double layer of paper towels.
3. Rub gently, then let them sit for 10 minutes. Many of the skins will loosen and fall off. Don't worry about them. Pick out the loose ones if you like, or leave them; they turn into little crisp bits.
4. Before you oil them, check one. It should feel dry and a bit tacky, not slick or wet.

If you have time, leave them uncovered on the towel for 30 minutes. Drier chickpeas crisp faster and more evenly.

## Oven Method

1. Heat the oven to 425°F with a rack in the middle.
2. Toss the dry chickpeas with the oil and salt in a bowl.
3. Spread them in a single layer on a rimmed sheet pan. Crowding traps steam, so use a full-size pan if you can.
4. Roast for 12 to 15 minutes, then shake the pan.
5. Roast 10 to 15 minutes more.
6. Pull the pan, sprinkle on the smoked paprika, garlic powder, and pepper, and toss. Return to the oven for 2 to 3 minutes.

Adding the spices at the end matters. Paprika and garlic powder can scorch and turn bitter over a long roast, and adding them late keeps the flavor bright.

### How to Tell They're Done

- They look deep golden, not pale tan.
- They rattle a little when you shake the pan.
- One cooled for a minute cracks when you bite it, with a slightly tender center.

They firm up as they cool, so don't wait for them to feel rock-hard in the oven. For extra crunch, turn the oven off, prop the door open, and leave the pan inside for 10 minutes.

## Air-Fryer Method

The air fryer moves hot, dry air quickly, which suits chickpeas well.

1. Dry the chickpeas as above.
2. Toss with 2 teaspoons of oil and the salt.
3. Cook at 390°F in a single layer (work in batches if needed) for 12 to 15 minutes, shaking the basket every 4 to 5 minutes.
4. Toss with the spices while they're still hot, then cook 1 more minute.

Small chickpeas can pop and jump around, so a shake every few minutes also keeps any from sticking to the basket. Use the same doneness cues as the oven version.

## Easy Substitutions

- **Other beans:** Canned white beans such as cannellini or navy beans work with the same drying step. They come out a little more tender in the middle and less crunchy than chickpeas.
- **No smoked paprika:** Use regular paprika plus a pinch of cumin, or chili powder.
- **Other flavors:** Curry powder, Italian seasoning, or ranch seasoning all work. Add them after roasting.
- **Sweet version:** Skip the garlic and paprika. Toss the hot chickpeas with 1 tablespoon sugar and ½ teaspoon cinnamon.
- **Cooking from dried:** Cook and drain them first, then dry them as thoroughly as you would canned ones.
- **Oil:** Any neutral oil is fine. Use less rather than more. A light coating browns better than a greasy one.

## If They Come Out Soft

Soft chickpeas are usually fixable.

- **Still chewy after the time is up:** Return them to the oven for 5 to 8 minutes. Check often.
- **Crisp at first, soft the next day:** That's normal. They draw moisture from the air. Re-crisp them in a 350°F oven or the air fryer for 3 to 5 minutes.
- **Uneven batch:** The pan was probably crowded, or some chickpeas were wetter than others. Next time, use a bigger pan and a longer drying time.

## Storing and Serving

They're best the day you make them. Once fully cooled, keep them in a container with the lid left slightly loose for up to 2 days. A tight lid traps moisture and softens them quickly.

Beyond snacking from the bowl, try them:

- Over a green salad in place of croutons
- Stirred into rice bowls or lentil soup right before serving
- On top of yogurt with a drizzle of olive oil and a squeeze of lemon
- Tossed into a taco or burrito bowl for crunch

## Make Them Tonight

Open the can, rinse, and spread the chickpeas on a towel. Give them 10 full minutes to dry before they go anywhere near oil or heat. That one pause is what turns a pantry can into a crunchy snack.
