One can of chickpeas can become a crunchy, salty snack in about 30 minutes. The difference between crisp and chewy comes down to one step: getting the chickpeas truly dry before they hit the heat. Here's the full method, with oven and air-fryer timings.

**Makes:** about 4 servings as a snack (roughly 1/2 cup each)
**Prep:** 10 minutes
**Cook:** 25 to 30 minutes in the oven, 12 to 15 minutes in the air fryer

## What You Need

- 1 can (15 oz) chickpeas
- 1 to 2 tablespoons olive oil
- 1 teaspoon smoked paprika
- 1/2 teaspoon salt
- 1/4 teaspoon garlic powder
- Pinch of black pepper or cayenne, optional

## The Step That Makes the Difference: Dry Them

Water is the enemy of crunch. Wet chickpeas steam in the oven instead of roasting, and you end up with soft, leathery beans.

1. Drain the can and rinse the chickpeas well.
2. Spread them on a clean kitchen towel or a few layers of paper towels.
3. Pat them dry, then roll them gently under the towel. Loose skins will slip off. You can discard those, though they're fine to leave on.
4. Let them sit for 10 minutes, or until they look matte instead of shiny. If you have time, leave them out for 30.

When you pick one up, it should feel dry to the touch, not slick.

## Oven Method

1. Heat the oven to 425°F with a rimmed sheet pan inside. A hot pan gives the chickpeas a head start.
2. Toss the dry chickpeas with the oil and salt in a bowl. Use just enough oil to coat lightly. Too much makes them greasy instead of crisp.
3. Spread them in a single layer on the hot pan. Crowding causes steaming, so use two pans if needed.
4. Roast for 20 minutes, then shake the pan.
5. Roast 5 to 10 minutes more.

**Doneness cues:** The chickpeas should look deep golden, and a few may split open. Shake the pan. You should hear a light rattle, like dried beans in a jar. Pull one out and let it cool for a minute before tasting, since they crisp up as they cool. If it's still soft in the center, give the pan another 5 minutes.

## Air-Fryer Method

1. Heat the air fryer to 400°F for a few minutes.
2. Add the dry, oiled chickpeas in a single layer. Work in batches if your basket is small.
3. Cook 12 to 15 minutes, shaking the basket every 4 to 5 minutes.

The doneness cues are the same: golden, rattly, and crunchy once cooled. The air fryer tends to run a little faster than the oven, so check early.

## Add the Seasoning at the Right Time

Smoked paprika can turn bitter if it burns, and spices stick best to a little oil. Here's a reliable approach:

- Toss the chickpeas with oil and salt before roasting.
- Roast until almost done.
- Pull them out, sprinkle on the smoked paprika and garlic powder, and toss right on the hot pan. The residual heat blooms the spices without scorching them.
- Return to the oven or air fryer for 2 minutes if you want the coating to set.

If you're in a hurry, you can add all the seasoning at the start. Just expect a slightly darker, more toasted flavor.

## Substitutions

- **No smoked paprika:** Use regular paprika plus a pinch of chili powder or cumin.
- **Sweet version:** Skip the paprika and garlic. Toss the hot chickpeas with 1 teaspoon cinnamon and 1 tablespoon sugar.
- **Other flavors:** Try curry powder, ranch seasoning, or grated Parmesan added after roasting.
- **Other oil:** Any neutral oil works. Avocado oil handles high heat well.
- **Other beans:** Drained, dried white beans work the same way, though they come out a little softer inside.
- **Dried chickpeas:** Cook them until tender, drain, and dry them just as thoroughly as canned.

## Common Problems

**They're chewy, not crunchy.** They were likely too wet, the pan was crowded, or they needed more time. Return them to the oven for 5 to 10 minutes.

**They were crisp, then went soft.** Moisture crept back in. Cool them fully on the pan, then store them uncovered or loosely covered the same day.

**They taste bitter.** The paprika probably burned. Add it near the end next time.

## Ways to Use Them

Crispy chickpeas are best eaten within a few hours, but they're handy for more than snacking:

- Scatter them over soup in place of croutons.
- Toss them into a rice bowl or grain salad for crunch.
- Spoon them over yogurt with a drizzle of olive oil and lemon.
- Add them to a plain pasta with garlic and olive oil for texture.

## Storing Leftovers

Let them cool completely, then keep them in a container that isn't airtight, or with the lid slightly ajar, for up to a day. They're at their crunchiest the day they're made. If they soften, a few minutes in a 400°F oven or air fryer brings most of the crunch back.

## The Takeaway

Before you reach for the oil or the spice jar, pull out a towel. Dry the chickpeas until they're matte, and the rest of the recipe takes care of itself.
