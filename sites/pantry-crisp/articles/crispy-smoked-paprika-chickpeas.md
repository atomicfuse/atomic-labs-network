A can of chickpeas can become a crunchy snack, a soup topper, or a stand-in for croutons in about half an hour. The difference between crunchy and chewy comes down to one step, and it happens before the oven is even involved. Here is the method, with oven, air-fryer, and stovetop timings.

**Serves:** 4 as a snack, 6 as a topping
**Prep:** 5 minutes, plus 10 minutes of drying
**Cook:** 25 to 35 minutes in the oven

## What You Need

- 1 can (15 oz) chickpeas
- 1 1/2 tablespoons olive oil or any neutral oil
- 1/2 teaspoon fine salt
- 1 teaspoon smoked paprika
- 1/2 teaspoon garlic powder
- Optional: a pinch of cayenne, or a squeeze of lemon at the end

## The Step That Makes the Difference: Dry Them Completely

Chickpeas straight from the can are wet, and wet food steams instead of roasting. Steam is what gives you soft, leathery chickpeas.

1. Drain the can and rinse the chickpeas well.
2. Spread them on a clean kitchen towel or a double layer of paper towels.
3. Roll them around, then pat the tops dry.
4. Let them sit uncovered for about 10 minutes. Many of the skins will loosen and slip off. Leave them on or discard them, since either works.

They are ready when they feel dry to the touch and no longer shiny. If you have time, give them a second pat before they go on the pan.

## Oven Method (Best for a Full Pan)

1. Heat the oven to 400°F with a rack in the middle.
2. Toss the dry chickpeas with the oil and salt in a bowl. Skip the spices for now (see the next section).
3. Spread them in a single layer on a rimmed sheet pan. Crowding makes steam, so use a second pan if they are touching in piles.
4. Roast for 15 minutes, then shake the pan.
5. Roast 10 to 20 minutes more, until deep golden and they rattle when you shake the pan.

**Doneness cue:** Press one between your fingers. It should crack or feel hard, not give. They continue to crisp as they cool for a few minutes on the pan, so slightly underdone in the oven often ends up perfect.

## Add the Spices at the Right Time

Smoked paprika and garlic powder can scorch in a hot oven and turn bitter. Toss the chickpeas with the spices in a bowl right when they come out, while the oil is still hot and the coating sticks. Add the salt at the start for flavor that goes all the way through, and adjust at the end if you like.

## Air-Fryer Method (Fastest)

1. Heat the air fryer to 390°F.
2. Toss the dried chickpeas with the oil and salt.
3. Cook in a single layer for 12 to 15 minutes, shaking the basket every 4 to 5 minutes.
4. Toss with the spices while hot.

The air fryer works best in batches. If your basket is small, cook half a can at a time rather than piling them in.

## Stovetop Method (No Oven Needed)

1. Heat the oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat.
2. Add the dry chickpeas. Stand back a bit, because any leftover moisture will pop.
3. Cook for 10 to 12 minutes, shaking or stirring often, until golden and crisp at the edges.
4. Remove from the heat and toss with the spices and salt.

Stovetop chickpeas are a touch less evenly crunchy than oven ones, with a crisp shell and a slightly softer center. That is great on a rice bowl.

## Easy Swaps

- **Oil:** Olive, avocado, or canola all work. Use just enough to coat lightly, about 1 1/2 tablespoons for one can.
- **Spices:** Swap the paprika for cumin and chili powder, curry powder, or Italian seasoning. For a sweeter snack, use cinnamon and a little sugar after roasting.
- **Other canned beans:** White beans and black beans crisp up the same way, though they may need a few minutes less.
- **Cooked chickpeas from dried:** Cook, drain, and dry them just as carefully. They tend to be a bit softer, so allow a few extra minutes.

## Ways to Use Them

- Scatter over tomato soup or lentil soup in place of croutons.
- Add to a rice bowl with a fried egg and yogurt.
- Toss into a green salad with lemon and olive oil.
- Stir into pasta with garlic and a spoonful of the pasta water for a crunchy contrast.
- Eat them out of the bowl. Nobody will judge.

## Storing and Re-Crisping

Crispy chickpeas are at their best the day you make them. Once they cool completely, keep them in a loosely covered container at room temperature for up to 2 days. A tight lid traps moisture and softens them. If they lose their snap, spread them on a pan and warm them in a 375°F oven for 5 to 8 minutes, or air-fry for 3 to 4 minutes.

## Troubleshooting

- **Chewy, not crunchy:** They were still damp, the pan was crowded, or they needed more time. Return them to the oven for 5 to 10 minutes.
- **Burnt spices:** Add them after cooking.
- **Uneven browning:** Shake the pan halfway, and move the pan to a different rack position if your oven runs hot in the back.
- **Pale after 35 minutes:** Raise the heat to 425°F for the last 5 minutes and watch closely.

## Make a Batch This Week

Next time you open a can of chickpeas, put a towel on the counter first. Drying them for ten minutes is the whole trick, and everything after that is just heat and a shake of the pan.
