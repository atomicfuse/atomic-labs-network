Real Simple recently ran a piece on why its writer plans every vacation around food, with nine tricks for making trips more flavorful and memorable. The nine tricks themselves aren't something I can lay out here, so I won't pretend to. The core idea is simple, though: when food leads, a trip feels more personal and more local than a sightseeing checklist.

You don't need a plane ticket to borrow that. This is a pantry version: one dinner built like a food-first trip, with a main, a crunchy finishing touch, and a little ritual around it. You'll end up with a meal that serves 2 and takes about 25 minutes.

## The idea: pick a destination, then shop your shelves

Food-led travel starts with choosing what you want to eat, then letting the plan follow. Do the same at home. Pick a flavor direction first, then see what your pantry can say about it.

Here are three easy directions using staples you likely own:

- **Coastal Mediterranean:** tinned fish, lemon, olive oil, rice, chickpeas.
- **Smoky Spanish-leaning:** smoked paprika, canned beans, garlic, crispy chickpeas.
- **Simple Japanese-style bowl:** rice, tinned tuna, soy sauce, a drizzle of sesame oil if you have it.

The recipe below uses the first two ideas together.

## The main: 15-Minute Tuna Rice Bowls

**Serves:** 2 | **Prep:** 5 minutes | **Cook:** 10 minutes (if rice is already cooked)

**You need:**

- 2 cups cooked rice (leftover works well; cold rice reheats better than fresh, mushy rice)
- 1 can (5 oz) tuna in olive oil, drained lightly
- 1 tablespoon olive oil
- 1 garlic clove, minced
- Juice of half a lemon
- Salt and pepper
- Optional: a handful of chopped parsley, a spoonful of capers, or sliced cucumber

**Steps:**

1. Warm the olive oil in a skillet over medium heat. Add the garlic and stir for 30 seconds, until fragrant but not browned.
2. Add the rice and break up clumps. Cook 3 to 4 minutes, until hot all the way through and a few grains start to crisp at the edges.
3. Take the pan off the heat. Flake in the tuna, add lemon juice, and season.
4. Divide into bowls and top with whatever extras you have.

**Substitutions:** Swap tuna for canned salmon or sardines. Use pasta instead of rice and you have a different dish entirely. No lemon? A splash of any mild vinegar works.

## The crunch: Crispy Chickpeas with Smoked Paprika

**Serves:** 2 as a topping | **Prep:** 5 minutes | **Cook:** 25 to 30 minutes

This is the part that makes the bowl feel like a restaurant meal instead of a pantry fix.

**You need:**

- 1 can (15 oz) chickpeas
- 1 tablespoon olive oil
- 1 teaspoon smoked paprika
- 1/2 teaspoon salt

**Steps:**

1. Heat the oven to 425°F.
2. Drain and rinse the chickpeas, then **dry them thoroughly**. Spread them on a clean towel, roll them around, and let them sit a few minutes. This is the step that makes the difference: surface moisture steams the chickpeas instead of crisping them.
3. Toss with oil, paprika, and salt on a sheet pan in a single layer.
4. Roast 25 to 30 minutes, shaking the pan halfway through.

**Doneness cue:** They should look deeply golden and sound dry and rattly when you shake the pan. They'll crisp a bit more as they cool, so give them 5 minutes before judging.

**Air-fryer option:** Cook at 390°F for 10 to 12 minutes, shaking once or twice.

## Make it feel like a trip, not just dinner

The Real Simple piece is about memory-making through meals, and you can do a small version of that at home without spending anything.

- **Eat somewhere different.** Move the bowls to the porch, a picnic blanket on the living room floor, or the counter with the lights low.
- **Add one market-style extra.** Pick up a single fresh thing, such as a lemon, a bunch of parsley, or a good tomato, so the meal has one fresh moment.
- **Make it a ritual.** Choose one night a week for a "destination" dinner. Keep the rules loose so it doesn't become another thing on the schedule.

## Keep it from getting over-planned or pricey

The stated worry with food-first travel is making a trip feel over-scheduled or expensive. The same trap applies at home. You don't need specialty ingredients. Smoked paprika and a lemon are the only mildly "extra" items here, and both stretch across many meals.

If you want to repeat the idea, rotate the pantry base: white beans and pasta one week, lentils and rice the next. Keep the crispy topping constant, since it's the thing people fight over.

## Your next step

Pick tonight's destination, dry your chickpeas well, and let the crunch do the storytelling.
