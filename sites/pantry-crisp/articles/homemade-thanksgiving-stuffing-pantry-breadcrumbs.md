Boxed stuffing is easy, but a pan of homemade stuffing with a crunchy top takes about 15 minutes of hands-on work and uses things you likely already own: stale bread, butter, an onion, broth. Below is a full recipe, plus the one step that makes the top shatter-crisp.

## Why this swap is in the news

Food activist Vani Hari, known as the "Food Babe," has been urging families to rethink ultra-processed holiday foods. Per Fox News, she is promoting her new cookbook of "real food" alternatives and has taken aim at what she calls the "holiday death aisle." Her warning centers on additives such as BHA, BHT and food dyes in packaged stuffing and canned cranberry sauce. Fox News also notes that the FDA is reviewing chemicals like BHA and BHT amid a broader debate over additives.

Hari's core argument, as reported, is that Thanksgiving can keep its nostalgia and joy without additives, dyes, preservatives and packaged shortcuts. You don't have to agree with every claim to see the practical point: homemade versions are cheap, simple, and put you in charge of the ingredient list. Whether you buy a box or not, reading the label takes ten seconds.

## Crispy-topped pantry stuffing

**Serves:** 8 as a side
**Prep:** 15 minutes
**Cook:** 45 minutes

### What you need

- 10 to 12 cups bread cubes (about 1 loaf of sandwich bread or baguette, cut in 3/4-inch cubes)
- 6 tablespoons butter, plus more for the dish
- 1 large onion, diced
- 2 celery stalks, diced
- 3 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 tablespoon dried sage, or 2 teaspoons dried sage plus 1 teaspoon dried thyme
- 1 1/2 to 2 cups broth, chicken or vegetable
- 2 eggs, beaten
- 1 teaspoon salt, plus black pepper

### Steps

1. **Dry the bread.** Spread cubes on two sheet pans and bake at 300°F for 20 to 25 minutes, flipping once. They should feel like croutons: hard through, not browned. Skipping this is how stuffing turns gummy.
2. **Soften the vegetables.** Melt the butter in a large skillet. Cook onion and celery with the salt for 8 to 10 minutes until soft and translucent. Add garlic and herbs for the last minute.
3. **Combine.** Toss bread with the vegetables in a large bowl. Whisk the eggs into 1 1/2 cups broth, pour over, and fold gently. The bread should be moist but not pooling. Add the remaining broth only if some cubes are still bone dry.
4. **Bake covered.** Transfer to a buttered 9x13 dish, cover with foil, and bake at 350°F for 25 minutes.
5. **Uncover and crisp.** Remove the foil and bake 15 to 20 minutes more, until the top is deep golden and the edges sound crunchy when you tap them with a spoon.

**Doneness cue:** the center should be hot and set, not soupy, and the top should be browned.

## The crunchy breadcrumb topping

For extra crunch, make a topping while the bread dries. Pulse 2 slices of dry bread into coarse crumbs, then toss with 2 tablespoons melted butter, a pinch of salt and a little dried thyme. Scatter over the stuffing when you remove the foil.

**The step that makes the difference:** use dry bread and coat it in fat. Fresh crumbs steam instead of toasting, and uncoated crumbs scorch before they brown. Butter or olive oil gives even color and a crisp texture.

This topping also works on baked pasta, roasted vegetables, and casseroles, so make a double batch and keep extras in a jar for a few days.

## Easy substitutions

- **No fresh bread?** Use plain, unseasoned croutons or toasted pita. Skip the oven drying step and reduce the broth slightly.
- **No eggs?** Use an extra 1/4 cup broth. The stuffing will be looser and less custardy.
- **Dairy-free?** Swap olive oil for the butter.
- **Add richness:** Brown sausage, mushrooms, or apples with the onions.
- **Make ahead:** Assemble through step 3, refrigerate overnight, then bake. Add 10 minutes to the covered time.

## A quick label-reading habit for the stuffing aisle

If you do buy boxed stuffing or canned cranberry sauce, scan the ingredient list for the items Hari flagged: BHA, BHT and artificial dyes. Fox News reports she is critical of these in packaged holiday staples. Whether they concern you is your call. The list simply tells you what you're serving, and shorter lists are easier to read.

Cranberry sauce is the easiest swap of all: simmer 12 ounces of cranberries with 3/4 cup sugar and 3/4 cup water for about 10 minutes until the berries pop and the sauce thickens. It firms up as it cools.

## Keep the tradition, change the pan

Nostalgia is about the flavors of sage, butter and toasted bread, not the box. This year, dry your bread cubes the night before, and the rest of the dish comes together in an hour.
