## A Starting Point for Mulled Cider

Michaels' exact measurements weren't shared in the coverage I can cite, so treat this as a flexible home version. Cider is forgiving.

**Serves:** about 4 | **Prep:** 5 minutes | **Heat:** 15 to 20 minutes

**You'll need:**
- 1 quart apple cider
- Mulling spices (a store-bought blend works, or use whole spices you already have, such as cinnamon sticks, whole cloves, and allspice berries)
- Caramel sauce, for the rim
- Whipped cream, for the top

**Steps:**
1. Pour the cider into a saucepan and add the spices. Tying them in a square of cheesecloth, or using a tea infuser, keeps you from fishing out cloves later.
2. Heat over medium-low until steaming, with tiny bubbles at the edge. Don't let it boil hard, which can dull the flavor.
3. Simmer gently for 15 to 20 minutes. You'll know it's ready when the kitchen smells strongly of spice and the cider tastes noticeably more fragrant than it did cold.
4. Strain or lift out the spices and ladle into glasses.

### Substitutions

- No mulling blend? Two cinnamon sticks plus a few whole cloves covers most of the flavor.
- Ground spices work in a pinch, but they cloud the cider and leave sediment. Use a small pinch and strain through a fine sieve.
- Whipped cream from a can is fine. So is whipping cold heavy cream with a spoonful of sugar.

## The Caramel Rim, Done Right

The rim is what makes this feel like something from a café. The step that makes the difference: warm the caramel slightly so it spreads, then rim the glasses before you pour the hot cider.

1. Spoon a few tablespoons of caramel sauce onto a small plate. Microwave 10 seconds if it's stiff.
2. Dip the top edge of each glass, turning it to coat evenly.
3. Let the glasses sit a few minutes so the caramel sets and won't slide down.
4. Use heat-safe glasses or mugs, since the cider goes in hot.

Add the whipped cream last, right before serving, so it doesn't melt away.

## Why Extra-Sharp Cheddar Belongs on the Plate

Michaels paired the cider with extra-sharp cheddar, which he presented as a classic fall flavor combination. My take on why it works: the cider is sweet and slightly tart, and a salty, tangy cheddar balances that sweetness. Each sip resets your palate for the next bite.

Keep the plate simple:
- Cut the cheddar into thick slices or cubes, and let it sit out 15 to 20 minutes so the flavor opens up.
- Add apple slices if you like, a natural fit with the cider.
- Skip heavily flavored crackers. A plain cracker lets the cheese and cider do the talking.

### A pantry-friendly crunch

If you want something crispy beside the cheese, homemade pita chips are an easy addition. Cut pita into wedges, toss with a little oil, and bake until golden and crisp, flipping once. They cool crunchier than they look straight out of the oven.

## Shopping Smart for Cider Night

This is a short list, which keeps costs down:

- **Cider:** The one ingredient worth a little attention. Fresh cider from an orchard or farm stand is the natural choice for a drink like this, though grocery cider works.
- **Cheese:** Buy a block and cut it yourself. It's usually better value than pre-sliced.
- **Spices:** Whole spices keep longer than ground and double for baking and oatmeal later.
- **Caramel and whipped cream:** Check your fridge and pantry first. A jar of caramel sauce meant for ice cream works perfectly.

## Making It a Party

Mulled cider holds well on the stove over the lowest heat or in a slow cooker set to warm, so you aren't stuck pouring while guests arrive. Set out the rimmed glasses, a bowl of whipped cream, and the cheese board, and let people serve themselves.

For a gathering, scale the recipe by the quart. Plan on roughly one generous mug per guest to start, and keep a second batch of spiced cider ready to top off the pot.
