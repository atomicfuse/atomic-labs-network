BuzzFeed Shopping recently rounded up 26 products to start you on what it calls your "home cook era," pitched as vetted, approachable tools for anyone who wants to cook more at home. The roundup's individual picks aren't something we can vouch for here, so we won't recount them. The idea behind it is worth taking seriously, though: cooking at home feels easier when you start small.

Here's the version that works with what you probably own already. A few tools, a short pantry list, and one first recipe that shows you why crispy food is so satisfying. By the end, you'll know what to buy first, what to skip for now, and how to make dinner this week.

## Start With the Tools You'll Use Every Time

This is our own take, not BuzzFeed's list. A beginner kit doesn't need 26 items. Most pantry cooking runs on a handful:

- **One sheet pan.** It roasts chickpeas, potatoes, vegetables, and pita chips. A heavy, rimmed pan resists warping and browns food better.
- **One medium pot with a lid.** Rice, pasta, lentils, and soups all happen here.
- **One large skillet.** Good for toasting breadcrumbs, searing, and quick rice bowls.
- **One sharp knife and a cutting board.** A sharp knife is safer than a dull one and makes prep faster.
- **A colander, a can opener, and a few mixing bowls.** Canned beans and pasta depend on them.

An air fryer is a nice extra if you want fast snacks, but it's not required. Your oven does the same job with a little more time.

## Stock the Pantry Before You Buy Gadgets

Good ingredients do more for a beginner than a drawer full of gear. A short, flexible list covers a lot of dinners:

- Canned beans (chickpeas, white beans, black beans)
- Rice and pasta
- Dried or canned lentils
- Tinned fish like tuna
- Olive oil, salt, and smoked paprika
- Breadcrumbs, oats, and peanut butter for snacks and baking

From these you can make Creamy White Bean Pasta, 15-Minute Tuna Rice Bowls, or Sheet-Pan Potato and Black Bean Tacos without a special trip to the store.

## Your First Recipe: Crispy Chickpeas with Smoked Paprika

This one teaches the most important lesson in crispy cooking, and it costs very little.

**Serves:** 2 as a snack  
**Prep:** 10 minutes  
**Cook:** 25 to 30 minutes

### What you need

- 1 can (15 oz) chickpeas
- 1 to 2 tablespoons olive oil
- 1/2 teaspoon smoked paprika
- 1/2 teaspoon salt

### Steps

1. Heat the oven to 425°F (220°C).
2. Drain and rinse the chickpeas.
3. **Dry them thoroughly.** This is the step that makes the difference. Spread them on a clean towel and pat until no moisture shows. Wet chickpeas steam instead of crisp.
4. Toss with the oil on a sheet pan and spread in a single layer, so they roast instead of crowding and steaming.
5. Roast 25 to 30 minutes, shaking the pan once halfway through.
6. Season with the paprika and salt right out of the oven.

### How to tell they're done

They should look deep golden, feel dry, and rattle slightly when you shake the pan. They'll crunch up more as they cool for a few minutes, so don't pull them out and judge too early.

### Easy substitutions

- No smoked paprika? Use chili powder, cumin, or garlic powder.
- No oven? Use an air fryer at 400°F for about 12 to 15 minutes, shaking the basket once or twice.
- Want it sweeter? Skip the paprika and toss with a little cinnamon and sugar after roasting.

## Build Your First Week Around Overlap

The trick to cooking at home without wasting money is using the same ingredients in different ways. A sample week might look like this:

- **Night one:** Creamy White Bean Pasta, using pasta and a can of white beans.
- **Night two:** 15-Minute Tuna Rice Bowls, using rice and tinned fish.
- **Night three:** Sheet-Pan Potato and Black Bean Tacos, using your sheet pan again.
- **Snack:** Crispy chickpeas, or Homemade Pita Chips baked on the same pan.
- **Weekend:** Peanut Butter Oat Cookies or No-Bake Chocolate Oat Bars from pantry basics.

Notice how little new equipment this needs. The sheet pan and pot do most of the work.

## Add Crunch to Anything

If a meal feels a little plain, toasted breadcrumbs are the easiest fix. Warm a skillet over medium heat, add a spoonful of olive oil, and stir in a handful of breadcrumbs with a pinch of salt. Cook, stirring often, for 2 to 4 minutes until golden and fragrant. Scatter them over pasta, beans, or roasted vegetables. They burn fast, so stay at the stove.

## What to Skip at First

It's easy to get swept up in the appeal of a "home cook era," which per BuzzFeed's framing is about making cooking feel approachable. The approachable part is the point. Skip single-use gadgets until you've cooked a few weeks and know what you keep reaching for. If you notice yourself wishing for a tool more than once, that's the time to buy it.

## Your Next Step

Pick one recipe from this list, buy only what it needs, and cook it this week. A can of chickpeas, a towel, and a hot oven are enough to start.
