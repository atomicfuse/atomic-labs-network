Steve Austin is skipping the big bird, or at least might. Per Fox News, the WWE legend told the outlet his Thanksgiving with his wife will be low-key and nontraditional, possibly featuring South Texas tamales instead of turkey. He called turkey "overrated," and Fox reports his wife is vegetarian and planning the menu. He also had a kind word for canned cranberry sauce.

You don't need a Thanksgiving crowd or a day of steaming to borrow the idea. Below is a pantry-built, turkey-free main inspired by tamales: a black bean and corn bake with a crisp cornmeal crust. It's a shortcut cousin, not a real tamale, and it works for a small table, a vegetarian guest, or a Friday-after-Thanksgiving dinner.

## Tamale-Style Black Bean Bake

**Serves:** 4 to 6
**Prep:** 15 minutes
**Cook:** 40 minutes

### What you need

- 2 cans (15 oz each) black beans, drained and rinsed
- 1 can (15 oz) corn, drained, or 1½ cups frozen
- 1 can (10 oz) red enchilada sauce, or 1 cup salsa plus a splash of water
- 1 small onion, diced
- 2 cloves garlic, minced (or ½ teaspoon garlic powder)
- 2 teaspoons chili powder
- 1 teaspoon cumin
- 1 tablespoon oil
- Salt to taste

**For the crust:**

- 1 cup cornmeal
- ½ cup flour
- 1 teaspoon baking powder
- ½ teaspoon salt
- 1 cup milk (any kind)
- 2 tablespoons oil or melted butter
- 1 egg
- Optional: ¾ cup shredded cheese

### Steps

1. **Heat the oven to 400°F.** Put a 9x13-inch dish or large oven-safe skillet in to warm while you cook.
2. **Cook the base.** Sauté the onion in the oil for about 5 minutes until soft. Add garlic, chili powder and cumin, and stir for 30 seconds until fragrant.
3. **Add beans, corn and enchilada sauce.** Simmer 5 minutes. Mash a handful of beans against the pan so the filling thickens. Taste and salt.
4. **Mix the crust.** Whisk the dry ingredients, then stir in milk, oil and egg until just combined. It should be pourable but thick, like pancake batter.
5. **Assemble.** Spread the filling in the dish, scatter on cheese if using, and spoon the batter over the top in dollops, then smooth lightly. It's fine if some filling peeks through.
6. **Bake 25 to 30 minutes.** It's done when the top is golden, the edges pull away from the dish, and a toothpick in the crust comes out clean. Rest 10 minutes before cutting.

## The step that makes the crust crisp

Preheating the dish and thickening the filling both matter, and they do the same job: keeping moisture away from the cornmeal. A watery filling steams the crust into mush. Simmering until the beans mash and the sauce clings gives you a topping that browns instead.

For extra crunch, brush the top with a tablespoon of oil before baking, or run it under the broiler for 1 to 2 minutes at the end. Watch it closely; cornmeal goes from golden to dark fast.

## Swaps that still work

- **No enchilada sauce:** Use salsa thinned with a few tablespoons of water, or tomato sauce with extra chili powder.
- **Pinto beans or lentils:** Pinto beans swap one for one. Use 2 cups cooked lentils for a heartier, earthier filling.
- **No egg:** Use ¼ cup unsweetened applesauce or a bit more milk; the crust will be more tender and slightly less sturdy.
- **No cornmeal:** Use a boxed corn muffin mix, prepared according to the package, as the topping.
- **Want meat:** Stir in shredded rotisserie chicken or cooked ground meat with the beans.

## Make it a holiday spread

This dish holds its own beside Thanksgiving sides. Serve it with:

- **Canned cranberry sauce.** Austin reportedly praised it, and there's no shame in the ridged cylinder. Slice it, or stir a spoonful of it into a little salsa for a sweet-tart topping on the bake.
- **Crispy chickpeas** as a crunchy garnish. Pat them very dry, toss with oil and smoked paprika, and roast at 425°F for 20 to 25 minutes until they rattle on the pan.
- **Rice or a simple green salad** to stretch the meal.

## Make ahead and leftovers

Assemble the filling a day ahead and refrigerate it, then mix the batter just before baking. Leftovers keep 3 to 4 days in the fridge. Reheat slices in an air fryer or oven at 350°F for 8 to 10 minutes so the crust re-crisps; the microwave will soften it.

## Is turkey really overrated?

That's Austin's take, per Fox News, and plenty of people will argue the other way. Our take is simpler: a holiday table is about food people are glad to eat. If a pot of beans and a cornmeal crust gets cleaned out while the turkey gets picked at, you have your answer.

This year, make the dish your table will actually finish, and keep the bean bake in your back pocket for the next time turkey feels like too much.
