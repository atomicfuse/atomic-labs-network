Five ice cream flavors that were once everywhere in the 1970s have largely vanished from mainstream freezer aisles, according to Fox News: Butter Brickle, Rum Raisin, Pink Bubblegum, Heavenly Hash and Maple Walnut. The report says fans still miss them. You don't have to wait for a brand to revive them. Here's how to chase each flavor in your own kitchen using things you probably already have.

The approach is simple: a no-churn base, then a flavor twist for each. I'm offering these as my own kitchen suggestions, not as the original recipes behind any of those flavors.

## The no-churn base that works for all five

**Makes:** about 6 servings. **Prep:** 10 minutes. **Freeze:** 6 hours or overnight.

- 2 cups cold heavy cream
- 1 can (14 oz) sweetened condensed milk
- 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
- Pinch of salt

Whip the cream to stiff peaks, which means the peaks stand up straight when you lift the whisk. Then fold in the condensed milk, vanilla and salt gently. Don't stir hard, or you'll knock out the air that keeps the texture scoopable.

**The step that makes the difference:** chill the bowl and beaters for 15 minutes first. Cold cream whips faster and higher, and that volume is what stands in for a churn.

Pour into a loaf pan, press parchment onto the surface to prevent ice crystals, and freeze until firm. It's ready when a spoon leaves a clean scoop mark rather than sinking in.

## Butter Brickle: toffee crunch from a skillet

Butter Brickle is the easiest flavor to fake, because it's about buttery toffee bits. Melt 4 tablespoons butter with 1/2 cup brown sugar in a skillet over medium heat, stirring for 2 to 3 minutes until it bubbles and smells like caramel. Stir in 1/2 cup chopped pecans or crushed pretzels, spread on parchment and cool completely.

Break it into small pieces and fold into the base. **Doneness cue:** the toffee should snap, not bend. If it's chewy, it needs another minute on the heat. Add the pieces right before freezing so they stay crisp, and save a handful to sprinkle on top.

## Rum Raisin: soak first, then swirl

Soak 1/2 cup raisins in 3 tablespoons dark rum for at least 30 minutes, or overnight. Drain them, fold into the base, and add a tablespoon of the soaking liquid if you want a stronger flavor.

**Substitution:** no rum? Warm 3 tablespoons of apple juice or strong black tea with 1/4 teaspoon vanilla and soak the raisins in that instead. Alcohol also keeps ice cream softer, so the non-alcoholic version will freeze a little firmer. Let it sit on the counter 5 minutes before scooping.

## Pink Bubblegum: the honest shortcut

This is the hardest one to match from the pantry, since the flavor is a candy-shop one. Your best bet is a small amount of bubblegum-flavored extract or syrup if you can find it, plus a drop of red food coloring for the pink. Start with 1 teaspoon of extract and taste the base before freezing; a little goes a long way.

If you can't find it, a strawberry-vanilla base with a few drops of almond extract gets you a pink, sweet, vaguely nostalgic scoop. It's not identical, but it scratches a similar itch. Fold in a handful of colorful gumballs only if you're serving immediately, since they harden in the freezer.

## Heavenly Hash: chocolate, nuts and marshmallow

Heavenly Hash is a chocolate-based flavor with mix-ins, and that's where your pantry comes in. Stir 1/3 cup cocoa powder into the condensed milk before folding it into the whipped cream. Then add:

- 1/2 cup mini marshmallows
- 1/2 cup chopped almonds or walnuts
- 1/3 cup chopped chocolate chips

**Tip:** toast the nuts in a dry skillet for 3 to 4 minutes, until fragrant, to keep them from tasting soft and flat inside the ice cream. Mini marshmallows can get a bit chewy once frozen, which many people like.

## Maple Walnut: use real syrup, and toast the walnuts

Replace half the condensed milk's sweetness with maple flavor by whisking 1/3 cup pure maple syrup into the condensed milk (the base will be slightly softer, so freeze it a full 8 hours). Fold in 3/4 cup toasted, chopped walnuts.

Pancake syrup works in a pinch, but pure maple syrup tastes noticeably deeper. Toast the walnuts at 350°F for 6 to 8 minutes, until they smell nutty, and cool them before folding them in.

## Why these flavors are having a moment

Fox News points to a broader appetite for nostalgia, with brands reviving retro snacks, diner staples and childhood favorites. The report describes "newstalgia" as a blend of comfort, memory and novelty. Making these flavors at home lets you skip the waiting and tune the sweetness, crunch and boozy kick to your own taste.

## Storage and serving

Homemade no-churn ice cream keeps well for about two weeks in a tightly covered container with parchment pressed on top. Beyond that, ice crystals tend to creep in. Let it soften on the counter for 5 to 10 minutes before scooping.

Pick one flavor this weekend, whip the cream the night before, and let the freezer do the rest. Start with Butter Brickle: it's the quickest to get right, and the toffee alone is worth the dirty skillet.
