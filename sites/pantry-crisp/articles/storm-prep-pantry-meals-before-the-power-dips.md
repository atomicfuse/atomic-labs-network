"Storm watch" is getting more searches this week, driven by coverage of Tesla Powerwall's Storm Watch Mode. BASENOR, a Tesla accessories retailer, published an explainer on how the feature works as part of the way Powerwall prepares a home for storm-related outages. Whether you have a home battery or just a flashlight in a drawer, the same question comes up when bad weather is in the forecast: what are we going to eat if the power gets flaky?

Here's a practical answer built from cans, grains, and snacks. You'll cook a few things ahead while the oven still works, then have two meals that need almost no heat.

## Do this first: the 45-minute cook-ahead

When a storm is a day or two out, spend one short stretch cooking things that hold well. The goal is food you can eat cold or warm gently.

- **Rice or pasta:** Cook a big batch, cool it quickly on a sheet pan, and refrigerate in shallow containers. Eat it within a few days.
- **Hard-boiled eggs:** 10 to 11 minutes in boiling water, then straight into ice water. They peel easier and hold for days in the fridge.
- **Crispy chickpeas:** Recipe below. This is your crunchy snack that needs no power at all once it's made.

Keep the fridge and freezer doors shut once the power is out. The USDA's general guidance is that an unopened fridge keeps food safely cold for about four hours, so plan to eat the most perishable things first.

## Crispy smoked paprika chickpeas (the make-ahead snack)

**Serves:** 4 as a snack
**Prep:** 5 minutes, plus drying
**Cook:** 25 to 30 minutes at 400°F

**You need:** one 15-ounce can chickpeas, 1 tablespoon olive oil, 1 teaspoon smoked paprika, 1/2 teaspoon salt, 1/4 teaspoon garlic powder.

1. Drain and rinse the chickpeas, then spread them on a clean towel.
2. **Dry them well.** This is the step that makes the difference. Roll them gently, then let them air-dry 10 minutes. Surface moisture steams the chickpeas instead of crisping them.
3. Toss with the oil and spices on a sheet pan in a single layer.
4. Roast at 400°F, shaking the pan at the 15-minute mark.

**Doneness cue:** They should look deep golden and sound like tiny maracas when you shake the pan. They'll crisp a little more as they cool, so pull them when they feel firm, not rock-hard.

**Substitutions:** No smoked paprika? Use chili powder or cumin. Air fryer? Cook at 390°F for 12 to 15 minutes, shaking twice. Let them cool fully before storing in a loosely covered jar, since sealing warm chickpeas traps steam and softens them. They're best within a day or two.

## No-cook tuna and white bean salad

**Serves:** 2
**Prep:** 10 minutes
**Cook:** none

**You need:** one 15-ounce can white beans (drained and rinsed), one 5-ounce can tuna, 2 tablespoons olive oil, 1 tablespoon lemon juice or vinegar, salt, pepper, and whatever crunchy extra you have, such as sliced onion, celery, or jarred roasted peppers.

1. Mash about a quarter of the beans with a fork so the salad holds together.
2. Stir in the remaining beans, flaked tuna, oil, and lemon juice.
3. Season, then taste. Canned beans and tuna soak up salt, so add a little at a time.

**Doneness cue:** There's no cooking, but you'll know it's ready when it tastes bright. If it seems flat, add more acid before more salt.

**Substitutions:** Chickpeas or cannellini both work. Canned salmon or sardines can replace tuna. Serve it on crackers, in a pita, or over the cold rice you made earlier.

## A one-pot dinner that works on a camp stove or gas burner

If you have a heat source that doesn't depend on the grid, a one-pot meal uses the least fuel.

**Lentils and rice with spices**
**Serves:** 3 to 4
**Prep:** 5 minutes
**Cook:** about 25 minutes

Combine 1 cup brown or green lentils, 1/2 cup rice, 4 cups water, 1 teaspoon cumin, and 1 teaspoon salt in a pot. Bring to a boil, then cover and simmer on low.

**Doneness cue:** The lentils should be tender with a little bite, and most of the water absorbed. If the pot looks dry before the lentils soften, add a splash of water and keep going. Finish with a squeeze of lemon and a spoonful of olive oil.

Use red lentils instead and the cook time drops, though they'll break down into a softer, porridge-like texture.

## What to stock if you only buy a few things

A storm-ready pantry doesn't need a special shopping trip. If you're picking up a short list, choose:

- Two or three cans of beans or chickpeas
- Tuna, salmon, or sardines
- A bag of rice or lentils
- Crackers or pita for something crunchy
- Peanut butter and oats, which cover breakfast and snacks with no cooking
- A jar of something bright, like lemon juice or vinegar, so no-cook meals don't taste flat

Don't forget a manual can opener. It's the item most people don't realize they're missing until they need it.

## A note on the home-battery angle

The Storm Watch Mode coverage is aimed at homeowners with Powerwall systems, and the BASENOR piece is the only item behind this week's spike, so check the details and your own system's settings with Tesla or your installer. What we can say from the kitchen side is that a battery or generator keeps the lights on, but it doesn't stock your shelves. Backup power changes how long you can run a fridge or charge a phone. It doesn't change the value of having dinner sorted before the weather turns.

## The one move worth making today

Open the pantry, find one can of chickpeas, and roast them now. Dry them well, and you'll have a crunchy, no-power snack ready before anyone checks the forecast again.
