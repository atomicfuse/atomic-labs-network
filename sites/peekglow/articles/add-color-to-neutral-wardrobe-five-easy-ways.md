If your closet leans black, beige, and gray, the talk of colorful dressing this season can feel like a dare. You don't have to take it. Per Glamour, you can try the fall color trends without overhauling your wardrobe, and the approach comes down to five small moves. Here they are, with ways to put each one to work this week.

Color is getting louder right now, with runway trends, "colormaxxing," and more whimsical fashion aesthetics all pushing in that direction. But you don't need a runway look. You need a low-risk way to experiment that still feels like you.

## 1. Start with the accessories

This is the gentlest entry point. A bag, scarf, belt, or pair of shoes adds color without asking you to rethink a single outfit. If something doesn't feel right, you swap it out and your core wardrobe is untouched.

Try it this way:

- Pick one accessory, not several. A single colored piece reads as intentional.
- Wear it with an outfit you already love, like your usual black trousers and knit.
- Notice how it feels over a few days before adding another.

Think of accessories as a gateway. Once a colored scarf feels normal, a colored sweater is a smaller leap.

## 2. Opt for colors that look good with black

If black is your default, you don't have to give it up. Glamour's advice is to choose colors that already sit well beside it, so your existing pieces keep working.

The practical upside: you can add a colored top or layer and pair it with the black bottoms, coat, or boots you own. No new foundation required. When you shop, hold the piece up against your black items first. If it looks good beside them, it will likely earn its place in your rotation.

## 3. Go the baby route

Bright, saturated shades can feel like too much if you're used to neutrals. Soft, pastel-leaning "baby" shades are a gentler option, and they tend to feel wearable and natural rather than loud.

A pale blue shirt or a soft pink knit can read almost like a neutral with a bit of personality. This is a good route if your instinct is to retreat to gray the moment a color feels too bold. Start with a soft shade in a simple shape, like a classic tee or a plain sweater, so the color is the only new thing.

## 4. Style them with beige and brown instead of black

Here's a switch worth trying: pair your colorful piece with beige and brown rather than black. Black can make a color feel stark and high-contrast. Warmer neutrals give it a softer backdrop.

An easy formula to test:

1. Choose one colorful top or layer.
2. Pair it with beige trousers or a brown skirt.
3. Add brown shoes or a brown bag to tie it together.

If you already own tan or brown basics, you may be one colorful piece away from a fresh outfit. It also works well for fall, when those earthy tones are already in heavy rotation.

## 5. Wear complementary colors

When you're ready to go a step further, try complementary colors, which sit opposite each other on the color wheel. Pairing them is a simple piece of color theory that gives an outfit a deliberate, put-together feel.

You don't need to memorize the wheel. Pick one colored piece you like, then look for its opposite in a smaller dose, perhaps in a bag, a scarf, or a top layered under a jacket. Keep one color as the star and let the other play support, so the outfit feels balanced instead of busy.

## How to choose where to begin

You don't need to do all five at once. Match the step to your comfort level:

- **Nervous about color at all:** start with an accessory (step 1).
- **Live in black:** look for shades that flatter it (step 2).
- **Find bright colors overwhelming:** try soft baby shades (step 3).
- **Want a gentler look than black offers:** pair with beige and brown (step 4).
- **Feeling confident:** experiment with complementary pairings (step 5).

These steps also build on each other. A colored bag worn with black becomes a pastel sweater worn with beige, which eventually becomes a complementary pairing you wouldn't have tried a month ago.

## A small shopping rule that keeps it affordable

Before you buy anything colorful, check that it works with at least two things you already own. That one habit keeps color additions practical, and it's a good guard against a closet full of lovely pieces that don't go with anything. Because the approach here is built around pairing with your existing neutrals, a single well-chosen piece can go a long way.

## The takeaway

Pick one colored piece this week, hold it up against the neutrals already in your closet, and wear it once. That's the whole experiment, and it's enough to find out how much color you actually want.
