Pilates in Bangkok has gone from niche recovery practice to a regular fixture of the weekly routine. Per Time Out's guide to the city's top studios, new spaces are spreading across Sukhumvit, demand is outpacing supply, and reformer slots can book out days in advance. That's good news if you want options. It's also a reason to know what you're looking for before you start booking.

Here's how to match a studio to your own style, using the six places Time Out highlights.

## Start with your personality, not the timetable

Time Out describes the scene as running "from sweaty reformer sessions to chill one-on-ones." That spread is the most useful thing to understand. Before you browse, ask yourself which of these sounds like you.

### You like structure and polish

An international chain such as Club Pilates tends to suit people who like a familiar format and a clear booking system. That's a general observation about chains, not a claim about any one class, so read the studio's own description before you commit.

### You like personal attention

If the idea of a room full of strangers makes you hesitate, look at the boutique end of the list. Time Out points to intimate reformer studios and one-on-one sessions. Smaller settings are a natural fit for beginners who want someone to watch their form and answer questions.

### You want a harder workout

Some sessions are described as sweaty. If you want your heart rate up, read class descriptions closely and look for the more energetic formats rather than the slower, restorative ones.

## Know the difference between mat and reformer

The guide covers reformer Pilates in particular, and that's the format most affected by the booking squeeze. A reformer is the sliding-carriage machine with springs. Mat classes use the floor and your own body weight.

A simple way to think about it:

- **Mat:** easier to fit in, usually simpler to book, a good way to learn the basic movements.
- **Reformer:** adds spring resistance and support, which many people find helpful for learning control, but the equipment means fewer spots per class.

If you're new, trying one of each is a sensible way to find out which you enjoy before you commit to a package.

## Book early, because slots fill up

Per Time Out, reformer slots now book out days in advance. A few habits help:

1. **Book your next class before you leave the current one.** Studios with limited reformers fill fastest.
2. **Look beyond peak times.** Weekday midday or less obvious slots are often easier to get, though you'll need to check each studio's timetable.
3. **Have a backup.** Keep a second studio on your list so a full class doesn't derail your week.

## Make your first class easy

You don't need a perfect setup, but a few small touches make the first visit smoother.

- **Grip socks.** Many studios expect them, and it's part of the look that has helped make Pilates so popular. Check whether your studio sells them or lets you borrow a pair.
- **Comfortable, fitted activewear.** Instructors can see your alignment more easily when clothes aren't baggy. You don't need to spend much; a simple set you already own works.
- **Arrive a little early.** It gives you time to meet the instructor and mention anything you want them to know, such as an old injury.
- **Ask questions.** Beginners are the reason studios run intro classes. If you have a health condition, talk to your doctor before starting.

## Make it part of a routine you enjoy

The appeal of Pilates is partly the workout and partly the ritual. Pick a regular slot, choose a studio close enough to your home or office that you'll actually go, and pair class with something pleasant, like a coffee or brunch nearby afterward. A routine you look forward to is the one that sticks.

Treat any single class as a starting point rather than a promise of results. Consistent, low-impact movement is the point, and how you feel after a few weeks is the best measure.

## Your next step

Pick two studios from the list, one chain and one boutique, and book a single trial class at each this month. Whichever one you're excited to return to is your answer.
