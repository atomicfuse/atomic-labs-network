A basic kid's room can look polished and lasting without a renovation. Apartment Therapy recently featured a boy's light blue bedroom that gets its timeless feel from DIY box trim and molding. The takeaway is simple: a few architectural details can make a child's room feel more elevated, and you can plan the same approach yourself.

## Why molding does so much work

Plain walls can read as flat, especially in a kid's room where the color is the main event. Box trim and molding add lines, shadows, and structure. Per Apartment Therapy, that detail is what makes the light blue room look so elevated.

The appeal for parents is practical. Molding is a way to get a "custom" look without major construction, and it fits the appetite for budget-friendly upgrades that look good in photos and keep their style over time.

## Pair a playful color with a classic detail

The room in the feature pairs light blue walls with traditional trim. That balance is the idea worth borrowing. Color gives the room personality. Molding gives it a grown-up backbone.

This matters because kids' tastes change quickly. Toys, bedding, and posters can be swapped in an afternoon. Walls with built-in character don't need to be redone when a favorite theme fades. The room can grow with the child while the architecture stays steady.

### Why light blue works

Light blue is a soft, familiar shade that sits comfortably beside crisp trim. Our read: it's calm enough to stay pleasant for years, yet clearly a color rather than a neutral. If you're nervous about committing, it's a gentle place to start.

## How to plan your own box molding

The feature points to DIY box trim, so here is a sensible way to approach a project like it. These steps are general planning advice, not details from the original room.

### 1. Pick one wall first

Start with the wall behind the bed or the largest unbroken wall. One finished wall shows you whether you like the look before you commit to the whole room.

### 2. Sketch the layout on paper

Measure the wall and draw the boxes to scale. Aim for even spacing and consistent margins so the pattern feels intentional. Marking the layout lightly on the wall with painter's tape before you cut anything helps you judge proportions in the actual room.

### 3. Cut, attach, and fill

Measure each piece, cut the corners carefully, and fasten the trim to the wall. Filling nail holes and seams is what makes DIY trim look built-in rather than stuck on. Take your time here, since small gaps are the usual giveaway.

### 4. Paint it all together

Painting trim and wall in the same light blue gives a soft, tone-on-tone effect. Painting the trim a contrasting shade gives a crisper outline. Test a swatch on the wall and look at it in morning and evening light before you choose.

## Keep the rest of the room simple

With molding doing the decorative work, the furnishings can stay easy. A few ideas that suit the approach:

- Choose bedding in one or two colors, and let the walls lead.
- Keep art and shelves low so a child can reach and rearrange them.
- Add personality through things that are easy to change, like pillows, lamps, and framed drawings.

That way, the room stays flexible while the walls stay timeless.

## Is it worth the effort?

If you're comfortable with basic tools and patient with measuring, molding is a project you can take in stages. Because it's a visual upgrade rather than a structural one, you can start small and stop whenever it looks right. Results will depend on your skill, wall condition, and materials, so give yourself more time than you think you need.

## Start with one wall

Tape out a box layout on a single wall this weekend and stand back. If the proportions look right, you've found your project, and your child's room is on its way to feeling timeless.
