House Beautiful recently rounded up 20 elegant Halloween pieces, mostly from Pottery Barn and other retailers, that lean spooky without veering into tacky. You don't need to buy all 20. The pattern across the list is the useful part: a handful of design rules that make Halloween look intentional. Here's how to use them in three spots at home, plus a few shortcuts for stretching a smaller budget.

## The Core Rule: Pick One Palette, Then Repeat It

Look at the items in the roundup and you'll notice they lean on materials rather than novelty: antique brass, smoked amber glass, bouclé, linen, ceramic, enameled cast iron, and gold detailing. That's what separates "elegant" from "party store."

Try this at home:

- Choose two or three colors, such as brass, amber, and cream, or black and gold.
- Pick one or two spooky motifs (ghosts, snakes, spiders) and repeat them rather than mixing everything.
- Let one or two pieces be the statement, and keep the rest quiet.

## The Entryway: Set the Tone Early

Your front door and hallway are the first impression, so a few details go a long way.

### Start with the floor and walls

The list includes a woodland critter doormat, an antique brass spider-web mirror, a serpentine wall hook, and a snake-wrapped mirror (Nagini-inspired, per the roundup). A mirror with a spooky frame is a smart choice because it reads as a decorative object first and a Halloween piece second. The same goes for a snake-shaped wall hook: it holds your keys or a scarf, and it works in October without shouting about it.

### Add something sculptural

A sculptural golden snake on a console or shelf follows the same logic. Metallic finishes feel more like art than costume. Place one piece where the light hits it, and let it stand alone rather than crowding it with extras.

## The Living Room: Cozy First, Spooky Second

This is where the "small touches" approach shines. You aren't redecorating, just nudging the room.

- **Swap a pillow.** The list features a Gus the Ghost throw pillow with a bouclé pumpkin. Textured fabric adds warmth, and a single seasonal pillow on a neutral sofa is an easy, low-commitment update.
- **Add amber light.** A smoked amber glass candle holder gives off a warm, moody look. Hanging taper candles, a Hogwarts-inspired idea in the roundup, bring height and drama to a mantel or corner.
- **Use a planter.** A light blue ceramic pumpkin planter is a good reminder that pumpkins don't have to be orange. A soft color feels fresh and lasts well past Halloween.
- **Try a ghost incense smoker.** If you enjoy scent, this is a playful pick. Follow the manufacturer's instructions and keep a window cracked for airflow.

For candles of any kind, keep them away from fabrics and never leave a flame unattended.

## The Table: Where Elegant Halloween Really Shows Off

More than half of the roundup is tableware, which tells you something: dinner parties are where this look is easiest to pull off. A table also resets after the party, so nothing stays up longer than you want.

### Build a simple tablescape

Start with a calm base, then add personality in small pieces:

1. **Linens first.** Linen napkins with an oak vine design add a seasonal feel that isn't strictly Halloween.
2. **Add serving pieces.** The list includes a skull charcuterie board, a lidded snack bowl, a gold-decaled "Hex Mix" snack bowl, and ghost-shaped pinch bowls. Pair one skull or ghost piece with plain white or wood items so it stands out.
3. **Set the mood with drinks.** Scorpion-topped cocktail picks and autumn leaf inlay coasters dress up a drink without much effort. Skeleton serving pieces and wine glasses, also on the list, work if you want a full theme.
4. **Finish with the main event.** Pumpkin-shaped enameled cast iron cocottes can double as serveware for soup or sides, and they're handsome enough to leave out all fall.

A good rule: one "wow" item per table, with everything else supporting it.

## Don't Forget the Pieces That Outlast October

The best advice hidden in this roundup is to favor items that work beyond the holiday. Several picks fit that idea:

- Autumn leaf coasters and oak vine napkins read as seasonal, not scary.
- Antique brushstroke birdcages are decorative on their own and can hold candles, greenery, or small ornaments.
- A ceramic pumpkin planter can carry you from Halloween through Thanksgiving.
- Cast iron cocottes belong in the kitchen year-round.

If you're deciding where to spend, put your money here first and keep the novelty pieces to one or two.

## Getting the Look on a Smaller Budget

The roundup is built around retail picks, and prices weren't part of the list, so check them yourself before you shop. The look itself is easy to approximate:

- **Raid what you own.** Brass candlesticks, glass jars, and neutral linens already fit the palette.
- **Thrift for metals and glass.** Amber glass, brass, and ceramics are common secondhand finds.
- **Use spray paint sparingly.** A coat of matte black or gold on an old frame or vase can mimic the antique finish.
- **Go natural.** Branches, dried leaves, and small gourds add texture for almost nothing.

## Your Next Step

Before you buy anything, pick your two colors and one motif, then choose a single spot to style this week. Start with the table or the entryway, add one standout piece, and stop there. Restraint is what keeps Halloween looking elegant.
