A free Pilates class in a public park sounds almost too easy. But in Columbia, South Carolina, it's a real weekly habit for a lot of people. Finlay Park, downtown, now offers nearly 10 free fitness classes per week, and one evening Pilates class draws around 200 participants, per The Post and Courier.

Here's what's happening there, and how to use it as a model for finding free movement classes in your own city.

## What Finlay Park is offering

The park reopened last year after a $25 million revamp, according to The Post and Courier. It now hosts free fitness classes each weekday, open to all skill levels.

The standout is the Pilates class led by instructor Michaela Leung. Per the report, her evening class became a viral sensation and now draws about 200 people a week. The coverage describes growth from roughly 20 participants to more than 200, and links the surge to a TikTok moment.

If you live near Columbia, check the park's official schedule for current days and times, since those details can change. I'm not listing a class-by-class timetable here because it's best confirmed at the source.

## Why a big, free class can work for beginners

A crowd of 200 might sound intimidating. For a first-timer, it can actually be a relief. These are reasons that make sense for a large outdoor class (this is my take, not from the report):

- Nobody is watching you. With that many people, you blend in.
- There's no commitment. Free means you can try it once and walk away.
- All levels are welcome, so you won't be the only beginner.
- Cost isn't a barrier, which matters when gym memberships and wellness spending keep climbing.

Personality matters too. The Post and Courier's coverage points to Leung's authentic teaching style as part of why the class grew. A good instructor makes a space feel friendly, and that's worth looking for anywhere.

## How to find free classes in your own city

You don't need a viral instructor. Try these steps, in order:

1. **Search your parks and recreation department.** Look for "free fitness" or "outdoor classes" on the city or county site. Many list seasonal schedules.
2. **Check the park itself.** Downtown parks, especially recently renovated ones, may run programming. Finlay Park is an example of a space designed to be a community hub.
3. **Look at social media.** Search your city name plus "free Pilates," "free yoga," or "park workout." Instructors often post times and locations there before anywhere else.
4. **Ask local studios.** Some host occasional free community sessions as an introduction to their space.
5. **Pick one class and try it twice.** The first visit is about logistics. The second tells you whether it fits.

## What to bring to an outdoor class

Keep it simple. A mat or large towel, water, and comfortable clothes you can stretch in are plenty. Arrive a little early at popular classes so you can find a spot, especially if a crowd is likely. Confirm with the organizer whether mats are provided.

## Make it a routine, not a one-off

The real value of a weekday class is rhythm. Choose a class that fits a time you can repeat, such as after work, and put it on your calendar like any other plan. Movement habits tend to stick when they're easy and enjoyable, and a free class removes one more excuse.

Take this one step today: open your city's parks and recreation page and look up this week's free classes. Pick one, and go.
