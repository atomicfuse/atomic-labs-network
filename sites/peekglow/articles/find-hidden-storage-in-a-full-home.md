BuzzFeed Shopping recently rounded up 26 products for people convinced they have run out of storage space, with a tagline that sums up the whole idea: shoes don't have to be on the floor. The full list isn't reproduced here, so rather than guess at what's on it, this guide borrows the premise and gives you a method you can use right now: find the space you already have, then buy only what fills a real gap.

## Start With the Floor

The BuzzFeed tagline points at a good first move. Floors are the most wasted storage real estate in most homes. Shoes, bags, laundry baskets, and stray boxes all end up there because nothing else is within reach.

Walk through your home and notice what sits on the floor that could live somewhere else. Then ask one question about each item: what's the closest empty vertical space to where this gets used?

### Shoes

Shoes are the classic example. If they pile up by the door, look at the wall space above and beside it. A slim rack, an over-the-door organizer, or a bench with a shelf beneath can lift a whole pile off the ground. Measure the spot before you shop, and check how many pairs you actually wear each week. Those are the ones that deserve the prime spot by the door. The rest can go somewhere less convenient.

## Think Up, Not Out

Most rooms have a lot of unused space between eye level and the ceiling. A few ways to use it:

- **Closets:** Add a second hanging rod or a shelf above the existing one for off-season items.
- **Doors:** The back of a closet, bathroom, or pantry door can hold shoes, cleaning supplies, wraps, or accessories.
- **Walls:** Narrow shelves or hooks near the entry or in the kitchen keep everyday things visible and off counters.

Keep the heaviest and most-used items between knee and shoulder height. Save the high shelves for things you reach for a few times a year.

## Check the Spaces You Don't See

The gaps people forget are often the easiest wins.

- **Under the bed:** Shallow bins or rolling drawers suit extra bedding, sweaters, or shoes.
- **Behind furniture:** A slim console or shelf can fit in a gap that's currently just dust.
- **Inside cabinets:** Stackable shelves double the usable height in a cabinet where plates or pantry items sit in a single layer.
- **Beside the fridge or washer:** A narrow rolling cart can hold a surprising amount.

## Declutter Before You Buy

No organizer fixes a space that's holding things you no longer use. Before you spend anything, take ten minutes on one zone, such as a single drawer, one shelf, or the entry. Pull everything out, sort it into keep, donate, and toss, and put back only what earns its spot.

This often changes what you need to buy. You may find you only need one bin instead of three, or none at all.

## Buy for the Problem, Not the Photo

Storage products look wonderful in lists and online photos. The ones that work at home solve a specific problem in a specific spot. A few habits help:

1. **Measure first.** Write down the width, depth, and height of the space.
2. **Pick one zone.** Fix the entry or the bathroom before moving on, so you can see what worked.
3. **Choose a consistent look.** Matching bins or baskets make a shelf feel calm instead of crowded.
4. **Favor flexible pieces.** Adjustable shelves and stackable bins adapt if you move or your needs change.
5. **Check return policies.** Sizes can be tricky, and a return option takes the pressure off.

If you rent, look for options that hang over doors, sit on shelves, or stand on their own, so you don't have to drill into walls.

## Make It Look Good, Too

Storage doesn't have to look like storage. Baskets with a woven texture, wooden crates, or labeled jars can read as decor. A shoe bench in the entry doubles as seating. A pretty tray on a shelf corrals small items so they feel intentional instead of scattered.

A good test: if you'd be happy to leave the item visible when guests come over, it's doing two jobs.

## A Simple Weekend Plan

- **Saturday morning:** Pick one cluttered zone and declutter it.
- **Saturday afternoon:** Measure the space and note what's still homeless.
- **Sunday:** Shop for only those items, set them up, and put everything back with a clear home.

One zone at a time is enough. Done well, it makes the next one easier.

## Your Next Step

Pick the one spot where things land on the floor, measure the wall above it, and start there. That single change will show you how much room your home was holding all along.
