By mid-afternoon, a lot of us feel it: tight shoulders, a stiff back, and a body that has been in one shape for hours. Here is a short, doable yoga reset built from five poses. Per Hindustan Times, these simple asanas target the back, shoulders, hips and spine, and are recommended to ease stiffness and improve posture after long desk hours and limited movement.

You don't need a mat studio or special gear. A bit of floor space and a few minutes are enough. Move slowly, breathe steadily, and stop if anything hurts.

## Why a short reset makes sense

Work-from-home habits and screen-heavy days have made stiffness, poor posture and low energy common complaints. Small, accessible movement breaks suit busy days better than a big workout you never get to. Think of this routine as a pause button, not a cure-all. It's a gentle way to check in with your body.

## The five poses, in doing order

The order below moves from standing to the floor, so you can start right where you finish work.

### 1. Tadasana (Mountain Pose)

Stand with feet about hip-width apart and let your arms rest at your sides. Stack your head over your shoulders and your shoulders over your hips. Take five slow breaths.

This pose is mostly about body awareness. It's a good moment to notice where you've been holding tension, whether that's your jaw, your shoulders or your lower back.

### 2. Marjariasana (Cat Pose)

Come onto your hands and knees, wrists under shoulders and knees under hips. As you exhale, round your spine toward the ceiling and let your head drop. Then return to a neutral back.

Repeat for six to eight slow breaths. This is a kind way to wake up a back that has been still for hours. Keep the movement smooth rather than forced.

### 3. Bhujangasana (Cobra Pose)

Lie on your stomach with your palms under your shoulders. Press gently through your hands and lift your chest, keeping your elbows soft and your shoulders relaxed away from your ears.

After hunching over a keyboard, opening the front of the body feels good. Lift only as far as is comfortable, and keep your hips grounded. Hold for a few breaths, then lower down with control.

### 4. Setu Bandhasana (Bridge Pose)

Lie on your back with knees bent and feet flat on the floor, hip-width apart. Press into your feet and lift your hips. Keep your knees pointing forward, not splaying out.

This one works the back and hips, which spend so much time folded in a chair. Hold for a few breaths, then lower slowly, one vertebra at a time.

### 5. Balasana (Child's Pose)

Kneel, sit back toward your heels, and fold forward with your arms stretched out in front or resting beside you. Let your forehead rest on the floor or a cushion.

This is your cool-down. Stay for as many breaths as feels good and let your exhales get longer.

## How to fit it into your day

A few simple ways to make this stick:

- **Pick a trigger.** Do it right after you close your laptop, so it becomes part of the end of your workday.
- **Keep it short.** Around five breaths per pose is plenty to start. Consistency matters more than length.
- **Use what you have.** A folded towel or cushion under your forehead in Child's Pose makes it more comfortable.
- **Go gently.** Yoga should feel like a stretch, never sharp pain. If you have a back, neck or other health concern, check with a doctor or qualified instructor before starting.

## What to expect

This routine won't undo a whole day of sitting on its own, and it isn't medical treatment. What it can do is give you a few minutes of mindful movement, a chance to breathe, and a reminder to stand up and stretch more often. For many people, that small habit is the real win.

Tonight, try it once: stand, round, lift, bridge, fold. Five poses, a few minutes, and a body that feels a little more like yours again.
