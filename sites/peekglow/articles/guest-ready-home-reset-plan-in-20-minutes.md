You know the moment. A text says "we're almost there," and suddenly you're stuffing mail, shoes, and a laundry basket into the oven. This guide gives you a 20-minute order of operations for getting a home guest-ready, plus the kinds of organizing products that make the next reset even quicker.

The idea comes from a BuzzFeed Shopping roundup of 29 Wayfair products for people who panic about mess before company arrives. The roundup's focus is products for hiding clutter, organizing entryways, tidying living rooms, and making a space look more put-together without hours of deep cleaning. The list as reported doesn't name specific items in the summary I have, so rather than guess at products, here is the method those product types point toward.

## Start With What Guests Actually See

Guests notice the entryway, the living area, and the bathroom. They almost never inspect your hallway closet. Spend your limited time where eyes land first.

For a 20-minute reset, a workable split looks like this:

- Entryway: 4 minutes
- Living room: 8 minutes
- Kitchen surfaces: 4 minutes
- Bathroom: 4 minutes

Set a timer. A deadline keeps you from drifting into reorganizing a drawer nobody will open.

## Clear the Entryway First

The entryway sets the tone the second the door opens. Loose shoes, bags, and mail make a home feel chaotic even if everything else is fine.

### What to do

1. Line shoes up against a wall, or move everyday pairs out of sight.
2. Gather mail and keys into one spot.
3. Clear the floor completely. Empty floor space reads as calm.

### What helps

The roundup includes products for organizing entryways and keeping essentials out of sight. In practice, that usually means a shoe rack, a lidded basket, or a wall hook setup. Pick one container for each type of item so your reset is a single sweep, not a sorting project.

## Hide Clutter in One Sweep

Here's the honest secret of guest prep: tidying is mostly about containing, not cleaning. You don't need to find a home for every item in 8 minutes. You need to get things out of view.

### The one-basket method

- Walk the living room with a single large basket or bin.
- Drop in cords, remotes you don't need, toys, mugs, and stray papers.
- Put the basket in a bedroom or closet.

After your guests leave, empty the basket and put everything away. That's a deferred chore, not a failure.

### What helps

Storage with doors, lids, or lined baskets hides contents better than open shelves. Several items in the roundup target hiding clutter and reducing visual clutter, which fits this approach. A storage ottoman or a basket with a lid does double duty as seating or a side table.

## Make Surfaces Look Neater

Surfaces carry a lot of visual weight. A cleared coffee table and a wiped counter can make a whole room look reset.

- Remove everything from the coffee table, then put back only two or three things, such as a candle and a book.
- Wipe kitchen counters and clear the sink.
- Fluff cushions and fold throws.

This is also where small decor touches earn their place. A tray gathers items into one tidy cluster, so a handful of objects looks intentional rather than scattered.

## Do a Fast Bathroom Pass

Guests will almost certainly see the bathroom, so give it four minutes.

1. Clear the counter of everything except soap and a hand towel.
2. Wipe the sink and faucet.
3. Put out a fresh hand towel.
4. Check the toilet paper supply.

A small caddy or bin under the sink can swallow daily items so the counter stays open.

## Think Small-Space, Not Big Overhaul

If you live in a smaller home, clutter has fewer places to go, which is why panic cleaning feels so intense. The roundup nods to this with products for small-space organization and upgrading small spaces.

A few principles that help any compact room:

- Use vertical space: wall hooks, shelves, and over-the-door storage keep floors clear.
- Choose furniture that stores things: ottomans, benches, and beds with storage.
- Keep one catch-all spot per room, so mess has a designated landing place.

## Build a Reset Kit So You're Never Scrambling

The real fix for hosting panic is making the reset repeatable. Keep these ready:

- One large lidded basket for sweeping clutter
- A small caddy with a cloth and all-purpose cleaner for quick wipes
- A tray for the coffee table
- A spare hand towel, folded and ready

None of this needs to be expensive. If you shop for storage, whether on Wayfair or elsewhere, measure your space first and choose pieces you'll genuinely use, since a bin that doesn't fit just becomes more clutter.

## Let Good Enough Be Good Enough

Guests are coming to see you, not inspect your baseboards. A calm entryway, clear surfaces, and a clean bathroom do most of the work. Next time the "we're almost there" text arrives, set a timer for 20 minutes, grab the basket, and start at the front door.
