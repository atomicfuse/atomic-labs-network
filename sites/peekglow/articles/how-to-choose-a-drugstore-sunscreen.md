You don't need a $50 bottle to get a good sunscreen. According to Glamour's roundup of dermatologist-approved drugstore picks, affordable formulas can perform as well as luxury ones when they check a few key boxes. This guide walks you through those boxes so you can stand in the sunscreen aisle and choose with confidence.

Glamour's list highlights options that promise no white cast, no pilling, and a price under $20. The specific products aren't covered here, so think of this as the shopping framework behind that list: what matters, what doesn't, and how to find a formula you'll actually wear.

## Start With the Label: Broad-Spectrum, SPF 30 or Higher

The core of Glamour's advice is simple. An effective drugstore sunscreen offers broad-spectrum protection at SPF 30 or higher. Check the front of the bottle for both.

If a formula has those two things, price is not what separates it from a luxury option. What separates them, mostly, is how pleasant they are to put on.

## Texture Is the Real Difference

The best sunscreen is the one you'll use consistently. That's why the Glamour piece leans on wearability: no chalky cast, no pilling under makeup or moisturizer, a finish that feels comfortable.

A formula that stings, feels greasy, or turns your face ghostly is one you'll skip on a busy morning. Before you commit to a full-size bottle, think about what bothers you most:

- **Leaves a white cast:** look for products marketed as sheer or invisible, and read the reviews from people with your skin tone.
- **Pills over other products:** try applying sunscreen as the last skincare step, give it a minute to settle, and avoid layering too many products underneath.
- **Feels heavy:** a lighter lotion, fluid, or gel-style formula may suit you better.

These are practical tweaks rather than guarantees, so expect a little trial and error.

## Mineral vs. Chemical, Explained Simply

You'll see both types at the drugstore. In broad terms, mineral sunscreens use ingredients like zinc oxide or titanium dioxide, while chemical sunscreens use other filters. Either can be a good choice if it's broad-spectrum and SPF 30 or higher.

The practical question is which one feels better on your skin. Mineral formulas are often picked by people with sensitive skin, though they can be the ones more likely to leave a cast. Chemical formulas tend to feel lighter. If one type irritates you or sits badly, try the other.

## Match the Formula to Your Skin

Skin type should guide your pick more than brand name.

### Sensitive skin
Look for gentle, simple formulas, and patch test on a small area first. If a product stings, switch.

### Oily skin
Choose a lightweight texture that doesn't feel slick by midday. Fluids and gels are good places to start.

### Skin prone to hyperpigmentation
Consistency matters most here. A sunscreen you'll wear every day, and reapply, is the one worth buying. Talk to a dermatologist about your specific concerns.

## Treat It as a Daily Step

Sunscreen is increasingly seen as daily skin care rather than a beach-day extra. That shift is also why affordability matters: if you're using it every morning, a bottle that costs under $20 is much easier to keep stocked, and to reapply generously.

A few habits make it easier to stick with:

1. **Keep one by the sink** and another in your bag or desk drawer.
2. **Make it the last skincare step** each morning, before makeup.
3. **Choose a texture you enjoy**, since you're more likely to reapply something comfortable.
4. **Replace it when it runs out** rather than stretching a nearly empty bottle thin.

## A Quick Aisle Checklist

Before you buy, run through this:

- Does the front say broad-spectrum?
- Is it SPF 30 or higher?
- Is it a formula type (mineral or chemical) that suits your skin?
- Do reviews mention a white cast, pilling, or stinging that would bother you?
- Is it comfortable enough that you'd put it on every day?

If you can answer yes to the first two and feel good about the rest, you've likely found a keeper at a fair price.

## Your Next Step

Pick one broad-spectrum SPF 30+ formula this week, wear it daily for a couple of weeks, and notice how it feels. If you like it, you're done. If not, change the texture, not the habit.
