BuzzFeed Shopping recently rounded up 26 shoes that reviewers say keep their feet blister free, covering everyday wear, travel, walking, and long days on your feet. The roundup's own tagline promises that "not a single sore shall ruin your adventures this year." That's a lovely goal, though no shoe can guarantee it. What you can do is shop smarter, and that's what this guide covers: how to use reviews, a few in-store checks, and a simple break-in routine so your next pair actually earns its spot in your rotation.

A note on what follows: BuzzFeed's list is the inspiration here, but I'm not going to name specific shoes from it. What I can offer is a method you can apply to any pair, from any brand, at any price.

## Start With How You'll Actually Use the Shoes

Before you open a single tab, decide what the shoes are for. "Comfortable" means different things on different days.

- **Travel days:** airports, cobblestones, and a lot of standing in lines.
- **Work:** hours on your feet, or a long commute with a walking stretch at each end.
- **City days:** miles of pavement with a coffee stop in the middle.
- **Dressier occasions:** weddings and events, where you want to look polished and still make it to the last dance.

Write down your one main use. A shoe that's wonderful for a museum day may not be what you want for a four-hour event, and vice versa. Shopping for one job at a time keeps you from buying a pair that's mediocre at everything.

## Read Reviews Like a Detective

Reviewer-backed picks resonate for a good reason: you want proof from real wearers before you spend money. But not all reviews are equally useful. Look for these details:

- **Time worn.** "Wore them all day at the airport" tells you more than "so cute!"
- **Foot type.** Comments from people with wide feet, narrow heels, or high arches help you predict your own fit.
- **Sizing notes.** Look for patterns. If several reviewers say a style runs small, believe them.
- **Break-in comments.** Some pairs feel great immediately; others need a few wears. Neither is wrong, but you should know which you're getting.
- **Mixed reviews.** Read the three-star ones. They usually name the exact situation where a shoe falls short.

Treat "blister free" as a reviewer's experience, not a promise. Feet differ, and a shoe that works beautifully for one person may rub another.

## Check the Hot Spots Before You Commit

Blisters tend to come from friction, so look at where a shoe is most likely to rub. Whether you're trying them on in a store or inspecting them after delivery, run through this list:

1. **Heel.** Your heel should stay put without slipping up and down when you walk. Slipping is a common source of rubbing.
2. **Toe box.** Wiggle your toes. They shouldn't feel pinched or press against the front.
3. **Seams and edges.** Run a finger along the inside. Stiff stitching or a rough edge near the ankle or toes is a warning sign.
4. **Straps and tops.** Anything that cuts across the foot or ankle should have a little give.
5. **Insole.** Press on it. Some cushioning is nice, but the shoe should also feel stable underfoot.

## Try Them On the Smart Way

A few small habits make a big difference:

- **Shop later in the day.** Feet often swell a bit as the day goes on, so an afternoon fitting is a more honest test.
- **Wear the socks you'll wear with them.** Trying on sandals-and-socks or thin dress socks that don't match your real plans skews the fit.
- **Walk around, not just stand.** Take a few minutes on a hard floor if you can, and go up and down a step.
- **Try both feet.** One foot is often slightly larger than the other, so fit the bigger one.

If you're ordering online, check the return policy first and try shoes on at home, indoors, on carpet. That way you can return them if they don't work, and you won't scuff the soles.

## Break Them In Before the Big Day

Even a great shoe deserves a trial run. Don't debut a new pair on the first morning of a trip or the day of an event.

1. **Wear them around the house** for an hour or two over a couple of days.
2. **Take a short walk,** such as a quick errand, and pay attention to any warm or tender spots.
3. **Build up gradually.** Lengthen the time on each outing before you rely on them for a full day.
4. **Keep a backup handy.** Tuck a second pair or a few adhesive bandages into your bag on the first few longer outings.

If a spot feels hot, don't push through it. Stopping early is easier than healing a sore later.

## Small Extras That Help

Socks matter more than most people expect. Snug, properly fitting socks that stay in place reduce bunching, which is another common cause of rubbing. Keep your feet dry as best you can, and if you know a particular spot tends to rub, a bit of padding or a bandage over it before you head out is a simple, inexpensive precaution. If you have ongoing foot pain or a condition that affects your feet, a podiatrist can give you advice tailored to you.

## Build a Short List, Then Test

Use a roundup like BuzzFeed's as a place to start, then narrow it down with your own criteria: one main use, reviews that mention your foot type, and a fit check at the heel, toe, and seams. Pick one or two finalists rather than ordering six. Wear them at home first, and keep the tags on until you're sure.

The best blister-resistant shoe is the one that fits your foot and your day. Make that your rule, and let the reviews help you find it.
