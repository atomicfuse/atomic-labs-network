BuzzFeed Shopping recently rounded up 30 beauty products it pitches as so good you'll become a repeat customer, and it suggests adding them to a subscription so you never run out. The full list isn't reproduced here, so this piece skips the product names. It takes the more useful idea underneath: how do you tell a product you'll rebuy from one you'll just tolerate until it's empty?

By the end, you'll have a short checklist you can run on any skincare, makeup, or hair product, whether it's a viral launch or a quiet staple.

## Start With Cost Per Use, Not Price Tag

A product's shelf price tells you very little. What matters is how many times you'll actually use it.

The math is simple:

1. Note the price.
2. Estimate how many uses you get (a daily moisturizer that lasts two months is roughly 60 uses).
3. Divide the price by the uses.

A pricier cleanser you use twice a day for three months can cost less per use than a cheap lipstick you wear once and abandon. This is the most honest way to compare a budget find with a splurge, and it often shifts which one feels like the better deal.

## Look for Products You Reach for Without Deciding To

A rebuy-worthy product doesn't need willpower. If you have to remind yourself to use it, it isn't fitting into your routine.

Ask yourself:

- Do I grab it automatically at the same point each day?
- Would I notice right away if it were missing?
- Have I used it steadily for at least a few weeks, not just the exciting first few days?

Three yeses are a good sign. Novelty fades fast, and a few weeks of steady use tells you far more than a first impression.

## Check Whether It Does One Job Well

The products people repurchase tend to have a clear purpose: a lip balm that feels comfortable, a shampoo that suits your hair, a mascara that wears the way you like. Be wary of anything promising to fix everything at once.

That also goes for claims. Be cautious with big promises about transforming skin or hair overnight. A good product should feel pleasant, work with your routine, and do what you reasonably expect it to. Your own experience is the best test, and if you have a skin concern, a dermatologist is a better guide than a product description.

## Separate the Hype From the Habit

Constant launches and viral recommendations make it easy to buy on impulse. Try a short cooling-off rule:

- Put a new product on a list for one week before buying.
- If you still want it, check whether it replaces something you already own or just adds to the pile.
- Buy the smallest size first when one exists.

If a viral favorite survives that process and still earns a daily spot a month later, that's a keeper. If it ends up in a drawer, you've saved yourself a full-size purchase.

## Know Where to Spend and Where to Save

Budget versus splurge is personal, but a useful rule is to spend more on what stays on your skin or hair longest, or what you use daily, and save on things that are quick or replaceable. Let cost per use settle the gray areas.

A few questions help:

- **Do I use it every day?** Worth investing in something you like.
- **Is it a rinse-off or occasional item?** A budget option often does the job.
- **Is it a tool or accessory?** Pay for durability, not branding.

## Use Subscriptions Carefully

BuzzFeed's pitch is to put your favorites on subscription so you never run out. That can make sense for true staples, since it saves you from a last-minute scramble and sometimes comes with a discount, depending on the retailer.

Set yourself up to benefit:

- Only subscribe after you've finished one full bottle or tube and wanted another.
- Choose a delivery schedule that matches your real usage, not an optimistic one.
- Set a calendar reminder to review it every few months, so you aren't stockpiling.
- Confirm the cancellation and skip policies before you sign up.

## Keep a Simple Rebuy List

The easiest way to build a routine you trust is to track it. Keep a note on your phone with three headings: **Rebuy**, **Maybe**, and **Never again**. When a product runs out, move it into one of them right away, while you remember how it felt to use.

Over a few months you'll have a personal version of the list BuzzFeed set out to create, built on your own skin, hair, and habits instead of someone else's.

## The One Habit to Take With You

Before you buy anything new, finish what you have and decide honestly whether you'd buy it again. The products that pass that test are your staples. Everything else is just a trial.
