BuzzFeed Shopping recently published a roundup of 24 products pitched as fixes for everyday annoyances, with a $15 puppy paw cleaner as the poster child for the "why didn't I buy this sooner" feeling. The full list lives on BuzzFeed, and I'm not going to guess at the other 23 items. What I can offer is something that outlasts any one list: a simple way to decide which convenience buys deserve your money and which will end up in a drawer.

## Start With the Annoyance, Not the Product

The paw cleaner works as an example because it targets one specific, repeated annoyance: muddy feet coming through the door. Per BuzzFeed's framing, the appeal of these finds is that they offer small upgrades without a major lifestyle change.

Before you add anything to a cart, name the frustration in one sentence. "Dirty paws track mess onto the floor after every walk" is a real problem. "This looks handy" is not.

## Run the Three-Question Test

Once you have a product in mind, ask:

1. **How often will I face this problem?** Daily or weekly annoyances justify a purchase. A once-a-year headache usually doesn't.
2. **Does it fit into what I already do?** The best fixes slot into an existing routine, like the end of a walk or the end of a dishwashing session. If it needs a new habit, it's more likely to be forgotten.
3. **Where will it live?** Decide on a spot before it arrives. If it has no home, it becomes clutter, which is the opposite of easier.

## Set a Price Ceiling for Small Fixes

The $15 price in BuzzFeed's example is part of the draw. Low-cost buys feel low-risk, which is exactly why they pile up. A useful rule: pick a number you'd be comfortable wasting, say $15 to $20, and treat that as your ceiling for experimental gadgets. Anything above it gets a week of waiting before you buy.

Check the return policy too. If a product doesn't solve your problem, an easy return makes the experiment free.

## Test It for Two Weeks

An item can seem brilliant on day one and irritating by day ten. Give each new purchase a short trial:

- Use it every time the problem comes up for two weeks.
- Notice whether you reach for it automatically or have to remind yourself.
- Ask whether it saved time, reduced frustration, or just moved the work somewhere else (for instance, a gadget that's harder to clean than the mess it handles).

If it passes, keep it where you'll see it. If not, return it or pass it along.

## Be Realistic About the Promise

Roundup headlines love big language like "life could've been so much easier." Treat that as enthusiasm, not a guarantee. A good gadget trims a little friction. It won't transform your home. Keep your expectations modest and you'll be happier with what works.

## Where Convenience Buys Make the Most Sense

These are the areas where small fixes tend to pay off, based on the kinds of everyday frustrations the BuzzFeed roundup points to:

### The Entryway
Mud, wet shoes, and tracked-in dirt all show up here. A small tool that handles the mess at the door keeps it out of the rest of the house. That's the logic behind the paw cleaner.

### The Kitchen
Look for repeat irritations: things that spill, stick, or take extra steps. One fix per annoyance is plenty.

### Daily Routines
If mornings feel rushed, one thing that saves two minutes, repeated every day, adds up more than a flashy item you use monthly.

## Keep a "Wish I'd Known" Note

Here's a habit that costs nothing. Whenever something irritates you twice in a week, jot it in your phone. When you next browse a roundup like BuzzFeed's, you'll be shopping against a list of real problems rather than being swayed by whatever looks clever. You may find you need two of the 24 items, or none, and that's a perfectly good result.

## The Takeaway

Before you buy your next helpful gadget, write down the one annoyance it needs to fix. If you can't, leave it in the cart.
