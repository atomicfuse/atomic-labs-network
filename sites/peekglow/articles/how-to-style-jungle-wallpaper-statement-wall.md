Designer Ken Fulk has introduced a wallcovering called "Monkey Business" for Perennials' fall collection, according to Elle Decor. It features spider monkeys, toucans, and butterflies printed on sisal grass cloth. Fulk reportedly pictures it as a statement piece for dining rooms and other bold design moments.

You probably aren't hanging a luxury grass cloth this week, and you don't need to. What the design shows is useful anyway: a single wall with personality can change how a whole room feels. Here's how to borrow that idea and style it so it feels joyful rather than chaotic.

## Start With One Wall, Not Four

A jungle scene works best when it gets room to be the star. Pick one wall, or one contained space, and let everything else step back.

Good candidates:

- **A dining room.** Per Elle Decor, this is where Fulk sees the wallpaper shining. You sit there for a meal or two at a time, so a bold pattern feels like an event rather than a daily demand.
- **A powder room.** Small, closed-off, and low-stakes, so it's a forgiving place to experiment.
- **Behind a bed or sofa.** The furniture anchors the pattern and gives your eye somewhere to rest.

If you're nervous, start with the smallest option. You can always grow your confidence later.

## Let the Texture Do Some of the Work

Part of what makes "Monkey Business" distinctive is the surface: the animals are printed on sisal grass cloth, per the report. That natural, woven texture adds warmth before you even look at the toucans.

You can echo that idea at home with inexpensive pieces:

- A woven jute or seagrass rug
- Rattan or cane chairs
- A basket or two for storage
- Linen curtains in a natural tone

These natural materials make a busy wall feel grounded, and they suit a nature-inspired theme without extra effort.

## Pick Two Colors From the Pattern and Repeat Them

This is the simplest trick for making a loud wall feel intentional. Look closely at your wallpaper (or print, or fabric) and choose two colors in it. Then repeat each one in small doses around the room:

1. A throw pillow in the first color
2. A vase or candle in the second
3. A piece of art with a touch of either one

Keep the rest neutral. Cream, warm white, wood tones, and soft green all play well with tropical patterns. The goal is a room that looks pulled together, not a scene where every item is competing.

## Keep Furniture Simple

When the wall is telling a story, furniture should be a calm supporting cast. Choose clean shapes and solid colors. A plain wood table, a sofa in one solid shade, or simple dining chairs all let the pattern speak.

If you want one lively piece, make it one. A single patterned chair or a bold lamp is plenty. Two or three will start to fight the wallpaper.

## Light It for Warmth

Bold patterns can look flat under harsh overhead light. Layer in softer sources instead:

- A table or floor lamp with a warm bulb
- Wall sconces on either side of the pattern
- Candles for dinners

Lower, warmer light makes a dense print feel cozy and gives it depth in the evening, when a dining room is used most.

## Get the Look on a Budget

The source doesn't give a price, and luxury wallcoverings generally sit at the high end, so it's worth knowing the lower-cost routes. These are suggestions, not products from the collection:

- **Peel-and-stick wallpaper.** Look for tropical, leaf, or animal motifs. It's removable, which makes it renter-friendly.
- **Wallpaper one section only.** Cover a single alcove, the inside of a bookcase, or a narrow wall to cut down on how much you need.
- **Framed wallpaper.** Mount a sample or leftover roll piece in large frames for a gallery-style jungle moment.
- **Art and textiles.** A botanical print, a bird illustration, or a leafy pillow cover brings the same mood with no commitment at all.

If you do hang paper, measure carefully and check the manufacturer's installation instructions. Large patterns often need extra length to line up the repeat.

## Why Bold Walls Are Having a Moment

Elle Decor's coverage places this wallpaper within a broader appetite for personality and escapism at home. That fits what many people want from their rooms: something with a little story in it. Fulk's design leans into that with whimsical wildlife and a tropical fantasy feel, per the report.

You don't have to follow a trend to benefit from the idea. A room that makes you smile when you walk in is doing its job.

## Your Next Step

Hold a toucan-bright idea up to your own space this weekend: pick one wall, order a few peel-and-stick samples or fabric swatches, tape them up, and live with them for a few days. If you still smile when you pass them, you've found your jungle.
