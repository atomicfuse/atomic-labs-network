If an all-white kitchen has started to feel more like a showroom than a place you actually live, red might be the nudge you need. Per House Beautiful, designers are moving away from neutral kitchens and toward red tones, from burgundy to brick, as a bold, warm choice with personality. Below is how to try it, from a full commitment down to a small test run, without the room feeling overwhelming.

## Why red, and why now

House Beautiful describes red as bold yet versatile. The broader shift it points to is homeowners growing tired of safe, all-white interiors and wanting spaces that feel personal, social, and energizing.

The kitchen is a natural place for that. It's where people gather, and a warm color suits a room built around cooking and conversation. That's my read, not a rule, but it explains why the trend feels less like a gimmick and more like a mood change.

## Start by choosing your kind of red

"Red" covers a lot of ground. The reporting highlights a range running from deep burgundy to earthy brick, and where you land on that spectrum changes the whole feel of the room.

- **Burgundy:** deeper and moodier. It tends to read as rich and dramatic.
- **Brick:** earthier and softer. It tends to feel grounded and welcoming.

Those descriptions are general color logic rather than specific paint recommendations, so treat them as a starting point. The practical step is to test before you commit.

### How to test a shade

1. Buy two or three sample pots across the range, from deep to earthy.
2. Paint large swatches (at least a couple of feet square) on different walls or on boards you can move.
3. Look at them in morning light, afternoon light, and with your kitchen lights on at night.
4. Check them next to your countertop, flooring, and any wood you're keeping.

Lighting changes red more than most colors, so the evening check matters. Live with the swatches for a few days before deciding.

## Balance red with neutrals

The advice House Beautiful gives is to pair red with neutrals, texture, and natural materials. That combination is what keeps a bold kitchen from feeling loud.

Here's a simple way to put it into practice:

- **Let red be the star, not the whole cast.** If the cabinets are red, keep walls, backsplash, and large surfaces calmer.
- **Add texture.** Think woven baskets, linen towels, or a ribbed glass canister. Texture gives the eye somewhere to rest.
- **Bring in natural materials.** Wood cutting boards, a stone countertop, or open wood shelving soften a strong color.
- **Keep neutrals warm.** Creamy whites and soft beiges tend to sit more comfortably beside red than stark, cool whites. Again, that's my styling take.

## Not ready for red cabinets? Try a small version

A full repaint is a big step. You can test the idea on a smaller scale first, and it costs far less.

- **A red or brick-toned accent:** a single piece like a stool, a runner, or a ceramic bowl on the counter.
- **One painted element:** a small island, a pantry door, or the inside of an open shelf.
- **Textiles:** dish towels, a rug, or a set of napkins in a deep red.

Live with it for a few weeks. If you keep reaching for the red and smiling at it, that's useful information. If it nags at you, you've spent very little to find out.

## What the trend says about home design

The interest in red fits a larger move toward homes that show who lives in them. Instead of choosing what's safest for resale or for a photo, more people are choosing what makes the room feel good to be in every day. Red won't suit every kitchen or every person, and that's fine. The point is that you get to decide.

## Your next step

Buy two sample pots, one burgundy-leaning and one brick-leaning, and paint a big swatch beside your current cabinets this weekend. Let it sit through a few days of real light before you make the call.
