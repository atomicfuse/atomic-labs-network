A desk in the corner of the bedroom can work. But if your workspace leaves you cold, the fix may not be a better chair. It may be borrowing a few ideas from the way homes have been turned into offices.

Architectural Digest India recently profiled five Indian homes that have taken on a second life as workspaces. They range from a minimalist retreat in Mumbai to a nostalgic yet cosy studio in Chennai. The thread running through them, per the outlet, is a move away from sterile corporate environments toward spaces that feel personal, adaptive, and rooted in place.

The published details I have are brief, so I won't invent specifics about floor plans or materials. What I can do is take the themes the story highlights (light, texture, courtyards, terraces, and old rooms) and show you how to apply them to the space where you work, whether that's a spare room or a kitchen table.

The titles alone suggest a range: a bungalow in Mumbai's Bandra, a ruin brought back to life, and a haveli (a traditional Indian mansion) revived for work. The common idea is that the domestic character of the building was kept rather than stripped away.

## Why Offices Are Feeling More Like Homes

As hybrid work reshapes office culture, the story points out, businesses are rethinking what a workplace should feel like. Many are choosing comfort, heritage, and human scale over glass-tower uniformity.

There's also a practical side in dense cities. Reusing existing homes is a design response to sustainability, preservation, and changing work habits, according to the report.

For you, the takeaway is simple: a workspace doesn't have to look like an office to function well. A room with character can be just as productive, and probably more pleasant to walk into on a Monday.

## Borrow the Ideas: Small Touches That Make a Workspace Warmer

These are my suggestions, inspired by the themes in the story rather than claims about what the featured offices contain.

### Start with the light

Domestic spaces usually have windows at human scale, and you can use that. Place your desk so natural light reaches your work surface from the side, if you can. Then swap one harsh overhead light for a warm lamp. Two or three light sources feel more homelike than one bright ceiling fixture.

### Add texture, not clutter

Corporate spaces tend to be smooth and uniform. Counter that with a few tactile things: a woven rug under your chair, a linen or cotton throw over the seat back, a wooden tray for pens and notebooks. Choose three textures and stop there. The goal is warmth, not a crowded desk.

### Keep what's old

Several of the featured projects revolve around heritage and existing structures. You can echo that on a small scale. Keep the old wooden side table, the framed family print, or the inherited bookshelf in your workspace. Pieces with history make a room feel rooted, and they cost nothing if you already own them.

### Use the in-between spaces

Courtyards and terraces appear in the themes of the story. Most of us don't have a courtyard, but a balcony, porch, or window seat can serve the same purpose: a spot to take a call, read, or reset between tasks. Put a chair and a small table there and call it your second desk.

### Let the room stay flexible

The spaces are described as adaptive. At home, that means choosing furniture that can shift roles. A drop-leaf table can be a desk by day and a dining table at night. A rolling cart can hold your laptop gear and tuck into a closet. If your workspace shares a room with your life, make it easy to put away.

## A Simple 30-Minute Refresh

Try this order the next time your desk feels flat:

1. **Clear the surface** completely, then return only what you use daily.
2. **Move the desk or chair** toward the best window light.
3. **Add one warm lamp** and turn off the overhead light for an afternoon to see how it feels.
4. **Bring in one textured item**, such as a rug, cushion, or basket.
5. **Place one personal object** within your line of sight: a plant, a print, an old book.

None of this requires a renovation, and you can judge the effect by the end of the day.

## What to Take From These Homes

The appeal of these Indian workspaces, as Architectural Digest India frames it, is that they feel human in scale and specific to their place. You can do the same in miniature. Look at the room you already have, notice what's charming about it, and build your workspace around that rather than against it.

Tonight, move your chair toward the window and swap in a warm lamp. See if Monday feels different.
