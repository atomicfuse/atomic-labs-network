Designer Emily Ruff took a small, stripped-back 1926 Seattle home and turned it into a warm, characterful retreat built for hosting, according to Homes & Gardens. The house had been uninhabited and, in the outlet's framing, redundant. Now it's layered, patterned, and welcoming.

You probably don't live in a 100-year-old house. You can still borrow the moves that gave this one its charm. Here is how, in the order I'd try them.

## Start With What the House Already Gives You

Per the report, the project leaned on the home's architectural details to restore charm and continuity, rather than erasing them. That's the first lesson: warmth comes from a sense that everything belongs together.

In your own home, that might mean:

- Noticing any arched openings, built-ins, or trim you already have, and painting or styling to highlight them rather than hide them.
- Choosing new pieces that echo existing shapes. If you have a curved doorway, a rounded mirror or arched cabinet will feel related to it.
- Keeping materials consistent from room to room so the house reads as one story.

If your home is newer and has few details, small architectural touches such as an arched opening or simple trim can start to supply that character.

## Use English-Inspired Decorating as Your Guide

The design draws on English-inspired decorating, a style that's having a moment as homeowners move away from stark renovations toward layered, lived-in rooms. The ingredients named in the story are easy to translate:

- **Floral wallpaper.** The home is pattern-filled, and florals are a central piece of that.
- **Warm woods.** Think of them as the grounding layer.
- **Brass.** Hardware, lamps, or a picture light can carry this.
- **Stone.** A counter, a hearth, or even a small tray gives weight and texture.

You don't need all four in every room. Pick two or three per space and repeat at least one of them elsewhere in the house.

## Try Floral Wallpaper in One Small Spot

Wallpaper can feel like a big commitment. Start where the stakes are low: a powder room, a pantry, the inside of a bookcase, or a single wall in a dining nook. A small room lets a pattern feel cozy instead of loud.

Pair the pattern with plain surroundings. Warm wood furniture and a simple, softly colored ceiling or trim give a floral room somewhere to rest.

## Add Soft Yellow Without Overwhelming the Room

Soft yellow tones are part of what makes this home feel sunny and welcoming, per the report. Yellow can go wrong when it's too bright, so keep it gentle and spread it around:

1. Choose a muted, buttery shade rather than a saturated one.
2. Use it on one element first, such as a wall, a set of curtains, or a pair of lamp shades.
3. Repeat it in small doses elsewhere, like a pillow, a vase, or a pattern's background color.
4. Let wood and brass sit beside it. Warm materials soften yellow instead of competing with it.

That repetition is the continuity trick again: a color that shows up in several places feels planned, not random.

## Build a Warm Neutral Base

Patterns need quiet space around them. Warm neutrals, meaning creamy whites, soft beiges, and wood tones, let a floral wallpaper or a yellow accent be the star. Cool, stark whites tend to make a pattern look sharper and the room feel less settled, so lean warm when you're layering.

## Mix in Vintage Pieces

One reason historic homes feel collected is that their furnishings don't all match. You can recreate that by adding a few older pieces: a wood side table, a brass lamp, a framed print. Secondhand shops and flea markets are budget-friendly places to look, and each piece brings a bit of age that new furniture can't fake.

Aim for a mix. A room of only antiques can feel like a museum, and a room of only new things can feel flat. A few of each is the sweet spot.

## Design for Hosting

The home was designed with entertaining in mind, and that shapes how it feels. Warm rooms invite people to linger. A few practical ways to borrow that:

- Give guests a comfortable place to land in every room, whether a chair or a bench.
- Use lamps rather than overhead lighting for a softer glow.
- Keep surfaces clear enough to set down a plate or glass.

## A Simple Weekend Plan

If you want to try this without a renovation, here's a manageable order:

1. **Saturday morning:** Walk through your home and note any details worth highlighting. Pick one color, such as soft yellow, to repeat.
2. **Saturday afternoon:** Swap in brass or warm wood accents, like a lamp or hardware.
3. **Sunday:** Choose one small space for a floral pattern, whether wallpaper, a fabric, or a print.

The lesson from Ruff's Seattle project is that a home feels warm when its details, colors, and materials echo each other. Pick one detail you love, then repeat it in three places this week.
