Annie Meyers-Shyer, daughter of filmmaker Nancy Meyers, has gone from renovating privately to being a decor influencer through her This Oak House project, per Vanity Fair. The magazine connects her Beverly Hills renovation to the "Nancy Meyers aesthetic" and argues that it is less a passing trend than a design language handed down through the family.

That's useful news if you love the look but worry your home will end up a beige showroom. Here is how to borrow the spirit of it, using the ingredients Vanity Fair highlights: cozy California interiors, antiques, natural materials, and rooms that reveal character.

## Start With the Idea, Not the Paint Color

It's tempting to reduce the look to a shopping list: soft neutral walls, a big sofa, something marble. Vanity Fair's framing points the other way. The aesthetic is about homes that tell you who lives there.

So before you buy anything, ask one question about each room: what does this space say about the people in it? If the answer is "nothing," that's your project. If it's "they love to cook" or "they read at night," build around that.

## Let Personal History Do the Decorating

The piece describes the look as rooted in character, and character is hard to buy new. Some doable ways to bring it in:

- **Display what you already own.** Stack the cookbooks you actually use on the counter. Hang family photos or a child's drawing in a good frame.
- **Use heirlooms on purpose.** A grandparent's side table or a mismatched set of mugs reads as story, not clutter, when it has a spot of its own.
- **Edit, don't empty.** Keep the things with meaning and clear away the things you merely tolerate.

This is also the most affordable route. Meaning costs nothing.

## Choose Natural Materials and Texture

Vanity Fair names antiques and natural materials as part of the Meyers design language, and it mentions wood and marble among the defining elements. You don't need a marble kitchen to get the effect. Think in terms of surfaces that feel real:

- A wooden cutting board or bowl left out on the counter
- A wood dining table with a few visible marks of life
- Linen or cotton throws and cushions in layered textures
- A small marble piece, such as a tray or a pastry slab, if a full renovation isn't in the budget

That last idea is my own suggestion, not something from the profile. The principle is simple: mix a few textures so the room feels collected over time rather than ordered in one afternoon.

## Add One Antique, Not a Whole Room of Them

Antiques help a space feel as though it has a past. You don't have to hunt down a full set. Pick one anchor piece, such as a vintage chest, a worn wooden bench, or an older mirror, and let newer furniture support it. Secondhand shops and estate sales are the usual places to look, and a single good find can change the mood of a room more than a cart full of accessories.

## Cozy Is Not the Same as Quiet Luxury

One angle in the coverage contrasts polished "quiet luxury" with a truly lived-in, cozy home. The difference is easy to feel. A room built to impress asks you not to touch anything. A cozy room invites you to sit down, put your feet up, and leave a book open on the arm of the chair.

A quick test: can you picture someone making tea in this room and not worrying about it? If so, you're on track.

## Make It Livable, Not Cinematic

Part of the appeal, according to Vanity Fair, is the fantasy of movie-set interiors, and Meyers-Shyer's new book aims to translate that fantasy into real renovation and styling advice. Real homes need real function, so keep these in mind:

- **Seating first.** Choose comfortable pieces you'll use daily over ones that only photograph well.
- **Warm light.** Add a lamp or two at eye level so evenings feel soft instead of overhead-bright.
- **Room to be messy.** Baskets, trays, and closed storage keep everyday items corralled without hiding your life.

## A Simple Weekend Plan

1. **Saturday morning:** Walk through each room and note one item with a story. Give it a place of honor.
2. **Saturday afternoon:** Remove three things that feel generic or purely decorative.
3. **Sunday:** Add one natural texture, such as a wood bowl, a linen throw, or a vintage find, and one warm lamp.

That's enough to shift a room's mood without repainting or buying new furniture.

## The Takeaway

The Nancy Meyers look isn't a paint swatch. Choose one thing in your home that carries a memory, give it a visible spot today, and build the rest of the room around it.
