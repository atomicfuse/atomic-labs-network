Some of us are not built for the big purge. Maybe you once tossed a cord and then needed it a month later. Maybe you just like your things. BuzzFeed recently rounded up 33 products under $25 for people who would rather organize than declutter, and the idea behind it is worth borrowing: you can make a space work better without getting rid of half of it.

This guide gives you a room-by-room approach to organizing what you already own, using small, inexpensive tools. We won't recommend specific products here, since the point is the method. You can shop for the tools that suit your space and your budget.

## Start With the Rule: Contain, Don't Cull

Decluttering asks "Do I need this?" Organizing asks "Where does this live?" That second question is easier to answer, and it keeps you from regretting a toss.

You'll still make a few easy calls along the way (an empty box, a dried-out pen), but you aren't aiming for a minimalist reset. You're aiming for a home where things are findable.

### Group before you buy anything

Pull everything from one drawer or shelf and sort it into piles of like items. Only then measure the space and decide what kind of container you need. Buying bins first is how people end up with a stack of the wrong size.

## The Kitchen: Drawers and Cabinets

Cluttered cabinets and drawers are where inexpensive organizers earn their keep, because the problem is usually depth and height, not quantity.

- **Drawers:** Use shallow trays or dividers so utensils, tools, and gadgets each have a lane.
- **Cabinets:** A simple shelf riser creates a second level, so you can see the plates or mugs at the back.
- **Pantry items:** Group by use, such as baking, snacks, and breakfast, and corral each group in an open bin you can pull out like a drawer.

Measure first. A quick tape measure check saves a return trip.

## The Entryway and Closet: Give Everyday Things a Home

The stuff you grab on the way out the door needs a visible, easy landing spot.

### Entryway

A hook or two, a small tray for keys and wallet, and a basket for shoes will handle most of the pile-up. Keep it simple enough that putting things away takes under five seconds. If it takes longer, it won't happen.

### Closet

Slim hangers, hanging shelves, and small bins for accessories let you keep what you own while seeing it clearly. Fold-and-bin storage works well for seasonal items you aren't ready to part with.

## The Bathroom: Corral the Small Stuff

Beauty and personal care items multiply quietly. Clear containers or small baskets let you sort by routine: morning, evening, hair, and extras. Keep daily items at the front and backups lower down or in a separate bin.

A tip for anyone with a lot of products: store a single week's worth where you use it, and keep the rest together elsewhere. It makes the counter feel calmer without throwing anything away.

## The Desk and Cord Corner: Keep the Cords

Here's the spot where organizing beats decluttering outright. Rather than guessing which cable you might need, keep them and make them identifiable.

1. Gather every cord and charger in one place.
2. Coil each one and secure it with a tie or clip.
3. Label it with a bit of tape saying what it's for.
4. Store them together in one box or pouch.

The cost is small, and you never again wonder what that mystery cable does.

## Renter-Friendly Tweaks

If you can't drill or repaint, focus on things that sit, hang over, or stack. Over-the-door hooks, freestanding shelves, tension-style solutions, and stackable bins add storage without changing the walls. Check your lease before using adhesive products, and look for options labeled removable.

## Make the Change Visible

Take a quick before photo of the messiest drawer or shelf, then another after. It's a small thing, but seeing the difference makes it easier to keep going, and it shows which tools actually helped.

## Pick One Spot Today

Don't try to do the whole house. Choose the one drawer, shelf, or corner that irritates you most, sort it into groups, measure it, and find a container that fits. One finished spot will tell you more about what works for you than any list of 33 will.
