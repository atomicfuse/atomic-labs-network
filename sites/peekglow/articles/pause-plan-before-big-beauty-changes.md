Celebrity makeup artists, manicurists, estheticians, and hairstylists see a lot of beauty regret. InStyle recently gathered the regrets these pros encounter most, and two stood out in the list shared with us: impulsively cut bangs and overdone injectables.

The common thread is speed. Trends move fast on TikTok, and cosmetic treatments have become more mainstream, so it's easier than ever to make an appearance decision in an afternoon. Some of those decisions take months or longer to undo. Here is a practical way to slow down just enough to choose changes you'll still like later, without giving up the fun of trying something new.

## Why the hard-to-reverse changes deserve a pause

A new lip color comes off with a wipe. A haircut or a cosmetic treatment doesn't. Per InStyle's reporting, the pros' regrets cluster around choices that are difficult to reverse. That gives you a useful sorting question: if I hate this tomorrow, how long until it's gone?

Use that question as a filter:

- **Washes off in a day:** try freely (lipstick, a blush shade, a hair accessory).
- **Takes weeks or months to change:** plan first (cuts, color, nail changes).
- **Hard to undo at all:** slow way down and get professional input (injectables).

## Bangs: try the idea before you try the scissors

Impulsively cut bangs are one of the two regrets the experts flagged. Bangs are a classic spur-of-the-moment move, and they're also a commitment: growing them out takes patience.

A few low-risk ways to test the idea first:

1. **Sleep on it.** If you still want them after a few days, that's a better sign than a late-night urge.
2. **Fake them.** Clip-in bangs, or pinning your hair forward in a mirror, show you the general shape and how it sits with your face.
3. **Book a consultation.** Ask your stylist whether your hair type and daily routine suit bangs. You'll be styling them often, so it helps to ask what upkeep to expect.
4. **Start conservative.** Hair can always be cut shorter, never longer. Ask for a longer, softer version you can adjust later.

The point isn't to talk yourself out of it. It's to make sure the bangs you get are ones you chose, not ones a trend chose for you.

## Injectables: a decision for a calm day

The other regret on the list is overdone injectables. We'll keep this part general, because specific medical guidance is beyond what we can responsibly offer here, and a licensed professional is the right person to answer it for your face and health.

What we can say is that the pattern is familiar: a trend makes a treatment look quick and casual, and it's easy to do more than you intended. A few habits that support a more considered choice:

- **Don't decide in the moment.** Treat the first appointment as a conversation, not a commitment.
- **Ask a lot of questions.** Ask the provider about their training, what results to realistically expect, and what happens if you don't like the outcome.
- **Go gradual.** If you're unsure, ask whether a smaller approach is an option.
- **Bring your own goal, not a trend.** Know what you want to change and why before you walk in.

If any of this feels rushed or pressured, that's your cue to leave and think it over.

## A simple pause plan for any trend

When something catches your eye online, run it through these steps:

1. **Save it, don't act on it.** Bookmark the idea and look again in a week.
2. **Ask what you like about it.** Is it the color, the shape, the confidence it seems to bring? That tells you what to ask for.
3. **Check your real life.** Consider your morning routine, your budget, and how much upkeep you'll tolerate.
4. **Talk to a pro you trust.** Good stylists, nail techs, and estheticians will tell you honestly what suits you.
5. **Start with the smallest version.** You can always go further later.

## Keep the fun in the small stuff

None of this means playing it safe forever. Everyday beauty is a pleasure, and plenty of experiments are low-stakes: a new nail shade, a different part in your hair, a fresh lip color. Save your caution for the changes that stick around.

The next time a big beauty idea hits, give it a week. If you still love it, book the consultation and go in with a clear picture of what you want.
