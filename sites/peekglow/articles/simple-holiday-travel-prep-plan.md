Holiday travel has a way of arriving faster than anyone expects. Buzzfeed's shopping team recently rounded up 38 travel products to grab before the season gets busy, with a simple message: prep early, and the trip feels easier when crowds, delays, and last-minute packing show up.

The roundup's full product details aren't something we can pass along here, so this article doesn't recommend specific items. What it offers instead is the method behind the idea: a short, doable plan for figuring out what you actually need, so you buy a few useful things early and skip the panic purchases later.

## Why Buying Early Makes Sense

Per the reporting, the logic is straightforward. Holiday travel planning starts earlier each year, and shoppers tend to look for practical buys before prices rise, shipping slows, or essentials sell out. That's a reasonable instinct, though it only pays off if you buy things you'll use. An early purchase you never touch is just clutter with a head start.

So before you add anything to a cart, spend ten minutes on the next step.

## Step 1: Name Your Biggest Headache

Think back to your last trip. What went wrong, or at least felt annoying? Pick the top one or two:

- Digging through a bag at security
- Overstuffed luggage that never closes
- Tangled cords and a dying phone
- A cluttered car with kids' snacks everywhere
- Arriving and realizing you forgot a small basic

The Buzzfeed roundup is built around the same idea: products that make trips smoother, more organized, and less stressful. Your list tells you which of those categories matters to you. Skip the rest.

## Step 2: Sort Your Trip Into Three Zones

A simple way to find gaps is to divide your trip into zones and ask what's missing in each.

### The Getting-There Zone

This is your airport or road-trip day. Think about what you reach for most: documents, phone, charger, snacks, something to do. Everything here should be within arm's reach, not buried in a suitcase.

### The Packing Zone

This is where overpackers and families feel it most. If your bag is always chaos, the fix is usually structure, such as ways to separate clothes, shoes, and toiletries so you can find things without unpacking everything. Check what you already own first. A few zip pouches or spare bags can do the job.

### The Staying Zone

Once you arrive, what makes a borrowed bed or a relative's guest room feel comfortable? Maybe it's a small comfort item or a way to keep your things tidy. Keep this list short and personal.

## Step 3: Set a Budget Before You Browse

Shopping roundups are easy to get swept up in, especially a list of 38. Set a number first. Something like a total you'd be happy to spend across two or three items keeps you honest. If you're buying for someone else, the same rule applies: travel finds make friendly, giftable presents precisely because small items can fit many budgets.

## Step 4: Buy Fewer Things, Then Test Them

Here's the part that makes early shopping worth it. Once your items arrive, use them before the trip:

1. Pack a bag with your new organizers and see whether everything fits.
2. Do a practice run of your carry-on, from the way you'd pull out your phone and documents to where you'd keep a charger.
3. Check that anything you bought works the way you expected.

If something doesn't, you still have time to return or swap it. That's the real advantage of shopping before the rush: a mistake costs you an errand, not a stressful trip.

## Step 5: Build a Reusable Checklist

Write down what worked. A short note on your phone titled "Holiday trip" does the job. List the items that earned their space and the small things you forgot. Next year, you won't be starting from scratch, and you'll probably buy less.

## A Quick Word on Claims

Product pages love big promises. Treat words like "effortless" or "stress-free" as marketing, not guarantees. A good travel product solves one small problem well. That's plenty.

## The Takeaway

Before you scroll any roundup, write down the one thing that annoyed you most on your last trip, then shop only for that. Future you will be grateful for a solved problem, not a full cart.
