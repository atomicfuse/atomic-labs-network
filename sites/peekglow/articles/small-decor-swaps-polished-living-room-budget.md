A living room rarely feels unfinished because of one big thing. More often it's the small layer that's missing: something on the coffee table, a rug underfoot, frames on a shelf. Real Simple recently shared a round-up from an interior designer of 11 budget-friendly living room pieces, all sold on Amazon, with prices starting at $9. The full list of 11 isn't spelled out here, but the categories that were named are enough to work from. Here's how to use each one so your room looks more pulled together.

## Start with what you touch and see most: the coffee table

The round-up includes coffee table books and coasters, two items that earn their spot because they do a job and add style at once.

### Coffee table books

A stack of books gives a table height and a focal point. Some simple ways to make it work:

- Stack two or three, largest on the bottom.
- Choose subjects you actually enjoy: travel, food, art, gardening. The room should reflect you.
- Set a small object on top, like a candle or a little dish, to finish the stack.

### Coasters

Coasters are an easy, low-cost upgrade, and they protect your table surface too. Keep a set in a small tray or bowl on the table so they're always within reach. A matching set looks intentional, which is half of what "designer" really means.

## Ground the room with an area rug

Area rugs are on the designer's list, and a rug is often the piece that makes a seating area feel like a defined space rather than furniture scattered on a floor. If you rent, it's also one of the most effective ways to add color, pattern, and softness without touching the walls or floors.

A few practical checks before you buy:

- Measure your seating area first, and tape the rug's outline on the floor to preview it.
- Check the product page for dimensions and pile height, so it suits your furniture and your foot traffic.
- Read recent reviews for notes on how it lies flat and how it cleans.

I can't tell you which specific rug the designer chose, so treat these as shopping habits rather than a product endorsement.

## Add a side table where you need a surface

Side tables are also on the list. They give you a spot for a lamp, a mug, or a book beside the sofa or an armchair. A room without one tends to feel less cozy, because there's nowhere to set anything down.

Placement matters more than price here. Aim for a table roughly level with the arm of your sofa or chair, so a drink is easy to reach. Small, light tables are easy to move around, which helps in a rental or a room you like to rearrange.

## Frame something you love

Picture frames round out the named categories. They're one of the cheapest ways to make a room feel personal rather than staged.

- Frame family photos, a postcard, a child's drawing, or a print you already own.
- Keep frames in a consistent finish, such as all black or all wood, so a mixed collection still looks cohesive.
- Lean a few on a shelf or console instead of hanging them if you'd rather not drill holes.

## Think in layers, not shopping lists

The round-up describes the rest as "more living room decor pieces," so I won't guess at what they are. But the pattern in what's named is useful on its own. Each item adds a different layer:

1. **Floor:** the rug
2. **Surfaces:** side tables, coffee table books, coasters
3. **Walls and shelves:** picture frames

If your room feels flat, ask which layer is missing. You may need only one or two purchases rather than a full makeover.

## A simple budget plan

With prices starting at $9, you can refresh a room in stages instead of all at once. Start with the cheapest swaps, such as coasters and frames. Live with them for a week. Then decide whether the room needs a larger piece like a rug or side table. Prices on Amazon change often, so check the current listing before you commit.

No small decor piece will transform a room on its own, and that's fine. A few thoughtful additions can make a space feel warmer and more finished.

## Your next step

Stand in the doorway of your living room tonight and note the one layer that looks emptiest: floor, surfaces, or walls. Fix just that one first.
