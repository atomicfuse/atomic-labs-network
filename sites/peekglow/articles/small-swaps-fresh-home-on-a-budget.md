BuzzFeed Shopping recently rounded up 23 Walmart products meant to give your home a glow-up, pitched around practical picks at accessible prices. The full list isn't reproduced here, so this piece does something different: it gives you a room-by-room method for using any budget roundup well, so you spend on the changes that show and skip the ones that don't.

By the end, you'll have a simple plan for refreshing one room this weekend without a renovation, whether you rent or own.

## Start With One Room, Not the Whole House

The most common budget-refresh mistake is buying a little of everything. A cart of random items spreads thin and rarely changes how a space feels.

Pick the room you see most, usually the living room or bedroom. Stand in the doorway and notice the first thing that bothers you. Clutter on a surface? Harsh lighting? A bare wall? Fix that one thing first. A single clear improvement does more than five scattered ones.

## Swap Soft Things First

Textiles are the cheapest way to change a room's mood, and they're easy to undo if you rent.

- **Throw pillow covers:** Covers cost less than full pillows, and you can reuse the inserts you already own.
- **A throw blanket:** Drape it over a sofa arm or the foot of the bed for instant coziness.
- **Curtains:** Hanging them higher and wider than the window frame can make a window look larger. Choose a fabric that matches your wall tone or sits just a shade off.

Keep to two or three colors across these pieces. A repeated color ties a room together and makes inexpensive items look intentional.

## Fix the Lighting

Lighting changes how everything else looks. Overhead light alone tends to flatten a room.

Aim for at least three light sources at different heights: a ceiling or floor lamp, a table lamp, and something small and warm, like a lamp on a shelf. Check the bulb packaging for warm white, which gives a softer, cozier glow than cool daylight bulbs. This is often the cheapest upgrade with the most noticeable payoff.

## Give Clutter a Home

A space looks more expensive when it looks calm. Baskets, trays, and simple storage bins give everyday items a landing spot.

- Put a tray on the coffee table to group remotes and candles.
- Use a lidded basket for blankets or toys.
- Add a small organizer by the door for keys and mail.

Match materials, such as woven baskets or matte neutral bins, so storage reads as decor rather than mess.

## Add One Thing on the Wall

Bare walls can make a room feel unfinished. A mirror, a framed print, or a small gallery of frames adds personality. Renters can look for removable hanging strips, but check the weight limit on the package and your lease terms before you stick anything up.

Tip: lay your frames on the floor first and arrange them until the spacing feels right, then hang. Keep about two inches between frames for a tidy look.

## Bring in Something Living (or Living-Looking)

A plant adds texture and color for very little money. If you don't have a green thumb, pick a forgiving variety and check the care tag. A good-quality faux plant works too, as long as it sits in a nice pot rather than its plastic nursery container.

## How to Shop a Roundup Without Overspending

Whether you're scanning BuzzFeed's list or Walmart's own aisles, a few habits help:

1. **Measure first.** Know your wall, shelf, and window dimensions before you buy.
2. **Read recent reviews.** Look for photos from real customers and comments on size and color accuracy.
3. **Set a cap per room.** Choose a number, say $50 or $100, before you start browsing.
4. **Buy in stages.** Add one or two pieces, live with them for a few days, then decide what's next.
5. **Check return policies.** Colors can look different in person than on screen.

Prices and availability change often, so confirm the current price before you plan your budget around any listed item.

## A Simple Weekend Plan

**Saturday morning:** Clear surfaces and decide your two or three colors.
**Saturday afternoon:** Swap pillow covers, add a throw, and set up a lamp with a warm bulb.
**Sunday:** Hang one piece of art, add a tray or basket, and place a plant.

That's a handful of small changes, and together they can make a room feel noticeably more finished.

## Your Next Step

Choose one room, write down the one thing that bugs you most, and fix only that this weekend. Let the rest of the refresh follow from there.
