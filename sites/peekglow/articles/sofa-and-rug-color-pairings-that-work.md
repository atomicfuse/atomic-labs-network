A sofa and a rug are two of the biggest purchases in a living room, and they have to get along. If you're staring at a sofa and wondering what goes underneath it, these five pairings, highlighted by Livingetc, give you a dependable starting point for building a whole room.

The main idea is a relief for anyone who dreads matching swatches: you don't need an exact match. Per Livingetc, the better approach is to focus on undertones, warmth, texture, and how the colors "talk" to each other. Pieces that share a mood look intentional, even when the colors differ.

## Start With Undertone, Not Color Name

Before picking a pair, figure out whether your sofa leans warm or cool. A warm cream and a cool white can both be called "neutral" and still clash side by side. Hold a rug sample next to your sofa in daylight and ask whether they feel like they belong to the same family. If one looks yellow-toned and the other looks gray-toned, keep looking.

This is also why the pairings below work: each one has colors that share a temperature or deliberately balance each other.

## 1. Red and Blue

This is the boldest combination on the list. Contrast is the point: a blue sofa with a red-toned rug (or the reverse) gives the room energy and a curated feel.

To keep it grown-up rather than loud, try these ideas:

- Choose muted or deeper versions of each color instead of primary brights.
- Pick a rug with a pattern that blends the two shades, so the colors feel connected.
- Let the rest of the room stay quiet, with plain walls and simple wood tones.

## 2. Earthy Brown and Muted Olive

Browns and olives are part of the richer, warmer palette showing up across interiors right now, per the trend context in Livingetc's coverage. Both come from nature, so they sit together easily.

An olive sofa on a brown-toned rug feels grounded and calm. Flip it, with a brown sofa and an olive rug, and you get the same mood with a different focal point. If brown worries you because it can read muddy, add lighter elements nearby, such as pale curtains or a light coffee table, so the palette has room to breathe.

## 3. Ivory and Umber

This is the timeless, tonal option. Ivory is soft and light; umber is a deep, warm brown. Together they create a gentle contrast without any one color shouting.

Try an ivory sofa over an umber rug for a grounded base, or a rich umber sofa with an ivory rug to lighten the floor. Either way, texture does a lot of work here. A chunky weave or nubby upholstery keeps a low-contrast palette from feeling flat.

## 4. Camel, Cognac, and Oatmeal

If you want cozy, this is the one. All three shades sit in the same warm, golden-brown family, so they layer without effort. Think a camel sofa, an oatmeal rug, and a cognac accent like a leather chair or a throw.

Because the colors are so close, vary the textures: a smooth leather, a woolly rug, a linen cushion. That variety is what makes a tonal room look layered instead of beige-on-beige.

## 5. Pink and Brown

Dusty pinks are also showing up across current interiors, and brown is the grown-up partner that keeps them from feeling sweet. A dusty pink sofa on a brown rug, or a brown sofa with a soft pink rug, reads warm and a little unexpected.

Keep the pink muted and dusty rather than bright, and let the brown be earthy. The result is playful but still calm.

## How to Choose Based on Mood

If you're not sure which pairing fits, pick by the feeling you want:

- **Bold:** red and blue
- **Calming:** brown and olive
- **Timeless:** ivory and umber
- **Cozy:** camel, cognac, and oatmeal
- **Soft with an edge:** pink and brown

## A Simple Way to Test Before You Buy

Since these are big, high-impact pieces, check before committing. Ask for fabric or rug samples and lay them together at home. Look at them in morning light and again in the evening, since warm and cool undertones shift with the light. If you can only change one piece, start with your existing sofa and treat these pairings as a menu for the rug.

Your next step: take a photo of your sofa in daylight, then compare it against the five pairings above and pick the mood you want the room to have. The rug choice gets much easier from there.
