BuzzFeed Shopping recently rounded up 27 versatile clothing pieces meant to carry you through the rest of the year, pitching clothes that work across many seasons as an excellent return on investment. The full list isn't reproduced here, but the idea behind it is worth borrowing right now: buy less, style more.

You don't need 27 new things to do that. You need a few adaptable pieces and a repeatable way to style them. Here's a simple method you can try with items already in your closet, plus a few guidelines for choosing anything new.

## Start With the One-Piece, Three-Ways Test

Before you buy anything, pick one piece you already own and try to build three distinct outfits around it. If you can't get to three, that piece is probably not pulling its weight. If you can, you've found a staple.

A plain button-up shirt, a mid-weight cardigan, or a pair of dark straight-leg pants all work well for this. Here's how it plays out with a simple knit sweater in a neutral shade:

### 1. The work version
Wear it with tailored trousers or a midi skirt and flat boots or loafers. Tuck the front hem in for a more polished line.

### 2. The weekend version
Pair it with relaxed jeans and sneakers. Push the sleeves up and leave it untucked.

### 3. The layered version
Put a collared shirt underneath so the collar and cuffs peek out, then add a coat on top. This is your answer for colder days, and it makes the same sweater look like a completely different outfit.

Take a photo of each combination. A small folder on your phone becomes a quick reference on mornings when nothing seems to work.

## Plan for Unpredictable Weather With Layers

Transitional weather is where versatile clothing earns its keep. A morning that starts chilly and turns mild by afternoon is easier when each layer can stand alone.

Try building outfits in three parts:

- **A base layer** you'd be comfortable wearing alone, like a tee or fitted long-sleeve top.
- **A middle layer** such as a cardigan, vest, or lightweight sweater.
- **An outer layer** you can take off easily, like a jacket or trench.

If every piece looks good with the other two, you can shed or add as the day changes without ending up with a mismatched outfit.

## Choose Pieces That Cross Occasions

The most useful clothes move between work, weekends, and travel without a fuss. When you're shopping, run each candidate through a few quick questions:

- Could I wear this to the office and to dinner afterward?
- Does it work with at least three things I already own?
- Can I dress it up with a belt, jewelry, or different shoes, and dress it down with sneakers?
- Would I still want to wear it if the weather swung ten degrees either way?

If the answer to most of these is yes, it's a strong candidate. If you'd need to buy a new top, new shoes, and a new bag just to make it work, it's not versatile. It's a project.

## Stick to a Cohesive Color Palette

Color is the quiet reason some wardrobes feel effortless. When most of your pieces share a handful of tones, nearly everything mixes with everything else.

A practical approach:

1. Pick two or three base neutrals, such as black, navy, cream, gray, or camel.
2. Add one or two accent colors you genuinely like wearing.
3. Let patterns and texture, like ribbed knits or a subtle stripe, add interest without clashing.

This also makes budget shopping easier. You'll spot quickly whether a sale item fits your palette, which helps you skip the impulse buys that never get worn.

## Make Affordable Pieces Look More Considered

Budget-friendly clothes can look polished when you pay attention to a few details. None of these require spending more.

- **Fit first.** A piece that fits your shoulders and length well looks better than a pricier one that doesn't. A small tailoring adjustment, like hemming pants, can make a big difference.
- **Care for fabric.** Steam or press wrinkles, remove lint, and shave pilling off knits.
- **Keep shoes clean.** Wiped-down boots or sneakers make the whole outfit look intentional.
- **Choose simple silhouettes.** Clean lines tend to look timeless and pair easily with other pieces.

## Do a Ten-Minute Closet Audit

Set a timer and pull out everything you've worn in the past month. Then look at what's left and ask why it sits there. Common reasons include poor fit, difficult-to-match colors, or being too specific to one occasion.

From there, write a short list of gaps, not wants. For example: "a layer for 50-degree mornings" or "shoes that work with both skirts and pants." Take that list shopping, whether you browse a roundup like BuzzFeed's or head to a secondhand shop. A list keeps you focused on filling real needs instead of collecting more one-off outfits.

## A Sample Mini Capsule for the Rest of the Year

If you want a starting framework, here's a small set that could produce a surprising number of outfits. This is a suggested template, not a prescription:

- Two pairs of pants (one dressier, one casual)
- One skirt or dress that works with tights and boots
- Three tops in your base neutrals
- Two sweaters or cardigans
- One jacket or coat
- Two pairs of shoes

That's eleven pieces. Mix and match them and you'll have far more outfits than items, especially once you add scarves, belts, or jewelry you already own.

## Your Next Step

Tonight, pick one piece from your closet and build three outfits around it. Photograph them. If the piece passes, you've just saved yourself a purchase. If it doesn't, you've learned exactly what to look for next time.
