BuzzFeed Shopping recently published a roundup of 28 Walmart organizers aimed at taming junk piles and making everyday spaces more functional. The post's tagline: "It's time to conquer those mountains." We can't reproduce the individual picks here, so this guide does something else: it gives you a simple method for clearing a clutter pile, and a way to shop that roundup (or any budget aisle) without buying a pile of empty containers.

By the end, you'll know how to reset one problem spot in an afternoon, spend only on what it needs, and keep it from creeping back.

## Start With One Pile, Not the Whole House

The fastest way to feel overwhelmed is to look at every cluttered corner at once. Pick a single spot: the junk drawer, the entryway table, the closet shelf, the corner of the counter. Set a timer for 30 to 45 minutes. A small, finishable goal beats a grand plan you abandon on Saturday afternoon.

## Empty It Completely and Sort Into Four Groups

Pull everything out. Yes, everything. Seeing it all on a table or the floor shows you what you actually own. Then sort quickly into four groups:

- **Keep here:** things you use in this spot regularly.
- **Keep elsewhere:** items that landed here by accident.
- **Donate or pass along:** working things you no longer use.
- **Toss or recycle:** dried-out pens, broken gadgets, mystery cords.

Don't agonize. If you can't decide in about ten seconds, put it in a "maybe" bag, label it with today's date, and revisit it in a few weeks.

## Measure Before You Shop

This step saves the most money. Before browsing any organizer roundup, write down:

1. The width, depth, and height of the space (drawer, shelf, cabinet).
2. What you're storing, in rough categories (for example: batteries and tape, school supplies, hair accessories).
3. How many of each category you're keeping.

Now you're shopping for a specific fit instead of a vague feeling of tidiness. A bin that's an inch too wide is just another thing to store.

## Match the Organizer to the Problem

Budget retailers like Walmart tend to carry a wide range of storage types, so it helps to know what job you're hiring for. Here's how to think about it:

### For drawers: dividers and small trays
Junk drawers get messy because everything shares one open space. Dividing it into zones gives each category a home. Choose trays that fit snugly so they don't slide around.

### For shelves and closets: bins and baskets
Use containers that hold loose items together so a shelf reads as a few neat blocks instead of dozens of small objects. Matching containers make a space look calmer, though you don't need a perfect set. Even two or three that coordinate help.

### For small spaces: go vertical
If floor and counter space is limited, look at what's above and behind you. Over-the-door storage, wall-mounted racks, and stackable bins use space you already have. Check the weight limits on the packaging before loading anything heavy.

### For entryways: a landing spot
A tray for keys, a hook for bags, and a single basket for odds and ends can stop a daily pile from forming. Keep it to what the household actually carries out the door.

## Buy Less Than You Think You Need

It's tempting to fill a cart with a dozen organizers because each one is inexpensive. Resist that. A better approach:

- Sort and purge first, so you know the real volume of stuff.
- Buy for one spot only.
- Try inexpensive stand-ins first, like shoe boxes or jars you already own, to test a layout before spending.
- Keep receipts and check the return policy in case something doesn't fit.

A roundup of 28 options is useful for browsing, but your measurements decide which ones deserve a spot in your cart. Choose the few that solve a problem you've written down.

## Make It Easy to Maintain

An organized space only stays that way if putting things back is effortless. A few habits help:

- **Label what's not obvious.** Masking tape and a marker work fine.
- **Leave a little breathing room.** A drawer packed to the brim won't stay tidy for long.
- **Do a two-minute reset.** Once a week, return strays to their homes.
- **Follow a one-in, one-out rule** for categories that tend to overflow.

## Take a Quick Before-and-After

Snap a photo of the mess before you start and another when you finish. It's satisfying, and it makes the next spot feel more doable. Organizing doesn't need a renovation, a big budget, or a whole weekend. It needs a measuring tape, a timer, and one pile.

Pick that pile today, set the timer, and empty it out.
