Exposed bulbs, sleek windows, raw materials: industrial style looks sharp, but it can feel a little chilly once the weather turns. If your space leans that way, Jennifer Aniston's dining room offers a useful blueprint for warming it up without a renovation.

Per Homes & Gardens, the actress's Beverly Hills dining room, designed by Stephen Shadley, pairs industrial elements like exposed bulbs and sleek windows with natural materials. The result is described as cocooning. The takeaway for the rest of us: you don't have to give up the industrial look. You balance it.

Below are the warming ingredients highlighted in the piece, with simple ways to try each one at home.

## Start With Wood

Wood is the quickest way to take the edge off metal, glass, and concrete. Its grain and color read as natural and soft next to hard, cool surfaces.

Ways to use it:

- Choose a wooden dining table or add wooden chairs if you have a metal-heavy set.
- Add a wood tray, bowl, or cutting board as a centerpiece.
- If you're keeping your budget low, secondhand wood pieces are an easy place to start.

You only need a few wooden pieces to shift the mood of the room. Notice where your eye lands first, and put wood there.

## Bring In Stone

Stone is another natural material the design leans on. It still feels solid and earthy, but it has a more organic character than polished metal.

You don't need a stone countertop. Think small: a stone bowl, a pair of stone candleholders, or a marble-look tray on the table. Pairing stone with wood adds variety in texture, which is what keeps a moody room from feeling flat.

## Layer a Rug Underfoot

Industrial spaces often come with hard floors, and hard floors can feel echoey and cold. A rug is the fix. It softens the look and the sound of the room, and it anchors the dining table so the space feels intentional.

A few practical pointers:

- Pick a rug large enough that chairs stay on it when pulled out.
- Look for a warm, muted tone rather than a bright one if you want that cocooning feel.
- Choose texture over pattern if your room already has a lot going on.

## Add Velvet for Softness

Velvet is a small touch with a big payoff. Its plush surface and the way it catches light make it a natural contrast to raw, hard materials.

You can test it cheaply: velvet cushions on dining chairs, a velvet table runner, or a set of velvet napkins. If you love the result, you can go bigger later with upholstered seating.

## Light the Room Warmly

Exposed bulbs are a hallmark of the room Homes & Gardens describes, and they can look stark if the light is harsh. The lesson is in the glow. Warm lighting makes the same industrial fixtures feel inviting rather than clinical.

Try these tweaks:

- Swap cool-white bulbs for warm-toned ones.
- Use a dimmer if your fixture allows it.
- Add a lamp or two at lower levels so the room isn't lit only from overhead.

Lower, softer light is a big part of the moody feeling. Dinner just looks better by it.

## Finish With Candles

Candles are the easiest finishing touch on the list. They add flicker and warmth and make an everyday meal feel a little special. Group a few in different heights on your wood or stone surfaces, and keep an eye on them whenever they're lit.

## A Simple Weekend Plan

You don't have to do everything at once. Here's one way to layer it:

1. **Swap your bulbs** for warm-toned ones and see how the room changes.
2. **Add one natural material** such as a wood tray or stone bowl on the table.
3. **Put a rug down** if your floor is bare.
4. **Bring in velvet** through cushions or a runner.
5. **Finish with candles** for the evening.

That's five small steps, most of them affordable, and each one adds something the room was missing.

## Why This Look Fits the Season

As fall settles in, many people want rooms that feel cozy, textured, and lived-in rather than cool and polished. Warm industrial style gets you there while keeping what you likely already like about your space: the clean lines, the raw character, the architectural bones.

You don't need a Beverly Hills budget to borrow the idea. Start with the light bulbs tonight, and let the rest of the room warm up from there.
