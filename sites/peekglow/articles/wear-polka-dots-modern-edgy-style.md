Polka dots have a reputation: sweet, vintage, maybe a little picnic-ready. This week, Kim Kardashian offered a very different take, and it comes with a few ideas you can borrow for a much more everyday wardrobe.

Per Elle, Kardashian wore a dramatic black-and-white polka-dot gown by Galia Lahav to the Rome premiere of *All's Fair* season 2. The piece comes from the designer's "One of One" bridal couture collection, and it uses dramatic hip cutouts to turn a classic print into something sculptural. Elle's take: this is not your typical polka-dot dress.

You probably aren't heading to a Rome premiere. But the thinking behind the look translates nicely to a closet that's far more attainable. Here's how.

## Start With Contrast, Not Cuteness

The gown is black and white, which is about as graphic as dots get. Pastels and small, soft dots read playful. High contrast reads sharper and more deliberate.

To try this at home:

- Choose a black-and-white dot over a colored one.
- Look at how the print is scaled. Larger dots tend to feel bolder and more modern than tiny ones.
- Pick a clean, simple shape so the pattern is the only thing talking.

## Let the Shape Do the Edgy Work

What makes Kardashian's gown feel unexpected isn't the print. It's the structure: those hip cutouts, which Elle highlights, give the dots a sculptural, red-carpet attitude. Same pattern, different cut, completely different mood.

You don't need cutouts to borrow the principle. Look for a silhouette that feels intentional rather than frilly:

- A fitted midi skirt with a sleek waist
- A shirt with sharp tailoring instead of a ruffled collar
- A column dress with a clean neckline

If a polka-dot piece has bows, puffed sleeves and a flared skirt, it leans sweet. Strip those details away and the same print starts to look sophisticated.

## Keep Accessories Quiet

When a print is the star, everything else should step back. Elle credits the look to Kardashian working with stylist Law Roach, and the takeaway for your own outfits is simple: a strong pattern doesn't need much company.

For daily wear, that could mean:

1. One metal tone only, such as simple gold or silver earrings.
2. A solid-color bag in black, cream or tan.
3. Shoes in a single neutral shade.

Before you leave the house, take one accessory off. With a statement print, that last removal often makes the outfit look more polished.

## Treat Polka Dots as a Neutral

Here's a practical way to think about it: a black-and-white dot behaves a lot like a stripe. It pairs easily with solids you already own.

- **With denim:** a dotted blouse and straight-leg jeans feels relaxed but put together.
- **With black:** a dotted top under a black blazer gives the print a serious backdrop.
- **With cream or camel:** a dotted scarf or skirt against warm neutrals feels softer without turning cutesy.

You don't need to buy anything new to test this. Check whether you have a dotted scarf, shirt or socks hiding in a drawer, and try them with your most tailored basics.

## Why Bridal-Inspired Style Is Having a Moment

Elle points out that the gown comes from a bridal couture collection, which fits into the ongoing crossover between bridal fashion and celebrity red carpets. Bridal design tends to emphasize craftsmanship, structure and dramatic shapes, and those qualities clearly translate beyond weddings.

For everyday style, you can take a gentler version of that idea: choose pieces with good structure and interesting construction rather than lots of surface decoration. A well-cut garment in a simple print often looks more special than something overly embellished.

## A Quick Outfit Test Before You Buy

If you're tempted by a polka-dot piece, run it through these questions:

1. Is the contrast strong enough to look graphic?
2. Is the silhouette clean, without extra frills?
3. Can you style it with at least three things you already own?
4. Does it still feel like you when you picture it with minimal accessories?

If you can say yes to three, it's likely a keeper.

## The Takeaway

Next time you pick up a polka-dot piece, ignore the print and look at the cut. Choose high contrast, a sharp shape and one quiet accessory, and the most retro pattern in your closet starts to look decidedly current.
