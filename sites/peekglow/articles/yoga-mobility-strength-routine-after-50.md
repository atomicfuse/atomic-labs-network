Manisha Koirala, the 56-year-old Nepali-Indian actress, recently showed how she stays fit, and her approach is refreshingly uncomplicated. Per NDTV, her regimen combines three things: yoga, mobility exercises, and strength training. By the end of this article, you'll know how to turn that same three-part mix into a gentle weekly rhythm that fits your own life.

A quick note first: the details of Koirala's exact workouts, such as sets, reps or schedules, weren't spelled out in the coverage we're drawing from. So what follows is a practical framework inspired by her three pillars, not a copy of her plan. If you have joint issues, an injury, or a health condition, check with your doctor or a qualified trainer before starting something new.

## Why Three Types of Movement Beat One

Most of us default to a single habit. Walkers walk, yogis flow, gym people lift. Koirala's mix is appealing because each piece covers something the others don't:

- **Yoga** builds a steady, calming practice with a mix of stretching and balance.
- **Mobility exercises** are gentle, joint-friendly movements that help you move through your full range comfortably.
- **Strength training** works your muscles against resistance, which is the part many people skip.

You don't need to do all three daily. You just need all three to show up in your week.

## A Sample Week You Can Actually Keep

Here's one way to arrange the pillars. Adjust freely.

### Two strength days
Pick two non-consecutive days, say Monday and Thursday. Start with 20 to 30 minutes. Choose a few basic moves: a sit-to-stand from a chair, a wall or counter push-up, a row with a resistance band or light dumbbells, and a glute bridge on the floor. Do one or two rounds of 8 to 12 repetitions each. Use a weight that feels challenging by the last few reps but never sharp or painful.

### Two yoga days
Tuesday and Saturday work well. A 20 to 40 minute session at home with a beginner video is plenty. Focus on slow breathing and poses you can hold comfortably. Skip anything that pinches.

### Daily mobility minutes
This is the easiest habit to stack onto your day. Spend five to ten minutes after waking or before bed on gentle neck rolls, shoulder circles, hip circles, cat-cow on hands and knees, and ankle rotations. Move slowly and stop short of discomfort.

### One rest or walk day
Leave at least one day open. A relaxed walk counts and keeps the habit pleasant instead of punishing.

## Make Strength the Friendly Part

Strength training can feel intimidating, especially if you've never set foot in a gym. It doesn't have to happen there. A pair of light dumbbells or a resistance band costs little and lives happily in a closet. Begin lighter than you think you need to, learn the movement, then add weight gradually.

If you want guidance, a few sessions with a certified trainer can teach you form so you feel confident on your own. That's a small investment that pays off every week after.

## Build the Routine Into Your Space

A routine sticks when it's easy to start. Try these small touches:

- Leave a yoga mat rolled up in a visible corner, not buried in a cupboard.
- Keep your bands or weights in a basket near the spot where you'll use them.
- Put workout clothes you like on top of the pile the night before.
- Pair mobility minutes with something you already do, like your morning coffee brewing.

None of this is about a perfect studio. It's about removing the friction between you and ten good minutes.

## Design Your Routine Around Your Life

The line that stands out from Koirala's own words, as quoted by NDTV, is this: "The best part of working since my teenage years is I get to decide how I want to lead my semi-retired life." You don't need a long career or a famous name to borrow the spirit of that idea. Fitness after 50 works best when you decide what it looks like, rather than chasing a plan built for someone else.

That might mean mornings instead of evenings, 15 minutes instead of 45, or chair-based moves instead of floor work. A routine you enjoy and repeat will outperform an ambitious one you abandon in two weeks.

## Start Small This Week

Pick one pillar you've been neglecting and give it two sessions in the next seven days. If that's strength, try the five simple moves above on Monday and Thursday. Add the other two pillars as they feel natural. Build slowly, keep it kind, and let the routine grow into something that's yours.
