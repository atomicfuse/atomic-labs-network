Ankles rarely get a thank-you. They carry you up stairs, across kitchens, and through every yoga class, and most of us only notice them when something feels stiff or wobbly. This guide walks you through five yoga poses that Yoga Journal highlights for stretching, strengthening, and stabilizing the ankles, plus simple ways to make each one friendlier if your ankles feel tight.

The reasoning, per Yoga Journal, is that ankle mobility matters for yoga, for how well you move, and for preventing lower-body injuries. Stability, as the piece puts it, starts from the ground up. That makes the ankles a gentle place to begin if you want to feel steadier overall.

A quick note: this is general movement advice, not medical guidance. If you have an ankle injury or ongoing pain, check with a physical therapist or doctor before adding new stretches.

## How to Use This Routine

You don't need special gear. A mat helps, and a chair or wall nearby is useful for the balance poses. Move slowly, breathe evenly, and stop if anything feels sharp rather than like a mild stretch. Hold each pose for a few calm breaths and see how it feels. The goal is paying attention, not pushing.

## 1. Chair Pose

Chair Pose asks your ankles to flex while your feet stay planted, which is exactly the kind of ankle work a deep squat or a bent-knee stance needs. Stand with feet together or hip-width apart, bend your knees, and sit your hips back as if lowering into a chair.

**Make it easier:** Keep the bend shallow, or stand with your back near a wall so you feel supported. Spread your toes and press evenly through all four corners of each foot.

## 2. Bound Angle Pose

Sit on the floor, bring the soles of your feet together, and let your knees fall open. It's a seated pose, so your ankles get attention without having to hold your body weight. Hold your feet or ankles gently and sit tall.

**Make it easier:** Sit on a folded blanket so your hips rise above your knees. Place the feet a little farther from your body if the closer position feels intense.

## 3. Squat

The squat is one of the most honest tests of ankle flexibility. If your heels lift or you tip backward, your ankles may be asking for more mobility work. Stand with feet a little wider than your hips, toes slightly turned out, and lower down with your chest lifted.

**Make it easier:** Slide a folded blanket or towel under your heels, or hold onto a sturdy surface in front of you. Lower only as far as you can keep your heels grounded, and go a little deeper over time.

## 4. Eagle Pose

Eagle Pose is a balance challenge: you stand on one leg, wrap the other over it, and keep your knees bent. Your standing ankle makes constant tiny adjustments to keep you upright, which is how stability gets built.

**Make it easier:** Skip the full wrap. Cross one leg over the other and rest your toes on the floor, or keep a hand on a wall. Fix your gaze on one still spot to help with wobbling, and repeat on both sides.

## 5. Half Moon Pose

Half Moon is another one-legged pose, with your body extended sideways over a standing leg. It asks your supporting ankle to stay steady while the rest of you reaches outward.

**Make it easier:** Use a wall for your back or a block under your lower hand. Keep a soft bend in the standing knee. Think about spreading your standing foot evenly rather than gripping with the toes.

## Putting It Together

You can string these into a short sequence: start seated in Bound Angle to ease in, move to Squat and Chair to work on bending, then finish with Eagle and Half Moon for balance. Take a few breaths in each, and rest between poses as needed. Even a few minutes is a reasonable starting point.

## Small Habits That Support the Practice

- **Go barefoot on your mat** so your feet and ankles can move and adjust freely.
- **Warm up first.** A few gentle ankle circles before you begin help you tune in.
- **Keep a prop within reach.** A blanket, block, and wall make every pose here more approachable.
- **Notice the difference between sides.** Many people find one ankle feels tighter than the other. That's useful information, not a flaw.

## Your Next Step

Pick one pose from this list and try it tonight for a few breaths on each side. Notice where your ankles feel stiff or wobbly, then come back to that pose tomorrow. Small, regular check-ins are how steadier ground gets built.
