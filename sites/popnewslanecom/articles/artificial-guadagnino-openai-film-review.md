Luca Guadagnino has made a movie about the founding of OpenAI, and per Deadline's review, it plays like a dark comedy-thriller dressed up as a modern Frankenstein story. *Artificial* stars Andrew Garfield as Sam Altman and centers on the relationship between Altman and AI researcher Ilya Sutskever. According to Deadline, Altman exploits Sutskever's genius while the technology spirals dangerously out of control.

That's the pitch. The more interesting part is the angle that makes it work, so let's commit to one: this is a movie about a man who tells every room what it wants to hear.

## Garfield's Altman: The Man Who Changes the Story

Deadline's review frames Garfield's take on Altman as a charismatic manipulator who changes his story depending on his audience. That's a character note, not a verdict on the real person, and it's worth keeping that line clear. This is a dramatization, and the film is a dark comedy-thriller with a point of view.

Still, as a storytelling device, it's smart. Tech origin stories usually hinge on a single question: who is the visionary, and who is the one doing the heavy lifting? Here, the film reportedly answers by putting the researcher's brilliance on one side of the ledger and the salesman's charm on the other. If you've watched *The Social Network*, you know the genre well. Garfield, notably, was in that one too, which makes his casting here a knowing wink for film fans. It's hard not to enjoy the symmetry.

## The Frankenstein Frame

The Frankenstein comparison is the film's big swing. In Mary Shelley's novel, the creator builds something extraordinary and then loses control of it. Per Deadline, *Artificial* applies that template to OpenAI's founding, with the technology getting away from the people who built it.

In this telling, Sutskever is the one who goes on the emotional journey. Deadline describes his arc as moving from idealistic believer in AI-for-good to horrified witness of the technology's darker potential. If Altman is the showman, Sutskever is the conscience slowly waking up in the third act. That's classic Shelley: the scariest part isn't the creation, it's the creator's dawning realization.

## Why the Timing Feels Uncanny

The review notes the film lands amid intense AI anxiety, with researchers warning about existential risk, governments debating regulation, and AI safety showing up in daily headlines. Deadline says scenes shot months ago now mirror real-world safety warnings, which makes the film feel closer to current events than speculative fiction.

That's a double-edged sword for any movie. Topical can mean urgent, or it can mean dated by the time the popcorn is gone. The film's bet is that the gap between what tech leaders say about safety and what they actually do is a durable theme, not a news cycle.

## A Crowded Tech-Movie Lane

*Artificial* doesn't arrive alone. Deadline places it alongside a wave of tech-personality films, including ones about Musk, *The Social Reckoning* and *You Can See Everything*. That raises a fair question: can narrative cinema do what documentaries have struggled to, and make audiences actually feel the stakes of tech power? A thriller with a charming lead and a monster-movie spine may sneak past defenses that a talking-head explainer never could. That's our take, not the review's, but it's a reasonable thing to wonder as the genre keeps growing.

## The Plot Twist Off Screen

Then there's the story behind the story. According to Deadline, Amazon shelved the film, and the review ties that to Jeff Bezos's reported $150 billion OpenAI investment. Neon then picked it up for a Christmas release.

We should be careful here: the review links the two facts, and we can't speak to anyone's internal motives. But the irony is hard to miss. A movie about a tech founder who tells different audiences different stories ended up with its own tangled corporate backstory, and the dramatic arc of its release schedule almost writes itself.

## What to Watch For

If you're planning a holiday movie night, here's what the review suggests you're signing up for:

- A Garfield performance built on charm that shifts with the audience
- A researcher-turned-reluctant-whistleblower arc for Sutskever
- A Guadagnino-directed mix of dark humor and thriller tension
- A Frankenstein structure, so don't expect the creation to behave

Whether *Artificial* moves public opinion on AI risk is an open question. Plenty of movies have warned about runaway machines, and the machines kept getting built anyway. But Shelley's monster has outlasted two centuries of warnings, and it's a fair bet Guadagnino knows that the best way to make people listen is to make them laugh nervously first.
