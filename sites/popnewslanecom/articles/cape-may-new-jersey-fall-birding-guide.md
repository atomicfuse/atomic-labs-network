Cape May, New Jersey, is in the middle of its peak fall migration season, which runs September through November, per Vogue's new birder's guide to the town. There's a second window in May. If you've ever wanted to try birding without committing to a pair of expensive binoculars and a Latin vocabulary, this is a fitting place to start: Vogue pitches it as a spot where you can come for the bird spotting and stay for the charming seaside town.

That pitch is the angle here. You don't have to be a birder to enjoy a birding destination. Think of it as a good-looking beach town with a built-in reason to get outside at sunrise.

## Where the birds are

Vogue's guide lists several nature spots, and the variety is the point. Some are huge, some are small, and all of them give you a reason to walk slowly and look up.

- **Sunset Boulevard nature preserve:** Vogue calls it the best birding spot in Cape May.
- **Forsythe National Wildlife Refuge:** a 48,000-acre waterfowl haven.
- **Higbees Beach:** a 1,100-acre nature preserve with trails.
- **801 Wilson Street nature preserve:** 180 acres for songbirds and raptors.
- **Captain Bob's waterway tour:** a four-hour trip on a boat called The Osprey, for anyone who'd rather see the wildlife from the water.

The guide's pitch is that you can spot a lot even on a short visit. Its angles mention 25-plus species in a quick trip, even in bad weather. Treat that as the guide's framing rather than a guarantee. Birds, famously, don't read itineraries.

## Where to sleep

The lodging list leans historic and cozy, which suits a trip built around early mornings.

- **Congress Hall:** billed as the oldest seaside resort in America, opened in 1816.
- **805 Stockton Avenue:** a historic 10-room bed and breakfast.
- **623 Columbia Avenue:** a Victorian bed and breakfast.
- **140 Stevens Street:** a hotel on a regenerative farm, with cottages.

It's hard not to notice that the options skew toward places with a porch-and-coffee kind of energy. For a hobby that rewards patience and quiet, that seems like a sensible match.

## Where to eat and drink

Birding works up an appetite, and the guide has a spread of options.

### Breakfast and daytime

**Beach Plum Cafe** (484 West Perry Street) is the breakfast pick. There's also a farm-to-table cafe at 104 Jackson Street, and a fudge and salt water taffy shop at 513 Washington Street Mall, because no seaside town trip is complete without a bag of something sticky.

### Dinner and evenings

At Congress Hall, you'll find the **Blue Pig Tavern** for a restaurant meal and **The Brown Room**, a jazz bar. Elsewhere, **The Olive Branch** (1891 Bayshore Road) is a Greek BYOB restaurant, and **The Ugly Mug** (426 Washington Street) is described as a local bar-food haunt.

## If the birds take a break

The guide doesn't assume everyone wants to spend all day with a field guide. A few non-bird options:

- **Beach Plum Farm** (400 Stevens Street): a farm and event space.
- **West End Garage** (484 West Perry Street): vintage and locally made goods.
- **Harriet Tubman Museum** (632 Lafayette Street): a cultural stop worth adding to the day.

That mix is what makes this a good trip for a mixed group. One person can chase a raptor sighting while another browses vintage finds, and everyone meets up for breakfast.

## The take

Birding has picked up as a nature-and-wellness pastime, and Cape May makes the case for why. The hobby asks very little: show up, be quiet, look around. In return, a town with Victorian inns, farm-to-table menus, and jazz in the evening fills in the rest of the weekend.

If you go, the timing matters. The fall migration window is open now, but it closes after November, and the next one doesn't arrive until May. The birds are on a schedule. You might as well join them.
