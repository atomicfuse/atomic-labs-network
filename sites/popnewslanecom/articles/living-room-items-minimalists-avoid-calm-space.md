Real Simple recently rounded up seven living room items minimalists tend to avoid, all in the name of a calmer, clutter-free space. The core idea, per the outlet: simplifying what's visible can make a room feel more intentional, more spacious, and more relaxing.

The angle worth taking here is the cheapest one: before you buy a single new decor piece, look at what you could take away. Think of it as a reset, not a makeover. No paint, no new sofa, no contractor.

## Why a Subtraction Reset Makes Sense Right Now

Minimalist design keeps its appeal as an antidote to overstimulation, especially as people spend more time tuning their homes for comfort and mental clarity. It also fits the seasonal refresh mindset, which is handy if you live in a small space and every square foot has to work.

Here are the seven items on the list, with some practical ways to act on each.

## The Seven Items Minimalists Avoid

### 1. Excess decorative objects

The key word is *excess*. Real Simple's list doesn't say minimalists ban decor entirely; it flags having too much of it. A shelf crowded with small trinkets gives the eye nowhere to rest.

A simple test: if you can't remember the last time you noticed an object, it may be doing more clutter work than decorating work. Keep a few favorites and let them have some breathing room.

### 2. Too many throw pillows

Again, the issue is quantity. A couch buried in pillows means somebody has to relocate them all before anyone can actually sit down. That's a small daily chore that quietly undercuts the "relaxing" part of a living room.

### 3. Unused furniture

This one is about pieces that take up floor space without earning it. Removing a chair nobody sits in is probably the fastest way to make a room feel bigger, and it costs nothing.

### 4. Visible cords and electronics clutter

Tangled cords and piles of gadgets are a classic source of visual noise. Even if everything is technically organized, a snarl of wires in plain sight can make a calm room feel busy. Tucking them out of view is the goal.

### 5. Stacks of magazines or books

Stacks are the kind of clutter that sneaks up on you, since each pile starts as a good intention. Real Simple lists them among the items minimalists avoid. If a stack has been sitting there for months, it's probably decor by accident.

### 6. Oversized coffee table items

Coffee tables are prime real estate, and big items parked on top can crowd the center of the room, which is exactly where the eye lands first. Scaling back what sits there is an easy way to open up the whole space.

### 7. Miscellaneous storage baskets or bins

Here's the plot twist: storage itself can become clutter. A collection of mismatched baskets and bins may hide mess, but it's still visible stuff. It's hard not to see the irony in buying containers to fix a problem that fewer belongings would solve outright.

## How to Run the Reset

The source's framing is an easy minimalist reset rather than a full home makeover, and that's the right scale. A few ideas for putting it into practice:

- **Start with one surface.** Clear the coffee table and see how the room feels.
- **Take a "before" photo.** Snap the room, make your changes, then compare. The difference is often more striking than expected.
- **Remove before you buy.** Subtract first, then decide whether the room actually needs anything new.
- **Go small-space first.** If your living room is compact, removing just a few of these items likely shows the biggest change.

## The Takeaway

There's a certain pop-culture-friendly appeal to the before-and-after: it's the home version of a good makeover montage, minus the budget. And the best part of this list is that the star of the transformation isn't something you buy. It's the stuff you stop displaying.

If you try only one thing this weekend, pick the item on this list that annoys you most and remove it. Chances are, the room will tell you whether you needed it.
