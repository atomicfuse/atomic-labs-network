Designer Nina Magon has a pitch for the future of wellness: skip the amenity floor and build the retreat into the place you actually live. Forbes profiled Magon this week, describing a career built on taking risks and constant evolution, and reporting that she believes wellness retreats belong at home. She intends to be the one who builds them.

That's a small sentence with a big shift tucked inside it.

## From the Amenity Floor to the Living Room

If you've toured a luxury building lately, you know the amenity floor: the spa-ish corner, the gym, the room with the good lighting that you visit twice a year. Per Forbes, Magon's argument is that real wellness shouldn't be a destination down the elevator. It should be woven into everyday home living.

It's hard not to see the logic. A wellness space you have to travel to, even if only a few floors, is one more thing on the schedule. A space that's part of the home is simply there when you need it.

## Why the Timing Makes Sense

The Forbes piece frames this as part of a post-pandemic shift. Homeowners are investing heavily in personal wellness spaces and putting mental health on the list of things a home should support. The market for residential wellness design is described as booming, and moving beyond the basic gym room.

That last part is the interesting bit. The home gym was the first wave: a treadmill, some weights, maybe a mirror. The next wave, as Forbes tells it, is the full retreat experience, a home designed around how you feel, not just how you work out.

## What Sets This Approach Apart

The takeaway from Magon's pitch is about placement and intention. Wellness isn't a bonus room tacked onto a floor plan or a perk listed in a building brochure. It's part of how the residence is laid out from the start.

Here's my read, and it's only that: this is the same idea behind plenty of everyday home trends, where the thing we used to go out for gets folded into daily life. Think of how streaming brought the cinema to the couch. Wellness is getting the same treatment, with better lighting.

## Ways to Borrow the Idea, Whatever Your Square Footage

Forbes' coverage focuses on Magon's design philosophy, not a shopping list, so consider these my own suggestions rather than her prescriptions. The principle is simple: make self-care a built-in part of the home, not an event.

### Start With a Corner

You don't need a dedicated room. A quiet corner with a comfortable seat and a spot for a book or a stretching mat can serve as a small daily reset.

### Think Beyond Workouts

If your home wellness plan is only exercise equipment, you're in the first wave. Ask what helps you unwind, sleep, or focus, and give that activity a permanent place.

### Make It Easy to Use

The amenity floor's biggest flaw is distance. Put your wellness spot where you'll pass it daily, and you'll use it more.

## The Bottom Line

Magon's bet, as reported by Forbes, is that the best retreat isn't a trip or a floor in the building. It's the front door you walk through every evening. If she's right, the most luxurious thing a home can offer won't be another amenity. It'll be a reason to stay in.
