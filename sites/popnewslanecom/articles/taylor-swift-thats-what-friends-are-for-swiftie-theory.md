Swifties have a new theory, and it comes with a soundtrack from 1985. Per Cosmopolitan, fans have noticed the phrase "That's What Friends Are For" turning up in multiple posts from Taylor Swift, Cara Delevingne, and *SNL*. The leading guess is that it could be the title of Taylor's next song, and that it may nod to Dionne Warwick's 1985 hit of the same name.

The timing is part of the fun. Taylor just released four new songs from *The Life of a Showgirl: The Encore*, and Cosmopolitan notes that the fanbase is in peak theory-crafting mode. When new music lands, any stray hint gets treated like a clue in a very glittery detective show.

## The Evidence So Far

According to Cosmopolitan's report, the case rests on one phrase appearing in three places: posts from Taylor herself, a post from Cara Delevingne, and a post from *SNL*. Three sources using the same phrase is the kind of overlap that makes a fan's eyebrows go up.

To be clear, that's the full extent of what has been reported. Nobody has confirmed a song title, and nothing in the coverage suggests Taylor or her team has said a word about it. This is a fan theory, and it is presented here as one.

## Enter the Numbers

No Swiftie theory is complete without numerology, and this one delivers. The numbers cited in the theorist's breakdown include:

- 2,121 days
- 12:12 timestamps
- a 12/12 birthdate

If you know Taylor's world, you know 12/12 is her birthday, and fans have long treated the number 13 as her lucky one. The 12s here are the theory's way of saying the clues are all pointing at her. It's hard not to admire the commitment: when a number can be spotted in a timestamp, it can also be spotted in a theory.

## Why Dionne Warwick?

The 1985 song "That's What Friends Are For" is a Dionne Warwick hit, and that's the connection the theory leans on. The pop-culture pleasure here is imagining what a Taylor song with that title would even be. A tribute to friendship? A wink at the famous friends in her orbit? A cheeky reference to the cast of characters who show up in her posts? All of that is pure speculation, and the reporting offers no hints about the sound or subject of any unreleased track.

What we can say is that the phrase is warm, familiar, and instantly singable, which is a nice fit for a fanbase that loves a good singalong.

## Convincing or Delusional?

Cosmopolitan's own description of the theory is that its writer has "never been more convinced or delusional." That's honestly the most accurate summary of Swiftie theorizing there is. Some of these theories end in a surprise announcement, and some end with everyone laughing about a coincidence. A common phrase can show up across several accounts without being a secret message, and it's fair to wonder how much of this is a clue and how much is a really fun group project.

Still, the pattern-spotting is the point. Decoding captions and numbers is part of how this fandom enjoys an album rollout, and the theory costs nothing to believe for a few days.

## What to Watch For

If the theory is right, you'll find out the way Swifties always do: an official announcement, a tracklist, or a title that suddenly looks a lot like a post you scrolled past last week. If it's wrong, you've still gotten a nice excuse to listen to a Dionne Warwick classic.

Either way, the fandom's favorite lesson holds: when it comes to Taylor, nobody's reading just the caption anymore.
