Carey Mulligan showed up to the London Film Festival screening of *Wildwood* on October 11 in a shimmering pleated two-piece by Celine, and per InStyle, the look had a liquid optical-illusion effect that set it apart from the usual festival-night parade.

That's the news. Here's the take: this is a masterclass in doing a lot while appearing to do very little.

## The Look, Briefly

According to InStyle, the outfit was a pleated, shimmering Celine set, paired with polished red-carpet styling. That's the extent of what we can confirm about the details, so we won't invent a hem length or a shoe brand for you. What the reporting does stress is the finish: the fabric reads as liquid, with the pleats creating that optical-illusion shimmer.

It's hard not to appreciate how much work the fabric is doing here. When a textile moves like molten metal, you don't need a train, a cape, or a neckline with a legal team. The pleats supply the drama on their own.

## Why a Two-Piece Works on a Festival Night

Film festival dressing has its own rules. You're sitting in a theater, then standing for photographs, then possibly mingling. A two-piece offers the polish of a gown with a bit more of an editorial, separates-driven attitude. That's our read, not InStyle's, but it fits the broader mood the outlet points to: an appetite for metallic textures, sculptural silhouettes, and understated glamour.

That last phrase is the key. "Understated" and "shimmering" don't always share a sentence, yet pleated metallics are one of the few places they overlap. The shine comes from the movement of the fabric rather than from embellishment, so the whole thing feels sleek rather than sparkly.

## Fall Eventwear Gets a Cheat Sheet

Per InStyle, festival fashion is feeding the seasonal style conversation, with celebrity looks quickly shaping fall eventwear and holiday outfit inspiration. Mulligan's moment lands right as party-season planning begins, which makes it a handy reference point.

If you want to borrow the idea (and you don't have a Celine budget), here's our takeaway, clearly opinion:

- **Pick one hero texture.** Let the pleated metallic be the entire statement.
- **Keep the silhouette clean.** Sculptural shapes and shine do better without competing details.
- **Style it quietly.** The polished, restrained approach is what makes the fabric look expensive.

No creative leaps required, just restraint, which is famously the hardest accessory to buy.

## The Wildwood Factor

The occasion matters too. A festival screening is a place where the film is supposed to be the headline, and a look like this honors that: eye-catching enough to be photographed everywhere, composed enough not to upstage the evening. Whether that was the strategy is anyone's guess, since we haven't seen Mulligan or her team say so. But the result reads as confident without being loud.

## The Bottom Line

Mulligan, 41, gave the London Film Festival a fall-ready lesson in liquid metallics: let the fabric shimmer, let the pleats move, and let everything else stay out of the way. In a season of increasingly maximal red carpets, sometimes the most striking thing in the room is the outfit that looks like it's gently melting, on purpose.
