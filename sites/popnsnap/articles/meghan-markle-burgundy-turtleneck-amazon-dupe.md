Meghan Markle has stepped out in a burgundy fall outfit, and Us Weekly says the shade is the season's "it" sweater color. Even better for shoppers: the outlet points to a $13 slim-fit turtleneck on Amazon as an affordable stand-in for Markle's designer Joseph style.

So the pitch is simple. Skip the beige, go wine-dark, and don't spend designer money doing it.

## What Markle Wore, and What Us Weekly Says to Buy

Per Us Weekly, Markle's look centered on a fitted burgundy turtleneck from Joseph, in a polished monochrome outfit. The outlet's roundup positions an Amazon slim-fit top, priced at $13, as the budget-friendly alternative. Us Weekly also describes the style as slimming, which is the outlet's own framing rather than anything I can verify from here.

The details of the Amazon top beyond its price and slim-fit cut aren't something I can vouch for, so check the product page for fabric, sizing and reviews before you add to cart. Prices on Amazon move around, too, so the $13 figure is as reported at publication.

## Why Burgundy Is Having a Moment

Here's my take: burgundy works as a fall move because it's a neutral that doesn't feel like a surrender. Us Weekly frames it as a richer alternative to beige and the other quiet-luxury neutrals that have dominated closets lately. Beige says "I have a tasteful linen sofa." Burgundy says "I have a tasteful linen sofa and a good red wine."

It's also an easy shade to wear. Deep red reads as a color without demanding a whole outfit's worth of commitment, and it sits comfortably next to the basics you probably already own.

## The Monochrome Lesson

The smartest part of Markle's outfit, at least as described, is the head-to-toe burgundy approach. Tone-on-tone dressing is one of the easiest ways to look put together, because the color does the styling work for you. A fitted turtleneck is the ideal anchor: clean neckline, no fuss, nothing to accessorize unless you want to.

If you're recreating it on a budget, the logic is straightforward:

- **Start with the turtleneck.** It's the hero piece, and at $13 it's the low-risk entry point.
- **Keep the rest quiet.** Matching or closely related tones in the same wine family will echo the monochrome effect.
- **Let texture do the heavy lifting.** If a cheaper top feels thinner than a designer knit, layering can help it look more intentional. That's general styling advice, not something from Us Weekly's piece.

## Three Ways to Wear a Fitted Turtleneck

These are my suggestions, not part of the reported look.

### For Work

Pair it with tailored trousers in black or charcoal and a structured blazer. The burgundy gives a sharp office outfit some personality without breaking dress code.

### For the Weekend

Wear it under a casual jacket with your favorite jeans. A fitted top keeps proportions tidy when the outer layer is relaxed.

### For Evening

Go sleek: a slim skirt or dark trousers, simple jewelry, and let the deep color carry the room. Burgundy under low light looks expensive almost by default.

## Designer vs. Dupe: Manage Expectations

A $13 top and a Joseph turtleneck are not the same garment, and nobody should pretend otherwise. Joseph is a designer label, and Us Weekly presents the Amazon option as a dupe, meaning it nails the silhouette and shade, not necessarily the feel. What you're buying is the look: fitted, rich-toned, easy to layer.

That's the quiet genius of this kind of celebrity-inspired shopping. Markle's outfit becomes a template, and the template costs less than lunch.

## The Takeaway

Fall fashion has spent a few seasons in oatmeal-colored hibernation. If Markle's burgundy is any indication, and Us Weekly certainly thinks it is, the palette is getting a little more daring. The best part is that the daring version starts at $13.
