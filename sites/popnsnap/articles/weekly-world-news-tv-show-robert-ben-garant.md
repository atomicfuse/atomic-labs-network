Bat Boy is about to get a workplace comedy. Per Vulture, Robert Ben Garant is developing an improvised mockumentary series based on the Weekly World News, with Cathy Shim showrunning alongside him. The premise: the tabloid's gloriously bizarre stories are treated as real, and the reporters covering them just want to be taken seriously.

That's the whole pitch, and it's a good one.

## What We Know So Far

The details are lean, so here is what the reporting supports:

- Garant and Shim are showrunning an improvised comedy about the Weekly World News, the tabloid known as the home of Bat Boy.
- The format is a mockumentary in a Reno 911!-style mode.
- The central joke is that the paper's absurd stories are "true" within the show's world, and the staff has to cover them with a straight face.

Things the reporting I've seen doesn't cover: a network or streamer, a cast, or a premiere date. Treat anything beyond the above as unannounced until someone says otherwise.

## Why This Pairing Makes Sense

The Reno 911! comparison is the key to the whole thing. That series is built on people playing it deadpan while chaos unfolds around them, and a newsroom full of reporters earnestly chasing impossible scoops is a natural fit for that approach. Garant's name attached to an improvised, documentary-style comedy about a tabloid reads less like a stretch and more like a match.

The engine of the premise is the gap between how seriously the reporters take themselves and how ridiculous their beats are. Anyone who has watched a mockumentary knows that gap is where the laughs live. Journalists desperate for respect, assigned to stories about a famous half-bat child? That's a character comedy with a built-in ceiling-high concept.

## The Angle That Interests Me: Fake News Then, Fake News Now

Here is my take, and it is just that: this show lands in a strange cultural moment. The timing suggests there's appetite for media satire right now, and it's hard not to wonder how a show about a tabloid that played its fakery for fun will sit next to today's conversation about misinformation and conspiracy culture.

The difference is worth chewing on. Weekly World News stories like Bat Boy were absurd enough that the wink was part of the product. Modern conspiracy media rarely winks. A mockumentary that treats impossible stories as real, and then shows the people tasked with reporting them, can poke at that contrast without having to lecture anyone. The "journalism in crisis" backdrop gives the jokes extra bite: these reporters are chasing credibility in a job where the material works against them.

That's the version of this show I'd most want to see. Not a history lesson, not a takedown, just a group of people committing to the bit while the world around them argues about what's real.

## The Nostalgia Factor

There's also a simpler pleasure here. Print-era weirdness has a pull of its own, and Bat Boy is about as recognizable a piece of tabloid iconography as exists. For viewers who remember the checkout-aisle era, this is a callback. For streamers who never met the paper, the premise explains itself in one sentence, which is a rare and valuable thing for a new comedy.

## What to Watch For

A few open questions will decide whether this works:

1. **How much does the improv carry?** The format lives or dies on the cast's ability to build a world in real time.
2. **Does Bat Boy appear?** The tabloid's best-known figure is the obvious draw, though nothing in the reporting says how he'll be used.
3. **How sharp is the satire?** The setup invites commentary on media and misinformation, but a lighter, purely goofy approach is just as possible.

## The Bottom Line

A tabloid that never apologized for being ridiculous is getting a show about the people who had to report it with a straight face. The format is proven, the premise is simple, and the subject is oddly timely. Now we wait for a cast, a home, and a date. Until then, somewhere a reporter is waiting for a call about a creature in a cave, and honestly, we'd watch him take it.
