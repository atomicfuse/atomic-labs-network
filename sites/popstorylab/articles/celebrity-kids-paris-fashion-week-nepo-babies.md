Paris Fashion Week has a new kind of regular, and they have famous last names. Per The Cut, celebrity children dominated this season, with several making runway debuts and others watching from the front row. Apple Martin made her Chloé debut, while Ella Bleu Travolta and Lily Brant each took their first runway walks.

The Cut's own framing is a gentle one: the next generation of nepo babies, it notes, are already very well-dressed. That's hard to argue with. The more interesting question is what it means when the people filling the runway and the front row increasingly share surnames with the people who used to fill the movie posters.

## Our Take: The Front Row Is the Product

Here's the angle worth chewing on. When a celebrity kid walks a show, the model is only part of what a brand gets. It's also getting the photographers, the social posts, and the "who's that?" search spike that follows. That's our read, not a reported fact, but it's hard not to wonder how much of the appeal is the walk itself and how much is the headline that comes attached.

That's why the front-row detail matters as much as the runway one. A celebrity kid in a seat is a free ambassador. A celebrity kid on the runway is the same ambassador with a job title. Fashion has always run on attention, and a famous surname is a pre-built audience.

### Why the Timing Stings

Nepotism in fashion is already a hot-button conversation about access, privilege, and merit. Paris putting that many famous families in one visible place amplifies it, because the evidence is right there in the photos. You don't need a spreadsheet when the street-style shots do the work.

To be fair, none of this makes the debuts less polished or the kids less capable. Walking a runway is a real skill, and nerves in front of a Paris crowd are nerves regardless of who raised you. The point isn't that these young people can't do it. The point is the many aspiring models with no famous parent who never get the chance to prove they can.

## The Gatekeeping Question

If casting leans on recognizable names, the pipeline for undiscovered talent narrows a little. Fashion weeks have historically been where unknown faces become famous ones. A season where the famous-adjacent are the story is a season where fewer column inches go to everyone else.

That's a structural critique, aimed at choices brands make rather than at twenty-somethings who said yes to an incredible invitation. Most of us would have said yes too, ideally with a better outfit budget.

## The Nepo-Baby Era, Now With Better Tailoring

The phrase "nepo baby" has traveled a long way from gossip-thread insult to a standard part of the entertainment vocabulary. What's changed is the tone. A few years ago, the discourse was mostly about outrage. Now there's a shrug mixed in, and a bit of resignation: of course the kids are at Chloé, and of course they look great.

The Cut's cheeky description lands on that mood exactly. The next generation is already well-dressed, and everyone knows why they're in the room. The open question is whether that acknowledgment leads to anything, or whether it just becomes part of the show.

## What to Watch Next

Seasons like this tend to set up a sequel. Once a celebrity kid has a debut, the natural next questions are whether they get booked again and whether the brand relationships stick. Those are the answers that will separate a one-season moment from a real career, and nobody can fill them in yet.

For now, Paris has handed us a very photogenic family album. Whether it turns into a modeling dynasty or just a great season of photos depends on what happens after the flashbulbs stop. The runway can be handed to you. The second booking, as any working model will tell you, usually has to be earned.
