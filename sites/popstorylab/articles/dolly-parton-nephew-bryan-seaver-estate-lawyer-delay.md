## The Problem Isn't the Case. It's the Phone Book.

Most estate-fight headlines are about who gets what. This one is more basic: who will take the case at all?

As TMZ reports it, Seaver's obstacle is structural rather than strategic. If one bank is tangled up in the trust being fought over, and the lawyers he calls keep turning out to have connections to that bank, then he can't simply hire someone down the road. A conflict of interest isn't a personality clash. It's the kind of thing that can legitimately stop an attorney from taking a client.

That's why the delay request matters. A hearing date is a hard deadline, and it's difficult to show up prepared without counsel. Asking for 10 more days is, on its face, a request for time to find someone allowed to represent him.

Here's our read, and it's ours rather than TMZ's: the most interesting part of this story is how a conflict-of-interest problem can snowball.
