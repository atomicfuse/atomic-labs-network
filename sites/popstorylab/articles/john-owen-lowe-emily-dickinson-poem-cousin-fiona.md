Rob Lowe's youngest son, John Owen Lowe, 30, shared Emily Dickinson's poem "Hope Is the Thing With Feathers" on his Instagram Stories over the weekend, following the death of his 13-year-old cousin Fiona, per the Daily Mail. Fiona died by suicide, according to the outlet.

This is not a story for jokes, and we won't make any. It is a story about what people reach for when words fail.

## Why a poem, and why that poem

John Owen didn't write a long caption or a statement. He posted a poem about hope, one of the most widely known in the English language, per the Daily Mail's account of his Stories.

It's hard not to see the logic in that choice. A Story disappears, and a poem on it is a small, quiet gesture. Dickinson's verse is about hope as something that keeps going, and posting it lets a grieving person say something gentle without having to compose it from scratch. That reading is ours, not his. He hasn't explained his choice, at least in the material reported by the Daily Mail.

It also reflects a wider habit. Public grieving on social media has become a way many people process loss, and borrowed words, whether a poem, lyrics, or a line from a favorite book, often do the work when original ones feel impossible.

## The family's tributes

John Owen wasn't the only one who posted. Per the Daily Mail, Fiona's older sister Mabel shared heartfelt tributes of her own. Messages also came from Fiona's dance teacher and a youth organization she was part of.

Those last two matter. A dance studio or youth group is often where a young person spends hours each week, builds friendships, and is seen by adults who aren't their parents. When a teacher or organization publicly says goodbye, it says something about the place a 13-year-old held in her community, beyond her immediate family.

## A family in a hard stretch

The Daily Mail's framing places this loss against the backdrop of the ongoing Los Angeles wildfires, which displaced the family. Stacking a natural disaster on top of a personal tragedy is a heavy load, and we won't speculate about how the two relate. Nothing in the reporting says they are connected, and it would be wrong to suggest otherwise.

## What the moment asks of the rest of us

Celebrity families inevitably grieve in public, even when they'd prefer not to. Most of us are on the other side of the screen, scrolling past an Instagram Story. The most useful response is a modest one: respect the family's space, avoid guessing at details they haven't shared, and treat the conversation around youth mental health as real rather than a trending topic.

If Fiona's story prompts anything, let it be a check-in with the young people in your life, and with the teachers, coaches, and mentors who see them every week.

A poem on an Instagram Story won't answer why a child is gone. But as the Lowe family's tributes suggest, sometimes the point is simply to say that someone mattered, and that they won't be forgotten.
