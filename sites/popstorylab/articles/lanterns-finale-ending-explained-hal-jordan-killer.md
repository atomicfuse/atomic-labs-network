Lanterns has wrapped its first season on HBO Max, and the finale did something superhero finales rarely do: it got quiet. According to Cosmopolitan's breakdown of the ending, the season closed on a deliberately understated note, with major reveals about the second Manhunter, the person who killed Hal Jordan, and John Stewart's unusual new job as Earth's protector.

That's a lot of plot for a finale that, by Cosmo's account, defies typical superhero spectacle. So let's take one angle and run with it: the most striking choice here is who killed Hal Jordan, and why the show's refusal to go big is what makes it land.

## The Reveals, Quickly

Here's what Cosmopolitan reports from the finale:

- **Kerry is Hal Jordan's killer.** The show subverts the usual superhero playbook with this one.
- **Noah is the second Manhunter.** He's also Kelly's son, which means his dual identity is the secret at the center of the reveal. Cosmo notes there are sci-fi mechanics behind how his existence was possible.
- **John Stewart ends up as Earth's protector** in an unconventional way, blending green and yellow Lantern powers.

The finale also leaves the door open to what's next, with potential crossovers looming for the DCU, per the same report.

## Why Kerry Killing Hal Is the Whole Point

The Hal Jordan name carries weight. He's one of the most recognizable Green Lanterns in DC lore, and a show titled Lanterns could easily have built toward a big heroic showdown with him at the center. Instead, per Cosmopolitan, the answer to "who killed Hal?" is Kerry.

That's the kind of choice that tells you what a show is about. A blockbuster would stage the death as an event, with a cosmic villain, a skyline in peril and a swelling score. A character-driven story makes it personal. It's hard not to read Kerry as the answer because the show cares more about the people in the room than the ring on the finger.

That's our read, not a confirmed statement of intent from the creators. But it fits the finale Cosmopolitan describes: understated, deliberate, and more interested in revelation than explosion.

## John Stewart Goes Hybrid

Then there's John Stewart. Per Cosmopolitan, he closes the season as Earth's protector in an unconventional role, combining green and yellow Lantern powers.

For longtime fans, that's a notable swerve. Cosmo frames it as a fresh take on DC lore that sets him apart from comic canon. Green and yellow are traditionally opposites in Lantern color-coding, so a character who uses both is a deliberate reshuffle of the rules rather than a simple promotion. Whether that approach makes him more powerful, more complicated or both is something the show leaves for future seasons to explore.

## A Lowkey Finale, Defended

The real argument here is about tone. Superhero TV has trained audiences to expect a finale that ends with a giant fight, a beam in the sky or a post-credits teaser designed to break the internet. Lanterns, according to Cosmopolitan, goes the other way.

We'd argue that works for three reasons, all of them our take:

1. **The reveals carry the drama.** When your finale's big moments are about identity and who did what, you don't need a CGI skyline to land them.
2. **It makes the genre feel less interchangeable.** A quiet ending stands out in a landscape of near-identical climaxes.
3. **It rewards attention.** A layered finale gives viewers something to unpack, which is exactly why explainers like Cosmo's exist.

## The Takeaway

Lanterns didn't end with a bang, and that may be the smartest thing about it.
