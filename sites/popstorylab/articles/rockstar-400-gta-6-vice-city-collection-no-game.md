Rockstar Games has unveiled the Goodtime State – Vice City Collection, an 11-item physical set priced at $399.99 and slated to ship November 19, per Forbes. The catch, and it's a big one: the game itself is not in the box.

Yes, you read that right. Four hundred dollars of Vice City, and you still need to buy GTA 6 separately.

## What's Actually in the Box

Per Forbes' rundown, the collection includes:

1. A replica Vice City Police Department badge
2. A neon Vice City sign
3. An exclusive art book
4. A vinyl soundtrack
5. Collectible figurines of multiple characters
6. Limited-edition apparel
7. A diorama display piece
8. A poster set
9. A replica in-game currency prop
10. A steelbook case
11. A certificate of authenticity

That's a genuinely hefty haul of stuff. It's also a very specific kind of stuff: things you display, hang, wear, or spin on a turntable.

## The Real Story: Rockstar Is Selling the Feeling, Not the Game

Here's the angle worth chewing on. Forbes frames the set as a premium merchandise offering for hardcore fans, and notes that GTA 6 is one of the most anticipated releases in years. Rockstar is, in effect, monetizing the anticipation itself, getting superfans to spend big before the game even drops.

That's my read on the strategy, but the product list backs it up. Look at what the box doesn't contain: no disc, no code, no gameplay. What it does contain is atmosphere. A neon sign. A vinyl soundtrack. A badge. A diorama. This is a shrine kit.

And honestly, that's a smart pitch for this particular franchise. Vice City has been pop-culture shorthand for pastel sunsets, synth-heavy radio and chaos since long before this new game. Selling the vibe as a standalone object is a natural next step. It's hard not to wonder if some buyers will care more about the neon sign on their wall than the hours they'll log in the game.

## The Steelbook Problem

Here's the funniest item on the list. Item 10 is a steelbook case. Steelbooks exist to hold a game. The set, per Forbes, does not include the game.

To be clear, I'm not claiming Rockstar overlooked this. Collectors buy empty cases all the time, and the set may well be designed so you can slot in your own copy later. But it does make the box a little poetic: a beautiful, empty vessel waiting for a release you have to buy elsewhere.

## Is $399.99 Reasonable? It Depends Who You Are

The math people will do immediately: a standard big-budget game runs roughly $60 to $70, per the Forbes piece. So the collection costs several times what the game does, before you've bought the game. That's the psychology at the heart of this release, and it's why it's trending.

But I'd argue the price tag is aimed at a very particular person, and it's not the casual player.

### The buyer who gets it

The collector who already owns a shelf of limited editions. For them, 11 items, a certificate of authenticity and a display diorama may feel like a coherent set rather than a random pile of merch. The certificate, in particular, is a signal that Rockstar wants this treated as a collectible, not a gift-shop haul.

### The buyer who might pass

Anyone who just wants to play. If your Vice City dreams involve a controller rather than a display cabinet, this box doesn't offer you a single playable minute.

That gap is the whole conversation. It's also why the internet will likely keep arguing about it all week: some will call it fan-service done right, others will call it a $400 flex with extra steps. Both takes are easy to understand.

## Why the Timing Matters

The November 19 ship date puts this squarely in the pre-launch hype window Forbes describes, when anticipation is at its peak and the appetite for anything GTA-adjacent is enormous. Whether you read that as shrewd marketing or fandom-squeezing probably says more about your relationship with publishers than with Rockstar specifically.

What's clear from the reported details is that major publishers increasingly see fandom as a product category of its own, separate from the game. The base game is the entry ticket. The collection is the VIP lounge.

## The Bottom Line

The Goodtime State – Vice City Collection is a very well-stocked love letter that you can't actually play. If you're the kind of fan who wants the badge, the sign and the vinyl on day one, Rockstar has built you a $399.99 altar. If you're not, there's a good chance the most Vice City thing about this release is the audacity.

Either way, a certificate of authenticity for a neon sign is the most gloriously over-the-top flourish of the whole set, and frankly, it feels right at home in a city built on excess.
