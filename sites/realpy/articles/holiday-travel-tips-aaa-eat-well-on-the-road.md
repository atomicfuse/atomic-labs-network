## What AAA is flagging for holiday travel

Per CBS News, AAA frames Thanksgiving and holiday travel as a peak stress point for families. Crowded roads and airports are the main pressure, and changing vacation habits are reshaping how people plan.

The coverage doesn't give detailed hour-by-hour windows or percentages in the material available here, so check AAA directly for current forecasts and the best times to drive or fly. What the reporting does make clear is the underlying logic: the busier the travel period, the more planning pays off.

## Plan the stop, not just the route

Here is a practical idea that fits AAA's theme of avoiding headaches. Most holiday road trips treat meals as an afterthought, which usually means a rushed fast-food stop at the busiest exit. Flip that.

### Pick one meal worth the detour

Before you leave, choose a single regional dish along your route. A diner known for pie, a barbecue spot, a bakery in a small town. One planned stop gives everyone something to look forward to and breaks up the drive.

### Call ahead or check hours

Holidays change opening hours. Independent restaurants and markets often close or run shortened schedules, so confirm before you commit. This is common sense, not an AAA finding, but it saves a disappointing detour.

### Pack for the gaps

Traffic and airport delays don't care about your reservation. Bring portable snacks and water so a delay doesn't force a bad, expensive meal. It also helps if you're budget-conscious, one of the audiences CBS News's coverage targets.

## Why cruises are the 2027 story

AAA, per CBS News, expects cruises to be a major travel choice in 2027. The reporting ties that to a broader shift toward bundled, easier-to-plan trips: transportation, lodging, and meals handled in one package.

For food-minded travelers, that's a trade-off worth knowing about. Bundled trips remove planning stress, but you give up some control over where you eat. If you cruise, look at which ports of call you'll visit and research one local dish or market at each stop, so you taste the place rather than only the ship's dining room.

## Mistakes to avoid when traveling with family

These are my suggestions, built around the stress points AAA highlights:

- **Leaving without a food plan.** Hungry kids in traffic make every delay worse.
- **Overpacking the itinerary.** One good stop beats four rushed ones.
- **Skipping a backup.** Have a second option for your meal stop in case the first is crowded or closed.
- **Booking late.** If you're considering a bundled trip, such as a cruise, earlier planning gives you more choice. Check current pricing and availability directly.

## A simple holiday trip checklist

1. Check AAA's current travel forecast for your dates.
2. Choose one regional dish or local market to visit on the way.
3. Confirm its holiday hours.
4. Pack snacks and water for delays.
5. Build in extra time so the meal stop feels like a pleasure, not a gamble.

## The takeaway

You can't control holiday traffic or airport crowds, but you can control what you're driving toward. Pick one meal worth the trip, confirm it's open, and let the rest of the journey build around it.
