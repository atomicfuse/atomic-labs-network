If you usually grab Pinot Grigio because it's familiar, this guide gives you a short list of Italian whites to try next, plus three label words that help you pick a better bottle without spending much more.

The list comes from a tasting video by WineOceans that works through 13 Italian white wines, from the famous to the obscure. Its central argument: don't stop at the usual names. Italy has plenty of more interesting, reasonably priced whites. Below is what the video covers, organized so you can use it in a wine shop.

## Start with the label words that signal quality

Before you memorize grapes, learn three phrases. According to the video's takeaways, they do a lot of the work for you.

- **Alto Adige:** In the video, an Alto Adige Pinot Grigio showed more freshness and clearer flavor than the standard supermarket style.
- **Classico:** This word often points to the original, historic heart of a wine region, and usually signals better quality. The video tasted a Soave Classico.
- **di Gavi:** Gavi di Gavi came across as more complex than a regular Gavi.

These are rules of thumb, not guarantees. But they're quick filters when you're staring at a wall of bottles.

## If you like Pinot Grigio, step up before you switch

The video tasted two Pinot Grigios: a Tesco Finest Pinot Grigio DOC and one from Alto Adige. The takeaway was that Alto Adige lifts the grape, with more refreshing character and more distinct flavor. If you're not ready to leave Pinot Grigio behind, changing the region is the easiest upgrade.

## The Gavi pair: same name, different depth

Gavi is a northern Italian white that appears twice in the lineup: one from Lidl and one labeled Gavi di Gavi. The video found the Gavi di Gavi more complex than the regular Gavi. That's a useful lesson for any shopper: when a more specific place name appears on the label, it can mean more depth in the glass.

Try it with simple, light dishes where a crisp white fits, and compare the two side by side if you can. Buying both is the cheapest wine education you'll get.

## Soave Classico, Lugana and Costozza: Veneto and the north

Three of the 13 bottles come from the northeast. Soave Classico shows how the Classico designation can matter. Lugana was one of the video's standout picks. Costozza from Veneto rounds out the group.

If Pinot Grigio is your baseline, Lugana is one of the strongest places to go next, based on the video's final choices.

## Central Italy: Verdicchio, Pecorino and Frascati

The middle of the country supplies three very different experiences:

- **Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi:** A Marche-region white that earned a place on the tasting list.
- **Pecorino from Abruzzo:** Yes, it shares its name with a cheese, but this is a wine grape. Look for it from Abruzzo.
- **Frascati:** The video's worst experience. The reviewer described it as lacking flavor.

That last point is worth taking seriously, but keep it in perspective. It's one reviewer's opinion on one bottle, not a verdict on the whole category. Still, if you buy Frascati, expect that quality varies.

## The south: Falanghina and Fiano

Southern Italy is where the video's adventurous side shows up.

### Falanghina from Campania

A Campania white that made the lineup. It's a good option if you want to try a regional grape you won't find at every party.

### Fiano from Salento, Puglia

Fiano was one of the video's top picks. Salento in Puglia is the heel of Italy's boot, a warm region, and the video highlights how region, grape and climate shape what ends up in the glass. Warm-climate whites often taste different from cooler northern ones, so Fiano is a good contrast to a crisp Alto Adige Pinot Grigio.

## Vermentino: the one to try first

The video lists Vermentino among its standout final choices alongside Lugana and Fiano. If you want a single bottle to test the idea that Italy has better whites than the default, this is a sensible place to begin, together with the other two.

## A simple shopping plan

You don't need to buy all 13. Here is a practical way to use the list:

1. **Buy your usual Pinot Grigio, then an Alto Adige one.** Notice the difference in freshness.
2. **Compare Gavi with Gavi di Gavi.** Same grape, different level of complexity.
3. **Pick one of the three favorites:** Vermentino, Lugana or Fiano.
4. **Check labels for Classico** when buying Soave.
5. **Be cautious with Frascati.** The video's reviewer found it flavorless.

The video also points out that rising wine prices make good-value recommendations more useful, including bottles from supermarkets. That's why even the Tesco and Lidl bottles appear in the tasting: you don't need a specialist shop to start exploring.

## Why the place matters

Each of these wines comes from somewhere specific: the mountains of Alto Adige, the hills around Jesi, Abruzzo, Campania, sunny Salento. Region, grape and climate shape flavor, and that's the real pleasure of drinking Italian white: every bottle is a small map. Pair it with the local food of the same area and the map gets even better.

Next time you reach for Pinot Grigio, pick up a Vermentino, Lugana or Fiano beside it, and taste the two together.
