Active travel keeps growing, and the best trips pair the effort with the eating. According to a Yahoo travel report on Backroads, the guided biking, hiking and multi-sport company is steering travelers toward emerging destinations, including a Krakow to Budapest route and Egypt. This guide shows you how to think about those trips through food, and how to plan one that rewards every mile.

One caveat up front: the report names these destinations but doesn't detail the itineraries, menus or stops. So what follows is a planning framework, not a claim about what Backroads serves on any given day. Check the company's own trip pages for specifics.

## Why active trips suit food-led travel

Yahoo's report describes active travel as surging since the pandemic, as travelers look for immersive, healthier, experience-led vacations beyond traditional sightseeing. That fits eating well. When you travel under your own power, meals stop being a break from the trip and become part of it. You arrive hungry, in a real place, at a real table.

The report also says Backroads' edge is building original itineraries in places travelers may not yet associate with active vacations. For a food traveler, that matters. Destinations that haven't been routinely packaged for visitors tend to still feel like themselves.

## Krakow to Budapest: a route worth reading as a menu

The first emerging route in the report links Krakow and Budapest. The report doesn't list the towns or terrain in between, so don't assume a specific path. But you can still prepare by learning what each end of the trip is known for.

### Before you go: build a short tasting list

- Pick two or three dishes you want to try in each city before you leave.
- Read up on whether each city has a covered market or food hall, and note its opening days.
- Save a few restaurant names for rest days, since an active schedule leaves limited evenings.

### On the trip: let the route set the pace

Ask your guides where they eat. Local recommendations from people who work the route beat any list. Treat lunch as the anchor of the day, and leave room for the unplanned bakery stop.

## Egypt: the second destination on the list

Egypt is the other emerging destination named in the report. Again, the report gives no itinerary details, so check with the operator about the format, season and daily distances. What you can do now is approach it as a food traveler.

- Look into the staples of everyday Egyptian eating and which ones appeal to you.
- Ask how meals are handled on the trip, whether group dinners, free evenings or both.
- Tell the company about dietary needs early.

## How to choose between them

Both trips fit the pitch in the report: places that feel fresh before they become mainstream. Use these questions to decide.

1. **What kind of eating do you want?** A trip across two cities invites comparing how neighboring places cook. A single-country trip invites depth.
2. **How active do you want to be?** Ask for daily mileage and elevation, and whether rides or hikes have easier options.
3. **When can you go?** Confirm seasons and departure dates directly with the operator.

## Questions to ask any guided active-trip company

Before you book, get answers to a handful of practical points:

- How many meals are included, and which are free?
- Do you stop at local markets, bakeries or producers along the way?
- Can the itinerary handle allergies and dietary restrictions?
- What is the pace on a typical day, and what are the alternatives?

These answers tell you more about how food-focused a trip really is than any brochure language.

## The takeaway

Pick one of these two destinations, write your tasting list before you book, and ask the operator how meals fit into each day. The trip that puts you at a good table after a good effort is the one you'll remember.
