Las Vegas brunch no longer means just eggs and a mimosa. Per Las Vegas Weekly, which rounded up four weekend options spanning Resorts World, STK, Play Playground and South Point, brunch here can come with a Pilates class, a live DJ, 20-plus games or a prime rib carving station. This guide helps you choose the one that fits your weekend.

A note on how to read this: the original roundup was a sponsored feature, and the details below come from its summary. It doesn't include prices, hours or reservation rules, so confirm those with each venue before you go.

## If You Want a Wellness Reset: Resorts World

Resorts World pairs movement with a meal. Las Vegas Weekly points to Poolside Pulse Pilates at AYU Dayclub, followed by brunch at FUHU. It's the one option here that lets you earn your plate before you sit down.

The same resort also offers a second route: a Latin-inspired brunch at ¡VIVA!. If Pilates isn't your thing, this is the pick for a more flavor-forward meal in the same place.

**Best for:** travelers who want to start Saturday or Sunday feeling good rather than recovering.

## If You Want a Party: STK Las Vegas

STK's All You Can Brunch combines chef-driven bites, cocktails and live DJs, according to Las Vegas Weekly. This is brunch as nightlife-lite: music up, drinks flowing, and food that's meant to keep pace with the energy.

**Best for:** groups celebrating something, or anyone who treats brunch as the opening act of the day.

## If You Want Games With Your Mimosa: Play Playground

Play Playground Las Vegas offers a brunch menu with bottomless mimosas and Bloody Marys, plus more than 20 interactive games, per the roundup. The games give a table something to do between rounds, which helps if your group includes people who don't want to sit and talk for two hours.

The summary frames the games as a draw, but it doesn't specify age policies. If you're planning around kids, call ahead and ask rather than assuming.

**Best for:** friend groups who want a built-in activity.

## If You Want a Classic Feast: South Point Garden Buffet

South Point's Garden Buffet serves a prime rib and Champagne brunch with global buffet stations, according to Las Vegas Weekly. It's the traditional Vegas move: you walk the stations, circle back for the carved beef, and pace yourself over a long meal.

South Point sits off the Strip, which suits anyone who wants an indulgent meal without the Strip's crowds. That's my read of the location, not a claim from the roundup.

**Best for:** big appetites and people who want variety over theme.

## Bottomless vs. Buffet: How to Choose

The four options split into two styles of value.

- **Bottomless drinks (STK, Play Playground):** the appeal is the drinks and atmosphere. You get the most out of it if you'll actually drink and want a lively room.
- **Buffet (South Point):** the appeal is food volume and choice. Prime rib and Champagne are the draws.
- **Experience-first (Resorts World):** you're paying for the class and setting as much as the meal.

Without published prices, I can't tell you which wins on cost. Pull up each menu and do the math for your group.

## How to Plan Your Day Around It

1. **Pick the vibe first.** Wellness, party, games or buffet. Matching mood to venue matters more than the menu.
2. **Check prices and hours directly.** The roundup doesn't list them.
3. **Book ahead where you can.** Weekend brunch is the hook for many visitors, so assume it fills up.
4. **Plan the rest of the day lightly.** After a Pilates class or a prime rib plate, you'll want a pool or a walk, not a packed schedule.

## The Takeaway

Decide what you want from brunch before you decide where. Choose Resorts World to start the day well, STK to start a party, Play Playground to keep a group busy, and South Point to eat until you can't.
