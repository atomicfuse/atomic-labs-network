Bill Michaels of Fly Creek Cider Mill & Orchard appeared on "Fox & Friends Weekend" to demonstrate autumn apple cider recipes, per Fox News. The centerpiece was a hot mulled cider with a caramel-rimmed glass, a swirl of whipped cream and mulling spices, served alongside extra-sharp cheddar cheese. By the end of this piece, you'll know how to build that drink and that pairing for your own fall table, and why the combination makes sense.

The cider mill itself is the starting point for the idea. Fly Creek Cider Mill & Orchard is the kind of place that turns a fall drink into a destination, and the approach Michaels showed is easy to borrow: start with good cider, treat it like a dessert drink, and put something sharp and savory next to it.

## The Drink: Hot Mulled Cider, Dressed Up

Per Fox News, Michaels' version has four parts: the cider, mulling spices, a caramel-rimmed glass, and whipped cream on top. The segment didn't publish exact measurements in the material I have, so treat the steps below as a framework and adjust to taste.

### How to build it

1. **Warm the cider with the mulling spices.** Heat it gently in a pot on the stove. Keep it below a hard boil so the apple flavor stays bright rather than cooked down.
2. **Rim the glass with caramel.** Dip or brush the edge of a mug or heatproof glass in caramel so it clings in a ring. Do this before pouring, so the rim has time to set slightly.
3. **Pour the hot cider in.** Strain out the whole spices as you pour if you don't want them in the cup.
4. **Top with whipped cream.** This turns a simple warm drink into something closer to a café dessert.

The caramel rim does real work here. Each sip picks up a little sweetness at the edge, so the drink changes as you go.

## The Pairing: Extra-Sharp Cheddar

Michaels paired the cider with extra-sharp cheddar, which Fox News described as a classic fall flavor combination. The reasoning is easy to taste for yourself, and it's my analysis rather than something he said on air: apple is sweet and slightly tart, while aged cheddar is salty, rich and tangy. Each balances the other. A bite of cheese before a sip of warm, spiced cider makes both taste more rounded.

### Serving tips

- Cut the cheddar into small blocks or thin wedges so people can grab a bite between sips.
- Pick a truly sharp cheese. A mild cheddar will disappear next to sweet, spiced cider.
- Let the cheese sit out briefly before serving so its flavor opens up.

## Turn It Into a Fall Hosting Spread

The two pieces together already make a small, low-effort entertaining plan. A few ways to build on them:

- **Set up a cider station.** Keep a pot of mulled cider warm, with caramel-rimmed mugs lined up and a bowl of whipped cream nearby, so guests can finish their own drinks.
- **Put out a simple board.** Cheddar is the anchor. Add whatever fall produce you have on hand, such as sliced apples, though that's my suggestion rather than part of Michaels' demonstration.
- **Use it for the holidays.** A caramel-rimmed, whipped-cream-topped cider reads as festive without requiring a cocktail menu, and it works for guests who don't drink alcohol.

## Why Start at an Orchard

Cider tastes best when it comes from somewhere you can picture. If you live within driving distance of a mill or orchard, buying fresh cider there gives you a better base for this recipe than a generic bottle. The orchard visit also becomes part of the outing, and you can pick up the apples, the cider, and ideas for the table at once.

If you're planning a trip to Fly Creek Cider Mill & Orchard, check current hours and seasonal offerings with the mill directly before you go, since those details weren't part of the broadcast coverage.

## The Takeaway

Next time you heat cider this fall, rim the glass with caramel, crown it with whipped cream, and set a plate of extra-sharp cheddar beside it. Sweet, warm, salty and sharp on one table is all you need.
