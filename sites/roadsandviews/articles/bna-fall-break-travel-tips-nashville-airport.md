Nashville International Airport (BNA) just put out a heads-up for fall break travelers: ongoing construction around the airport means you cannot treat this like a normal travel day. If you're flying out of BNA this fall break, here's exactly what the airport recommends so you don't end up watching your flight leave without you.

## Give Yourself More Time Than You Think You Need

This is the one tip that covers almost every other risk. Construction at and around BNA is actively affecting how traffic moves, and fall break is one of the busiest travel windows of the year. That combination means drop-off loops, parking areas, and entry roads can back up fast.

BNA recommends arriving earlier than you normally would. If your pre-construction habit was showing up 90 minutes before a domestic flight, bump that to at least two hours. For international flights or if you're traveling with kids, two and a half hours is a safer buffer. The extra time costs you nothing but a little waiting at the gate — missing your flight costs a lot more.

## Take Alternate Routes to the Airport

The airport is specifically flagging congestion around the usual access roads due to construction activity. Before you leave, check current traffic conditions and look up the alternate routes BNA recommends. Don't rely only on a navigation app defaulting to its usual path — it may not account for construction-specific slowdowns that aren't coded in as a full closure.

A few practical moves here:

- **Search before you leave.** Pull up BNA's official website or social channels for any posted detour or construction alerts specific to your travel dates.
- **Leave buffer time for the drive itself.** If your normal drive to the airport is 25 minutes, plan for 40.
- **Communicate the plan to anyone dropping you off.** If someone's driving you, make sure they know the alternate route too — getting separated or turned around at drop-off during peak congestion is a real stressor you don't need.

## Sort Out Parking Before You Get There

Fall break means parking lots fill up quickly. Showing up without a plan and circling for a spot eats directly into your flight buffer. BNA recommends thinking through your parking strategy in advance.

Your options generally break down like this:

- **Pre-book a spot.** If BNA offers online parking reservations (check their website before your trip), use them. Guaranteed spots remove one entire variable from your morning.
- **Know which lots are closest to your terminal.** If you're traveling with a lot of luggage or with kids, the walking distance between your parked car and check-in matters more than it does on a solo business trip.
- **Budget the time.** Even with a spot secured, getting from your car to the check-in counter takes longer during peak periods. Parking is not the finish line — it's just the start of the airport portion of your trip.

## Have Your ID Ready Before You Hit the TSA Line

BNA is reminding travelers to come prepared with proper identification for TSA screening. This sounds basic, but it's consistently one of the things that slows people down when they're already rushing.

What counts as proper ID:

- A **REAL ID-compliant driver's license or state ID** is required for domestic flights if you're using a state-issued ID. If yours isn't REAL ID compliant, you'll need a passport or another accepted federal document.
- **Passports** work for both domestic and international flights and are always a safe fallback.
- For **travelers under 18**, TSA does not require ID for domestic travel, but having some form of identification on hand for kids is still smart.

If you're not sure whether your license is REAL ID compliant, look for the star in the upper right corner. No star, no go — bring your passport instead.

## Traveling with Family? Add These to Your Checklist

Fall break is a heavily family-oriented travel period, and airports with construction are genuinely harder to navigate with kids, strollers, and multiple bags. A few add-ons worth thinking through:

**Strollers and car seats:** TSA allows strollers and car seats through security, but they go through the X-ray belt, which takes time. Gate-check your stroller so you have it right up until boarding.

**Snacks and entertainment:** Security lines during peak fall break are longer than usual. Having snacks and something for kids to do in line makes the wait more manageable for everyone around you, not just your own family.

**Split the tasks at security:** If you're with a partner, one person manages kids through the scanner while the other handles the bins, bags, and shoes. Decide this before you get to the front of the line, not at it.

## The One Thing That Ties It All Together

Every tip BNA is sharing this fall break points back to the same underlying principle: the airport is operating in a more complicated state than usual, and the travelers who account for that in their planning will have a dramatically smoother experience than those who don't.

Check BNA's official channels for any construction-specific updates before you leave for the airport. Build in the extra time. Get your ID in order. And if you can pre-book parking, do it.

The flight itself is the easy part — getting through BNA efficiently is what takes a little prep this season.
