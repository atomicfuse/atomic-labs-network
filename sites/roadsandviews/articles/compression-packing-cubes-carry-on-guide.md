Most of us love being somewhere new. The getting there part, with its sprawling airports, overstuffed bags, and baggage fees, is the tax we pay. A recent BuzzFeed shopping roundup of 28 products for people who love travel but hate the actual travel part included one deceptively simple fix for the packing side of that: compression packing cubes.

Here's how to use them to get more into one bag, find your stuff faster, and keep airport stress low. I'm writing this as someone who lives out of a bag, so the focus is on what's practical, not what looks cute on a shelf.

## What compression packing cubes actually do

Per BuzzFeed's roundup, compression packing cubes are meant to maximize suitcase space and keep clothes organized. That's the whole pitch, and it's a good one. Regular cubes sort your stuff into categories. Compression versions go a step further and squeeze the bulk out of soft items so they take up less room.

That matters most when you're trying to avoid checking a bag. Less volume means a better shot at keeping everything in your carry-on.

## Why this matters if you hate airports

The airport pain points are predictable: checked-bag fees, chaotic repacking at the gate, and the stress of digging through a bag in a crowded terminal. Compression cubes go after all three at once.

- **Fewer fees:** If everything fits in a carry-on, you may skip the checked-bag charge. Check your airline's rules, since sizes and fees vary.
- **Less chaos:** Every item has a home, so you're not turning your bag inside out for a charger.
- **Faster repacking:** Pull a cube out, swap it in, zip it up.

## How to pack with them, step by step

These are practical habits, not anything from the roundup, so treat them as my own take.

### 1. Sort before you stuff
Decide what goes in each cube before you pack. Tops in one, bottoms in another, underwear and socks in a small one. Mixed-up cubes defeat the point.

### 2. Roll or fold flat, then compress
Soft stuff like tees, leggings, and hoodies compresses best. Fill the cube evenly, close it, then squeeze the extra bulk out.

### 3. Put the heavy, bulky things at the bottom of your bag
Cubes make neat, stackable blocks. Build a base with the dense ones and fit shoes and toiletries around them.

### 4. Keep one cube as your dirty-laundry cube
Once a cube is empty, it becomes your used-clothes bag. Your clean stuff stays clean, and you aren't repacking a mess at the end of the trip.

## Packing mistakes that waste space

- **Overfilling a single cube.** If it won't zip, you're fighting it. Split it into two.
- **Compressing everything.** Bulky, stiff items like a structured jacket don't squash well and can wrinkle. Wear those on the plane instead.
- **Skipping the plan.** Cubes organize your stuff, but they don't decide how much you bring. A tighter wardrobe still does the heavy lifting.

## The nomad angle: why it's worth it

If you're moving between cities every few weeks, packing is a recurring chore, not a one-off. A system that takes ten minutes instead of forty adds up fast. It also helps in shared hostels, coworking-friendly Airbnbs, and short-stay rentals, where you may not have drawers. Living out of cubes means your suitcase is your closet.

## Is it worth buying?

BuzzFeed's roundup frames these as an easy, affordable upgrade, and that's the right way to think about them. You don't need to change how you travel. You just need a better way to fit what you already bring. Try them on one trip, see whether you stay under your airline's carry-on limit, and decide from there.

## Your next move

Before your next flight, lay out everything you plan to bring, sort it into cubes, and see if it all fits in a carry-on. If it does, you've just saved yourself a fee and a trip to the baggage carousel.
