Rewatching a favorite sitcom feels like comfort food until one episode makes you wince. With American Dad past 400 episodes and still easy to find on streaming, plenty of fans now pick and choose instead of watching straight through. By the end of this piece, you'll have four episodes to consider skipping and a simple test for spotting which jokes tend to age badly.

The idea comes from a skip list published by TVLine, which opens from a simple premise: American Dad is arguably one of Seth MacFarlane's greatest creations, but that doesn't mean every episode is a slam dunk or required viewing. Per that piece, the weaker entries tend to lean on uncomfortable shock value, animal cruelty, or incest-adjacent premises.

The source ranks ten episodes. I only have details on four of them, so those are the four covered here.

## The four episodes flagged

### "The 42-Year-Old Virgin" (Season 3, Episode 6)
This is the earliest of the group. If you're rewatching in order, it's an easy one to leave out without losing the thread of the show's bigger running gags.

### "Choosy Wives Choose Smith" (Season 4, Episode 4)
Season 4 turns up twice on the list. This episode is one of the two entries the list identifies from that stretch.

### "Pulling Double Booty" (Season 4, Episode 6)
The second Season 4 pick, just two episodes after the last one. Skipping both means a short, painless detour through that part of the season.

### "Stan's Best Friend" (Season 7, Episode 14)
The latest of the four. It shows that the list isn't about the show's early rough patch alone; the flagged material is spread across the run.

TVLine's reasons for the full list center on the three categories above: shock for shock's sake, animal cruelty, and incest-adjacent setups. I can't tell you which reason applies to which episode, so check the full write-up if that detail matters to you.

## Why jokes age differently

Here's my take, not TVLine's. Comedy that works on a rewatch usually has a second layer. A great character gag, a well-built running joke, or a sharp bit of satire gives you something new on the third viewing. A joke whose whole engine is "this is gross" or "this is uncomfortable" has nothing left once the surprise is gone.

That's a useful rule beyond this one show. Surprise is a one-time resource. Craft is renewable.

## A quick test for any rewatch

Before you press play on an episode you half remember, ask three things:

1. **Do I remember the joke or the jolt?** If you only recall feeling uneasy, the humor probably leaned on shock.
2. **Would I show this to a friend who loves the show?** If you'd hesitate, that's a signal.
3. **Does the episode matter to the plot?** Most American Dad episodes are self-contained, so skipping one rarely costs you anything.

## How to build your own skip list

With 400+ episodes, nobody needs to watch everything. Keep a running note of episodes that made you cringe, mark them, and move on. Comparing your list with TVLine's is a fun way to see where fans agree.

The takeaway: treat a long-running show like a playlist, not a book. Skip the tracks that don't work, and spend your time on the ones that still make you laugh.
