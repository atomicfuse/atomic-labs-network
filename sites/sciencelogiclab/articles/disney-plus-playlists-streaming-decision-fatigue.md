Disney+ has expanded its "Playlists" feature, and it targets a habit most of us know well: opening a streaming app, scrolling for 40 minutes, and watching nothing. Per a Yahoo-published piece by a writer who admits to doing exactly that, the feature groups movies and shows by theme, genre, franchise, rating, or vibe, so you spend less time browsing and more time watching.

Below, you'll get a clear picture of why picking something to watch feels so draining, how Playlists try to fix it, and a simple way to use any streaming app's curated lists without falling back into the scroll.

## What Disney+ Playlists Actually Do

Instead of handing you a giant grid of titles, Playlists bundle content into ready-made groups. According to the Yahoo article, those groups can be built around a theme, a genre, a franchise, a rating, or a mood. Seasonal moments, such as Halloween, are part of the push.

The idea is to make watching more "lean-back." You pick a category that fits your mood, and the app does the sorting. The article's writer likes this as a fix for decision fatigue, the mental tiredness that comes from making too many small choices.

## Why Choosing Feels So Hard

Decision fatigue is the useful name for what happens when a choice list gets long. Every title demands a quick judgment: Do I want this? Is it too long? Have I seen it? Each judgment is tiny, but they add up, and at some point the easiest option is to quit choosing and keep scrolling.

That's the trap. Scrolling feels like progress because you're still "looking," but you haven't narrowed anything down. A curated list changes the job. You make one decision (what kind of evening is this?) instead of dozens.

## The Catch: You Can't See What You're Getting

The Yahoo writer's main criticism is that Playlists need a better preview, or a "Surprise Me" layer, before they start autoplaying content. In other words, if a playlist jumps straight into something, you may not know what you've landed on or why it was picked.

That's a fair design point. A shortcut that makes you commit blind can send you right back to browsing, now with the extra step of backing out. A quick preview of what's in the list would keep the speed without the gamble.

## Why Streamers Are Competing on Convenience

Per the same coverage, streaming users are overwhelmed by choice, and platforms are responding with curated, lean-back discovery tools. Expanding Playlists around seasonal moments like Halloween suggests services are competing on how easy it is to find something, not just how big their library is. That's our read on the trend, and it makes sense: a huge catalog is only valuable if you can find something in it.

## How to Use Any Curated List Without Scrolling

These steps work with Disney+ Playlists or any similar feature:

1. **Pick the mood first.** Decide "cozy," "spooky," or "family-friendly" before you open the app.
2. **Choose one list.** Resist hopping between categories. Switching is how the scroll starts again.
3. **Set a two-minute limit.** If nothing grabs you, take the first option that passes a basic test: right length, right rating.
4. **Give it ten minutes.** If it isn't working, move to the next item on the same list rather than starting over.

## What Would Make Playlists Better

Based on the Yahoo writer's complaint, the clearest upgrade is a preview before autoplay. A "Surprise Me" option would also suit people who want the app to make the call outright. Neither is confirmed as coming, so treat them as a wishlist rather than news.

## The Takeaway

The next time you open a streaming app, decide on a mood before you look at a single title, then let one curated list do the narrowing. Fewer choices is the real feature.
