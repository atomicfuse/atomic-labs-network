## Why a plane can decide a route

A long-haul flight is a balancing act between fuel, passengers, and cargo. Fuel is heavy, and the farther you fly, the more of it you have to carry, which leaves less room for paying customers. A route that loses money on an older, thirstier aircraft can become profitable on a more efficient one.

Think of it like a road trip in two cars. Both can make the drive, but one burns far less gas. Over a very long trip, that difference decides whether the journey is worth making at all.

That's why "more fuel-efficient planes" is more than an environmental talking point. It's also a business tool. Burning less fuel per seat can make thinner routes viable, such as cities that don't have enough demand to fill a huge jet every day.
