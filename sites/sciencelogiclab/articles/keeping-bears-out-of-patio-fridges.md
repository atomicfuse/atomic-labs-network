A bear strolled through an unlocked gate at a home in Naples, Florida, dug through the trash, opened a patio fridge and helped itself to an alcoholic drink, per Fox News. The homeowner got quite a surprise. You'll get a clearer idea of why a bear would do this, and a short list of ways to keep one from treating your patio like a bar.

## What happened in Naples

Fox News reports that the bear wandered into the yard through a gate that was not latched. Once inside, it rummaged through the trash, then opened the outdoor refrigerator and took a drink.

The details I can't give you are the ones the report doesn't include here, such as how long the bear stayed or how it ended. What the story does show is a bear moving through an ordinary suburban yard as if it were a buffet.

## Why a bear would do this

The reporting doesn't explain the bear's motives, so what follows is general reasoning rather than a finding about this animal. Still, the sequence makes sense. Bears are generally drawn to places where food is easy to reach. Trash smells like food. A fridge is a box that holds food. A bear that finds one reward in a yard has a reason to check the next thing.

The lesson is that the bear wasn't being clever so much as being efficient. An open gate, accessible garbage and a fridge it could pull open added up to a low-effort meal.

## The real story: easy access

Suburban wildlife stories like this one keep going viral, and it's tempting to read them as animals acting strangely. A better reading is that animals move through suburban spaces looking for food, and every unsecured item is an invitation.

Notice what actually let this bear in. It wasn't a break-in. It was an unlocked gate. That is the cheapest fix of all.

## How to bear-proof your patio

If you live somewhere bears roam, these steps address exactly what the Naples bear took advantage of.

### 1. Latch the gate

Make it a habit to check that every gate is closed and latched, especially at night. A gate that swings open on its own is not a barrier.

### 2. Lock down the trash

Keep garbage in a closed, secure spot such as a garage or shed until pickup day, rather than leaving it out in the yard. Trash was the bear's first stop, so it is the first thing to fix.

### 3. Treat the outdoor fridge like a pantry

If you keep food or drinks in a patio fridge, make sure it can't be pulled open easily. A simple latch or lock, or moving the fridge to a closed area, takes away the reward.

### 4. Clear out other food smells

Put away anything edible left outside, including leftovers, pet dishes and grill remnants. If it smells like food to you, it smells much stronger to a bear.

## If a bear gets into your yard

Stay inside and give it space. Don't approach it for a photo, even though plenty of people would be tempted. Let it leave on its own, and if it lingers or seems unwilling to go, contact your local wildlife officials. Afterward, figure out what drew it in and remove that attraction so it doesn't come back.

## The takeaway

Go check your gate latch and your trash storage tonight. The Naples bear needed no special skill, only a door someone forgot to close.
