## How to judge a new model yourself in 20 minutes

Benchmarks make headlines, but your own tasks tell you more. Once Qwen4-Max is available to you, or any model is, try this:

### 1. Pick three real tasks

Choose things you actually do: summarize a long article, rewrite a clumsy paragraph, or explain a confusing bill. Generic trivia tells you little.

### 2. Run the same prompt everywhere

Paste identical wording into each model you want to compare. Changing the prompt changes the result, which muddies the test.

### 3. Check facts you already know

Ask about a topic you know well and look for confident mistakes. Fluent but wrong is the classic failure of language models.

### 4. Note speed and cost

A slightly better answer that takes much longer or costs much more may not be the better choice for you.

### 5. Repeat in a week

Models get updated, and first impressions fade. A second round gives you a steadier picture.

## The surprising takeaway: the leaderboard matters less than your task

It's tempting to treat AI like a sports league with a single champion. In practice, models differ in what they do well, and the "best" one for coding may not be best for writing or translation. The headline question, "is Qwen4-Max better than the others?", is less useful than "is it better at the thing I need?"

That shift in thinking makes you harder to fool by launch-day marketing, from any company.
