Every October, Breast Cancer Awareness Month urges women to get screened. Far less gets said about the moment after: the call-back, the abnormal result, and the question of how to pay for what comes next. By the end of this article, you'll know why that gap matters and how one Malaysian campaign is trying to close it.

Per a report originally published by The Star, UMMC's Pink ESG Wellness campaign focuses on helping women, especially those from B40 (lower-income) households, get follow-up diagnostics and treatment after an abnormal breast screening result. The campaign's argument is that awareness alone isn't enough. Support has to go further.

## Screening is the start, not the finish

A screening is designed to flag anything that needs a closer look. An abnormal result means you need more testing. It does not, on its own, mean you have cancer. That distinction is worth holding onto, because the anxiety of waiting can be heavy.

What the campaign highlights is what happens next: follow-up diagnostics, and then treatment if a diagnosis is confirmed. According to the report, this is where cost and emotional burden pile up, and where women from lower-income households can be most at risk of stalling.

## What to do if your result is abnormal

The source article doesn't lay out a clinical checklist, so take these as sensible general steps rather than medical instructions from the campaign:

1. **Don't ignore the call-back.** Follow-up is the whole point of screening. Delay is the real risk.
2. **Ask what the next test is and why.** Knowing the purpose of each step makes the process less frightening.
3. **Ask about costs early.** Find out what the follow-up will cost, and whether financial assistance exists, before you decide to put it off.
4. **Bring someone with you.** The emotional load is real, and a second set of ears helps.
5. **Talk to your doctor about any concerns.** They can explain your specific result.

## The hidden cost problem

The campaign's premise is that for B40 households, the price of getting a diagnosis and care can be a barrier in itself. The report points to rising healthcare costs and unequal access as the backdrop. A woman can do everything right by turning up for screening and still hit a wall when the bill for the next stage arrives.

That's why Pink ESG Wellness frames its work as moving from awareness to action. Telling women to screen is easy. Making sure an abnormal result leads to a diagnosis and treatment is harder, and arguably matters more.

## Support that goes beyond treatment

According to the report, the campaign's view of support is broad. It covers:

- Financial aid for follow-up diagnostics
- Access to treatment
- Income opportunities for survivors
- Long-term community reintegration

The last two points are easy to overlook. Finishing treatment doesn't automatically mean life returns to normal. Survivors may need ways to earn an income and to rebuild their place in their community. A campaign that accounts for that treats breast cancer as a long journey, not a single medical event.

## How you can help

The report points readers to Pink ESG Wellness at Pavilion Damansara Heights as a way to show support this October. The source doesn't detail specific donation amounts or activities, so check the campaign's own channels or the venue for current ways to take part.

Beyond that campaign, a few things any of us can do:

- **Encourage someone to screen,** and offer to go with her.
- **Ask about follow-up,** not just the first appointment, when a friend or relative gets a call-back.
- **Support local initiatives** that fund diagnostics and survivor livelihoods.

## The takeaway

If you or someone close to you has been asked to come back after a screening, treat the follow-up as the priority and ask about financial help right away. Screening only saves lives when the next step actually happens.
