If you're thinking about trying for a baby, you've probably run into a wall of advice: eat this, skip that, buy this supplement. An illustrated comic from NPR, shared by WomenDevision, tries to cut through the noise with seven evidence-backed lifestyle habits that may improve your chances of getting pregnant. Its biggest message is that fertility is not just a woman's job. It's a shared project.

A caveat up front: the summary of the comic available to us lists the themes of the seven habits, not the detailed advice, numbers or specific foods inside each panel. So this article won't invent specifics. Instead, it shows you how the guide is organized, what each theme asks of you, and how to turn it into a conversation and a plan. For the exact foods and activities, read the full comic at NPR.

## Fertility Is a Two-Person Topic

The most useful framing in the guide is that it offers advice for both men and women. That matters because fertility conversations often land on one partner by default.

Per the guide's description, support for fertility applies to both partners. In practice, that gives you a reason to approach this together: sit down, read the guide side by side, and decide which changes you'll make as a team. Shared habits are easier to keep, and nobody feels singled out.

## The Seven Themes the Guide Covers

Here is how the guide breaks things down, per the outline of its seven habits:

1. **Eat fertility-supporting foods.** Diet is a core focus. The comic points to foods that may help when you're trying to conceive.
2. **Avoid activities that may harm fertility.** The guide flags things to steer clear of, not only things to add.
3. **Support fertility for both partners.** Neither person is off the hook, and neither is to blame.
4. **Make evidence-backed lifestyle changes.** The emphasis is on what research supports rather than folk wisdom.
5. **Build healthier daily routines.** Small, repeatable habits over dramatic overhauls.
6. **Reduce fertility-related risk factors.** Identify what could work against you and address it.
7. **Prepare the body for conception.** Think of this as a lead-up period, not a last-minute scramble.

Notice the pattern: add helpful things, remove harmful ones, and make it routine.

## How to Use This as a Checklist

A guide only helps if it changes what you do on a Tuesday. Here's one way to make that happen without needing details beyond the themes above.

### Start with the food

Food is the most concrete theme, so begin there. Read the comic's food section and pick one or two changes you can make this week, such as adjusting what goes on your shared grocery list. Changing everything at once rarely sticks.

### Make a "what to avoid" list together

The comic calls out activities that may harm fertility. Write down the ones it names that apply to you. Doing this as a pair keeps it from feeling like a lecture, and it makes it easier to support each other when habits are hard to drop.

### Turn changes into routines

The guide stresses daily routines. Attach new habits to things you already do: a meal you already cook, a walk you already take. That's our suggestion, not the comic's, but it fits the guide's focus on repeatable habits.

### Check in on risk factors

Reducing risk factors is one of the seven themes. If you have a health condition, take medication, or have concerns about your own situation, a doctor is the right person to sort out which risk factors apply to you. A general guide can't do that.

## Why This Conversation Is Everywhere

More people are delaying parenthood, and many want proactive steps they can take on their own before or alongside medical care. That's the appeal of a lifestyle-focused guide: it offers things within your control, without a clinic visit.

It's also worth keeping expectations realistic. Lifestyle habits may improve your odds, as the guide's own wording suggests, but they aren't guarantees. If you've been trying for a while without success, or you're worried, talking with a healthcare provider is a sound next step, and it's not a sign you did something wrong.

## Keep It Low-Pressure

Fertility advice can easily tip into guilt. A good approach is the one this guide models: practical, evidence-based, and shared between partners. Treat the seven themes as a menu of things to try, not a test you can fail.

## The Takeaway

Open the full NPR comic with your partner this week, pick one food change and one thing to avoid, and agree on them together. Starting small and starting as a team is the habit that makes the other six easier.
