Some symptoms are easy to explain away. A bloated stomach? Must be something you ate. Spotting between periods? Probably stress. This article walks you through four signs that Dr Mary McCormack, a gynaecological oncologist with 24 years of experience, says women often disregard out of embarrassment, so you can recognise them and act sooner.

The advice comes from an interview reported by Inews. It lands in September, which is Gynaecological Cancer Awareness Month. Per the report, 60 people are diagnosed with these cancers every day in the UK, and many cases are highly treatable if caught early.

## Why These Cancers Get Missed

"Gynaecological cancers" covers ovarian, womb, cervical, vulval and vaginal cancers. According to the report, the main problem is not a lack of symptoms. It is that women normalise them, blame them on something else, or feel too embarrassed to mention them.

That delay matters because early detection saves lives. The practical goal here is simple: notice a change, name it, and say it out loud to a doctor.

## 1. Bloating and Abdominal Pain

Bloating is one of the most common complaints there is, which is exactly why it gets brushed off. It is often mistaken for digestive trouble such as IBS, or put down to diet.

The warning sign is bloating or abdominal pain that does not behave like your usual pattern. If it keeps happening or doesn't settle, don't wait for it to become obvious. Mention it, and say how long it has been going on.

## 2. Abnormal Bleeding

Bleeding that is out of the ordinary for you is a signal worth checking. That can mean bleeding between periods or any bleeding that doesn't fit your normal pattern.

It is easy to blame stress, hormones or menopause. Per the report, this kind of misattribution is one of the things that causes dangerous delays. Unusual bleeding is a reason to book an appointment, not a reason to wait and see.

## 3. Vulval Soreness or Lumps

This is the symptom where embarrassment tends to win. Soreness, or a lump, in the vulval area can feel too awkward to raise, so people hope it goes away.

Doctors examine intimate areas routinely. A lump or lasting soreness is the kind of thing they want to hear about early, when options are best.

## 4. Pelvic Pain

Pelvic pain is another symptom that can be explained away as period pain or just part of getting older. If it is new, persistent, or different from what you normally experience, treat it as information rather than something to tolerate.

## How to Raise It Without Dreading the Appointment

The shame is the real obstacle, so make the conversation easier on yourself. These are practical suggestions of mine, not from the interview:

- **Write it down first.** Note what the symptom is, when it started and how often it happens.
- **Lead with the symptom.** Say it plainly in your first sentence, before small talk eats the time.
- **Ask what happens next.** If you're told it's probably nothing, ask what would change that and when to come back.
- **Go back if it continues.** Persistent symptoms deserve a second look.

## Prevention Counts Too

Symptom awareness is only one layer. HPV vaccination and cervical screening are established ways to reduce risk and catch problems early. Keep up with your screening invitations, even when life is busy.

## The Takeaway

If something in your body has changed and it hasn't gone away, tell a doctor this week. Feeling embarrassed for five minutes is a far better trade than waiting months.
