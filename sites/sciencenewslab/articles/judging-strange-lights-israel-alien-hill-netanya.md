On a secluded cliff above the Mediterranean in Netanya, a group of UFO enthusiasts gathers after dark. They call the spot "Alien Hill," according to Ynetnews. They come to watch for mysterious lights, swap accounts of unexplained sightings and try to contact extraterrestrial beings through meditation.

You don't need to believe in aliens to learn something from them. By the end of this piece, you'll have a practical way to evaluate a strange light in the sky, and a clearer sense of why dark, open coastlines draw people hoping to see one.

## What happens on Alien Hill

Per Ynetnews, the gathering mixes three activities: watching the sky, documenting sightings and practicing CE5 meditation, a method aimed at making contact with extraterrestrials. The report balances believers' dramatic accounts with more cautious explanations, including the possibility that some of the lights are simply airplanes.

That tension is the most useful part of the story. The same patch of sky can read as a visitor or as air traffic, depending on who is looking and what they know.

## Why the timing feels familiar

UFOs, or UAPs (unidentified anomalous phenomena), are back in Israeli headlines, per the Ynetnews coverage, after recent reports of aerial objects near the Lebanese border raised security concerns. That is a different matter from hobbyists on a cliff. Still, it shows how quickly an unexplained object in the sky becomes a public conversation, whether the worry is security or wonder.

## A skeptic's checklist for strange lights

The Ynetnews report doesn't lay out a formal method, so what follows is general sky-watching practice, offered as my own suggestions rather than anything the group reportedly does.

### 1. Start with the boring answers

Airplanes are the explanation Ynetnews itself raises, and for good reason: aircraft carry bright lights and can look odd from a distance or an angle. Rule out flights first.

### 2. Note what the light actually does

Write down how it moves, how long you see it, its color and whether it blinks. Specifics turn a vague impression into something you can check later.

### 3. Record the where and when

Save the time, your location and the direction you were facing. Without those, no one, including you, can compare your sighting against flight schedules or other reports.

### 4. Get a second observer

Groups have an advantage here. When several people describe the same light differently, you learn how much of the experience comes from perception rather than the object.

### 5. Hold the conclusion loosely

"I can't identify this" is an honest result. It is not the same as "this is extraterrestrial." Unidentified simply means unidentified.

## CE5 meditation, explained plainly

Per Ynetnews, the group attempts to contact extraterrestrial beings through meditation. Nothing in the report offers evidence that this works, and it shouldn't be read as a scientific method. It is better understood as a ritual: a quiet, intentional way of spending a night under the sky. Whether or not anything answers, the practice reflects how closely spirituality and sky-watching overlap for many participants.

## Why coastlines and cliffs become legend

The report doesn't argue this point, so treat the following as my reasoning. A cliff over the sea offers a wide, open horizon with little in the way. Lights over water are hard to judge: with few landmarks, distance and size are difficult to gauge. Darkness removes context, and the mind fills the gap. Those conditions make a good setting for both real observation and good stories.

## The real draw: mystery you can stand inside

The most interesting question here isn't whether aliens exist. It's why people climb a hill at night to look up. Curiosity about the sky is one of the oldest human habits, and amateur watchers, the believers and skeptics alike, are practicing a rough version of citizen observation: look, record, compare notes.

You can borrow the good parts without the leap of faith. Find a dark, open spot, give your eyes time to adjust, and look up with a notebook.

Next time a strange light crosses your sky, write down what you see before you decide what it is.
