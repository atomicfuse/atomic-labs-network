- **Collision with a large celestial body**: This could be anything from a large asteroid or comet impacting the planet to the collision of two planets in a theoretical scenario.
- **Supernova effects**: If a nearby star goes supernova, the shockwave might strip a planet of its atmosphere and surface layers.

Each of these methods delves into fundamental principles of physics, such as gravity, energy transfer, and the laws of motion, making it a fascinating subject to explore scientifically.

## Impact of a Collision

Let’s focus on the collision scenario, which is the most relatable to Earth. An object roughly the size of a city can cause widespread destruction, and if it were one kilometer wide, the effects would be catastrophic. A collision of this size could release the same amount of energy as millions of nuclear bombs. The kinetic energy released upon impact is calculated using the formula:

**K.E. = 0.5 * m * v^2**

where **m** represents mass and **v** the velocity of the colliding object. For instance, considering a one-kilometer-diameter asteroid traveling at 15 kilometers per second, we can see just how severe the implications could be.
