Hello, Upper East Siders of the sports-and-sidelines set. Ella here, and I have a confession to share. It isn't mine, it's the GOAT's.

## The Scoop

Tom Brady has admitted to making "a lot" of parenting mistakes, per Page Six. The seven-time Super Bowl champ opened up on the *High Performance* podcast, and he reached for the one language he's fluent in: football. He compared his parenting missteps to throwing interceptions as a quarterback.

Yes, the man who spent decades making the rest of the league look like a cautionary tale is now saying he's thrown a few picks at home. Spotted: humility. Rare sighting.

## Why the Interception Metaphor Works

Here's my take, and it's mine alone: the metaphor is smarter than it sounds. Any quarterback will tell you an interception isn't the end of the game. It's what you do on the next drive that matters. Brady, per Page Six, framed his parenting the same way: you make the mistake, you learn from it, you go back out there.

It's hard not to notice how neatly this fits a man whose whole brand has been about relentless improvement. The playbook is just being applied to a tougher opponent: a household.

## What Brady Says He's Working On

According to the report, Brady is focused on a few things:

- **Being present.** Not just in the room, but actually there.
- **Understanding his kids' perspectives.** Seeing things from their side rather than only his own.
- **Building their resilience.** Teaching them to take a hit and keep playing.
- **Balancing mentor mode with non-judgmental support.** Knowing when to coach and when to simply be there.

That last one is the juicy bit, darlings. Anyone who has watched a high achiever try to turn every conversation into a film session knows the temptation. Brady, by his own telling, is learning that kids don't always need a coach. Sometimes they need a teammate who listens. (Reading between the lines is my specialty. That's my interpretation, not a direct quote.)

## The Bigger Trend

Brady's candor lands at a moment when public figures are getting more open about divorce, co-parenting, emotional presence, and raising teenagers. The era of the flawless celebrity parent, all curated holiday cards and no cracks, seems to be fading. Vulnerability is the new brand.

I'll admit it's a clever pivot. Nobody believes the perfect-parent act anyway, and "I've thrown a lot of interceptions" is far more believable than a highlight reel. It's also more relatable, which any good publicist (and any good gossip) knows is gold.

## Ella's Take

Brady didn't share specifics about which interceptions he means, and I won't invent any. Some secrets stay in the locker room. What he did share is a mindset: mistakes happen, the scoreboard isn't final, and the best thing a father can do is read the defense, meaning his kids, before calling the next play.

The man who never wanted to lose just admitted that the best parents aren't the ones who never get picked off. They're the ones who walk back onto the field.
