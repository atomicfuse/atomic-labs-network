Per reports from Fox Sports, Lionel Messi is expected to be added to Argentina's squad for an October 6 friendly against Benin in Buenos Aires — a match the Argentine Football Association is framing as an official farewell for its greatest-ever player. If it goes ahead as planned, that night in Buenos Aires won't just be a football match. It will be the closing chapter of the most decorated international career the sport has ever seen.

Here's what we know, what's still uncertain, and why this particular friendly carries weight well beyond the result.

## The Match: What We Know So Far

The October 6 fixture against Benin in Buenos Aires is being positioned explicitly as a farewell match, according to Fox Sports. It sits inside an upcoming friendly window for Argentina, and Messi — who has been on international retirement — is reportedly expected to be included in the squad for the occasion.

The location matters. Buenos Aires is where Messi was celebrated as a World Cup winner in December 2022, when millions flooded the streets for scenes that felt almost unprecedented in Argentine history. Bringing him back there, one more time, in a controlled farewell setting makes obvious emotional sense. It gives fans a proper goodbye rather than the open-ended ambiguity that often follows a player quietly stepping away.

That said, his inclusion is still being reported as "expected" rather than confirmed. Until Argentina officially release the squad, treat this as very likely rather than certain.

## Why a Friendly Against Benin Can Feel Historic

On paper, Argentina versus Benin is not a fixture that demands attention. Benin are not a powerhouse, and the result will count for nothing in any table or qualification group. But football has always understood that context transforms a match.

Think of Andrés Iniesta's last Spain appearance, or Philipp Lahm's farewell in a Munich shirt — moments that transcended the scoreline because they marked the end of something genuinely irreplaceable. This is that, scaled up considerably.

Messi's Argentina journey was never straightforward. He retired from international football once before, in 2016 after the Copa América Centenario final defeat, before reversing that decision. There were years of frustration, near-misses, and a vocal section of the Argentine public that questioned whether he truly cared about the Albiceleste the way he cared about Barcelona. The 2021 Copa América win — Argentina's first major international trophy in 28 years — began to settle that argument. The 2022 World Cup in Qatar buried it entirely.

Watching him lift the World Cup trophy, after everything, was one of those rare moments where a sport produces the ending it deserves. An official farewell match in Buenos Aires feels like the right coda to that story.

## Scaloni's Future Adds Another Layer

The farewell moment arrives alongside genuine uncertainty at the top of Argentina's coaching setup. Per the Fox Sports report, Lionel Scaloni's future with the national team is not settled. That matters because Scaloni is the architect of Argentina's golden period — the man who trusted this squad, built the system around Messi, and delivered back-to-back tournament wins.

If Scaloni departs alongside Messi's farewell, Argentina face a generational reset at both player and management level simultaneously. That's a significant transition for any football nation to manage, even one with deep talent reserves.

For now, neither departure is confirmed — but the uncertainty itself is worth watching. The weeks around the October window may tell us a lot about where Argentina's football project heads next.

## FIFA Bans Complicate the Squad Picture

The October window comes with an additional wrinkle: the Fox Sports report notes that several Argentina players are currently serving FIFA bans. The brief does not specify which players or the precise nature of those bans, but it adds a practical complication to squad selection beyond the Messi farewell narrative.

Coach Scaloni — or whoever is managing the team — will need to build a squad around those absences while also accommodating what is essentially a ceremonial occasion. Balancing sporting utility with the emotional weight of the moment is not a straightforward task.

## The Real Question: Who Carries Argentina Forward?

Messi's farewell, whenever it officially lands, forces a question that Argentine football cannot defer much longer: who becomes the face of this team?

For the better part of two decades, the answer was automatic. Everything was built around Messi — tactically, commercially, emotionally. The entire fanbase and the global perception of the Albiceleste ran through him. That's not a criticism; it's simply what happens when you have arguably the best player in the history of the sport.

Post-Messi, Argentina still have serious players. Julián Álvarez has established himself as a genuine world-class striker. Enzo Fernández has the profile of a long-term midfield anchor. Others will step into larger roles. But inheriting the symbolic weight Messi carried — not just the goals and assists, but the identity he gave the national team — is a different challenge entirely. No single player takes that on overnight.

The October farewell match will be watched globally for Messi, but the sharper football minds in the crowd will also be scanning the team around him, looking for clues about what Argentina's next chapter looks like.
