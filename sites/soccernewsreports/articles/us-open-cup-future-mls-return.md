The U.S. Open Cup has spent a few years with a murky outlook. Now, per the New York Times, that picture is clearing. After a spirited final, and with all U.S.-based MLS teams set to participate once again, the tournament looks to be moving from uncertainty toward renewed relevance.

This piece makes one argument: full MLS participation is the single biggest lever for the Open Cup's future, and a good final is the proof of what the competition can be when everyone is in it. Here's why that matters to you as a fan.

## Why MLS Returning Changes Everything

MLS participation had been a major flashpoint for the Open Cup's credibility and visibility, according to the Times. That makes sense when you think about what the tournament is. It's a national cup meant to bring together clubs from across the American soccer pyramid. When the top division's teams aren't all there, the competition feels like it's missing its headline act.

A full return signals what the Times describes as a potentially important reset. Our read: it's less about any single fixture and more about restoring the basic promise of the event. Everyone is in the same draw, and everyone can be beaten.

## What a Good Final Does for the Tournament

Cup competitions live or die on stories. A spirited final gives fans something to talk about and gives casual viewers a reason to check what happens next year. The Times frames this final as the kind of showcase the tournament can build on.

It won't fix everything alone. But a compelling final paired with a clearer structure is a much better starting point than a good game in an event few people were sure about.

## What It Means for Lower-Division Clubs

The Open Cup's appeal has always been the chance for smaller clubs to meet bigger ones. When every U.S.-based MLS team takes part, those matchups return to the table. For a lower-division side, that can mean:

- A shot at a marquee opponent
- A bigger audience than a typical league night
- A reason for local supporters to show up and pay attention

That's our framing of the opportunity rather than a reported outcome, but it's the logic behind why the tournament matters to grassroots soccer culture. The Open Cup works as a bridge between that world and the top professional level.

## The Case for Making It a Bigger Event

Fan interest in U.S. soccer is growing, per the Times, and the 2026 World Cup on home soil is part of that backdrop. That gives the Open Cup a chance to reclaim momentum. Whether it does will depend on things the Times piece doesn't settle, such as scheduling, promotion and how the competition is presented to fans.

If you want a quick test of whether the revival is real, watch for these signs next season:

1. Whether every U.S.-based MLS team does take part, as planned
2. How the early rounds are promoted and broadcast
3. Whether the lower-division matchups get the attention they deserve

## The Takeaway

The Open Cup's future is brighter because the right teams are returning and the last final reminded people why it's worth watching. Next season, make a point of attending or tuning in to an early-round match involving a lower-division club. That is where the tournament's identity lives, and your attention is part of how it gets rebuilt.
