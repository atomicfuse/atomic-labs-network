Below Deck Season 13 has barely left the dock, and the chief stew is already saying the quiet part out loud. Per Us Weekly, Fraser Olender called a charter guest a "shady bitch" after she criticized the crew's teamwork during an overwhelming first charter.

That's the news, and it's a reliably Below Deck way to open a season. Here's what's worth chewing on.

## The Setup: A Guest Critique and a Chief Stew's Reaction

According to Us Weekly's recap, the guest took issue with how the crew was working together. Fraser's response, the "shady bitch" line, came afterward. The report frames the first charter as overwhelming, which is a polite way of describing the genre's oldest tradition: a new crew, a new boat, and a guest list that doesn't care you haven't learned where the napkins live.

The report details are thin on exactly what the guest said or how the rest of the crew reacted, so we won't pretend to have the full transcript. What we do have is the shape of the moment: a critique about teamwork, aimed at a team that is, by definition, still figuring out how to be one.

## Why a First Charter Is Its Own Genre

Here's our take, and it's clearly take: the first charter of any Below Deck season is a stress test nobody has studied for. Everyone is meeting each other, learning the boat, and trying to look competent while doing it. A comment about teamwork on day one lands differently than it would in week six, because there isn't much teamwork yet to critique.

That's why Fraser's reaction is more interesting as a leadership moment than as a quotable insult. A chief stew is the person who sits between the guests and the interior team. When a guest takes a swipe at the crew's cohesion, the chief stew is the one who feels it first. It's hard not to wonder whether the sharpness of the comment reflected the pressure of the role as much as an opinion of the guest.

Whether the label was fair is another matter. Calling someone shady on the basis of a critique is a judgment about motive, and from the outside we only have a recap to go on. Reality TV edits love a quick, clean villain, and a first episode has to establish stakes fast. Fraser's line may be the sound bite, not the whole story.

## Captain Kerry Teases Surprises

The Us Weekly piece also previews comments from Captain Kerry Titheradge about the season ahead. He points to surprise changes, new crew dynamics, and the season's Thailand setting. The report also mentions teased franchise-first twists, though the specifics aren't spelled out, so anyone claiming to know what they are is guessing, and so are we.

What the tease does tell us is that this season is positioning itself as different. Season 13 is a long way into a franchise that fans know well, and a promise of something unprecedented is the kind of line that makes longtime viewers lean in. It also puts the captain, not just the crew, at the center of the story. If the changes land on the leadership side of the boat, a chaotic first charter could be just the warm-up.

## What to Watch Next

A few things are worth tracking as the season rolls on:

- **Fraser's standing with the guest-facing team.** A blunt reaction on day one can either build trust with a crew or become a recurring problem. We'll see which.
- **How the new crew gels.** The report highlights new crew dynamics, and teamwork was exactly what the guest picked on.
- **The Thailand backdrop.** It's the setting Captain Kerry flagged, and a fresh location tends to bring fresh logistical headaches.

## The Bottom Line

Fraser Olender's line will make the highlight reels, but the sharper story is what it says about the pressure of a first charter: a crew still learning each other's names is being graded on chemistry. If Captain Kerry's surprises are as big as he suggests, Season 13 may be remembered less for who got called what, and more for how fast this crew had to grow up.
