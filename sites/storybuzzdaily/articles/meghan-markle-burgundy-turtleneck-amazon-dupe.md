Beige, it's been a good run. Per Us Weekly, Meghan Markle recently stepped out in a burgundy fall outfit, and the outlet is calling the fitted burgundy turtleneck the season's affordable "It" sweater. Its budget pick is a $13 slim-fit top on Amazon, offered as a dupe for the designer Joseph style Markle wore.

That's the news. Here's the take: a $13 turtleneck is a low-risk way to test whether burgundy deserves a spot in your cold-weather rotation.

## What Markle Wore, and What Us Weekly Says to Buy

According to Us Weekly, Markle's look centered on burgundy, with a fitted turtleneck from Joseph as the key piece. The outlet pairs that with an Amazon slim-fit turtleneck at $13, pitched as a way to get the same polished, monochrome effect without the designer price tag.

Us Weekly also describes the style as slimming. That's the outlet's characterization, and fit is personal, so treat it as a suggestion rather than a promise. The practical point is that a close-fitting knit in one deep color tends to read as put-together with very little effort.

## Why Burgundy Is Edging Out Beige

Per the Us Weekly framing, burgundy is emerging as a richer fall alternative to beige and the other quiet-luxury neutrals that have dominated closets. Our read: it's hard not to see the appeal. Burgundy keeps the muted, grown-up mood of camel and oatmeal, but it actually looks like a choice. It's still a neutral in spirit, since it works with denim, black, grey and cream, yet it doesn't vanish into a beige-on-beige blur.

There's also the celebrity factor. When a high-profile name wears a shade in a clean, simple outfit, shoppers tend to look for a version they can actually afford. Dupe culture runs on exactly that instinct, and a plain turtleneck is about the easiest silhouette to replicate.

## Designer vs. Dupe: The Honest Comparison

What we know from Us Weekly: the original is a Joseph piece, the alternative costs $13 on Amazon, and both are fitted burgundy turtlenecks. What we can't say is how the fabric, weight or longevity of the two compare, because that hasn't been reported. Anyone buying should check the reviews and the material listing before assuming a $13 top feels like a luxury knit.

That said, the math is friendly. At that price, you could buy the top in burgundy and still have money left over for a second color if it works out.

## How to Wear It Without Copying Markle Exactly

The monochrome idea at the heart of Markle's outfit is easy to adapt. A few approaches that follow naturally from a single fitted burgundy layer:

- **For work:** Pair it with tailored trousers in a neutral tone and keep accessories minimal. The turtleneck does the heavy lifting.
- **For weekends:** Throw it under a relaxed jacket with your usual jeans. Instant fall, no effort.
- **For evening:** Wear it with dark bottoms and one statement piece, such as earrings or a good coat.

If you want the full tonal effect, keep the other pieces in the same color family and vary the textures. That's our styling suggestion, not something Us Weekly specified.

## The Bottom Line

Burgundy won't dethrone beige overnight, and plenty of closets will keep both. But if one celebrity outfit and one $13 turtleneck can nudge your wardrobe out of its neutral rut, that's a pretty cheap experiment. Beige will be fine. It always is.
