A Medium piece titled "How Rock Biopics Handle the Music" takes a look behind the soundtracks of five memorable biographical films. It examines how those movies use music to tell artists' stories, from soundtrack choices to the recreation of performances, and how they balance historical authenticity, emotional storytelling, and what audiences expect.

The piece doesn't need a fresh premiere to feel timely. Music biopics remain a major entertainment trend, with studios turning iconic artists' lives into prestige films and streaming hits. And as viewers argue over accuracy versus spectacle, the soundtrack has become one of the biggest factors in whether these movies feel convincing.

So here's the take, and it's mine, not the Medium writer's: in a rock biopic, the music is the performance that matters most.

## The Soundtrack Is the Real Lead Role

Casting gets the headlines. Who's playing the legend? How good is the transformation? Those are fun debates, and they dominate the pre-release chatter.

But a biopic about a musician has a unique problem. Every other kind of biographical film can ask us to believe in a person. This one also asks us to believe in a sound. If the songs land, the audience forgives a lot. If they don't, no amount of period-perfect styling can fix it.

That's the logic behind the Medium piece's focus. Its core question is how these films balance authenticity, emotion, and audience expectation, and the soundtrack is where all three collide in a single scene.

## Three Ways to Handle a Song

The Medium piece looks at recreated performances, original recordings, and actor-sung moments. Each choice does something different, and each carries its own risk.

### Original recordings

Using the real track is the shortcut to credibility. It's the sound fans already carry in their heads, so nothing has to be taken on faith. The trade-off, in my view, is that the actor can end up feeling like a very well-dressed lip-syncer, and the performance risks looking like imitation rather than interpretation.

### Actor-sung moments

Having the cast sing is the high-wire act. When it works, you get the feeling of watching something live and a little dangerous. When it doesn't, the illusion cracks, and everyone in the audience who knows the original hears the gap immediately.

### Recreated performances

Staging a famous concert or session is where authenticity and spectacle wrestle most openly. Do you copy the moment as faithfully as possible, or shape it to serve the story? The Medium piece frames this as part of the larger balancing act, and it's hard not to see it as the heart of the genre's debate.

## Myth Over Minutes

Here's the part I find most interesting. A biopic's music choices don't just illustrate a life; they argue for a version of it. Which song arrives at the emotional peak? Which gets a full performance and which gets a few bars under a montage? Those are editorial decisions, and they shape the myth of an artist as much as the script does.

That's why accuracy arguments around these films can feel slightly beside the point. Fans will happily catalog timeline changes, but the emotional truth of a scene often lives in how a song is placed and played. A film can get a date wrong and still make you feel exactly why a track mattered. The reverse is also true: a perfectly accurate scene with a flat musical moment feels like a museum label.

## The Hard Part Nobody Sees

The tension at the center of the genre is simple to state and tough to solve. A life is messy and spread across decades. A song is compact and builds to a single release. Turning one into the other means deciding what the music is for: proof of genius, a window into pain, or a crowd-pleasing payoff.

The five films in the Medium piece each answer that differently, which is what makes the comparison useful for anyone who watches these movies on streaming and wonders why some stick and others fade.

## What to Listen For Next Time

The next time you queue up a rock biopic, try a small experiment, and treat it as my suggestion rather than anything from the original piece:

- Notice whether you're hearing the original recording, the actor, or a re-staged version, and how your reaction changes.
- Pay attention to which songs get full performances and which are trimmed. That's where the film is telling you what the artist's story is *about*.
- Ask yourself whether you believed the music before you believed the performance.

That last one is the tell. When a rock biopic works, the audience isn't thinking about casting at all. They're thinking about the song, and for a movie about a musician, that may be the only review that counts.
