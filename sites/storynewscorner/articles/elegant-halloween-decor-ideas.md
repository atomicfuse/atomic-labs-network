Halloween has a reputation problem: the inflatable-everything, fake-cobweb-on-the-hedges look that makes a front yard resemble a clearance aisle after an earthquake. House Beautiful pushed back with a roundup of 20 elegant Halloween decorations, mostly from Pottery Barn plus other retailers, built around a simple promise: festive, not tacky. Its description sums up the mission as making your home "ghoulishly glamorous."

The list covers wall mirrors, candle holders, tableware and planters. Here's the angle worth pulling out of it: the best "elegant Halloween" pieces are the ones that work like regular decor wearing a costume.

## The Rule Hiding in the List

Per House Beautiful's picks, the sophisticated approach leans on materials and shapes rather than gore. Think antique brass, smoked amber glass, bouclé, ceramic and enameled cast iron. The spooky part is the motif; the grown-up part is everything else.

Take the opening items. There's an antique brass spider-web mirror and a smoked amber glass candle holder. Neither screams holiday. Put them on a wall or console and, unless a guest looks closely, they just read as moody. That's the trick, and it's why the list fits the timing: Halloween shopping is in full swing, and shoppers are increasingly looking for decor that feels elevated and reusable instead of disposable. (It's hard not to see the "quiet luxury" trend peeking through the cobwebs.)

## The Snakes Are Having a Moment

Serpents turn up repeatedly in the roundup: a serpentine wall hook, a sculptural golden snake, and a snake-wrapped mirror that's Nagini-inspired. For anyone who has read or watched Harry Potter, that Nagini nod is a fun wink, and a reminder that the most stylish fictional villains tend to be the ones with great accessories.

The Potter-adjacent theme continues with hanging taper candles described as Hogwarts-inspired. Floating candles are a famous piece of that world's Great Hall atmosphere, and the idea translates well to a dining room: it's drama achieved with wax and a ceiling hook, no fog machine required.

What makes the snake picks smart is their restraint. A gold snake reads as sculpture. A mirror with a serpent coiled around it reads as an antique-shop find. Neither requires you to explain yourself to your in-laws.

## Where the Fun Lives: The Table

If the wall pieces are the sophisticated half of the list, the tableware is where it gets playful. House Beautiful's lineup includes:

- Scorpion-topped cocktail picks
- Autumn leaf inlay coasters
- A lidded snack bowl
- Ghost-shaped pinch bowls
- A gold-decaled "Hex Mix" snack bowl
- A skull charcuterie board
- Linen napkins with an oak vine design
- Pumpkin-shaped enameled cast iron cocottes
- A skeleton serving collection with wine glasses

That's a surprisingly complete dinner party. The charm is in the mix: linen napkins and oak vines are quietly seasonal, while the skull board and skeleton serving pieces carry the theatrics. Put them together and you get a table that's festive without looking like a party store exploded on it. A snack bowl labeled "Hex Mix" is also, frankly, a pun that earns its counter space.

The pumpkin cocottes are the sleeper pick. Enameled cast iron is the kind of cookware people already want in November, and the pumpkin shape means it won't feel out of season when Thanksgiving rolls around. That's my read, not the list's, but it's the clearest example of the reusable idea at work.

## Cute Without Being Cartoonish

Not everything here is moody. There's a Gus the Ghost throw pillow with a bouclé pumpkin, a light blue ceramic pumpkin planter, a woodland critter doormat and a ghost incense smoker. These are the pieces that prove "elegant" doesn't have to mean "serious." A pale blue pumpkin is a nice break from the usual orange, and a friendly ghost on a textured pillow is closer to a children's-book illustration than a horror prop.

The list also includes antique brushstroke birdcages, which sit in a lovely in-between zone: vintage-feeling objects that look right on a mantel with candles, pumpkins or nothing at all.

## How to Shop It Without Overdoing It

Twenty items is a lot, and the list itself isn't a shopping order. Treating it as a menu is more sensible. Based on the pieces House Beautiful highlights, a restrained approach might look like this:

1. **Pick one statement.** The spider-web mirror or the snake-wrapped mirror can anchor a room on their own.
2. **Add warm light.** The smoked amber candle holder and hanging tapers do the atmospheric heavy lifting.
3. **Spend the fun on the table.** Cocktail picks, pinch bowls and snack bowls are small, cheap-feeling to commit to, and easy to put away.
4. **Keep the rest neutral.** Linen, brass and ceramic blend into regular decor once the holiday is over.

That last point is where the list is most persuasive. A good number of these items, such as the brass mirror, the planter, the napkins and the birdcages, wouldn't look odd on November 1.

## The Verdict

The real argument behind House Beautiful's roundup isn't that Halloween should be subdued. It's that spooky looks best when it's well made. A skeleton wine glass can be glamorous if the napkin under it is linen. The tacky stuff was never about the ghosts and skulls; it was about everything surrounding them being plastic. Swap the materials, keep the mischief, and your house can look haunted in the nicest possible way.
