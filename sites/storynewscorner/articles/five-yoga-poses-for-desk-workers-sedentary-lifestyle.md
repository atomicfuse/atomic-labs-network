Your chair has been getting the best of you. This week, Hindustan Times published a rundown of five yoga poses meant to ease stiffness, improve posture, and counter the effects of long desk hours and limited movement. The poses are simple asanas aimed at the back, shoulders, hips, spine, and general body awareness.

None of them requires a studio, a special mat ritual, or the ability to bend like a pretzel. That's the appeal. Here's the lineup, plus a take on how to string it into something you might do between the last meeting and dinner.

## Why This Keeps Coming Up

Per the report, sedentary work routines, screen-heavy lifestyles, and work-from-home habits have made stiffness, poor posture, and low energy common daily complaints. It's hard not to see the pattern: when your commute is ten steps from bed to laptop, movement has to be something you add on purpose.

That's the logic behind quick, accessible practices. The pitch isn't a transformation; it's a low-effort reset for busy days.

My angle: treat these five as a short sequence, a reset button you press after sitting, rather than five separate chores. The source lists the poses but doesn't prescribe an order or timing, so the sequencing below is my suggestion, not the outlet's.

## The Five Poses

### 1. Tadasana (Mountain Pose)

Start here. It looks like standing around, which is exactly why it's sneaky. Hindustan Times includes it among poses that build body awareness, and it's a sensible opener: before you stretch anything, notice how you've been holding yourself. Feet grounded, spine tall, shoulders away from your ears. Think of it as a check-in with a body that's been ignored since the 9 a.m. stand-up.

### 2. Marjariasana (Cat Pose)

The Sanskrit name is a mouthful, but anyone who has watched a cat stretch after a nap knows the idea. This pose works the spine, one of the areas the article says these asanas target. If you've spent hours hunched forward, this is the pose that lets you round the other way on purpose, with some intention behind it.

### 3. Bhujangasana (Cobra Pose)

Cobra is the counterweight to a day of slumping. It focuses on the back and, in the usual yoga logic, opens the chest that a keyboard has been closing. Go gently. The point here is relief from stiffness, not winning a flexibility contest with the person on the next mat.

### 4. Setu Bandhasana (Bridge Pose)

Bridge brings in the hips and back, two zones the article names as targets. Hips are the quiet casualties of sitting, and this pose is a reasonable way to wake them up. It's also the one that feels most like actual work, so consider it your reward for finishing the earlier, gentler poses.

### 5. Balasana (Child's Pose)

The closer. After four poses of effort, Child's Pose is permission to stop. It's a resting posture, and it makes a fitting end to the sequence for anyone whose energy is running low by late afternoon.

## A Desk-Worker Run Order

Here's my own suggested flow, since the report names the poses but not how to combine them:

- **Stand and settle** with Mountain Pose.
- **Warm the spine** with Cat Pose.
- **Open up** with Cobra.
- **Build** with Bridge.
- **Rest** in Child's Pose.

That's a few minutes of your day, and you can do it in the space next to your desk or on a living room floor. If something hurts, ease off. Gentle is the entire brief.

## The Bigger Takeaway

What's refreshing about this list is how unglamorous it is. No viral challenge, no pricey gear, no 30-day promise. Just five foundational poses that have been around far longer than the open-plan office, repurposed for a problem the open-plan office helped create.

There's a small irony in that. Technology gave us the screen-heavy workday, and one of the more sensible responses is a set of postures that need nothing but floor space and a few quiet breaths. It won't replace a lifestyle with more movement in it, and the report doesn't claim it will. But as a daily nudge toward standing up, stretching out, and noticing your own shoulders, it's hard to argue with.

So the next time your chair starts to feel like a permanent address, consider this your eviction notice. Five poses, one floor, and a spine that might thank you.
