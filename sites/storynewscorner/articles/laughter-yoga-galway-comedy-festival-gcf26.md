Galway Comedy Festival is adding something to its lineup that requires no stand-up set, no two-drink minimum and, crucially, no ability to touch your toes. As part of its Gailimh Ah-Boo celebrations, the festival is offering Laughter Yoga, which Thisisgalway describes as a practice that combines deep breathing with laughter exercises to get people giggling and feeling good together. The workshops tie into #GCF26 and the October Bank Holiday.

## Yoga, Minus the Yoga

The pitch, per Thisisgalway, is essentially "yoga without the poses." Instead of holding a warrior stance while quietly questioning your hamstrings, participants use laughter exercises, playfulness and breathing. The event is positioned as an accessible way for both families and adults to boost their mood, connect with others and try something new.

That last part is the quiet selling point. Plenty of wellness offerings come with an unspoken entry exam: the right leggings, the right vocabulary, the right level of flexibility. A session built around laughing and breathing seems to skip the audition entirely.

## Why a Comedy Festival Is the Obvious Home

It's hard not to see the logic here. A comedy festival already trades in the idea that laughing together feels good. Laughter Yoga simply takes the audience out of the seats and makes them the act. Instead of waiting for a comedian to land a punchline, you're invited to generate the laughing yourself, alongside a room of people doing the same.

That's our read, not the organisers' claim, but it fits the stated aim of getting people "giggling and feeling good together." The emphasis is on the *together*. Anyone who has caught a case of the giggles in a quiet room knows how contagious it is, and a format built to lean into that seems less like a gimmick than like a clever use of how people actually behave.

## The Low-Pressure Weekend Option

The timing helps. With the October Bank Holiday approaching, people are typically looking for things to do that don't require a big commitment. A workshop that blends wellness and comedy slots neatly between "go see a show" and "stay home." It also suits mixed groups: because the event is pitched at families and adults alike, it's the rare activity where a nervous teenager and a curious grandparent might plausibly be in the same circle.

There's also a broader moment at play. Interest in mental health, community connection and experiential events has been growing, and a laughter workshop sits right at that intersection. You don't need to buy into any big claims about wellness to see the appeal of an hour spent laughing with strangers.
