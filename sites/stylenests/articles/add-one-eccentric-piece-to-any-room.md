BuzzFeed Shopping recently rounded up 33 eccentric home products for people "who've never been accused of being boring," with the tagline "In this house, we never pick the beige option." The full list is behind their link, so this isn't a rundown of those 33 items. What it does point to is a useful idea: you don't need a full redesign to make a home feel like yours. One well-chosen, slightly weird piece can do the job.

Here's how to choose that piece, place it, and keep it from looking like a random impulse buy.

## Why one bold piece beats a full makeover

BuzzFeed frames quirky decor as a way to show personality at home, and the timing fits a broader pattern. Maximalist styling and what's often called "dopamine decor" (colorful, playful, personal) keep resonating as people move away from sterile minimalism.

The practical upside is cost and risk. Replacing a sofa because you're bored is expensive. Adding a funny lamp, a loud tray, or an oddly shaped vase is cheap, and if you tire of it, you've lost very little.

## Step 1: Start with a room that feels generic

Walk through your home and notice which room could belong to anyone. It's usually the living room, entryway, or a rental bedroom where everything is neutral and nothing says anything about you.

Pick one room only. Spreading eccentric items thinly across the whole house tends to read as clutter. Concentrating them gives a deliberate look.

## Step 2: Choose something useful first

The safest way into quirky decor is a functional item with a personality. Think of things you'd use anyway:

- A light with an unexpected shape or color
- A tray, bowl, or catch-all for keys and mail
- A planter or vase with a sculptural form
- Storage that doubles as a visual feature, such as a bold hook, stool, or basket

Because it earns its spot, you won't regret it when the novelty fades. Useful items disguised as conversation pieces are the sweet spot: guests ask about them, and you still use them daily.

## Step 3: Give it a stage

A weird object works best when it has room to be noticed. Some placements that work in almost any space:

### On a clear surface
Clear most of a console table, shelf, or nightstand, then let the odd piece sit with just one or two quiet companions. A plain book stack or a small plant is plenty.

### Against a calm backdrop
If your walls are beige or white, you already have the ideal setting. The neutral background is what makes the eccentric piece pop.

### At eye level
Pieces placed too high or too low get ignored. Aim for where people naturally look when they sit or enter.

## Step 4: Keep the budget honest

Shoppers want expressive pieces that don't cost a fortune, and you can get there several ways.

- **Set a ceiling before you browse.** Decide on a number, say the price of a nice dinner out, and stick to it.
- **Buy one, live with it a week.** If you still smile at it after seven days, it's a keeper.
- **Check secondhand shops and flea markets.** Odd, characterful objects are their specialty, and prices are usually low.
- **Repaint or restyle what you own.** A bold color on an old frame, stool, or small cabinet can be as striking as a new purchase.

## Step 5: Adjust for your space size

### Small apartments
Choose compact items and go vertical or functional. A statement lamp, a colorful stool that doubles as a side table, or one loud piece of art does more than a crowded shelf of knickknacks.

### Larger homes
You can afford a bolder scale. A big sculptural piece in an entryway or a vivid accent chair gives a large room a focal point it may be missing.

### Rentals
Stick with things that don't require permanent changes: lighting you can plug in, freestanding decor, textiles, and leaning art.

## Step 6: Know when to stop

The goal is a home that feels expressive, not chaotic. A quick test: if you can't tell which item is the star of the room, you've added too many. Remove one, then look again.

A good rule of thumb is one eccentric focal point per surface or wall. Give your new piece a few weeks. If it still makes you happy, consider adding a second item in a different spot, ideally echoing a color or shape from the first.

## Your next move

Tonight, pick the plainest room in your home and find the one spot where a conversation starter could live. Then shop for just that one thing, and make sure it's something you'd be happy to use every day.
