BuzzFeed recently rounded up 36 products meant to make your home feel warmer, softer, and cozier before winter arrives. The full list isn't something we can walk you through item by item, but the idea behind it is worth borrowing: you don't need a redesign to make staying in feel good. Here's a simple, budget-minded way to winterize your space, whatever its size.

The approach is to work in layers, starting with what you touch and sit in, then what you see, then what you feel in the air.

## Start with what you touch

Soft things do the most work for the least money. If you only change one thing, change this.

- **Throws:** Keep one within arm's reach of wherever you sit most. A blanket you can grab without getting up gets used.
- **Bedding:** Swap thin summer layers for heavier ones. A second blanket folded at the foot of the bed lets you adjust in the night.
- **Pillows:** Trade smooth, cool fabrics for textured ones like knit, fleece, or corduroy. Two or three on a sofa is enough.
- **Slippers or warm socks:** Park them by the door or bed. Cold floors make a room feel colder than it is.

These are the kind of upgrades that work in a studio apartment or a big house alike, and you can add them one at a time.

## Warm up the floors

Hard floors are often the coldest surface in a home. A rug gives your feet a soft landing and makes a room look finished.

- Put a rug under the spot where you spend the most time: beside the bed, in front of the sofa, or at the kitchen sink.
- In a small space, one larger rug usually looks calmer than several little ones.
- Renters can add a rug pad to keep things from sliding, with no changes to the floor itself.

## Change the light

Winter means darker afternoons, so lighting matters more than you'd expect. Harsh overhead light can make a room feel like an office.

- Use lamps at different heights instead of relying on the ceiling fixture.
- Choose warm-toned bulbs. Packaging usually labels them as "warm white" or lists a lower Kelvin number.
- Add a small lamp or string lights to a dark corner. It makes the room feel bigger and more inviting.
- Candles or flameless candles can finish an evening setup. If you use real ones, never leave them unattended.

## Seal out the drafts

Cozy is partly about what you see and partly about what you stop feeling. A draft near your favorite chair will undo every blanket you own.

- Walk around on a cool day and feel along window edges and the bottom of exterior doors.
- A door draft stopper (you can sew a simple one or fold a rolled towel along the base) blocks cold air at the bottom.
- Heavier curtains over windows add a layer between you and the glass. Close them at dusk.
- Weatherstripping and window insulation film are inexpensive options if you want something more permanent. Check the product's instructions to see whether it's safe for your type of window or rental.

## Build a night-in corner

You don't need a whole room. Pick one corner and make it the spot you want to be in when it's dark by 5 p.m.

- A comfortable chair or the end of your sofa
- A throw and a pillow
- A lamp with a warm bulb
- A small table for a drink or book
- A rug underfoot

If you've got a tiny home, a bed with a bedside lamp and extra pillows can serve as that corner.

## Add a little warmth you can see

Color and material affect how warm a room feels. You can nudge a space toward cozy without repainting.

- Bring in earthy or deep tones through pillows, a throw, or a framed print.
- Mix in natural textures: wood, wicker, wool, or ceramic.
- Swap lightweight summer decor for heavier pieces. A chunky knit basket for blankets is both decor and storage.
- Greenery and dried stems can keep a room from feeling bare once the outdoors turns gray.

## Check your heating setup

If you have a heating system, now is a good time to make sure it's ready. Replacing a dirty filter, if your system uses one, is a common maintenance step, and your manufacturer or landlord can tell you what's right for your setup. Make sure furniture isn't blocking vents or radiators so heat can circulate.

If you use a space heater, follow the manufacturer's safety instructions, keep it away from fabric and anything flammable, and don't leave it running unattended.

## How to spend, by budget

**Under a small budget:** Rearrange what you have. Move a blanket basket to the living room, pull a lamp into a dark corner, and hang curtains you already own.

**A bit more to spend:** Add a rug, a couple of textured pillows, and warm bulbs.

**Ready to invest:** Look at better window coverings, quality bedding, and draft-proofing.

## The takeaway

Pick one room tonight, the one you spend the most time in, and make three changes: add a throw, swap in a warm bulb, and block one draft. You'll feel the difference before winter really sets in.
