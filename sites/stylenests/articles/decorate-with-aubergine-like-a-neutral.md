Deep purple is having a moment, and Ed Sheeran's living room is the example people are pointing to. Per Homes & Gardens, aubergine is emerging as a "new neutral": a rich, cocooning shade that works in more rooms than you'd expect. Here's how to use it without turning your space into a dark cave.

The short version: treat aubergine as the anchor, then surround it with softer, warmer things.

## Why aubergine can act like a neutral

Homes & Gardens reports that rich, enveloping colors are starting to replace minimalist beige as interiors move toward warmth, depth, and personality for fall and the 2026 color trend cycle.

That's the logic behind calling it a neutral. Like navy or charcoal, aubergine is deep and moody, and it plays well with lots of other tones. It just has a little more personality.

The key, according to the piece, is balance. Aubergine works best when paired with cream walls, wood floors, natural textures, warm metallics, and patterned accents.

## Start with a light, warm backdrop

If you take one idea from Sheeran's living room, make it this: give the purple room to breathe.

- **Cream walls** keep the space bright and make aubergine pieces look intentional rather than heavy.
- **Wood floors or wood furniture** add warmth that softens the purple's cool edge.
- **Renting?** Skip paint. A cream or warm white rug and curtains do a lot of the same work.

A good rule of thumb (my take, not a hard law): let aubergine cover roughly a quarter of what you see in the room, and let the lighter shades handle the rest.

## Pick one big piece, then repeat the color small

You don't need to go all-in. Choose one anchor and echo it in a couple of spots.

### If you have the budget: the sofa

The Artless Wide Sofa & Chaise is one of the pieces featured in the Homes & Gardens roundup. A deep-toned sofa against cream walls is the most direct way to copy the look. Check the available colors before you commit, since I can't confirm which shades it comes in.

### If you're on a budget: pillows and throws

These are the lowest-risk way to test the trend:

- **Edgar Square 100% Cotton Pillow** for a natural-fiber accent
- **Cheer Collection Set of 2 Microfleece Throw Pillows** for a two-pack that's easy to swap in
- **Chenille Throw Blanket** to add a soft, textured layer over a sofa arm or chair

If the color gets old, you've only changed a few small things.

## Add texture so the color feels cozy, not flat

Texture is what keeps a deep shade from looking heavy. Chenille, cotton, and microfleece all catch light differently, so even pieces in the same color look layered.

Try mixing two or three textures on one seating area. For example, a smooth cotton pillow, a chunky chenille throw, and a plush microfleece pillow. Add something natural like a wood side table or woven basket, and the room feels collected.

## Bring in warm metallics and a little pattern

Homes & Gardens points to warm metallics and patterned accents as the finishing touches.

- **Metallics:** think brass or gold-toned picture frames, lamp bases, or candle holders. They pick up light against deep purple.
- **Pattern:** a rug, a patterned pillow, or a print on the wall breaks up solid color. Keep the pattern in cream, wood, or warm tones so it doesn't compete.

You don't need much. One metallic and one pattern is enough for a small room.

## Small ways to try it this fall

Not ready to commit? These are easy, low-cost entry points:

1. **Glass Flower Vase:** the Artibetter Glass Flower Vase is on the list. Fill it with seasonal stems and let the room's neutrals do the rest.
2. **Candle:** the Black Cherry Merlot Scented Candle brings in the mood through scent and a deep wine tone.
3. **Throw blanket:** drape the chenille throw over a chair to see how you like the color in daylight.
4. **Pillow swap:** replace two existing pillows with aubergine ones and live with them for a week.

Prices vary and change often, so compare before buying.

## Small space tips

In a small room, deep purple can feel closer and cozier, which is great for a reading corner or bedroom but less so for a dim apartment. Keep walls light, put the color on pieces you can move, and make sure it sits near a window or lamp so it glows instead of disappearing.

## The takeaway

This week, grab one aubergine pillow or throw, set it against your lightest wall or sofa, and look at it for a few days. If it feels calm and warm, add a second piece. That's how a bold color becomes a neutral.
