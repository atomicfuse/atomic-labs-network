Diwali's five days began Friday, November 6, and per Vogue, the celebration in New York stretched across weeks of parties, dinners, and gatherings beforehand, with L.A. getting in on it too. Vogue pointed to events tied to ŌUSHQ and the CFDA, Gold House, and Sabyasachi as part of the season's buzz.

You don't need a guest list like that to join in. Here's how to give your home a warm, festive Diwali feel on a modest budget, whether you have a studio apartment or a house with a dining room.

A quick note: the Vogue piece, as summarized here, focuses on fashion and social events rather than home decor. The ideas below are my own practical suggestions, not details from those parties.

## Start With Light

Diwali is widely known as a festival of lights, so light is the easiest place to begin. It's also cheap.

- **Group candles in odd numbers.** Three to five in varied heights on a tray looks intentional.
- **Use flameless tea lights** if you have pets, kids, or a rental with strict rules.
- **String lights** along a bookshelf, window frame, or headboard add glow without taking up floor space.

Turn off the overhead light in the evening. Low, warm light does more for a room than any new purchase.

## Pick a Simple Color Palette

Festive doesn't have to mean busy. Choose two or three colors and repeat them.

- Marigold yellow, deep pink, and gold feel celebratory.
- Jewel tones like emerald and burgundy feel richer and more formal.

Shop what you already own first: throw pillows, a scarf draped over a chair, or a colorful dish towel can all join the scheme. Then fill gaps with thrift-store brass, fabric remnants, or dollar-store accents.

## Make a Small-Space Focal Point

In a tight space, one strong moment beats scattered decor.

1. Pick a surface: a console, windowsill, or coffee table.
2. Lay down a runner or piece of fabric.
3. Add a tray of candles, a bowl of flowers, and one or two meaningful objects.

That's it. Clear the clutter around it so the display can breathe.

## Bring in Flowers Affordably

Fresh flowers, especially in warm tones, make a space feel celebratory. Grocery-store bouquets work fine. Split one bunch into several small jars or glasses and scatter them across tables, the entryway, and the bathroom sink. Several small arrangements read as lush and cost the same as one big one.

## Set Up for Gathering

The Vogue story is really about people coming together, so make your space easy to host in.

- **Clear a path.** Push furniture back so guests can move and mingle.
- **Create a serving spot.** A cart, a sideboard, or even a cleared bookshelf works for snacks and sweets.
- **Add floor seating.** Large cushions or a layered rug give small rooms extra seats and a relaxed feel.
- **Keep drinks and plates reachable** from more than one spot so no one clusters in the kitchen.

## Dress the Entry

First impressions matter, and this is a cheap fix. Place a small arrangement of candles or flowers by the door, hang a simple garland, or lay down a bright mat. Guests (and you) will feel the shift as soon as you walk in.

## Keep It Personal

The most meaningful touches are the ones that reflect your family's traditions. Display photos, heirloom pieces, or handmade items you already have. If you're new to celebrating or hosting for friends, ask someone who grew up with Diwali what the day means to them, and let that guide your choices rather than copying a look.

## The Takeaway

Pick one corner tonight, add light and one bunch of flowers, and turn off the overhead lamp. That alone will change how your home feels for the rest of the festival.
