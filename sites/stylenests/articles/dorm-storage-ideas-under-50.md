Dorm rooms give you a bed, a desk, a closet and not much else. Good Housekeeping rounded up 12 storage picks, each under $50, to help students make the most of that. This guide groups them by the problem they solve, so you can buy only what your room needs.

One caution first: the original roundup doesn't give prices or brand details for most items beyond the "under $50" cap (and an IKEA trolley). Check current prices before you buy, and measure your room first.

## Start with the biggest wasted space: under the bed

Most dorm beds can be raised or have clearance underneath. That's the most underused square footage in the room.

### Slim under-bed containers with wheels

Wheels mean you can pull out winter sweaters or spare shoes without dragging a box across the floor. Pick slim ones so they fit under a standard bed frame, and measure the clearance before you order.

### Heavy-duty moving bags

These are on the list for a reason: they hold bulky things like bedding, off-season coats and extra pillows. Once they're empty, they fold flat, which helps at move-out time.

## Go vertical when the floor runs out

When the floor is small, use the walls and the backs of doors.

### Over-the-door mirror

It's a mirror and a storage spot in one, and it takes no floor space. It also bounces light around a small room.

### Felt bulletin board

Pin up schedules, photos and reminders without drilling into walls. Keeping paper off your desk is a quiet way to keep the whole room tidier.

### Desk hutch

A hutch adds shelves above your work surface, so books, supplies and a lamp don't eat up desk space. It's one of the best ways to double what a small desk can hold.

## Make the closet work harder

Dorm closets are often tiny. Three picks help you fit more without cramming.

### Velvet hangers

They're slim, so you can fit more clothes on the rod. The velvet grip also keeps slippery tops and straps from sliding off.

### Hanging closet organizer

This adds shelves or pockets to the rod, turning vertical space into a spot for sweaters, shoes or accessories.

### Three-tiered drawer divider

Dividers keep small items from becoming one big pile in a drawer. Sort socks, chargers and everyday odds and ends so you can find them fast.

## Pick furniture that does two jobs

When every piece has to earn its spot, choose items with a second purpose.

### Three-tier rolling cart

Use it as a bedside table, a bathroom caddy or a snack station. Roll it where you need it and tuck it away when you don't.

### Tiered trolley from IKEA

The roundup lists an IKEA tiered trolley as another rolling option. It works well beside a desk or bed for supplies or toiletries.

### Ottoman with storage

It's extra seating for guests, a footrest, and a hidden spot for blankets or shoes. If your room has no couch, this pulls triple duty.

## Don't forget the small stuff

### Makeup organizer

Skincare and makeup sprawl fast on a shared or small surface. An organizer corrals them in one place, whether on a dresser or in a drawer.

## How to spend your $50 wisely

You don't need all 12. Here's a simple way to choose:

1. **Find your biggest pain point.** Is it clothes, desk clutter or too much stuff in general?
2. **Start with the unused space.** Under-bed storage and over-the-door items add room without taking any.
3. **Buy one multi-use piece.** A rolling cart or storage ottoman gives you the most flexibility.
4. **Wait on the rest.** After a couple of weeks, you'll know what's still bugging you.

## Check the rules before you buy

Schools differ on what you can attach to walls, doors and furniture. Before you buy a hutch, an over-the-door mirror or a bulletin board, check your housing guidelines and your room's measurements.

Pick the one thing that annoys you most on day one, solve that first, and let the room tell you what to add next.
