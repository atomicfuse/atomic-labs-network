Halloween decor has a reputation problem: plastic skeletons, neon cobwebs, and a pile of items that end up in the trash by November. House Beautiful recently rounded up 20 elegant Halloween pieces, mostly from Pottery Barn and other retailers, that aim for spooky without the tackiness. You don't have to buy any of it to learn from it. The list shows a clear pattern, and you can copy that pattern on any budget.

Here is how to apply it, in the order that will make the biggest difference.

## Start with materials, not motifs

Look at what the list leans on: antique brass, smoked amber glass, bouclé, linen, enameled cast iron, and ceramic. The spooky part is the shape or the small detail, while the material does the grown-up work.

Examples from the roundup:

- An antique brass spider-web mirror
- A smoked amber glass candle holder
- A Gus the Ghost throw pillow paired with a bouclé pumpkin
- Linen napkins with an oak vine design
- Pumpkin-shaped enameled cast iron cocottes

The practical rule: choose one spooky shape and make it in a nice material. A ghost in bouclé reads as cozy. A ghost in thin printed plastic reads as party store.

## Use one statement piece on a wall

Two of the 20 picks are mirrors: the spider-web one and a snake-wrapped mirror inspired by Nagini. A serpentine wall hook also made the list. Walls are an easy place to be bold because a single object does the whole job.

Try this:

1. Pick one wall, such as the entry or above a console.
2. Hang one striking piece, not five small ones.
3. Leave space around it so it looks intentional.

On a budget, a thrifted mirror with a brass-toned frame gets you most of the way there. If you're comfortable with a DIY project, a little wire or a plain decorative element can add the Halloween twist. Keep it simple and test the look before committing.

## Light it moody

Candles do more for atmosphere than any prop. The list includes a smoked amber glass candle holder and hanging taper candles in a Hogwarts-inspired style. A ghost incense smoker also made the cut for those who want scent as part of the mood.

A few ways to use that idea:

- Group candles in odd numbers (three or five) at different heights.
- Choose dark or amber-toned holders over bright orange accents.
- If you have kids or pets, or just want less fuss, flameless tapers get you the same silhouette.

Small apartment? A single cluster on a tray on the coffee table is enough. Hanging tapers need ceiling height and a safe setup, so skip them if your space doesn't allow it.

## Set a table that does the decorating

More than half the list is for serving and hosting, which makes sense: a Halloween table is decor you actually use. Picks include:

- Scorpion-topped cocktail picks
- Autumn leaf inlay coasters
- A lidded snack bowl
- Ghost-shaped pinch bowls
- A gold-decaled "Hex Mix" snack bowl
- A skull charcuterie board
- A skeleton serving collection with wine glasses

### A simple tablescape formula

You don't need a full collection. Try one base, one spooky serving piece, and one small detail:

- **Base:** plain linen napkins or a neutral runner. The oak vine napkins in the roundup show how a quiet pattern can set a seasonal tone.
- **Spooky piece:** one board, bowl, or set of pinch bowls with a Halloween shape.
- **Detail:** cocktail picks, coasters, or a candle.

The restraint is what makes it look styled. If every item is themed, the table starts to look like a costume.

## Bring in nature and neutrals

Not every pick is overtly Halloween. The roundup also includes a light blue ceramic pumpkin planter, antique brushstroke birdcages, a woodland critter doormat, and a sculptural golden snake. These are the pieces most likely to survive past October 31.

That's where the value is. Items that read as autumn, or just as interesting objects, can stay out through November. A pumpkin planter can hold greenery or flowers, and a birdcage can sit on a shelf all season with candles or dried stems inside.

When you shop, ask: would I still like this on November 2? If yes, it's a good buy.

## Make it work on a smaller budget

The roundup draws heavily from Pottery Barn, and prices vary, so check current listings before you plan around any piece. Here are ways to get the same look for less:

- **Thrift first:** look for brass candle holders, amber or smoked glassware, mirrors, birdcages, and ceramic planters.
- **Pick one splurge:** spend on the piece you'll use most, such as a serving board or a pillow, and fill in around it.
- **Go neutral on textiles:** plain linen napkins and a bouclé or textured pillow you already own can anchor the look.
- **Use what's in the yard:** oak leaves and branches fit the same vine-and-leaf mood as the napkins and coasters on the list.

## Small spaces: edit hard

If you're in an apartment or a compact home, you have less room to hide clutter, which works in your favor. Choose three zones: the entry (a doormat or wall piece), one surface (a candle cluster or a planter), and the table or counter (one serving piece). That's enough for a space to feel decorated.

## What to take from the list

The 20 pieces in House Beautiful's roundup vary a lot, but they share a few habits: quality materials, restrained color, one clear spooky detail per item, and a lot of things you can use for hosting. Copy those habits, not the exact products.

Before you buy anything this season, walk through your home and pick the single spot that would benefit most from one good Halloween piece. Start there, and stop when it looks finished.
