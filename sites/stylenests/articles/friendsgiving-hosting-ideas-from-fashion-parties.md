Vogue recently took a look inside the season's standout Friendsgiving parties, and the guest lists are the glamorous part: Net-a-Porter x Jessica McCormack, Thom Browne x Mr Porter, Gabriella Khalil x Olivia von Halle, Jordan Firstman, Pamela Anderson x Pandora, and Urban Jürgensen all hosted. Per Vogue, Friendsgiving has become fashion's favorite excuse for a long, unhurried soirée, with formats ranging from candlelit suppers to art gallery takeovers to apartment hangouts.

You don't need a gallery or a designer partner to borrow the spirit. Here's how to translate the idea into a gathering in your own place, whatever the square footage.

## Pick a Format, Not a Theme

Vogue's roundup covers a wide range: candlelit suppers, gallery takeovers, and plain old apartment hangouts. Notice that the apartment hangout made the list right alongside the brand-sponsored ones. That's the permission slip.

Before you plan anything, choose which of those three moods you want:

- **Candlelit supper:** seated, slower, a bit formal.
- **Takeover:** you reshape a space you already have (more on that below).
- **Hangout:** food on the counter, people on the couch, no assigned seats.

Picking one keeps your shopping list short and your stress low.

## Light the Room Like It's the Main Event

Candlelight is the cheapest upgrade in hosting. A few ideas that work in any size space:

- Buy unscented taper candles in a multipack and use mismatched holders you already own: jars, small glasses, even sturdy bottles.
- Cluster candles in odd numbers down the middle of the table instead of one big centerpiece.
- Turn off the overhead light. Add a lamp or two on low in the corners.
- Skip scented candles near the food. They compete with dinner.

Always keep flames away from napkins, branches, and anything dangling.

## Do a Mini Takeover of Your Own Space

The gallery takeover idea is really about changing how a room feels for one night. At home, that can mean:

- **Clear one surface.** A bare console or bookshelf shelf becomes a display for a few objects you love.
- **Move the furniture.** Push the sofa back, or use a coffee table as a low second dining spot if your table is small.
- **Borrow a table.** A folding table with a floor-length cloth or bedsheet reads as intentional once candles are on it.
- **Use what you have as art.** Lean a framed print or a favorite book on the mantel or counter.

Small apartment? Seat people on the floor around the coffee table with cushions. It's cozy, and it's the apartment-hangout look done on purpose.

## Set a Dress Code That Takes Pressure Off

Fashion parties have an obvious advantage here, but you can keep it simple. Give guests one easy line in the invite, such as "dress up a little, dress comfy, or both." A few options:

- **Dressy-cozy:** a nice knit or blazer with your favorite soft pants.
- **Festive tailoring:** a sharp jacket or a good shirt, pulled from your own closet.
- **Loungewear with a twist:** silky pajama sets with a statement earring or a good pair of slippers.

The goal is for nobody to spend money or feel underdressed. Mention that stretchy waistbands are encouraged. Everyone will stay longer.

## Plan the Menu Around Potluck Logic

The whole point of Friendsgiving is that the host doesn't do it alone. Make it easy:

1. **You cover the main** (a roast chicken or a pan of something baked is plenty) and one drink.
2. **Assign categories, not recipes.** Ask people to bring a side, a dessert, or a snack. You'll avoid six bowls of mashed potatoes.
3. **Label dishes** with torn cardstock or masking tape so allergies and dietary needs are obvious.
4. **Serve in what you have.** Mismatched plates look relaxed, not sloppy, when the lighting is warm.

For a signature drink, make one batch cocktail or a big pitcher of spiced cider with an optional splash. It beats taking individual orders all night.

## Make It Feel Unhurried

Vogue's framing is a long, unhurried soirée, and that's the detail most worth stealing. A few small moves help:

- Put out snacks when people arrive so dinner doesn't have to be punctual.
- Start a playlist an hour before guests show up and let it run on low.
- Leave dessert for later. A second wave of food keeps people lingering.
- Choose a simple ritual, like everyone sharing one thing they're grateful for, or one dish they're proud of.

## A Quick Budget Plan

If you want the look without much spend, put your money in just two places: candles and one good thing to eat or drink. Everything else (plates, glasses, seating, music) can be borrowed from your own home or from friends who are coming anyway.

The fashion crowd may have the guest lists, but the formula is available to anyone. Pick one mood, dim the lights, and invite people you actually want to sit next to for three hours.
