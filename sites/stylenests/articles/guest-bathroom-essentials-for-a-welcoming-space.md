Your guests may only spend a few minutes in your guest bathroom, but those minutes stick with them. Good Housekeeping recently rounded up seven essentials that interior designers recommend for making that room feel less like an afterthought and more like a hotel stay. Below you'll find each one, plus practical, budget-conscious ways to pull it off in a big bathroom or a tiny powder room.

The angle here is simple: you don't need to do all seven at once, and you don't need to spend a lot. Start with the ones that fit your space and budget.

## 1. Features for Comfort

The first item on the list is comfort features, and it sets the tone for everything else. Think about what a guest actually does in the room: stands, sits, dries off, gets ready. Then ask whether the room supports that.

A few low-cost ways to put this into practice:

- Put a soft, absorbent bath mat by the shower or tub so no one steps onto cold tile.
- Keep a small basket of toilet paper within easy reach, with a spare roll visible.
- Add a hook or two at a sensible height for robes and clothes.

These are my suggestions for covering the comfort category, not specifics from the designers. The principle is to walk through the room as a visitor would and fix whatever feels awkward.

## 2. A Good Night Light

A night light made the list, and it's one of the cheapest upgrades here. Anyone who has stumbled into an unfamiliar bathroom at 2 a.m. understands why. Flipping on a bright overhead light wakes you up fully; a soft glow gets you there and back.

Plug-in and battery-powered options are widely available at low prices. Place one low, near an outlet, where it lights the path without shining in anyone's eyes. If your guest bathroom is a long walk from the guest bedroom, a second one in the hallway is worth considering.

## 3. Lighted Magnifying Mirror

A lighted magnifying mirror is the next designer pick. It's a thoughtful touch for guests who want to shave, apply makeup, or handle contact lenses in a room where the lighting may not be ideal.

It's also small. A countertop mirror takes up very little space, which makes it a good fit for compact bathrooms where a wall-mounted fixture isn't an option. Store it on the counter or tuck it in a drawer and mention it when you show guests the room.

## 4. Calming Shower Spray

Calming shower spray is on the list as a spa-style detail. The idea is a scented spray designed to be used in the shower, so the steam carries the fragrance.

The article's key angles mention high-end names like Aesop and Diptyque as the kind of brands associated with this level of polish. You don't have to buy them. If the price gives you pause, look for a gentler, lower-cost scented spray and try it yourself first. A quiet, calming scent usually works better than a strong one, since guests have different sensitivities.

## 5. Hair Essentials

Hair essentials round out the practical side. Guests forget things, or travel light, or simply didn't expect to need them. Having a few basics on hand removes a small but real stress.

A tidy way to do it:

1. Choose a small tray or basket that fits under the sink or on a shelf.
2. Stock it with items a guest might reasonably need for their hair.
3. Refresh it after each visit so it's always ready.

Travel-size products keep this affordable, and a basket keeps it from looking cluttered.

## 6. Luxurious Towels

Towels are probably the most visible upgrade on the list. Designers recommend luxurious ones because guests touch them, notice them, and remember them.

You can do this without replacing everything:

- Buy one matching set just for guests, and keep it separate from your everyday towels.
- Stick to one or two calm colors so the room looks cohesive.
- Fold them neatly or roll them in a basket where they're easy to find.

Because guest towels get less wear, a good set can last a long time, which helps the cost per use.

## 7. A Beautiful Hand Soap

Last on the list is hand soap. It sits right at eye level next to the sink, and every guest uses it. A nice bottle or dispenser makes the whole counter look considered.

This is probably the easiest swap on the list. Pour a pleasant soap into an attractive refillable dispenser and you get the look without a big purchase. Pick a scent that complements the shower spray, or go unscented if you're layering fragrance.

## Where to Start if Your Budget Is Tight

The source doesn't rank these by price, so here's my own take on the order I'd tackle them for the best return:

1. **Hand soap and a dispenser.** Smallest cost, most visible.
2. **A night light.** Inexpensive and genuinely helpful.
3. **A bath mat and towel set.** A bigger spend, but the most noticeable change.
4. **Hair essentials in travel sizes.** Cheap to assemble.
5. **A lighted magnifying mirror and calming shower spray.** Add these when you're ready to go the extra step.

If you have a small powder room with no shower, items like the shower spray won't apply. Focus on soap, towels, lighting, and comfort, and skip the rest.

## Why Guests Notice These Details

Good Housekeeping frames the trend this way: as home entertaining picks up, guests expect spaces that feel as thoughtful as a hotel. Designers, per the piece, are focusing on small details that signal hospitality. That's good news for your wallet, because small details cost less than renovations.

Pick one item from this list this week, add it, and then stay in your own guest bathroom for five minutes the way a visitor would. What bothers you there is your next upgrade.
