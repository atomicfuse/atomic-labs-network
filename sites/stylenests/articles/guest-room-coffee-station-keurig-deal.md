Holiday guests are coming, and one small upgrade can make their room feel like a boutique hotel: coffee within arm's reach. Per Homes & Gardens, the compact Keurig K-Supreme Single Serve Coffee Maker is $45 off in Black Friday sales right now. Below, you'll find how to turn a machine like that into a guest-room coffee station that looks intentional and doesn't eat your space.

A quick note on timing: sales like this tend to be short, so check the current price before you buy.

## Why in-room coffee feels like a hotel perk

Homes & Gardens frames the Keurig as a small but thoughtful guest-room upgrade, adding comfort, convenience and a touch of everyday luxury for holiday visitors. That tracks with how hotels work: the little things guests notice are the ones that save them a trip. Nobody wants to tiptoe through a dark kitchen at 6 a.m. in socks, hunting for a mug.

A single-serve machine suits this well because there's no pot to wash and no measuring. Your guest presses a button and gets one cup.

## Pick the right spot

You don't need a whole kitchen counter. Any of these will work:

- **A dresser top.** Clear one end and you have a ready-made station.
- **A narrow console or small table.** Great for tight rooms.
- **A bedside table with enough surface.** Only if the machine fits comfortably next to a lamp.
- **A rolling cart.** Easy to tuck away when the room isn't hosting.

Two practical checks before you commit. First, make sure there's an outlet within reach of the cord so nothing stretches across a walkway. Second, the machine needs water, so choose a surface that can handle the odd drip, or add a tray.

## Style it like a boutique hotel

Hotels make a coffee corner look calm by keeping it tidy and limited. Copy that.

### Use a tray to define the zone

A wood, marble-look or woven tray corrals everything and protects the furniture underneath. A thrifted one works just as well as a new one.

### Keep the supplies minimal

Stock a small basket or jar with a handful of pods, plus sugar, stirrers and napkins. A few options beat a giant pile. Ask guests ahead of time whether they prefer regular or decaf, so you aren't guessing.

### Add real mugs

Two matching mugs say "we planned for you" far better than mismatched ones from the back of the cupboard. If you want a budget version, a set of simple white or neutral mugs does the job.

### Include a water option

A small pitcher or a couple of bottles of water on the tray covers the refill question, so your guest doesn't need to hunt for the bathroom tap.

## Add a few small extras

You can stop at coffee, but these take almost no effort:

- A handwritten card with the Wi-Fi password
- A small dish for used pods
- A hand towel or cloth for spills
- A little note on how to use the machine, if it's not obvious

These cost very little and make the corner feel complete.

## Budget ideas for any home size

**Small apartment or studio:** If you have a pullout couch rather than a guest room, set the machine on a console or bookshelf and treat it as a shared coffee nook your guest can use.

**Mid-size home:** Use the dresser method and keep the setup on a tray so it can be cleared away between visits.

**Bigger home or frequent hosts:** Give the guest room its own permanent station with a cart, a mug rack and a pod drawer.

If the Keurig isn't in your budget even at a discount, you can still copy the idea with a kettle, instant coffee and tea bags on the same kind of tray. The styling does most of the work.

## It isn't only for guests

Homes & Gardens also points to the compact format as useful beyond the guest room. The same setup can work in a home office or a small kitchen, so the machine won't sit idle once the holidays pass. When the room is empty, you can move it or keep it as a bedroom perk for yourself.

## Before you buy

Sales prices change quickly, so confirm the $45 discount at checkout rather than relying on a headline. Measure your chosen spot against the machine's dimensions, and check the water reservoir size so you know how often it'll need refilling.

Your next step: pick the surface, set down a tray and mugs, and you've got a hotel-style corner before the first guest arrives.
