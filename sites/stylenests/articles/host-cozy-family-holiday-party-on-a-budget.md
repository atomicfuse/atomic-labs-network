Macy's reimagined Santaland at Herald Square got a preview this week, co-hosted by Ciara and Russell Wilson, per Vogue. The evening featured festive cocktails, holiday traditions, and family fun in New York City, with celebrity guests and holiday activations. Santaland opens to the public on November 27.

You don't need a Herald Square budget to borrow the idea. Here's how to host a family holiday party that feels special, stays cozy, and doesn't drain your wallet.

A quick note on what we know: Vogue's coverage tells us the event centered on festive drinks, tradition, and family-friendly fun. The ideas below are our own practical take on that spirit, not a reflection of how the Wilsons host at home.

## Start With One Tradition

The preview leaned on tradition, and that's the easiest thing to copy. Pick one ritual and make it the anchor of your gathering.

- A cookie-decorating table for kids and adults
- An ornament swap where everyone brings one wrapped ornament
- A holiday movie after dinner, with blankets required
- A group photo in the same spot every year

One repeatable ritual beats five scattered activities. It also costs almost nothing, and it's the thing people remember next year.

## Set Up a Festive Drink Station

Cocktails were part of the Santaland evening, and a self-serve drink station is a smart way to echo that at home without playing bartender all night.

- Use a tray, a cart, or the end of a console table.
- Offer one batch cocktail or punch and one non-alcoholic option, such as warm cider or hot cocoa.
- Add simple garnishes: orange slices, cinnamon sticks, cranberries.
- Keep cups, napkins, and a small ice bucket in one spot so guests help themselves.

Batching a single drink in a pitcher or slow cooker keeps costs down and keeps you out of the kitchen.

## Make Your Space Feel Warm, Not Staged

You don't need to redecorate. Focus on light and texture, which do most of the work.

### Lighting
Dim the overheads. Use lamps, string lights, and a few candles (or battery-operated ones if kids and pets will be roaming). Warm-white bulbs read cozier than cool ones.

### Texture
Toss throw blankets over sofa arms and chairs. Swap in a few pillow covers in deep red, forest green, or plaid. Covers are cheaper than new pillows and store flat.

### Greenery
A few branches in a vase or a simple garland over a doorway looks full without a big spend. Cheap grocery-store evergreens work fine.

## Small Space? Plan the Flow

In a smaller home, the party lives or dies on where people stand.

1. Move the drink station away from the kitchen so traffic spreads out.
2. Serve food buffet-style so you don't need a full seated table.
3. Clear a corner for kids with a blanket, a few games, and a basket of books.
4. Use ottomans, stools, and floor cushions as extra seating you can tuck away.

## Pick Your Gifting Style Before You Shop

Holiday gifting tends to split into two camps: thoughtful and extravagant. Deciding which one you are keeps you from overspending out of panic.

If you lean thoughtful, set a per-person cap and spend your effort instead. A framed family photo, a handwritten note, a homemade treat, or a small item tied to an inside joke often lands better than something expensive.

If you like going bigger, choose one or two people to splurge on and keep the rest simple. A group gift for a household can also stretch your budget.

## Keep the Kids Involved

Santaland is built for families, so build your own party with kids in mind rather than around them.

- Give them a job: placing name cards, hanging ornaments, or passing out napkins.
- Set up a low-effort craft, like paper snowflakes or decorating gift tags.
- Plan a quiet wind-down activity so the evening ends calmly.

## Dress for the Room You're Hosting

Comfort matters when you're on your feet and cooking. Choose something that feels a little dressed up but lets you move: a soft knit, a velvet top, a collared shirt with a sweater. Pick it ahead of time so party day isn't spent in front of a closet.

## Your Next Step

Before Santaland opens on November 27, pick your one tradition and write it on your calendar. Everything else, from drinks to decor, can stay simple as long as that one moment is planned.
