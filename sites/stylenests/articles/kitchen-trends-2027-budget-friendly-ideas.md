Veranda recently rounded up 12 kitchen trends designers expect to be everywhere in 2027. The big picture: calmer, warmer, more integrated kitchens that hide clutter, lean on natural materials, and feel more like living spaces than workrooms.

Most of those predictions come from luxury design, and not everyone is gutting a kitchen next year. So here's the more useful question: which of these ideas can you borrow in a small, affordable way? Below, the trends are grouped by what you can realistically do, with my own suggestions clearly marked as suggestions.

## Why the shift is happening

Per Veranda, open-concept living continues, so kitchens are more visible than ever. Homeowners want rooms that look beautiful, feel calm, and are easy to maintain. The trends also point away from sterile minimalism and toward warmth, craftsmanship, and practical luxury.

That gives you a simple filter for any kitchen decision: does it make the room calmer, warmer, or less cluttered?

## Start here: hide the mess

Three of the 12 trends are about concealment and flow.

### Invisible kitchens

The idea is a kitchen that blends into the rest of the home instead of announcing itself. A low-cost way to move in that direction (my suggestion, not Veranda's): clear the counters down to a few daily items, and store small appliances in cabinets or a single closed spot.

### The rise of the scullery

Veranda lists the scullery, a secondary workspace for messy tasks, as a coming trend. You probably don't have room for one. In a smaller home, you can mimic the logic by choosing one zone, such as a cart, a pantry corner, or a section of cabinets, where the coffee maker, toaster, and dish clutter live out of sight.

### Kitchens that feel like living rooms

This trend is about making the kitchen feel finished and comfortable. Easy ways to nudge it there: add a rug if the layout allows, swap harsh overhead bulbs for warmer ones, and bring in a lamp or a piece of art. Those are inexpensive and reversible.

## Add warmth with materials

Four trends center on texture and natural character.

### Richer wood tones

Designers are leaning toward deeper wood. If new cabinets aren't in the budget, try wooden cutting boards on display, a wood stool, or open shelves in a warm finish. Small doses carry the feeling.

### Natural materials and craftsmanship

Think handmade and tactile over glossy and mass-produced. Ceramic bowls, linen towels, and woven baskets are budget-friendly stand-ins.

### Statement stone

A bold slab is a luxury move, and a pricey one. If that's out of reach, a smaller stone piece, like a marble board or a stone tray by the stove, gives a taste of the look.

### Tonal kitchens

A tonal kitchen keeps its colors within one family for a calm, cohesive feel. This one is friendly to a paint budget: pick a single hue and use close shades on walls, cabinets, and accessories. Repainting cabinets is a classic DIY project, though prep and good primer matter.

## Details that add personality

These are the trends that tend to cost more, but they're also the ones where a single choice can change the room.

- **Creative millwork:** Designers are playing with trim and cabinet detailing. Adding simple trim or molding to a plain cabinet or island is a project many DIYers tackle.
- **Decorative range hoods:** The hood becomes a feature rather than an afterthought. If replacing it isn't realistic, painting or wrapping an existing hood surround in a color that matches your walls can make it recede or stand out on purpose.
- **Artistic tile backsplashes:** Tile is moving toward the expressive. A small backsplash area is a good place to splurge, since you need fewer tiles.

## Make it work for real life

### Kitchen islands as gathering tables

Veranda points to islands that function like tables, built for lingering rather than just prep. If you have an island, add comfortable stools and clear one end for sitting. If you don't, a small table at the edge of the kitchen can serve the same role.

### Smart kitchens that don't look high-tech

The tech is staying, but it's blending in. When you add smart devices, favor ones that look good or can be tucked away, so the room keeps its calm look.

## How to choose what to do first

You don't need all 12. A sensible order, based on cost and impact (my take):

1. Clear the counters and designate one hidden zone for appliances.
2. Warm up the lighting and add one natural material.
3. Pick a single color family and let it repeat.
4. Save the bigger spends, like stone, tile, or a new hood, for the one spot you see most.

## The takeaway

Before you buy anything, stand in your kitchen doorway and ask what feels loud or cluttered. Fix that first. Calm is the common thread in these 2027 predictions, and it's the cheapest trend to start on today.
