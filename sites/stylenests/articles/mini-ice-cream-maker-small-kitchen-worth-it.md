Buzzfeed recently rounded up 35 products for people trying to make the most of a small space. One item in that list caught our eye: a compact, mug-style ice cream maker set designed for easy storage, where you eat straight from the container. That's the whole pitch, and it raises a question every tiny-kitchen owner eventually faces: is a fun gadget worth the room it takes?

We haven't tested this one, and the details we have are limited to what Buzzfeed's excerpt describes. So instead of pretending to review it, here's a practical way to judge it, and any other compact gadget, before you buy.

## What the mini ice cream maker is promising

Per Buzzfeed, the product is a compact, mug-style ice cream maker set. Two things stand out as small-space selling points:

- **It's built for easy storage.** A small footprint is the whole reason it made a small-space list.
- **You eat from the container.** That means no scooping into a bowl, and one less dish to wash.

That second point is the sneaky clever part. In a tiny kitchen, dishes are clutter too. A tool that doubles as its own serving vessel cuts down on sink time.

What we can't tell you: how well it works, how long it takes, or what it costs. Check current reviews and the listing before you commit.

## The three-question test for any compact gadget

Fun purchases are fine. They just need to pass a quick filter when you've got maybe four feet of counter.

### 1. Where will it live, specifically?

Pick the exact shelf, drawer, or cabinet corner before you click buy. "I'll figure it out" is how gadgets end up on the counter permanently. If you can't name the spot, skip it.

### 2. Will it replace something or add something?

The best small-space buys swap out existing stuff. A mug-style maker that doubles as a bowl is a good sign. If it needs extra bowls, spoons, or special tools on top, it's adding clutter.

### 3. Will you realistically use it more than a few times?

Be honest. If you make ice cream monthly, great. If this is a one-weekend novelty, borrow one or skip it. A rough rule: if you wouldn't use it at least once a month, it should be small enough to forget about.

## Freezer space is the hidden cost

Counter and cabinet space get all the attention, but small-kitchen freezers are tight. Any ice cream maker that needs chilling or storing in the freezer is competing with your ice trays and frozen vegetables. Before buying, check the product listing for whether anything has to be frozen, and measure the free space in your freezer.

## Small-space kitchen swaps that cut dishes and clutter

If the ice cream maker isn't for you, the same logic works elsewhere. Look for items that:

- **Serve and store in one piece,** like containers you can eat from directly.
- **Nest or stack,** so they take up a fraction of the room when not in use.
- **Do one fun thing very well** instead of five things badly.

This is especially handy in dorms, studios, and tiny apartments, where one thoughtful treat-yourself item feels better than a drawer of random gadgets.

## When a fun gadget is worth it

Small spaces don't have to be all-practical. A little novelty makes a tiny home feel lived in, and a dessert gadget that stores neatly is a low-stakes way to add some joy. The key is that it earns its spot rather than assuming one.

## Your next move

Before buying anything from a small-space list, measure the spot it will live in and write the number down. If the product fits with room to spare, and replaces a dish or two rather than adding them, it's earned a place.
