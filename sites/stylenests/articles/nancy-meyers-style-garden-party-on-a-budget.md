Vogue's Eileen Kelly recently visited a garden party in the back garden of Nancy Meyers's Los Angeles home, held to celebrate her daughter Annie Meyers-Shyer's renovation book, *This Oak House*. Per Vogue, the Meyers family's approach to entertaining is polished, relaxed, detail-obsessed, and designed to look effortless.

You don't need a Los Angeles garden to use that approach. Below is a version you can pull off in a small yard, on a patio, or on a balcony, without spending much. The specifics are my own suggestions, built around the themes Vogue highlighted rather than a record of what the Meyers family did.

## Start With the Idea: Effortless Takes Prep

The key phrase in the Vogue piece is "designed to look effortless." That means the ease is planned. Guests relax when the host is relaxed, and the host relaxes when the work is finished before the doorbell rings.

Treat the party like a short project with a deadline:

- **Three days out:** Decide the menu, check your seating count, and buy anything non-perishable.
- **Day before:** Wash and set out serving dishes, pick up flowers, set the table, and clear the entryway.
- **Morning of:** Prep food that can sit, chill drinks, and put on the playlist.
- **One hour before:** Change clothes, light candles if it's evening, and stop fiddling.

If you do only one thing from this article, set the table the night before. It's the task most likely to eat your last hour.

## Keep the Flowers Loose and Cheap

A relaxed look doesn't call for a florist. Grocery store bunches work well if you skip the tight, formal arrangement.

- Split one or two bunches across several small vessels: jars, pitchers, drinking glasses, even a clean olive oil tin.
- Cut stems at different heights so nothing looks stiff.
- Add clippings from your own yard, such as rosemary, mint, or greenery, to fill gaps for free.
- Keep arrangements low enough that guests can see each other across the table.

One bunch of flowers in six small containers looks more generous than one big bouquet, and it costs the same.

## Serve Easy Food That Still Feels Considered

The sweet spot is food that needs almost no attention once guests arrive. Think platters and bowls rather than plated courses.

- Choose one make-ahead main, like a cold pasta salad, a roasted chicken, or a big tray of roasted vegetables.
- Add two sides and a simple green salad.
- Put out one dessert you can slice or scoop, such as a fruit tart or a bowl of berries with cookies.
- Offer one pitcher drink (lemonade, iced tea, or a spritz) plus water, so you aren't playing bartender.

Serving on mismatched plates is fine. What matters is that the food is arranged with care: a handful of herbs scattered on top, a lemon wedge by the fish, a cloth under the bread.

## Set the Table With Texture, Not Matching Sets

Detail-obsessed doesn't mean expensive. Small touches carry the look:

- **A linen or cotton tablecloth or runner.** Wrinkles are fine; a thrifted one is even better.
- **Cloth napkins.** Buy a set of inexpensive cotton ones or cut and hem fabric.
- **Real glassware** if you can borrow or thrift it. It reads as more thoughtful than disposable cups.
- **A place to rest.** Add cushions or throws on outdoor chairs so people stay a while.

No table? Use a picnic blanket with floor cushions, or a console pushed against a wall as a buffet.

## Make the Space Work for Your Size

### Small balcony or patio

Keep the guest list to what the space holds comfortably. Use a folding table as a buffet, stack stools that double as side tables, and hang string lights for atmosphere.

### Average backyard

Create two zones: one for eating and one for lounging. Even a couple of chairs under a tree gives guests somewhere to drift.

### No outdoor space at all

Bring the garden party inside. Open the windows, line a table with greenery, and use the same food and flower tricks. The mood travels.

## Let the House Look Lived In

Vogue describes the party as relaxed and polished at once, and that balance is the one to copy. Tidy, but don't stage. A few things help:

- Clear surfaces in the entry and bathroom, then stop.
- Leave a stack of books or a favorite throw where people can see it.
- Put out a basket by the door for shoes or bags if the party flows indoors.
- Do a quick walk-through as a guest would, from the front door to the drinks.

Nobody expects a showroom. A home that looks used and loved is more inviting than one that looks untouchable.

## Add Music and Light Last

A good playlist fills lulls in conversation and makes a small gathering feel like an event. Start it before guests arrive, keep the volume low enough to talk over, and make it long enough that you won't need to touch it again.

If the party runs into evening, candles in jars and string lights are inexpensive ways to get warm light without rewiring anything.

## The Takeaway

Pick one task you'd normally leave to the last minute, whether that's setting the table, chilling the drinks, or picking flowers, and finish it the day before. That single head start is what makes a party feel effortless.
