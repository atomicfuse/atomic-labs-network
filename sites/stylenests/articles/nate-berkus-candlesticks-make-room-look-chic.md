You don't need new furniture, fresh paint, or a full room refresh to make your home look more expensive. According to Thecoolist, designer Nate Berkus's pick for the one thing worth investing in is a pair of vintage-style candlesticks.

That's the whole idea, and it's a useful one. Here's what sits behind it and how to put it to work in your own space, whatever your budget or square footage.

## Why candlesticks, according to Berkus

Thecoolist reports that Berkus's approach to a chic home rests on a few habits: edit what you already own, don't feel pressured by trends, and invest in timeless pieces that elevate a room. Candlesticks fit all three. They're small, they don't date quickly, and they make everyday entertaining feel more polished.

That last point is the practical hook. A pair of candlesticks changes how a table looks without changing anything else about it.

## Start by editing, not shopping

Before you buy anything, look at what's already on your surfaces. Berkus's philosophy, as Thecoolist describes it, begins with editing. A few ways to do that this weekend:

- Clear one surface completely, such as a dining table, console, or shelf.
- Put back only the things you actually love or use.
- Notice the gap. If the surface feels flat or unfinished, that's where a pair of candlesticks can go.

Editing first means the new piece has room to stand out instead of joining the clutter.

## Use a pair for everyday entertaining

You don't need a special occasion. Set the candlesticks on the table for a weeknight dinner or a casual get-together and the meal feels more considered.

Here's a simple way to do it:

1. Place the pair on either side of the table's center, or at each end of a console.
2. Keep everything else simple. Plain dishes let the candlesticks do the talking.
3. Light the candles when you sit down.

If you own white dishes, this is a good moment to lean on them. One vintage-style piece beside plain tableware can make the whole setting look intentional.

## Go vintage-style to keep the cost down

The pick is vintage-style, which gives you flexibility. You can look at secondhand shops, flea markets, or online resale listings for older pieces, or choose newer ones with a classic shape. I can't give you a guaranteed price, but candlesticks are generally a small purchase compared with a sofa or a rug, which is what makes them a smart place to start.

A few things to check when you shop:

- **Pairs matter.** Two matching or closely related pieces read as deliberate.
- **Heft.** If you can pick them up in person, a solid, stable base is worth more than a fussy shape.
- **Fit.** Make sure they hold the candle size you plan to use.

## Think timeless, not trendy

Thecoolist frames Berkus's advice as a counterpoint to social media's constant push toward maximalism and endless home refreshes. The takeaway is restraint: choose things that reflect your personal style and will last, rather than buying on impulse.

A useful test before any purchase: would you still want this in several years? Candlesticks tend to pass because their shape isn't tied to a single moment. That's our read, not a rule from Berkus, but it's a handy filter for any decor buy.

## Where else to apply the same thinking

Candlesticks are the headline, but the logic carries over to other small upgrades that make a room feel finished:

- **Layered lighting.** Candlelight adds a warm layer to lamps and overheads.
- **Tabletop styling.** Group a few items at different heights instead of spreading many small ones around.
- **Rugs.** If you're ready for a bigger investment, choose one with lasting style over a trendy pattern.

These are our suggestions for extending the idea, not claims Berkus makes in the piece.

## Small spaces and tight budgets

This upgrade works in a studio as easily as a big dining room. A pair of candlesticks can sit on a small café table, a bookshelf, a windowsill, or the end of a narrow entry console. If your budget is limited, buy one pair, use it everywhere, and move it from room to room.

## Your next step

Clear one surface today, set a pair of candlesticks on it (borrow a pair if you have to), and light them at dinner. If the room feels more put-together, you've found your first intentional investment.
