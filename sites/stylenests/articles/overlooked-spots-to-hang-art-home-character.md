Your living room wall already has a framed print on it. Fine. But the spots people forget are often where art does the most work: they surprise you, make everyday corners feel designed, and cost next to nothing to try.

Good Housekeeping recently rounded up unconventional places to display art, and the idea is simple: think of it as hiding little treasures around your home. Here are the seven spots it highlights, plus practical ways to make each one work in your space.

## 1. On a Kitchen Shelf

Kitchen shelves usually hold dishes and jars. Leaning a small framed piece among them makes the whole shelf look styled instead of stocked.

**Try it:**
- Lean the art instead of hanging it, so there are no holes and it's easy to swap.
- Pick a piece small enough to sit next to your everyday mugs or bowls.
- A thrifted frame is a cheap way to get something that feels collected.

## 2. Conceal Your TV

A big black rectangle can dominate a room. Art is one of the ideas on the list for dealing with it, so the screen stops being the focal point when it's off.

The details depend on your setup, so check how any cover or frame would attach and whether it blocks ventilation or access before you commit. Keep it simple and renter-friendly if you can.

## 3. Above a Door Frame

The space over a doorway is wasted wall in almost every home. A piece hung there draws the eye up and makes a plain entry feel finished.

**Try it:**
- Choose something lightweight, since it sits high and you'll want secure hanging.
- Go wider than tall, so it follows the shape of the frame.
- Use it in hallways or between rooms where you have no other wall space.

## 4. Tucked Into the Bathroom

Bathrooms get skipped because they're small and functional. That's exactly why art works there: a tiny piece in an unexpected room feels like a little reward.

Because bathrooms get steamy, choose a piece you won't mind if it ages a bit, such as an inexpensive print or thrifted find rather than something precious.

## 5. Beneath a Staircase

The awkward wall or nook under a staircase often sits empty. Art here turns a leftover space into a deliberate one.

**Try it:**
- Group a few small pieces to fill the odd angles.
- Treat it like a mini gallery, since the space is already framed by the stairs.
- Check that nothing sticks out where people walk or duck.

## 6. In a Cupboard

This is the most "easter egg" idea of the bunch. Art inside a cupboard is something you and the people who open it get to enjoy, not something that shows up from across the room.

Stick with lightweight pieces and removable hanging options if you rent. It's a nice use for a small print you love but have no wall for.

## 7. On the Face of a Bookshelf

Instead of keeping art on the wall beside your shelves, put it on the shelves themselves. Placing it on the face of a bookshelf layers it with your books and objects, which makes the whole unit feel more personal.

This pairs well with thrifted art and collected objects, since the mix of textures is the point.

## Why This Works Without a Renovation

Small design choices are a low-cost way to make a home feel curated. Unexpected details fit neatly with thrifted art, collected objects, and a lived-in style, so none of this requires a big budget or a perfect matching set.

It also works across home sizes. In a small apartment, one tiny piece in a cupboard or bathroom adds personality without taking up floor or wall space. In a larger home, the same spots help connect rooms that feel plain.

## A Simple Way to Start

1. Walk through your home and pick the one spot from the list that's currently empty.
2. Choose a small piece you already own or grab one secondhand.
3. Try leaning or hanging it with a removable method first.
4. Live with it for a week before adding more.

Pick one overlooked corner today and put something you love in it. That's the whole trick.
