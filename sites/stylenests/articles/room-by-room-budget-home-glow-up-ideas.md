BuzzFeed Shopping recently published a roundup of 23 Walmart products aimed at giving your home a glow-up. The full product list isn't something we can reproduce here, so we won't guess at specific items or prices. What we can do is take the idea behind it and make it useful: a room-by-room plan for making your place look cleaner, cozier, and more expensive without a renovation.

Use this as a shopping filter, whether you browse Walmart or anywhere else. By the end, you'll know where to spend your first twenty dollars and what to skip.

## Start With the Room You See Most

Pick one room. Not the whole house. The space you walk through or sit in every day will give you the biggest return on effort, and a single finished room motivates you more than five half-done ones.

Before you buy anything, stand in the doorway and take a photo. Photos show you what your eyes have learned to ignore: the cord tangle, the bare wall, the lamp that's too small.

## Living Room: Layers Beat Big Purchases

A living room usually feels flat for one reason: everything is at the same level and in the same texture. Fix that with small swaps.

- **Throw pillow covers:** Replace covers, not whole pillows. It's cheaper, and you can store off-season sets flat.
- **A throw blanket:** Drape it over one arm of the sofa, not folded perfectly on the back.
- **Warm lighting:** Swap harsh bulbs for warm-toned ones and add a lamp if the room relies on one overhead light.
- **A rug that fits:** If your rug is smaller than your seating area, at least the front legs of the sofa should rest on it.

## Bedroom: Make It Feel Like a Hotel

The bedroom is where textiles do the heavy lifting.

### Bedding first
One coordinated set in a calm color changes the whole room. Stick to two or three tones so it looks intentional.

### Then the nightstand
Clear it down to a lamp, one book, and one small tray or dish. Everything else goes in a drawer.

### Curtains last
Hang them higher and wider than the window frame. It makes the ceiling look taller and the window look bigger, and it costs nothing extra.

## Kitchen and Dining: Small Upgrades, Daily Payoff

You don't need new cabinets to make a kitchen feel fresher.

- Decant dish soap and hand soap into matching pump bottles.
- Put a cutting board or tray under oils and everyday items so the counter looks styled, not cluttered.
- Swap worn dish towels for a fresh set.
- Add a small plant or herb pot near the window.

The point is repetition. Matching materials in three or four places read as a design choice.

## Bathroom: Cheap Fixes That Read as Clean

Bathrooms look dated mostly because of wear. Replace what's worn.

- New towels in one color family
- A fresh shower curtain or liner
- A tidy tray for daily products
- A bath mat that isn't stained or flattened

## Entryway: Your First Impression

Even a tiny entry benefits from a plan. Add hooks, a small tray for keys, and a mat. If you rent, adhesive hooks and a freestanding shoe rack keep the deposit safe.

## Renter or Homeowner: Adjust the Plan

Renters should favor things that leave no trace: plug-in lamps, curtains on tension rods, removable hooks, rugs, and textiles. Homeowners can go a step further with swapping cabinet hardware or light fixtures, which are easy DIY upgrades with a screwdriver.

## How to Shop Any Roundup Without Overbuying

Roundups like BuzzFeed's are fun to scroll, but they work best with a few rules:

1. **Measure first.** Write down dimensions for the rug, window, or shelf you're shopping for.
2. **Check reviews.** Look for recent reviews with photos, especially for textiles and storage.
3. **Set a cap per room.** Even $40 to $75 can transform a space if you spend it on the right things.
4. **Buy in stages.** Add one or two items, live with them for a week, then decide what's next.
5. **Confirm return policy.** If a color looks different at home, you want an easy exit.

## Where to Spend First

If you only have a small budget, spend it in this order: lighting, textiles, then accessories. Lighting changes the mood, textiles add comfort and color, and accessories finish the look.

This weekend, pick one room, take the photo, and fix the single thing that bothers you most. That's the glow-up that actually sticks.
