Plain apple cider is lovely. Apple cider that arrives in a caramel-rimmed glass, crowned with whipped cream and fragrant with mulling spices, feels like something you'd pay café prices for. You can make it at home, and you can serve it with one very simple savory partner: extra-sharp cheddar.

That's the combination Bill Michaels of Fly Creek Cider Mill & Orchard demonstrated on *Fox & Friends Weekend*, per Fox News. Here's how to turn the idea into a fall drink you'd be proud to hand a guest.

## The Idea: Treat Cider Like a Dessert Drink

According to Fox News, Michaels showed a hot mulled cider with three upgrades: a caramel-rimmed glass, a topping of whipped cream, and mulling spices. Each one does a job. The caramel adds sweetness at the first sip, the spices add warmth and aroma, and the cream gives the drink a soft, rich finish.

The segment didn't come with a published measurement list that I can verify, so what follows is a method rather than a precise formula. Adjust to your taste, and taste as you go.

## How to Build the Caramel-Rimmed Mulled Cider

### 1. Start with good cider

The drink is only as good as its base. Fresh-pressed cider from an orchard or farm stand gives you the most apple flavor to build on, and since this is a cider mill's recipe, that's the spirit of the thing.

### 2. Warm it gently with spices

Heat the cider in a pot with your mulling spices. Keep it at a gentle warm-through rather than a hard boil, so the flavors stay bright. Common mulling spices include cinnamon sticks, whole cloves, and allspice, though Michaels' exact blend wasn't detailed in the coverage. A tied bundle or a spice bag makes them easy to remove.

### 3. Rim the glasses

Do this before you pour. Spread a little caramel on a small plate, dip the rim of each mug or glass, and let it set for a few minutes. Use heatproof glasses, since the cider goes in hot.

### 4. Pour and top

Remove the spices, ladle the cider into the glasses, and finish with a spoonful of whipped cream. Serve right away, while the cream is still holding its shape.

## Why Sharp Cheddar Belongs Next to the Cup

Michaels paired the cider with extra-sharp cheddar, a classic fall flavor combination, per Fox News. The logic is easy to taste for yourself. Apple brings sweetness and a little acidity, while aged cheddar brings salt, savory depth, and a tangy bite. Each makes the other taste more like itself.

To try it at home:

- Cut the cheddar into thick slices or chunky cubes so it doesn't disappear next to the sweet drink.
- Alternate bites and sips. A bite of cheese, then a mouthful of warm cider.
- Keep the cheese at cool room temperature. Straight from the fridge it tastes muted.

If you want to stretch it into a spread, sliced apples are a natural addition. That's my suggestion, not part of Michaels' demonstration.

## Serving It for a Crowd

This works well for gatherings because the work splits cleanly into stages:

- **Ahead of time:** Rim the glasses with caramel and slice the cheddar.
- **Before guests settle in:** Warm the cider with the spices and keep it on low heat.
- **As you serve:** Pour, add whipped cream, and hand it over.

The result looks considered without demanding much from you once people arrive. And because the cider itself is non-alcoholic, it suits a mixed crowd, including drivers and kids who'd like something festive.

## Make It Your Own

Since the published details are light on quantities, treat this as a template. Start with a modest amount of spices, taste after a few minutes of warming, and add more if you want it bolder. Go easy on the caramel rim if your cider is already sweet.

The takeaway: this fall, warm up a pot of good cider with spices, rim the glasses with caramel, add whipped cream, and put a plate of extra-sharp cheddar beside it. That's the whole party.
