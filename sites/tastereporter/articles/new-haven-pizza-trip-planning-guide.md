## Who Colin Kaplan Is, and Why He Matters to Your Trip

Per Fox News, Kaplan is New Haven's official "pizza guru." He's described as a pizza historian and tour guide who walks visitors through the Elm City's culinary traditions, including its legendary local pizzerias.

That combination is the useful part. A historian-guide can tell you not just where to eat but why a place matters, which changes how you taste what's in front of you. If you're visiting for the first time, a guided outing is the fastest way to skip guesswork.

## Start With a Guide, Then Go Off-Script

Here's a simple two-part approach:

1. **Take a guided outing first.** Let someone like Kaplan set the context on day one. You'll learn the city's pizza vocabulary and traditions before you start choosing for yourself.
2. **Spend the rest of your time revisiting.** Once you know what you like, go back to the places that spoke to you and order differently.

This beats trying to hit every famous name in a single frantic afternoon.

## Treat It as a Crawl, Not a Meal

The pizza-crawl idea is one of the strongest ways to see a city like this. A few practical principles apply to any multi-stop pizza day:

- **Share everything.** Order one pie between two or three people at each stop so you can reach several pizzerias without hitting a wall.
- **Keep your orders comparable.** Choosing a similar style of pie at each stop lets you taste the differences between shops. Changing everything at once makes comparison impossible.
- **Take notes.** A quick phone note on crust, char, and sauce after each stop will save you from blurring everything together by stop four.
- **Leave gaps.** Walk between stops when you can. It's part of seeing the city.

These are general crawl tactics, not details from the Fox News report, but they fit what the piece describes: a city where pizza is the tour.

## Why a City Like New Haven Works for Food Travel

The report frames New Haven as part of a broader shift: travelers seeking hyper-local experiences and cities turning their culinary identity into a tourism draw. That's worth thinking about as a traveler.

Big-name destinations get the attention, but a smaller city with a deep, specific food tradition can offer something more focused. You're not sampling everything. You're going deep on one thing, and the locals around you are invested in it.

When you plan a trip this way, pick the dish first and the city second. It narrows your decisions and makes the itinerary almost write itself.

## A Simple Plan for Your First Visit

If you're building a New Haven pizza weekend, this is a sensible shape for it:

- **Day one:** Book a guided pizza experience if one is offered, and use it to learn the history and pick favorites.
- **Day two:** Make your own crawl. Return to one or two spots from the first day and add something new.
- **Anytime:** Ask your guide or the staff at each shop where they'd go next. Local recommendations compound.

Check current tour availability and shop hours before you go, since both can change.

## The Takeaway

When you visit a food city, hire the expert for the first day and spend the second one eating on your own terms. A guru like Kaplan gives you the map; the best part of the trip is figuring out where you want to go back.
