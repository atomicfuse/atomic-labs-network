Starting November 20, 2026, Airbnb hosts can no longer require you to strip the beds, run a load of laundry, wash the dishes, or haul out the garbage before you leave. By the end of this guide, you'll know what the rule covers, what it doesn't, and how to plan a food-focused stay around it.

The change was reported by Afar, which noted that guests have complained about long checkout chore lists for years. The policy also bans surprise house rules that weren't disclosed upfront.

## What Hosts Can't Ask For Anymore

Per Afar's reporting, the following can't be mandatory checkout tasks from November 20:

- Stripping the beds
- Doing laundry
- Washing dishes
- Removing garbage

The second part of the policy matters just as much: hosts can't spring house rules on you that weren't disclosed before booking. If a requirement isn't in the listing, it shouldn't show up on a note taped to the fridge.

## Why This Matters If You Cook on Vacation

One of the best ways to eat well in a new place is to shop a local market and cook in a rental kitchen. Fresh produce, regional cheeses, a bag of something you've never tried: it's a great way to learn a destination's flavors.

The catch used to be the aftermath. A big market-haul dinner could mean a sink full of pans and a bin full of scraps on your last morning, plus a host's instructions on top. Under the new policy, dishes and trash can't be required checkout duties.

That said, basic courtesy still goes a long way. Rinsing a pan or bagging up fish scraps is a kindness, even if it's no longer a rule. Just treat it as optional.

## The Cleaning Fee Question

Here's the trade-off to expect. Afar's report points to the reality that cleaning fees may rise as hosts adjust. If a host can't ask you to do the work, it's reasonable to think the cost could shift into the price instead. That's analysis, not a confirmed outcome, so watch how listings change after the effective date.

When comparing rentals, look at the total price including cleaning fees, not just the nightly rate. A cheaper nightly price with a high fee can cost more than a pricier listing with a low one, especially on short stays.

## Exceptions That Still Apply

The policy isn't a free pass on everything. Per the coverage, some rules can still stand:

- **Bear country garbage rules:** where wildlife safety depends on how trash is handled, those requirements remain.
- **Drought restrictions:** local water rules can still apply.
- **Damage charges:** you can still be charged for damage.

If you're renting a cabin in bear country and planning to cook, read the listing's safety notes closely. Food waste is exactly what those rules are about.

## If a Host Breaks the Rules

The report details on enforcement are limited, so check Airbnb's own help pages for the official process. Sensible steps in the meantime:

1. Screenshot the listing and house rules before you book.
2. Save any messages where the host adds new requirements.
3. Contact Airbnb support if a host demands chores or fees that weren't disclosed.

Keeping a record of what was posted at booking time is your best protection.

## A Quick Note on the Amex Partnership

Afar also reported that Airbnb is rolling out a partnership with American Express to offer rewards and perks on vacation rental bookings. Details beyond that weren't spelled out in the coverage, so if you carry an Amex card, check the card issuer's announcement before assuming specific credits or points rates.

## A Simple Checkout Plan for Food-Loving Travelers

- **Before booking:** compare total prices, read house rules, and screenshot them.
- **During your stay:** cook with local ingredients and keep the kitchen tidy as you go.
- **At checkout:** do what's reasonable, but know that stripping beds, laundry, dishes, and trash can't be demanded.

The best way to use this change is to book smart now: compare the full cost, save the listing details, and spend your final morning on one more market stop instead of a chore list.
