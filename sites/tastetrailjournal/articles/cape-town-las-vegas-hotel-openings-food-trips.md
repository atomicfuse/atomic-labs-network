## Why a hotel opening matters to a food traveler

A hotel's restaurant or bar is often the easiest first meal in a new city. You can use it as an anchor, then spend the rest of your time on the local dishes and markets that define a place. A few ways to use that:

- **Treat the hotel bar as a warm-up or a finale.** A rooftop drink works well on arrival night, when you want a view and a short walk back to bed.

- **Ask the front desk early.** Staff can usually point you to places locals actually go, so ask on day one, not day three.

## Cape Town: build the trip around the food, not just the hotel

A new Edition property gives Cape Town visitors one more polished base. Since we only know the opening itself was reported, here is how to plan the eating side using the kinds of dishes the city is generally known for.

### Dishes to look for

- **Bunny chow:** hollowed-out bread filled with curry, closely associated with Durban's Indian community and widely eaten across South Africa.
- **Braai:** South Africa's version of a barbecue, as much a social ritual as a meal. Look for grilled meats and sausages like boerewors.
- **Cape Malay cooking:** fragrant, gently spiced dishes such as bobotie, a baked spiced mince topped with an egg custard.
- **Seafood:** Cape Town sits on the coast, so fish and shellfish are an easy order.

### A simple planning approach

1. Pick one meal a day that's a local classic.
2. Pick one meal that's flexible, so you can follow a recommendation.
3. Keep the hotel's own dining for a night when you're tired or jet-lagged.

## A recipe to bring the trip home: quick South African-style spiced mince

This approachable dish is inspired by the Cape Malay tradition of gently spiced mince. It's a simplified weeknight take, not a traditional bobotie.

**Ingredients (serves 4):** 500 g ground beef or lamb, 1 chopped onion, 2 garlic cloves, 1 tablespoon curry powder, 1 teaspoon turmeric, 2 tablespoons chutney or apricot jam, 1 tablespoon vinegar, salt, and oil.

**Steps:**
1. Soften the onion in oil over medium heat for about 5 minutes. Add the garlic.
2. Stir in curry powder and turmeric for 30 seconds until fragrant.
3. Add the meat and cook until browned.
4. Stir in chutney and vinegar, then simmer for 10 minutes. Season with salt.
5. Serve over rice with a simple tomato and onion salad.

For a more traditional version, spoon the mince into a dish, top with beaten egg and milk, and bake until set.
