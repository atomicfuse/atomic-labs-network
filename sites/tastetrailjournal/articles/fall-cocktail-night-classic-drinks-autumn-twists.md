Forbes recently rounded up 17 fall cocktails, spanning hot toddies, martinis, margaritas and sours, with seasonal flavors from apple to pumpkin. The full list isn't reproduced here, but the pattern in it is useful: you don't need 17 new recipes. You need a few classics you already know and a couple of autumn flavors to swap in. This guide shows how to plan a fall cocktail night around that idea.

A note on recipes: I'm sticking to the drinks and flavors named in the Forbes roundup and giving general approaches rather than exact formulas, since the specifics of its 17 recipes aren't something I can vouch for here.

## Start With Four Classics

The roundup is built on familiar drink families. Per Forbes, those include:

- Whiskey sours
- Hot toddies
- Martinis
- Margaritas

That's the whole strategy: pick drinks that guests recognize, then let the season do the work. A familiar base keeps things easy to make and easy to order.

## Pick Your Flavor: Apple or Pumpkin

The two autumn flavors the roundup calls out are apple and pumpkin. Think of them as a choice, not a pile-on.

### Apple

Apple is the lighter, brighter option. It suits drinks that already have some tartness, such as a sour or a margaritas-style drink, where fruit and citrus can share the stage. It also works in a martini for a crisper, cleaner autumn feel.

### Pumpkin

Pumpkin is the richer, more dessert-leaning choice. It makes sense when you want a drink to feel like a treat at the end of a meal. Because it's heavier, one pumpkin drink on a menu is plenty.

If your guests are split, offer one of each and let them argue about it. That's half the fun of a themed night.

## Build a Short, Smart Menu

A fall cocktail night works best when the menu is small. Here's a simple structure:

1. **One shaken sour.** A whiskey sour is the anchor. It's a recognizable classic and sits comfortably in cooler weather.
2. **One warm drink.** A hot toddy gives guests something to hold when the evening cools off, and it's a good fit for the end of the night.
3. **One spirit-forward option.** A martini covers people who want something sharp and simple.
4. **One wildcard.** A margarita with an apple or pumpkin spin keeps the menu from feeling predictable.

Four drinks is enough to feel generous without turning your kitchen into a bar shift.

## Make It Easy on Yourself

The appeal of a seasonal cocktail night is comfort and hosting, not complexity. A few habits keep it relaxed:

- **Prep your flavors ahead.** Decide on apple or pumpkin before guests arrive so you aren't improvising mid-party.
- **Keep the warm drink simple.** Hot drinks are easiest when you can make them one at a time, in the mug, without a lot of steps.
- **Set out a self-serve spot.** Glasses, ice and garnishes in one place let guests help themselves.
- **Offer a no-alcohol option.** Apple and pumpkin flavors translate well to non-alcoholic drinks, so a mocktail version of your wildcard is an easy way to include everyone.

## Match the Drink to the Moment

Not every cold evening calls for the same glass. A rough guide:

- **Weather just turning:** a sour or margarita with apple still feels fresh.
- **Properly chilly night:** go straight to the hot toddy.
- **Dinner party:** a martini works as a pre-dinner pour.
- **Dessert hour:** the pumpkin drink earns its place here.

## Why This Approach Holds Up

Seasonal drinking tends to lean on comfort and familiarity, and that's why classics with a small twist work so well. You aren't asking guests to try something unfamiliar; you're handing them something they know, dressed for autumn.
