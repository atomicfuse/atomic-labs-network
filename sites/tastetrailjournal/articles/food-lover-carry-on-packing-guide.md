BuzzFeed Shopping recently rounded up 28 travel products that flight attendants rave about. The published summary doesn't include the actual product list, so this guide doesn't pass along specific picks. It borrows the idea behind the roundup: people who fly constantly pack with a system. If your trips are built around eating, that system matters even more, because the best part of the journey often ends up in your bag on the way home.

By the end of this guide, you'll have a packing approach for the flight itself, a plan for bringing edible souvenirs home, and a simple recipe to cook once you're back.

## Start With the Flight, Not the Destination

Most people pack for the place they're going and treat the flight as something to endure. Flip that. Pack a small, easy-to-reach pouch for the hours in transit.

A sensible food-lover's flight pouch might hold:

- A reusable water bottle, emptied before security and filled after
- A snack that doesn't crumble or smell strongly, such as nuts or dried fruit
- A few wet wipes or hand sanitizer for eating with your hands
- A small cloth napkin or bandana that doubles as a placemat

Keep this pouch at the top of your bag or under the seat so you aren't hauling down the overhead bin mid-flight. Check your airline's and airport's current rules on liquids and food before you go, since they vary by country and change over time.

## Build a Packing Zone System

The idea behind a lot of expert packing advice is organization: everything has a home. For a trip focused on markets and street food, try three zones.

### Zone 1: Flight essentials
The pouch above, plus whatever keeps you comfortable, like a layer for cold cabins. This zone stays accessible.

### Zone 2: Eating kit
A small kit you'll use daily at your destination: a compact fork or spoon, a few reusable bags, and a thin tote for market shopping. Street food and market stalls often mean eating standing up, so a few simple tools help.

### Zone 3: Return space
Leave roughly a quarter of your bag empty, or pack a foldable bag. You will buy something. Planning for it avoids last-minute airport stress.

## Bringing Edible Souvenirs Home

Food souvenirs are one of the best ways to extend a trip, but rules differ by country. Many places restrict fresh produce, meat, and dairy at the border, so check the customs guidance for your home country before buying.

Shelf-stable items usually travel best:

- **Dried spices and spice blends:** light, flat-packing, and easy to use
- **Dried chiles, herbs, and teas:** low risk for breakage
- **Sealed jars and tins:** wrap in clothing and place in the center of your bag, away from the shell
- **Hard sweets and sealed packaged snacks:** sturdy and easy to share

For anything that might leak, double-bag it in zip-style bags. Put jars in a sock or wrap them in a shirt, and pack them in the middle of your suitcase so clothing cushions them. Liquids over carry-on limits, like olive oil or sauces, generally need to go in checked luggage.

## Ask Before You Buy

At markets, vendors can often tell you whether something is sealed for travel or will keep for several days. A simple question helps: "Will this be okay in a suitcase for a day or two?" Many sellers vacuum-seal or box goods for visitors, but you shouldn't assume it.

## A Simple Recipe for When You Get Home

Even if your souvenirs are only a bag of dried spices, you can turn them into a meal. This pantry-style spiced chickpea skillet works with almost any blend you bring back.

### Spiced Chickpea Skillet (serves 2)

**You'll need:** 1 tablespoon olive oil, 1 small chopped onion, 2 cloves garlic, 1 can (about 15 oz) drained chickpeas, 1 to 2 teaspoons of your souvenir spice blend, 1 can (14 oz) chopped tomatoes, salt, and something fresh to finish, like herbs or yogurt.

1. Warm the oil in a skillet over medium heat. Cook the onion for about 5 minutes until soft.
2. Add the garlic and spice blend. Stir for 30 seconds until fragrant.
3. Add the chickpeas and tomatoes. Simmer for 10 to 15 minutes until thickened.
4. Season with salt, taste, and adjust the spice. Top with herbs or yogurt.
5. Serve with bread or rice.

Start with a smaller amount of spice, since blends vary a lot in heat and salt.

## What Flight Attendant-Style Packing Gets Right

The appeal of the BuzzFeed roundup is that frequent flyers refine their routines over time. You can borrow that mindset without buying anything: keep essentials reachable, pack in zones, and leave room to bring something back.

Before your next trip, set aside a quarter of your bag for the food you'll want to carry home. That one decision changes how you shop, and how well the trip lasts after you land.
