Wirecutter, the product-testing arm of The New York Times, recently rounded up 44 travel tips from more than a decade of reporting. The source summary we have covers 30 of them, so we won't pretend to cover all 44. Instead, we picked the ones that matter most when your trip is built around eating: markets, street stalls, regional specialties, and the occasional jar or bottle you'll want to carry home.

By the end, you'll have a practical packing and prep plan for a food-focused trip, with every tip credited to Wirecutter's reporting.

## Pack light so you can bring food home

A food trip tends to end with a heavier bag than it started with. A few of Wirecutter's packing tips leave you room for that.

### Use a simple system

For weeklong trips, Wirecutter suggests the 5,4,3,2,1 packing method. The summary doesn't spell out the breakdown, so look up the details in the original piece. It also recommends packing versatile clothes in one color palette, so everything mixes and matches, and folding formal clothes while rolling casual items.

### Shrink and rewear

Compression bags can shrink bulky clothing. Wirecutter also suggests merino wool pieces, which you can rewear without odor. That is handy after a day of grilled-meat smoke or a crowded night market.

### Bring a packable daypack or tote

This is the most useful tip for market days. A packable daypack or tote folds flat in your suitcase, then carries produce, snacks, and picnic supplies once you arrive.

### Skip the garment bag

Wirecutter says to skip it unless it's truly necessary. It takes up space you could use for souvenirs.

## Handle liquids, sauces, and toiletries smartly

This section matters most to anyone planning to bring flavors home or eat well on the road.

- **Use a Ziploc freezer bag** for TSA-compliant liquids, per Wirecutter.
- **Use travel containers for non-toiletry items** like hot sauce. Your favorite condiment can travel with you.
- **Don't decant sensitive skin-care products**, Wirecutter advises.
- **Transfer full-size toothpaste into a refillable tube** rather than buying a travel size.
- **Use a soap bag** so bar soap doesn't get mushy.
- **Use a pill case as a jewelry organizer**, a small trick that frees up space.

The hot sauce tip is a reminder to check airline and security rules for any liquid or sauce, since the summary only mentions it as an example of using travel containers.

## Decide on your bag before you shop

If you expect to buy olive oil, spices, or preserves, luggage choices matter.

### Don't be afraid to check a bag

Wirecutter encourages travelers not to fear checking a bag. For a trip where you'll collect edible souvenirs, it may be the easiest option.

### Choose the right size

Wirecutter recommends a medium checked bag over a large one. It also says not to assume lighter suitcases are always better. For international trips, it suggests a smaller carry-on, and it notes that many carry-ons exceed listed airline dimensions.

### Weigh it and tag it

A luggage scale helps you avoid overweight-bag stress, which is especially useful if your bag is filling up with jars and tins. Wirecutter also says to use an analog luggage tag even if you use a Bluetooth tracker.

## Prep your phone and documents for eating abroad

Markets and small restaurants are easier when your phone works and you can read the menu.

- **Install an inexpensive eSIM** before international trips, per Wirecutter.
- **Download maps, translation languages, and phone updates** before you arrive. Offline translation is useful when a handwritten menu has no English.
- **Carry paper copies of key travel details**, such as reservations.
- **Photograph or photocopy your passport.**
- **Protect your phone's privacy** when crossing borders.
- **Pack personal items in an unsuspecting small bag.**

## Make the flight comfortable

You'll want to arrive ready to eat. Wirecutter's flight advice:

- Wear polished but comfortable clothes.
- Choose broken-in, versatile shoes over cute ones. You'll be walking food streets all day.
- Pick a travel pillow that matches how you sleep.
- Get a better eye mask than the free airline version.

## A simple food-trip checklist

Here is how we'd apply these tips, as our own suggestion rather than Wirecutter's:

1. Pack one color palette and a merino piece or two.
2. Tuck a folded tote or daypack on top.
3. Put liquids in a freezer bag and leave space for a small container of your favorite sauce.
4. Weigh your bag before you leave, and again before you come home.
5. Download offline maps and your translation language, and save paper copies of reservations.
6. Wear your most broken-in shoes on the plane.
