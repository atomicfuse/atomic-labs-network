## Build Your Own Charleston Food Map

Take the restaurants Hall names and turn them into a plan.

### 1. Sort by meal, not by name
Place each recommendation into breakfast, lunch, dinner, or a snack stop. Beginners often stack three dinner reservations and leave no room for casual eating.

### 2. Cluster by neighborhood
Pin every pick on a map. Group stops that sit near each other so you spend your time eating, not in transit.

### 3. Book the hard ones first
Reserve the dinner spots as soon as your dates are set, then keep lunches flexible. Walk-in meals leave space for discovery.

### 4. Leave one slot open
Good trips need one unplanned meal. Keep it for a place a local points you toward.

## How to Eat Like a Local Anywhere

These habits work in Charleston and elsewhere, and they follow the spirit of a chef-led approach to finding food.

- **Ask a specific question.** Instead of "Where should I eat?", ask "Where do you go on a regular Tuesday?" People answer honestly when the question is personal.
- **Ask people in food-adjacent jobs.** Market vendors, bakers, and bartenders spend their days around good ingredients and know where the kitchens care.
- **Order the regional dish.** When a menu features a local specialty, that's usually where the kitchen's pride lies. In Charleston, that means looking for Lowcountry cooking on the menu.
- **Visit at off-peak hours.** An early lunch or a late-afternoon snack gives you a calmer room and more conversation with staff.
- **Check what's seasonal.** A short menu that changes often tends to signal fresh sourcing.

## A Simple Lowcountry-Inspired Dish to Cook at Home

If you want to bring the trip home, try a simple shrimp and grits, a dish closely associated with Charleston and the Lowcountry. This is a general home-cook approach, not a recipe from Hall.

**You'll need:** 1 cup stone-ground grits, 4 cups water or stock, 1 pound peeled shrimp, 3 slices bacon, 2 cloves garlic, a squeeze of lemon, butter, salt, and pepper.

1. Simmer the grits in the liquid, stirring often, for 25 to 40 minutes until creamy. Finish with butter and salt.
2. Cook the chopped bacon until crisp and set it aside, leaving the fat in the pan.
3. Sauté the shrimp in the bacon fat for about 2 minutes per side until pink.
4. Add garlic for the last 30 seconds, then finish with lemon.
5. Spoon over the grits and top with bacon.
