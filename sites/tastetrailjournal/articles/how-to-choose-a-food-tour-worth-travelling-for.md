## Why food tours have become a reason to travel

Per Euronews, culinary tourism is now a major driver of travel decisions. People increasingly want immersive, experience-led holidays rather than a checklist of sights. Food and drink tours fit that mood because they tie into local culture, sustainability and authentic regional storytelling.

That's the good news. The catch is that "food tour" covers everything from a thoughtful morning with a market vendor to a rushed walk past five identical tasting stops. The checklist below helps you tell them apart.

## A five-point test for a tour worth the journey

### 1. Does it teach you something specific to the place?

A tour worth travelling for explains why a dish or drink belongs to its region: the ingredient, the climate, the tradition behind it. If the description could apply to any city, it probably isn't a destination-level experience.

### 2. Is the format right for what you love?

Decide what kind of eater you are before you book:

- **Street food and market lovers:** look for walking tours that move between stalls and small counters.
- **Wine drinkers:** look for tours based in the growing region itself, not a tasting room far from the vineyards.
- **Beer fans:** look for tours that include the brewery or a venue that serves local styles.

### 3. Is the pace realistic?

Check how many stops are included and how long the tour runs. Several tastings in a short window means small bites you can't appreciate. Fewer stops with time to ask questions usually beats a packed route.

### 4. Does it support local producers?

Sustainability and regional storytelling are part of what's driving this trend, according to Euronews. Look for tours that name the producers, markets or family businesses they visit, rather than staying vague.

### 5. Would you still go if it were the only plan that day?

This is the real "worth travelling for" test. If the tour is the highlight rather than a filler between sights, build the day around it.

## How to plan a holiday around eating and drinking

**Start with the craving, not the map.** Pick the experience first: a wine region, a street food city, a brewery town. Then choose a place that delivers it.

**Anchor each day with one big food event.** One tour or market visit per day leaves room for wandering, snacking and following recommendations.

**Book the tour early in your stay.** Whatever you learn on day one, like which dishes are regional or which stalls locals favor, shapes every meal after.

**Match the trip to your budget.** Street food cities tend to stretch a budget through many small, inexpensive tastings, while wine regions often involve transport and tasting fees. Check the full costs before you commit.

**Leave gaps.** Some of the best finds come from a market stall you stumble on between planned stops.

## Wine, beer or street food: picking your first trip

If this is your first food-led trip, street food is the easiest entry. It needs little planning, suits most budgets and lets you sample widely. Wine regions reward slower, more deliberate travel. Beer trips suit travelers who like pubs, brewery visits and casual conversation. Choose the one that matches how you already like to spend a free afternoon.

## A simple recipe to bring the trip home

Tours are best when they change how you cook. This flexible tasting-plate approach works with whatever you discover on your travels.

### Market-Plate Supper (serves 2)

**You'll need:**
- A small wedge of local-style cheese
- A handful of olives or pickles
- Fresh bread
- Seasonal fruit or tomatoes
- Cured meat or a hard-boiled egg
- Olive oil, salt and pepper

**Method:**
1. Slice the bread and warm it in a dry pan for a minute per side.
2. Slice the tomatoes or fruit, then dress lightly with oil, salt and pepper.
3. Arrange everything on a board with the cheese, olives and meat.
4. Pour a glass of whatever you brought back from your trip, and eat slowly.

Swap in ingredients from wherever you've been. The point is the pace: small portions, tasted one at a time, the way a good tour teaches you to eat.

## The takeaway

Before you book any food or drink tour, ask whether it teaches you something you couldn't learn at home. If the answer is yes, plan the trip around it.
