## The Food: Hyperlocal, Not Trend-Driven

The culinary direction comes from chef Christopher Santopinto, who is committed to Bay Area ingredients. Per the reporting, he rejects trends such as imported Wagyu in favor of a hyperlocal approach.

For you as a diner, that suggests a few practical things:

- Expect a menu that reflects what's grown and produced around the Bay rather than headline luxury imports.
- Ask your server what's local and seasonal that night. A local-first kitchen is usually happy to explain.
- Don't go in expecting a greatest-hits list of global fine-dining staples. The point is a sense of place.

Specific dishes and prices aren't included in the reporting, so check the menu closer to your visit.

## The Drinks: Classic San Francisco, Reimagined

Bar leadership comes from Max Monroe, who is reimagining classic San Francisco drinks. Two named in the coverage are the Pisco Punch and the Black Manhattan. The updates reportedly use garden herbs and local citrus.

## The Room: Queen Anne Antiques Meet Modern Atmosphere

Designer Jon de la Cruz blended Queen Anne antiques with modern, atmospheric interiors. The stated aim, as Afar frames it, is to honor the past without freezing it in time.

That balance is the thing to look for when you walk in. Notice which pieces feel like heirlooms and which feel new. A restoration that works usually makes that difference visible without making either side feel out of place.
