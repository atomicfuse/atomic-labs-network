Las Vegas brunch has outgrown eggs and a single mimosa. Per Las Vegas Weekly, which rounded up four weekend options at Resorts World, STK, Play Playground and South Point, the city's brunch scene now runs from a wellness class to a live DJ to a carving station. The practical question is which one fits your group. This guide sorts the four by vibe so you can choose in a minute.

One note up front: the original roundup was sponsored, and the details here come from its descriptions. It doesn't list prices, hours or reservation rules, so check each venue directly before you plan around them.

## If You Want a Wellness Reset: Resorts World

Resorts World pairs movement with a meal. The roundup describes Poolside Pulse Pilates at AYU Dayclub, followed by brunch at FUHU. It's a way to start a weekend morning with a class and then sit down to eat, rather than treating brunch as a recovery mission.

Resorts World also has a second brunch option, a Latin-inspired brunch at ¡VIVA!. If your group is split between people who want a workout first and people who just want to eat, having both options at one resort makes planning simpler.

**Best for:** travelers who want a lighter, more active start to the day, and anyone trying to keep a Vegas weekend from becoming a blur.

## If You Want a Party Brunch: STK Las Vegas

STK offers an All You Can Brunch with chef-driven bites, cocktails and live DJs, per the roundup. This is the nightlife-adjacent pick: the music is part of the meal, so expect a livelier room than a quiet table by a window.

The format matters here. "All You Can" means you can pace yourself over the meal instead of ordering everything at once. Confirm exactly what's included, including drinks, when you book, since the roundup doesn't spell out the terms.

**Best for:** friend groups, celebrations, and anyone who thinks brunch should come with a soundtrack.

## If You Want Brunch as an Activity: Play Playground Las Vegas

Play Playground combines a brunch menu with bottomless mimosas and Bloody Marys and more than 20 interactive games, according to the roundup. The food is only part of the plan; the games give the table something to do between courses.

That makes it a good fit for groups that run out of conversation by the second round, or for people who'd rather compete than linger. Whether it suits kids depends on the venue's age policies, which the roundup doesn't cover, so ask before bringing younger guests, especially with alcohol on the menu.

**Best for:** groups who want built-in entertainment and a casual, playful mood.

## If You Want the Classic Feast: South Point Garden Buffet

South Point's Garden Buffet offers a prime rib and Champagne brunch with global buffet stations, per the roundup. This is the old-school Vegas choice: a big spread, a carved roast and room to build a plate exactly how you like it.

Buffets reward a strategy. A few approaches that work anywhere:

- Walk the whole buffet once before picking up a plate.
- Start with the thing you can't get elsewhere, here the prime rib.
- Take small portions from the global stations so you can sample more.

**Best for:** big appetites, mixed groups with different tastes, and travelers who like choosing their own dishes.

## Bottomless vs. Buffet: How to Decide

The four options really come down to two formats. STK and Play Playground lean on the bottomless or all-you-can model, where the drinks and atmosphere drive the experience. South Point leans on the buffet, where the food variety does. Resorts World sits apart, with wellness and a Latin-inspired alternative.

A simple way to choose:

1. **Decide what the morning is for.** Movement, music, games or eating.
2. **Count your group's drinkers.** Bottomless formats pay off only if people actually drink.
3. **Check the fine print.** Time limits, reservations and pricing aren't in the roundup, so confirm them before you commit.

## Build a Day Around Your Pick

Brunch works best as an anchor, not a finale. Book it for late morning, then leave the afternoon open. If you pick the Pilates option at Resorts World, you've already got a morning plan. If you pick the buffet at South Point, a big midday meal means you can keep dinner light or skip it.

Pick one vibe, call ahead for current details, and let brunch set the tone for the rest of your Vegas Saturday.
