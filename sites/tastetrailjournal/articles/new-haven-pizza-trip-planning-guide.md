## Build Your Own Pizza Crawl

If you'd rather go solo, or want to add stops to a guided tour, here's a simple framework that works in any pizza-proud town.

### Pick a few pizzerias, not a dozen

The report highlights legendary local pizzerias as the heart of the scene. Choose two or three of the best-known spots rather than racing through many. Pizza is filling, and the point is to taste differences.

### Order the same thing everywhere

Start each stop with a plain pie, or the shop's simplest option, so you can compare crust, char, sauce, and cheese fairly. Then add one house specialty if you have room.

### Share and pace

Split a pie among your group, and order one at a time if you can. A walk between stops helps, and so does water.

### Ask what locals order

Staff at long-running pizzerias usually have strong opinions. Asking "what do you get here?" is the quickest way into a regional dining custom.

## Bring It Home: A Crisp-Crust Pizza Night

You can't replicate a coal-fired pizzeria at home, but you can borrow the spirit: a thin, well-browned crust and restrained toppings. This is an approachable, general-purpose approach, not a recipe attributed to any New Haven shop.

**What you need**

- Pizza dough (store-bought is fine)
- Canned crushed tomatoes, lightly salted
- Olive oil
- Garlic
- Grated hard cheese
- A heavy baking sheet or pizza stone

**Steps**

1. Heat your oven as high as it goes, with the baking sheet or stone inside, for at least 30 minutes.
2. Stretch the dough thin, aiming for an irregular shape. Rustic is fine.
3. Brush with olive oil and rub with a little garlic.
4. Spread a thin layer of crushed tomatoes. Less is more.
5. Scatter a light dusting of cheese.
6. Slide onto the hot surface and bake until the edges are deeply browned and the bottom is crisp, usually 8 to 12 minutes depending on your oven.

Serve it plain first. Then you're doing your own mini taste test.
