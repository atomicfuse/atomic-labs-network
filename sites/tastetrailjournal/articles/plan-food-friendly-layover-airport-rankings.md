## Why a smoother airport means a better meal

Every minute you don't spend in a slow security line or hunting for a gate is a minute you can spend eating something worth remembering. A well-run airport helps in a few concrete ways:

- **Predictable security times** let you decide when to eat before or after the checkpoint.
- **Clear layouts** mean less backtracking between your gate and the food hall.
- **Fewer delays and less crowding** keep restaurant lines manageable.

Passenger volumes remain high, and travelers are increasingly sensitive to delays, crowding and friction, per the Fox News report. That makes the airport you choose a real part of your trip's quality, not an afterthought.

## How to use rankings when you book

### Compare your options, not just the winners

If you have a choice of departure cities or connection points, look up how each airport scored. A higher-ranked hub can be the better bet for a tight connection, since the whole point of the ranking is how easily travelers get through.

### Pick the layover with breathing room

A ranking is not a guarantee. If you want to leave the terminal for a meal, give yourself a generous window. If you're staying inside, a shorter layover at a well-organized airport can still work.

### Check the practical features before you commit

Before booking, look for these things on the airport's own website:

1. How check-in and security are organized.
2. How far the gates are from the main food areas.
3. Which restaurants and food stalls are located past security.
4. Whether the airport has local-specialty vendors, not only national chains.

## Eat like you're in the place you're passing through

Southern cities have strong regional food traditions, and many airports try to reflect their home cities with local vendors. Whether any particular winning airport does so is something to confirm on its website. As a rule, look for the stall that sells something you couldn't easily get back home: a regional barbecue plate, a local seafood dish, a classic baked good.

A good habit is to search the airport's dining directory before you fly and pick one local-minded spot for each terminal you'll pass through. Then you're not choosing in a rush at the gate.

## A simple airport-ready snack to carry on

When a layover is too tight for a sit-down meal, a homemade, security-friendly snack saves you. This cheese-and-pepper biscuit riff is inspired by southern baking traditions and travels well.

### Cheddar black pepper biscuits

**Makes about 8 biscuits.**

- 2 cups all-purpose flour
- 1 tablespoon baking powder
- 1/2 teaspoon salt
- 1 teaspoon cracked black pepper
- 6 tablespoons cold butter, cubed
- 3/4 cup grated sharp cheddar
- 3/4 cup cold buttermilk

**Steps:**

1. Heat the oven to 425°F (220°C) and line a baking sheet.
2. Whisk the flour, baking powder, salt and pepper in a bowl.
3. Rub in the cold butter with your fingertips until the mixture looks like coarse crumbs with some pea-sized pieces.
4. Stir in the cheddar, then the buttermilk, just until a shaggy dough forms.
5. Pat the dough to about 1 inch thick, cut rounds, and place them close together on the sheet.
6. Bake 15 to 18 minutes, until golden. Cool fully before wrapping.

Wrap them in foil or a reusable container. Solid foods like these are generally fine to bring through security, but check your airport's and airline's rules, especially for anything international.

## Build your own airport scorecard

Rankings are a starting point. Your own priorities matter too. Before your next trip, take five minutes to write down what a good airport means to you: short walks between gates, quick security, a decent local meal. Then check your departure and connection airports against that list, and book the route that gives you the most unhurried time to eat well.
