Five ice cream flavors that were once everywhere in the 1970s have largely vanished from mainstream freezer aisles, according to Fox News: Butter Brickle, rum raisin, pink bubblegum, Heavenly Hash and maple walnut. Fans, the outlet reports, still miss them. If you can't find them at the store, you can build a close version at home. This guide gives you what defines each flavor and a simple way to approximate it in your own kitchen.

The timing suits the moment. Per the Fox News report, nostalgia-driven food trends are gaining traction as brands revive retro snacks, diner staples and childhood favorites. The term for it is "newstalgia," a blend of comfort, memory and novelty.

One note before you start: the original report names the flavors but doesn't supply recipes or brand histories. The ideas below are general home-cooking suggestions, not reconstructions of any particular brand's formula.

## The Easy Base That Works for All Five

Start with a no-churn base, which needs no machine. Whip 2 cups of cold heavy cream to stiff peaks, then fold in one 14-ounce can of sweetened condensed milk and a teaspoon of vanilla. Freeze in a loaf pan for at least 6 hours. Every flavor below is a variation on this base.

## 1. Butter Brickle

Butter Brickle is built around buttery toffee-style candy pieces. To approximate it at home, fold in about 1 cup of crushed toffee bits, either homemade or store-bought. Add a pinch of salt to the base so the butter flavor reads clearly.

**Tip:** Stir in the toffee just before freezing, then sprinkle a little more on top. Pieces left in the base too long can soften.

## 2. Rum Raisin

Rum raisin is the most grown-up flavor on the list. Soak 3/4 cup of raisins in 1/4 cup of dark rum for at least an hour, or overnight if you have time. Drain them, fold them into the base, and add a spoonful of the leftover rum if you want a stronger flavor.

**Tip:** Alcohol lowers the freezing point, so keep the rum to a modest amount or the ice cream stays soft. For a version without alcohol, soak the raisins in warm water with a splash of rum extract.

## 3. Pink Bubblegum

This is the playful one. Use a few drops of pink food coloring and a bubblegum flavoring extract in the base, adding the extract a little at a time and tasting as you go. Brands typically build the flavor with candy-style flavorings rather than real gum, so an extract is the practical route at home.

**Tip:** Check the extract's label for strength, since concentrations vary widely. Start with 1/2 teaspoon.

## 4. Heavenly Hash

Heavenly Hash is traditionally described as a chocolate-based ice cream loaded with mix-ins. A common combination is marshmallow, nuts and chocolate. For a home version, swap 1/4 cup of cocoa powder into the base (whisk it into the condensed milk first), then fold in mini marshmallows and chopped almonds or walnuts.

**Tip:** Mini marshmallows firm up in the freezer, so add them last and stir gently to keep them whole.

## 5. Maple Walnut

Maple walnut is the simplest to pull off. Replace some of the sweetness with real maple syrup, using about 1/3 cup in the base, and cut the vanilla in half. Toast 3/4 cup of chopped walnuts in a dry skillet for a few minutes until fragrant, cool them, and fold them in.

**Tip:** Use real maple syrup rather than pancake syrup. The flavor is the whole point.

## Which Flavor to Make First

If you want the quickest win, make maple walnut or Butter Brickle, since both rely on easy-to-find ingredients. If you want a project for a gathering, Heavenly Hash gives you the most to talk about. Pink bubblegum is the one to make for kids, and rum raisin is for the adults at the table.

## Turn It Into a Tasting Night

Because all five share the same base, you can make small batches and set up a retro tasting. Split the base into portions and make two or three flavors in separate small containers. Invite people from different generations and ask which flavors they remember, and which deserve a comeback. That question is the heart of the Fox News piece, and it tends to start good conversations.

## Storage Tips

- Press parchment or wax paper directly onto the surface before sealing to limit ice crystals.
- Let the container sit on the counter for about 5 minutes before scooping.
- Homemade ice cream is best within about two weeks.

Pick one flavor from this list, mix the base tonight, and let it freeze while you ask your family which forgotten flavor they'd vote back into the freezer aisle.
