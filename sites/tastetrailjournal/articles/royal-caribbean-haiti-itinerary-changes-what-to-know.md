If you have a Royal Caribbean cruise on the calendar, or you're eyeing one, here's the short version: Haiti is off the menu for a long while. Per TravelPulse, the cruise line has extended its suspension of Haiti calls through spring 2028, rerouting dozens of itineraries that previously stopped at Labadee. The report ties the decision to ongoing military operations by El Salvador's forces in Haiti.

Below, you'll find what this means for your booking, how to check your own sailing, and how to think about Caribbean cruises while the region's map keeps shifting.

## What has changed

According to TravelPulse, Royal Caribbean has lengthened its existing ban on Haiti stops. Itineraries that included Labadee are being reworked rather than simply canceled, so ships still sail, but the port list looks different.

The report frames this as part of a broader pattern: Haiti's security crisis continues to worsen, and major cruise operators are making longer-term adjustments to Caribbean routes. The practical takeaway is that limited or altered access to some traditional ports may be the norm for the foreseeable future.

The details of each replacement port aren't spelled out in the coverage we have, so don't assume where your ship will go instead. Your cruise line will confirm the specifics for your sailing.

## How to check your own sailing

Do these in order:

1. **Find your booking confirmation** and look at the listed ports of call.
2. **Log in to your cruise account** or app and compare the current itinerary with what you originally booked. Itineraries that change are typically updated there.
3. **Check your email** (including spam) for notices from the cruise line or your travel agent.
4. **Contact your travel advisor** if you booked through one. They can explain options if the new route changes your plans.
5. **Read your cruise contract's itinerary section.** Most cruise lines reserve the right to change ports, and the terms on compensation vary, so check yours rather than guessing.

If Labadee was the reason you picked a particular sailing, ask directly what the replacement stop is and whether your options change. It's a fair question to put to the line.

## Travel insurance and port changes

A port swap is a different thing from a cancelled cruise, and insurance treats them differently. Policies commonly differ on whether an itinerary change counts as a covered event. Before you buy or file anything, read the policy wording on itinerary changes, and ask the insurer or your agent what is and isn't covered. We can't say from the report how any particular policy responds, so treat this as a checklist item, not a promise.

## Planning a Caribbean cruise while routes shift

A few habits make you more flexible:

- **Pick the ship and the cruise line for the whole experience**, not a single port. Itineraries can change, so a sailing built around one stop is a bigger gamble.
- **Book with a refundable or flexible option when you can**, and read the fine print on port changes.
- **Look at the full route**, not just the headline destination. Several stops give you more room if one changes.
- **Re-check your itinerary** a few weeks before departure, then again closer in.

## Eat your way through the replacement ports

This is the silver lining for food-minded travelers. When a route changes, you get a new place to eat. Instead of mourning the missing stop, look up the local specialty at wherever your ship docks. Search for the regional dish, the market near the pier, and the street-food stall locals recommend. A cruise port day is short, so pick one dish and one market and do them well.

If you want a taste of the Caribbean at home while you wait for your sailing, make a simple pineapple-lime rice and beans side:

### Easy coconut rice and beans

- 1 cup long-grain rice, rinsed
- 1 can (about 14 oz) coconut milk plus enough water to make 2 cups liquid
- 1 can black or red beans, drained
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 2 garlic cloves, minced
- Lime wedges, to serve

Simmer the garlic, coconut liquid, and salt. Add the rice, cover, and cook on low about 18 minutes. Stir in the beans, cover for 5 minutes off the heat, then fluff and squeeze lime over the top. It pairs well with grilled chicken or fish.
