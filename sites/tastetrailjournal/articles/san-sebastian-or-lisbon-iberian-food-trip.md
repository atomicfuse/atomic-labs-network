When Travel + Leisure asked six chefs and travel experts to name a favorite food destination, they all landed on the same place: the Iberian Peninsula. Two cities came up in particular, San Sebastián and Lisbon. If you're planning a trip around eating, this guide helps you decide which city suits you, what to look for in each, and how to bring a taste of it home.

One caveat up front: the original piece is the source for the experts' consensus, and this guide doesn't put words in their mouths or claim to know their individual picks. What follows is a practical way to think about the choice, built around the qualities the coverage highlights: acclaimed restaurants, markets, coastal flavors, and food culture that's easy to explore.

## Why the Iberian Peninsula keeps winning

Per Travel + Leisure, food-focused travel continues to drive how people plan trips, and travelers want destinations that offer both world-class dining and a strong local food culture. Iberia delivers both. You can book a celebrated restaurant for one night and spend the next day grazing through markets and casual spots, all in the same city.

That range is the real draw. You don't need a huge budget or a rigid reservation plan to eat well. You need a sense of how each city's food scene works.

## San Sebastián: for travelers who like to graze

San Sebastián, on Spain's northern coast, is the pick if your ideal food day is a series of small stops rather than one long sit-down meal. The city is closely associated with pintxos, the small bites served in bars, typically eaten standing up with a drink before you move on to the next place.

Why it suits some travelers better than others:

- **You like variety.** Small plates let you try many things without committing to one big order.
- **You like to wander.** A bar-hopping rhythm rewards curiosity and a loose schedule.
- **You want coastal flavors.** The city's position on the water shapes what's on the counter.

It also has the acclaimed-restaurant side. If a splurge meal is part of your plan, book it first and build the casual eating around it.

## Lisbon: for travelers who like markets and variety

Lisbon is the pick if you want food woven through a big, walkable city. Markets, coastal ingredients, and a mix of traditional and modern dining make it easy to eat well at nearly any budget. Seafood, including the salt cod (bacalhau) Portugal is famous for, and the custard tarts known as pastéis de nata are classic starting points.

Why it might be your city:

- **You like a mix of old and new.** Traditional tavern-style cooking sits alongside contemporary restaurants.
- **You like markets.** Browsing stalls is a low-pressure way to learn what locals eat.
- **You want a full city trip.** Lisbon gives you sightseeing and eating in the same stroll.

## Quick way to choose

**Choose San Sebastián** if food is the main reason you're going and you'd happily plan your day around where to eat next.

**Choose Lisbon** if you want great food as one big part of a broader city trip.

**Can't decide?** Both cities are on the same peninsula, and the experts in the original piece singled out both. A trip that includes both is realistic if you have enough days, though you'll want to check flight and rail options yourself before committing.

## How to eat beyond the obvious tourist stops

These habits work in either city:

1. **Go to the market first.** Walk the stalls before you pick restaurants. You'll see what's in season and what's sold in volume, which tells you what to order later.
2. **Order what's written on the board.** Daily specials or chalkboard items usually reflect what's fresh.
3. **Eat in small bursts.** Share plates, taste more, and keep your appetite for the next stop.
4. **Watch the locals.** If a spot is full of people who clearly aren't sightseeing, you're probably in the right place.
5. **Book one big meal, leave the rest open.** Reservations at acclaimed restaurants can fill, so lock in that one meal and keep everything else flexible.

## A simple recipe to try at home: the gilda

The gilda is a classic bar snack from the San Sebastián pintxo tradition: salty, briny, and sharp. It takes five minutes and no cooking.

**Makes:** 4 skewers

**You'll need:**
- 12 pitted green olives
- 4 oil-packed anchovy fillets
- 4 pickled green peppers (guindilla peppers if you can find them; mild pickled peppers or pepperoncini work too)
- 4 toothpicks or small skewers
- Good olive oil

**Steps:**
1. Thread one olive onto a toothpick.
2. Fold an anchovy fillet in a loose accordion shape and thread it on.
3. Add a pickled pepper, then two more olives.
4. Repeat with the remaining skewers.
5. Arrange on a plate and drizzle lightly with olive oil.

Serve with something cold and crisp to drink. If your anchovies are very salty, pat them dry on a paper towel first.
