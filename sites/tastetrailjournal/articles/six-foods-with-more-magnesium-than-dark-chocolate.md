Dark chocolate gets a lot of credit as the magnesium snack. It is a good source, but according to Health, it is not the richest one. Six everyday foods contain more: pumpkin seeds, spinach, amaranth, Brazil nuts, chia, and soybeans.

Below, you'll find what each one is, how it shows up in kitchens, and an easy way to use it this week. Keep the chocolate. Just give it some company.

## Why look beyond the chocolate bar?

Magnesium is a popular wellness topic, often linked in conversation to sleep, stress, and muscle recovery. That has more people asking what they can do with food alone before reaching for anything else. Dark chocolate is an appealing answer, but the foods below are, per Health, stronger options. They are also staples you can cook with, not just snack on.

This piece doesn't give exact milligram figures, since those vary by brand, portion, and preparation. Check the nutrition label on what you buy if you want precise numbers.

## 1. Pumpkin seeds

Pumpkin seeds (sometimes sold as pepitas) are the easiest swap on the list. They need no cooking and keep well in a jar.

**Try it:** Toast a handful in a dry pan for a few minutes until they begin to pop, then sprinkle them over soup, salad, or yogurt. They are also a classic topping for roasted squash.

## 2. Spinach

Spinach is the everyday green that does double duty: raw in a salad, wilted into almost anything hot.

**Try it:** Stir a big handful into hot pasta, eggs, or lentil soup at the very end. It collapses in under a minute, so it practically disappears into the dish.

## 3. Amaranth

Amaranth is a tiny grain with a long culinary history in several regional food traditions. It cooks into a soft, porridge-like texture, which makes it a natural breakfast bowl or a thickener for stews.

**Try it:** Simmer it in water or broth until tender, using roughly one part amaranth to two or three parts liquid. Check the package, as directions vary. Top it with fruit and pumpkin seeds for a breakfast that hits two items on this list.

## 4. Brazil nuts

Brazil nuts are large, rich, and creamy. They are easy to eat by the handful, but they are also good chopped.

**Try it:** Chop a few and scatter them over roasted vegetables or oatmeal, or blitz them into a crumbly topping for baked fish or grain bowls.

## 5. Chia

Chia is the most low-effort item here. The seeds swell in liquid and turn gel-like, which is why they are so popular in puddings.

**Try it:** Stir a few spoonfuls into milk or a plant-based alternative, add a little sweetener or fruit, and leave it in the fridge overnight. You'll have a spoonable breakfast by morning. You can also sprinkle dry chia over smoothies and yogurt.

## 6. Soybeans

Soybeans are the legume on the list, and they show up in many forms. You can buy them as edamame, cook them from dried, or use them in other soy-based foods.

**Try it:** Boil edamame in salted water for a few minutes and eat them as a snack, or toss shelled ones into fried rice, salads, or noodle bowls.

## A simple one-bowl magnesium upgrade

You don't need a recipe so much as a template. Here is a bowl that uses four of the six foods:

1. Cook amaranth until soft and creamy.
2. Wilt in a handful of spinach for the last minute.
3. Top with shelled edamame.
4. Finish with toasted pumpkin seeds and a drizzle of olive oil, plus salt and pepper.

It works warm for dinner, and leftovers reheat well with a splash of water. For a sweet version, swap the spinach and edamame for fruit, and use chia and chopped Brazil nuts as toppings.

## Easy swaps for a normal week

- **Instead of a chocolate square after lunch:** a small handful of pumpkin seeds and a few Brazil nuts.
- **Instead of plain oatmeal:** amaranth porridge, or oatmeal with chia stirred in.
- **Instead of a side salad of mixed lettuce:** spinach as the base.
- **Instead of chips:** warm edamame with salt.

None of this means giving up dark chocolate. It means treating it as one source among several, not the only one.

## The takeaway

Pick one item from this list and add it to a meal you already make this week. A spoonful of seeds on your soup is enough to start, and the rest of the list will be easy to work in from there.
