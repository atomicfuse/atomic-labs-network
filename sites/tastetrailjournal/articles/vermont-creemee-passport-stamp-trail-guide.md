Vermont's signature soft serve now comes with a passport. Per Afar, the Vermont Creemee passport, launched in April 2026, is a 20-page booklet with a companion digital directory covering 189 places to try a creemee. The booklet has pages for collecting stamps, and proceeds support Vermont nonprofits. Below, you'll find what a creemee is, how to approach the passport, and a few ways to plan a trip around it.

## What a Creemee Actually Is

A creemee is Vermont's version of soft serve. According to Afar, it's richer and higher in butterfat than the soft serve you may be used to. That is the key difference: a denser, creamier texture and a fuller dairy flavor.

The report details on specific flavors are limited, but Afar's coverage ties the creemee to maple culture, local farms, and small-town Vermont. That makes maple a natural first order if you want to taste something tied to the state.

## How the Passport Works

The setup is simple:

- **The booklet:** 20 pages, with space to collect stamps.
- **The directory:** a digital listing of 189 shops, so you can find stops near wherever you happen to be.
- **The purpose:** proceeds go to Vermont nonprofits, so your souvenir doubles as a small donation.

## Plan Your Trip Around Stamps, Not Mileage

With 189 shops across the state, trying to hit them all is unrealistic for one visit. A better approach is to pick a goal and build around it.

### Option 1: A walkable town crawl
Pick one town with several listed shops and sample on foot. Use the digital directory to see what's clustered together. Share a creemee between two people so you can taste more stops without overdoing it.

### Option 2: A one-weekend sprint
Filling the whole booklet in a weekend is a fun challenge, but treat it as a stretch goal. Group stops by region using the directory, keep portions small, and accept that a half-full passport still counts as a good trip.

### Option 3: The slow road trip
Let the passport guide your route between other things you want to see. Stop for a creemee whenever a listed shop appears. This is the easiest way to experience what Afar describes as the passport's real appeal: giving travelers a reason to visit small businesses they might otherwise drive past.

## Tips for Tasting Well

- **Start with maple.** It's the flavor most closely tied to Vermont's food identity, and it gives you a baseline for comparing shops.
- **Then try something unusual.** Order the shop's odd or seasonal flavor at a few stops to see how they differ.
- **Compare, don't rank too fast.** Texture and butterfat richness vary by shop, so keep notes in the booklet margins.
- **Talk to the staff.** Ask about local farms or ingredients. Small shops often have a story behind what they serve.

## Make Your Own Maple Creemee-Style Treat at Home

You can't replicate a commercial soft-serve machine at home, but you can get the spirit of it. This is a simple, approachable no-churn version inspired by Vermont's flavors, not an authentic creemee recipe.

**Ingredients**
- 2 cups cold heavy cream
- 1/2 cup pure maple syrup
- Pinch of salt

**Steps**
1. Whip the cream to soft peaks.
2. Fold in the maple syrup and salt gently.
3. Freeze in a shallow container for 4 to 6 hours, stirring once after the first hour.
4. Let it sit for 5 minutes at room temperature before scooping.

The high fat content of the cream gives it a rich, smooth feel, in the same spirit as a creemee's butterfat-forward style.
