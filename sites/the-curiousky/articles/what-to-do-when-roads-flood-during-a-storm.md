Thanksgiving week brought heavy rain, high winds, coastal flooding and snow to much of the country. Per HuffPost, the storms forced the postponement of Macy's 100th Thanksgiving Day Parade and caused power outages, flight delays and infrastructure damage, with impacts stretching from the East Coast to California. It all landed during one of the busiest travel periods of the year.

You can't control the sky. You can control what you do when you reach a flooded road. This guide covers the decisions that matter, in the order you'll face them.

## Decide Before You Leave

The best flood decision happens in your driveway.

- **Check the forecast for your whole route,** not just your destination. A storm can be fine at both ends and miserable in the middle.
- **Know the alerts.** A flood watch means conditions could produce flooding. A flood warning means it is happening or about to. Treat a warning as a reason to rethink the trip.
- **Build in slack.** If a delay of several hours would be a disaster, leave earlier or later, or consider changing the day you travel.
- **Charge your phone and keep a car charger handy.** Power outages, which were widespread in this storm per the report, can leave you without a place to recharge.

Holiday pressure makes people take risks they'd never take on a normal Tuesday. A late arrival at dinner is a better story than a rescue.

## When You See Water Across the Road

The standard advice from weather safety agencies is short: turn around, don't drown. It is worth understanding why it works.

### You can't judge depth by looking

Moving water looks the same whether it's three inches or three feet deep. The road underneath may be washed out, and storm water hides debris, potholes and missing pavement. Muddy water makes it worse.

### Small amounts of water can still be dangerous

Shallow water can cause a car to lose traction. Deeper, moving water can push a vehicle sideways. Rather than testing your car's limits, find another route.

### What to do instead

1. Slow down early when you see standing water or hear spray.
2. Stop well before the water, not at the edge of it.
3. Turn around and take a route you know is higher.
4. Don't drive around barricades. They are there because someone already found the problem.

## If Your Car Stalls or Water Is Rising

If the engine dies in rising water, don't try to restart it. Get out if you can do so safely and move to higher ground. If water is rising around the car and you can't leave, call emergency services and tell them where you are.

Keep a few things in the car for storm weeks: a flashlight, water, snacks, a blanket, a phone charger and any medication you need.

## Why Coastal Flooding Can Surprise You

The report points to coastal flooding and king tides as part of the story. A king tide is an especially high tide, which happens when the Sun, Moon and Earth line up so that tidal pull is stronger than usual. That is a sky connection worth knowing: the Moon's position helps set how high the water climbs.

On a normal day, a king tide may only creep into low streets. Add heavy rain and strong wind pushing water toward shore, and the same tide can flood areas that are usually dry. Rain also needs somewhere to drain, and a high tide can block storm drains from emptying.

If you live or travel near the coast, check a tide chart alongside the weather forecast. Many local weather services publish them. A storm that arrives at high tide is a different event than the same storm at low tide.

## What Storm Damage Means for Your Plans

Per HuffPost, the storms disrupted transportation and damaged infrastructure. Even after rain stops, some problems linger:

- Roads may stay closed or damaged after water recedes.
- Traffic signals may be out where power is down. Treat dark intersections as four-way stops.
- Fallen lines and trees can be hazards. Stay away from downed power lines and report them.
- Flights can stay delayed after the weather clears, since planes and crews end up out of position. Check your airline's app before heading to the airport.

## Making Sense of the Bigger Picture

The source report frames the week as part of a pattern of coastal flooding and extreme storms increasingly disrupting public events and local infrastructure. What matters for your plans is simple: storm days are no longer rare interruptions you can ignore, so it pays to have a flood plan ready before you need one.

## The One Rule to Remember

When you reach water across the road, turn around. No dinner, flight or parade is worth the gamble.
