Holiday driving is stressful mostly because of the stretches you can't control: crowded roads, packed rest areas, and a car full of people who need a break at different moments. This guide shows how to build a planned stop into your drive so the trip feels less like an endurance test. It's built on a short CBS News report on AAA's holiday travel advice, plus some practical thinking of our own.

## What AAA's holiday advice covers

Per CBS News, AAA shared holiday travel tips, Thanksgiving travel forecasts, and emerging vacation trends. That includes why cruises are expected to be a major travel choice in 2027. The report frames holiday travel as a peak stress point for families, with crowded roads and airports and changing vacation habits shaping how people plan.

The CBS News summary we have doesn't include AAA's specific forecast numbers, peak driving hours, or its full do's and don'ts list. We won't guess at them. For current figures and timing, check AAA's own travel pages and your state's transportation or traffic site before you leave.

## Why a planned stop beats an emergency stop

This part is our own advice, not AAA's. On a crowded travel day, the worst stops are the unplanned ones: the exit with one gas station and a line out the door, or the rest area you reach with a kid at the end of their patience. A stop you chose in advance changes the feel of the drive.

Planning one also gives you something to look forward to, which helps when traffic slows.

## How to pick a holiday detour

The goal is a stop that's easy to reach and easy to leave. Look for these traits:

- **Close to your route.** Aim for a stop within a few minutes of an exit. Measure the extra drive separately from the time you'll spend there.
- **Easy parking.** A small-town main street or a diner with its own lot beats anything with a tight garage.
- **A walkable stretch.** Fifteen minutes on foot does more for a stiff driver than another coffee.
- **Food that isn't a chain on the highway ramp.** A local counter or bakery can make the stop the best part of the day.
- **A fallback.** If a place is closed or full, know the next option down the road.

## Confirm the details before you commit

Hours and seasonal schedules can change around holidays, and many small businesses close on or around Thanksgiving. Before you plan a stop, check:

1. The business's official website or social page for current holiday hours.
2. Any seasonal closures at parks, overlooks, or museums.
3. Road conditions and construction through your state's traffic information service.
4. Parking details, especially for downtown stops that may be busier on holiday weekends.

If a detour fails any of these, swap it out rather than hoping for the best.

## Budget a little time, not a lot

You don't need a three-hour side trip. For most holiday drives, a 30 to 45 minute stop is enough: a few minutes of extra driving, a meal or a coffee, a short walk, and back on the road. Anything longer is worth it only if you've built slack into your schedule and aren't racing a deadline or daylight.

A useful rule: if the stop adds more than 15 minutes of driving each way, treat it as a destination, not a break.

## The bigger trend: easier-to-plan trips

CBS News reports that cruises are expected to be a major travel choice in 2027, and the underlying story seems to be a shift toward bundled, easier-to-plan vacations. That reading is ours, not AAA's, but it fits what holiday drivers want too. People are looking for fewer decisions and fewer surprises.

You can bring that same spirit to a road trip. Decide where you'll eat, where you'll stretch your legs, and where you'll stop for gas before you leave the driveway. Fewer choices on the road means less stress in the car.

## A simple holiday-drive plan

- **The night before:** Pick one primary stop and one backup near your route. Check hours.
- **Before you leave:** Check traffic and weather. Fill the tank and pack snacks and water.
- **At the halfway point:** Take your planned break, even if everyone feels fine.
- **Back on the road:** Keep a few offline playlists or podcasts ready for slow stretches.

## The takeaway

Before your next holiday drive, choose one stop, confirm its hours, and put it in your navigation app. A planned break turns the most frustrating stretch of the trip into something you actually chose.
