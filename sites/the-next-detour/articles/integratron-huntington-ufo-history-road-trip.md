The Integratron, the dome in the Mojave Desert built in the 1950s by UFO contactee George Van Tassel, is now the subject of an exhibition at the Huntington Library. That gives Southern California road-trippers something unusual: a chance to see the story in a major museum setting and, if you're up for a longer drive, the odd desert landmark itself.

This isn't a turn-by-turn guide, and some details below are things I can't confirm from my reporting. I'll flag those clearly so you know what to check before you go.

## What the story is

Per LAist, the exhibition came together through an unlikely trio: a podcaster, a historian and a pool contractor. Their collaboration brought the Integratron's UFO history to the Huntington, an institution not usually associated with desert contactee lore.

The Integratron itself is a mysterious dome structure in the Mojave Desert. Van Tassel, a UFO contactee, built it in the 1950s. That's the core of the oddity: a landmark in the middle of the desert tied to one man's claims about contact with extraterrestrials.

The LAist piece frames this as part of a broader moment, with UFO and paranormal culture getting more mainstream attention. Whether that reading convinces you or not, it's a useful lens for the visit. You're not just looking at a quirky attraction; you're seeing how a fringe piece of Americana ended up in a prestigious cultural space.

## Is it worth the detour?

**Short answer:** If you like roadside oddities and the stories behind them, yes. If you only want a quick leg-stretch, plan carefully.

The exhibition is the easier add-on if you're already in the Los Angeles area. The Integratron itself is a different commitment, because it's out in the Mojave Desert rather than on a freeway exit you'll pass by accident. Treat them as two separate decisions.

## How to sequence the two

If you can do both, see the exhibition first. The history gives the dome context: who Van Tassel was, what he claimed, and why people still care. Then the desert visit becomes more than a building in a landscape.

If you can only do one, the choice is simple. Pick the exhibition for the story. Pick the Integratron for the setting and the sense of place.

## What makes this one memorable

The appeal here is the people behind it. A podcaster, a historian and a pool contractor aren't the usual cast for a museum exhibition, and that's part of what makes the story fun. It's grassroots enthusiasm meeting a major institution.
