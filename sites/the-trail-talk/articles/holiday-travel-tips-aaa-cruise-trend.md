## Build your holiday plan around three decisions

The specifics below are our suggestions, not AAA's findings. They're common-sense ways to act on the advice to plan smarter and avoid headaches.

### 1. Decide how you're getting there

Driving gives you control over timing and what you bring. Flying can save hours on a long trip but puts you at the mercy of airport crowds. Pick one early so you can book or prepare accordingly.

### 2. Choose your departure window deliberately

Times of day and days of the week can change how a trip feels. Check AAA's forecast for the busiest windows, then leave outside them if your schedule allows. Even a few hours can matter on a crowded travel day.

### 3. Pack for the slowest version of the trip

If you're driving with kids, plan for delays: water, snacks, a charged phone, and a full tank before you hit the road. If you're flying, assume lines will be long and allow extra time.

## Make the trip about more than the drive

Here's where a trail site can add something. If you're already on the road for the holiday, a short walk can break up the day and give restless kids a place to burn energy.

A few ways to do it:

- Look for a short loop or lakeside path near your route and plan to stretch your legs there at the halfway point.
- Choose an easy, well-marked trail if you're traveling with children or older relatives.
- Check the land manager's website for closures, fees, and seasonal access before you go, since late-fall conditions can vary.
- Bring layers and sturdy shoes, even for a 30-minute walk.

An hour outside can make a long travel day feel like part of the trip instead of the price of admission.

## Mistakes worth avoiding

AAA's specific list of mistakes isn't included in the coverage we have, so treat these as general planning habits:

- **Leaving everything to the last minute.** Holiday demand is high, and last-minute choices tend to be more expensive and more stressful.
- **Skipping the buffer.** Build extra time into every leg of the trip.
- **Overpacking the schedule.** One planned stop is easier to enjoy than five.
- **Ignoring the budget until you arrive.** Set a spending limit for gas, food, and activities before you leave.
