Holiday travel is crowded, and a trip built around a hike has less room for error than most. Daylight is short in late fall and winter, and a missed connection can eat the one good morning you planned for the trail. Time Out's editors recently published 11 tried-and-true holiday travel tips for cutting stress and avoiding airport and road-trip headaches. Here's how the most useful ones translate when you're headed somewhere scenic.

Time Out's piece lists the tips as headlines, and I'm working from those. The how-to-use-it advice below is my own take, not theirs.

## Build in a buffer day (or two)

The first tip on Time Out's list is to plan a buffer day, or two, around travel. This matters even more when your destination is the point of the trip.

If your plan hinges on one hike, don't schedule it for the morning you land. Put it in the middle of the trip. Then a delayed flight or a slow drive costs you a lazy afternoon, not the whole reason you came.

## Leave at least two hours between connections

Another Time Out tip: leave at least two hours between connections. Gateway towns near national parks often sit at the end of a connecting flight or a long drive from a major airport, so one tight layover can ripple through the whole itinerary.

A two-hour gap also gives you time to sit down, eat, and reset before the next leg.

## Travel on the holiday itself, if you can

Time Out's editors suggest traveling on the actual holiday day if you can. For a trail trip, that can be a real advantage. You may start your hike a day later than everyone else, but you may also reach the trailhead after the biggest rush has cleared.

This is my read, not a guarantee. Check the official park or land-manager website for holiday hours, closures, and fees before you commit to a date.

## Schedule your ride

Time Out also recommends scheduling your ride. If you're flying into a smaller airport near a trail town, ground transportation can be thin. Book it ahead, and confirm pickup details before you leave home. If you're driving, the same idea applies to your timing: decide when you'll leave, and stick to it.

## Don't pack what you can get delivered

Another of Time Out's tips: don't pack what you can get delivered instead. On a hiking trip, bulky items are the ones that hurt. Think about what you can have waiting at your lodging or pick up in the destination town, so your bag stays light for the airport and the trail.

Some things, like broken-in boots and a layer you know fits, should never be left to chance. Bring those yourself.

## Moisturize

Time Out's list includes a simple one-word tip: moisturize. Dry cabin air is a familiar travel complaint, and the cold, dry air of a winter trail doesn't help. Keep a small moisturizer and lip balm in your day pack, not just your carry-on.

## Bring your own airport food

Two items on Time Out's list are about airport eating: bring your own food, and, in their words, "Airport charcuterie." I can't tell you exactly how the editors build theirs, but the idea fits a hiker's mindset well. Snacks you packed yourself are cheaper than terminal prices and available when your gate changes.

The same snack bag can pull double duty. Whatever you don't eat at the airport goes straight into your day pack for the trail.

## Celebrate on another day, or don't go

The last two tips on Time Out's list are the boldest: celebrate the holiday on another day, and, finally, just don't travel. These are honest options, not jokes.

If the trip you want is a hike in a quiet mountain town, a different weekend might get you a calmer trailhead, a better table at lunch, and a less frantic drive. Moving your gathering costs nothing but a conversation. Staying home is also a legitimate plan, and a local short loop near your own door can still give you the day outside.

## A short holiday trail checklist

Here's how I'd combine the tips above:

- Put the hike in the middle of the trip, not on arrival or departure day.
- Leave two hours between connections.
- Book your ride before you leave.
- Carry your own snacks, and keep a few for the trail.
- Check the official park or land-manager site for closures, permits, fees, and seasonal access.
- Ship, deliver, or buy locally anything bulky you don't need to carry.
- Keep lip balm and moisturizer in your day pack.

## Pick the plan that leaves you daylight

The best holiday travel tip for a trail trip is the one that protects your time outside. Choose the buffer, the early ride, or the different weekend, whichever gets you to the trailhead rested and with daylight to spare.
