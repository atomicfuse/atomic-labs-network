A dated bathroom in an older home doesn't have to be demolished to become useful. Per Apartment Therapy, a DIY renovation in a 1962 home turned a mismatched primary bath into a brighter, storage-rich space, and it kept some vintage flair along the way. The approach is worth borrowing: pick a few smart, budget-friendly swaps, and restore the one feature that has real character.

Below, you'll find the moves that made this project work and how to adapt them to your own bathroom. Exact dimensions and costs weren't part of the reporting, so where I suggest numbers, treat them as general guidance to check against your own space.

## Start With the Vanity: An IKEA Double Sink for Storage

The biggest storage win in this makeover was an IKEA double vanity. A double vanity gives you more than a second sink: it means more drawers or cabinet space in one footprint, and it is often the largest storage piece in the room.

If you want to try this in a smaller bathroom:

- **Measure the wall first.** Note the full width between walls, plus door swing and any radiator or window trim. Standard double vanities are generally wider than single ones, so confirm clearance before you fall in love with a model.
- **Check drawer depth.** Plumbing often cuts into drawers under sinks. Look at how the cabinet is configured so you know what will actually hold towels and toiletries.
- **Plan the plumbing.** Moving from one sink to two usually means adjusting supply and drain lines. Decide early whether that's a DIY job for you or one for a plumber.

IKEA-based vanities are a popular budget route because you can often assemble a high-end look from standard cabinet parts, though pricing varies by configuration and over time.

## Restore the Vintage Tub Instead of Replacing It

The original vintage tub stayed. That choice does two things at once: it saves the cost of removing and replacing a heavy fixture, and it gives the room an anchor of period character that new products rarely match.

The lesson for your own bathroom is to look for the one original element worth saving. In a 1960s home, that might be the tub, original tile, or a distinctive fixture. Restoring it, rather than hiding or ripping it out, lets everything new around it feel intentional rather than generic.

Before you commit, check the condition honestly. Surface wear can often be improved, but major damage may change the math. A professional refinishing quote is a smart step before you build a whole design around it.

## Choose Waterproof Flooring for Warmth

The floor in this redo is waterproof engineered hardwood. The appeal is clear: you get the warmth of a wood look in a room where moisture is constant.

When shopping for something similar:

- Confirm the product is specifically rated waterproof, not just water-resistant.
- Ask about installation requirements for wet rooms, including how edges and transitions are sealed.
- Order a sample and test it next to your vanity finish and wall color before buying.

Warm wood tones also soften the cool surfaces common in bathrooms, like porcelain, glass and metal, which is a quick way to keep a mid-century bathroom from feeling cold.

## Add Wall Paneling for Texture

The makeover also uses wall paneling. Paneling adds depth to flat, dated walls and can cover imperfect surfaces, which helps in an older home where walls may not be perfectly even.

A few practical thoughts:

- Use moisture-appropriate materials, especially near the tub and sinks.
- Keep the paneling scale in proportion to the room. In a small bath, simple, slim vertical lines usually read cleaner than a busy pattern.
- Paint or finish it in a light shade if brightness is your goal, since the stated result here was a brighter room.

## Make the Whole Room Feel Cohesive

The original bath was described as dated and mismatched. Much of the transformation comes from pulling finishes into one family. If you take only one design principle from this project, take this one: choose a limited palette and repeat it.

A simple way to do that:

1. Pick one warm material (wood-look flooring, for example).
2. Pick one light wall color.
3. Let one vintage piece, like a restored tub, be the star.
4. Echo the metal finish across faucets, lighting and hardware.

This kind of editing is free, and it does more for a space than adding more stuff.

## Budget Options for Different Wallets

This is a conceptual guide based on the project's choices, not a pricing breakdown from the original reporting.

- **Lower spend:** Keep the existing tub and refresh the walls with paint. Add storage with wall-mounted shelves or an over-toilet unit.
- **Mid-range:** Swap in an IKEA vanity and add paneling to one wall, such as behind the vanity.
- **Bigger project:** Add waterproof engineered hardwood flooring, a double vanity and full paneling, while restoring the original tub.

Start with whichever step solves your biggest annoyance. If you're constantly short on counter and cabinet space, the vanity comes first. If the room just feels dingy, light walls and better flooring will show up fastest.

## Your Next Step

Walk into your bathroom with a tape measure and a notepad. Write down the wall width available for a vanity, then decide which single original feature deserves saving. Those two decisions will shape every other choice.
