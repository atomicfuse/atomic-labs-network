BuzzFeed Shopping recently rounded up 28 affordable Target finds meant to help you upgrade your bedroom without overspending. The published list doesn't give us item names or prices to work from, so this isn't a rundown of those products. Instead, here is the order of operations that makes a budget bedroom refresh actually look finished, and how to use a store like Target to carry it out.

By the end, you'll have a four-step plan, a spending split for different budgets, and a few measurements to check before you add anything to your cart.

*A note on labels: the budget splits and sizing numbers below are a conceptual plan, not a makeover I've photographed or a price list from the BuzzFeed roundup. Check current prices before you plan around them.*

## Start With Lighting, Because It Changes Everything

Overhead light is the biggest reason bedrooms feel like bedrooms-in-a-rental. Fix it first.

- **Add at least two lamp sources at different heights.** A table lamp on a nightstand plus a floor lamp in a corner is the classic pairing.
- **Choose warm bulbs.** Look for 2700K on the box. Cooler bulbs make a bedroom feel like an office.
- **Keep lampshades light and fabric-based.** They diffuse light instead of casting a hard cone.
- **Put one lamp on a dimmer or smart plug** so you can lower the lights in the evening without rewiring anything.

If you can only spend money on one thing, spend it here. A $0 rearrangement doesn't beat good lamps.

## Upgrade the Bedding Next

The bed is roughly the largest visual surface in the room, so it sets the tone.

### Build the bed in layers

1. A fitted sheet and flat sheet (or just a good duvet cover).
2. A duvet or comforter in a solid, calm color.
3. A textured blanket folded across the bottom third of the bed, about 15 to 20 inches deep.
4. Two sleeping pillows plus two or three decorative ones. Mixing 20-inch and 18-inch squares looks intentional.

### Pick a simple palette

Stick to two main colors plus one accent. For example: soft white and warm gray, with a terracotta or sage accent in the pillows. Texture (linen-look, waffle, knit) does more work than pattern on a small budget.

## Solve Storage Before You Decorate

Clutter undoes even great bedding. Before buying decor, look for what's making the room feel busy.

- **Under the bed:** measure the clearance first. Many frames leave 6 to 12 inches, which determines whether you need low, flat bins or can fit taller ones.
- **Closet:** matching slim hangers and a shelf divider or two make a closet look calmer, even if the contents are the same.
- **Nightstand:** a lidded basket or a small drawer unit hides chargers and random items.
- **Vertical space:** a tall narrow shelf or over-the-door hooks free up floor space in a small room.

Open floor area is what makes a small bedroom feel bigger, so storage that goes up or under is worth more than storage that sits out.

## Finish With Small Decor Swaps

Once lighting, bedding, and storage are handled, add personality. This is the cheapest layer and the most fun.

- **Frame what you already own.** Prints, postcards, or photos in matching frames read as a gallery wall.
- **Hang art at the right height.** Center it about 57 inches from the floor, and keep it 6 to 8 inches above the headboard or furniture below.
- **Add a rug.** In a bedroom, it should extend at least 18 to 24 inches beyond each side of the bed so your feet land on it.
- **Bring in something living.** A plant or a faux stem in a simple vase softens the room.
- **Swap curtains.** Hang the rod a few inches below the ceiling and let panels reach the floor to make walls look taller.

## How to Split Your Budget

These are rough proportions, not prices. Scale them to whatever you have.

### If you have a very small budget

Put most of it into bulbs and one lamp, then a duvet cover or throw. Reuse everything else. Move your bed, nightstand, and dresser to see whether a new layout opens up the room.

### If you have a moderate budget

Split it roughly 35 percent lighting, 35 percent bedding, 20 percent storage, 10 percent decor. That covers the four steps without leaving a gap.

### If you have room to spend

Add a rug and curtains, which are the pieces that most change how finished a room looks, and invest in better pillows.

## Renter-Friendly Tips

BuzzFeed's roundup is aimed at people trying to upgrade without overspending, and Target is a common stop for renters, students, and first-apartment shoppers. If that's you, lean on things that leave no trace: plug-in lamps, removable hooks, freestanding shelves, curtains on tension rods, and rugs. Keep the packaging and receipts for anything you're unsure about.

## Before You Check Out

Walk through the room with a tape measure. Note your bed size, the space under the bed, the wall width above the headboard, and the window width. Then shop with those numbers in your phone. Most budget decorating regrets come from items that are the wrong size, not the wrong style.
