A living room rarely looks unfinished because of the sofa. It looks unfinished because of the layer on top: bare surfaces, a rug that's too small, walls with nothing personal on them. This guide shows you how to fix that with small, affordable pieces, and how to place each one so the room reads as designed rather than assembled.

The starting point is a roundup from Real Simple, in which an interior designer shared 11 of her picks for living room decor available at Amazon, with prices starting at just $9. The published summary names several categories: coffee table books, coasters, area rugs, side tables and picture frames, plus other accessories. I can't vouch for the full list or specific products, so I'm not going to name items or prices beyond that. What I can do is show you how to use each category well. The placement advice below is general styling guidance, not the designer's words.

## Start With the Rug, Because It Sets the Scale

If you spend the most on one item, make it the area rug. A rug that is too small makes furniture look like it's floating and makes the room feel smaller.

A common rule of thumb in styling:

- **Front legs on:** At minimum, the front legs of your sofa and chairs should sit on the rug.
- **Common sizes:** 5x8 feet suits a small seating area, and 8x10 feet suits most average living rooms.
- **Leave a border:** Aim for roughly 12 to 18 inches of bare floor between the rug's edge and the walls in a larger room.

Measure your floor with painter's tape before you buy. Tape out the rug's outline and live with it for a day.

**Budget tip:** Look for flat-weave or low-pile styles if you have a robot vacuum or move furniture often. A rug pad (even a basic one) keeps it from creeping and adds cushion.

## Use Coffee Table Books as Instant Styling Props

Coffee table books are one of the cheapest ways to add height, color and personality. They also give you something to build on.

### How to stack them

1. Choose two or three books in a mix of sizes, with the largest at the bottom.
2. Pick spines or covers that fit your palette. If your room is neutral, one book with a bold cover becomes your accent.
3. Top the stack with a small object: a candle, a small dish, a little bowl or a sprig of greenery in a vase.

A stack about 4 to 8 inches tall works well on a standard coffee table. Taller than that and it starts to block conversation across the table.

Choose subjects you'd actually pick up: travel, food, art, or your favorite hobby. A styled room looks best when it hints at who lives there.

## Don't Skip Coasters

Coasters sound minor, but they do two jobs. They protect wood and veneer from rings, and a good set makes the coffee table look intentionally finished.

- **Material matters:** Cork and stone absorb moisture well. Wood and leather look warm. Metal-edged sets look sleeker.
- **Match your finish:** Pair warm wood coasters with brass or black accents, or choose a neutral stone look for a cooler room.
- **Keep them visible:** Stack four in a small tray or bowl on the table so guests know where to put a glass.

This is also one of the lowest-cost swaps on the list, so it's an easy place to start.

## Add a Side Table for Function and Balance

A sofa with nothing beside it feels lopsided, and you end up balancing a drink on an armrest. A side table fixes both.

### Getting the size right

- **Height:** Aim for a table about the same height as your sofa arm, or a couple of inches lower.
- **Width:** For a small space, 14 to 18 inches in diameter or width is usually plenty.
- **Shape:** Round tables are friendlier in tight rooms because there are no corners to bump.

If you're short on floor space, a C-shaped table that slides over the sofa edge gives you a surface without taking up any footprint. Whatever you choose, make sure the material fits what you already own. Mixing one wood tone with one metal finish reads as curated. Matching everything exactly can look flat.

## Fill Your Walls With Picture Frames

Picture frames are the cheapest way to make a room feel personal. Printed photos, kids' drawings, postcards, or a map of a place you love all become art when they're framed well.

### A simple gallery layout

1. Lay your frames on the floor first and arrange them until the grouping feels balanced.
2. Trace each frame on paper, cut the shapes out, and tape them to the wall to test spacing.
3. Keep gaps consistent, about 2 to 3 inches between frames.
4. Center the group at roughly 57 to 60 inches from the floor, which is standard gallery eye level. If it hangs over a sofa, keep the bottom edge about 6 to 10 inches above the back cushions.

**Renter tip:** Adhesive picture strips avoid nail holes. Check the weight rating on the package and follow it. You can also lean larger frames on a shelf or console instead of hanging them.

Using frames in one finish, such as all black or all natural wood, makes a mixed collection of images look cohesive.

## Pull It Together With a Simple Palette

The reason designers can make a budget-friendly room look expensive is usually restraint. Choose three colors and repeat them:

- **A base neutral** (cream, warm gray, white) for the rug and walls.
- **A secondary tone** (wood, tan, deep green) for furniture and frames.
- **One accent** (rust, navy, mustard) for the book cover, a pillow, or a vase.

When the same colors show up in at least three places around the room, the eye reads it as intentional. Add a lamp with a warm bulb, around 2700K, to make the whole arrangement look softer in the evening.

## A Realistic Way to Spend Your Money

You don't have to buy everything at once. Here's a sensible order, which is my suggestion rather than anything from the original list:

1. **Lowest spend:** Coasters and a couple of coffee table books, starting at the sort of price point Real Simple's roundup mentions.
2. **Mid spend:** Picture frames and a side table.
3. **Biggest spend:** The area rug, since it changes the room the most.

Before you click "buy" on anything, check dimensions, material descriptions and return policies. Online photos can hide scale, and a table that looks substantial may be smaller in person.

## Start With One Surface

Pick the coffee table. Clear it, add a tray, a short stack of books, a few coasters and one small object. When that surface looks right, you'll know what the rest of the room needs next.
