A light blue boy's bedroom featured by Apartment Therapy looks polished and lasting for one main reason: DIY box trim and molding. The piece shows how a simple architectural detail can make a child's room feel more elevated and more enduring. Below is a practical plan for borrowing that idea, including how to lay it out, what to buy at different budgets, and how to keep the room from feeling dated in three years.

A note on what follows: the original story's details are limited to the light blue color, the DIY box trim and the molding. The measurements and steps below are general guidance and a conceptual plan, not a recap of that room's exact specs. Adjust them to your walls.

## Why molding works in a kid's room

Playful rooms often age out fast. Themed bedding, character decor and bright accents all get swapped as a child grows. Molding doesn't. It's part of the walls, so it gives the room a classic structure that stays put while everything else changes.

That's the appeal of the room Apartment Therapy highlighted: a soft color paired with traditional detailing. The child's personality can live in the bedding, art and shelves, while the walls stay calm and grown-up enough to last.

## Why light blue is a smart base color

Light blue reads as fresh and gentle without being tied to a theme. Because it's a soft, familiar color, it pairs easily with white trim, natural wood, navy, gray or warm accents. When the boy's interests shift from dinosaurs to sports to music, you can change the accessories without repainting.

Two practical tips:

- Test swatches on the wall and look at them morning and evening. Blues can shift toward gray or green depending on your light.
- Painting the trim the same color as the wall gives a subtle, tone-on-tone look. Painting it white gives a crisper contrast. Both work. Choose based on how much definition you want.

## How to plan box molding (conceptual layout)

Box molding is a set of rectangles made from thin trim and mounted on the wall. Here's a general approach you can adapt.

### Step 1: Measure your wall

Measure the wall's width and height, and note windows, doors and outlets. Sketch it on paper. Many people place boxes in the lower two-thirds of the wall, but you can also go floor to ceiling on a single accent wall, such as behind the bed.

### Step 2: Decide on size and spacing

A common starting point is equal spacing between boxes, roughly 3 to 4 inches, and equal margins at the edges. Divide your usable width by the number of boxes you want, subtract the gaps, and you have each box's width. Adjust until the numbers feel balanced. Odd numbers of boxes often look pleasing, but follow what fits your wall.

### Step 3: Mark with painter's tape first

Before cutting anything, tape the layout on the wall and step back. Check it from the doorway and from the bed. Move the tape until it looks right. This costs nothing and prevents most mistakes.

### Step 4: Cut, attach and finish

- Cut the trim with a miter saw, or use a hand miter box for a lower-cost option. Corners are typically cut at 45 degrees.
- Attach pieces with construction adhesive and a brad nailer, or small finishing nails driven by hand.
- Fill nail holes with wood filler and sand lightly.
- Run a thin bead of paintable caulk along the edges where trim meets wall. This is what makes the result look built-in rather than stuck on.
- Paint with a satin or semi-gloss finish, which wipes clean more easily in a kid's room.

## Budget options

Box molding is popular because you can scale it to your budget.

### Lowest cost

Use thin, lightweight primed trim (such as MDF or similar) in a single accent wall behind the bed. Hand-cut the miters with a basic miter box. Your main purchases are trim, adhesive, caulk, filler and paint.

### Mid-range

Cover two or three walls, or run the boxes around the whole room at a consistent height. Renting or borrowing a miter saw and nailer speeds things up and gives cleaner joints.

### Higher cost

Choose thicker, more detailed profiles, or add a chair rail, picture rail or crown molding to complete the look. Hiring a carpenter for the cuts while you handle the painting is a middle path.

Prices vary by region and material, so price your own wall's total linear footage before you commit.

## Make the room grow with your child

The molding is the long-term investment. Everything else can be flexible:

- **Bedding:** Keep it simple and swap it out as tastes change.
- **Art:** Hang framed pieces inside some of the boxes for a gallery effect, then replace the prints over time.
- **Lighting:** Choose a simple lamp or sconce in a neutral finish rather than a themed fixture.
- **Furniture:** Pick pieces in wood or white that won't look babyish later.

## Small room? Keep it light

In a small bedroom, stick to slim trim and keep the color palette tight. Painting the trim and wall the same light blue keeps the walls from feeling chopped up, while still adding depth through shadow lines. A single accent wall can deliver the effect without crowding the space.

## The takeaway

Tape your layout on the wall this weekend. If it looks good in painter's tape, you already know the molding will look good in paint.
