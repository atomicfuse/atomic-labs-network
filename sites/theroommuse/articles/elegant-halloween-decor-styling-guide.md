Halloween decor doesn't have to mean plastic skeletons and orange streamers. House Beautiful recently rounded up 20 elegant pieces, mostly from Pottery Barn and a few other retailers, that lean spooky without looking cheap. Here is how to borrow the thinking behind that list and build a Halloween look you'll be glad to see in your home, whatever you spend.

The pieces below come from that list. Prices and exact measurements weren't included in the roundup, so check retailer pages before you buy. Where I suggest budget swaps or layouts, those are my own conceptual ideas, not tested makeovers.

## The Rule Behind the List: Pick Objects, Not Costumes

Look at what House Beautiful chose: an antique brass spider-web mirror, a smoked amber glass candle holder, linen napkins with an oak vine design, a light blue ceramic pumpkin planter. Most of these read as nice home goods first and Halloween second. That's the whole trick. A piece that works in a normal room on November 1st is not tacky.

When you shop or thrift, ask one question: would I keep this out if you removed the holiday? If yes, buy it.

## Start With Materials and Color

The list leans on brass, gold, amber glass, linen, ceramic, enameled cast iron and bouclé. Those are the materials of a well-dressed home, which is why the spooky motifs feel grown up.

**A simple palette to copy:**
- Warm metals: brass and gold (the spider-web mirror, the sculptural golden snake, the gold-decaled "Hex Mix" snack bowl)
- Smoky glass: amber candle holders
- Soft textures: bouclé and linen (Gus the Ghost pillow with a bouclé pumpkin, oak vine napkins)
- One unexpected color: light blue (the ceramic pumpkin planter)

Keep orange to a minimum, and let the metals and textures do the work.

## Walls and Entryways: Where Mirrors and Hooks Earn Their Place

The list includes two mirrors: the antique brass spider-web mirror and a snake-wrapped mirror inspired by Nagini from Harry Potter. It also features a serpentine wall hook and a woodland critter doormat.

**Try this in a small entry:**
1. Hang one mirror at eye level near the door. Mirrors bounce light and make tight spaces feel larger, which is useful when you add seasonal objects.
2. Place the wall hook beside it for a coat or bag, so the decor is functional.
3. Lay the doormat outside. It's a low-commitment way to signal the season.

**Budget option (conceptual):** Hang a thrifted brass or gold-framed mirror and drape a bit of faux ivy or a spray-painted gold plastic snake over one corner. Secure it with removable adhesive.

## Lighting: Candles Do the Heavy Lifting

Two items are all about glow: the smoked amber glass candle holder and the hanging taper candles, a Hogwarts-inspired idea. Candlelight is the fastest way to make a room feel moody without adding clutter.

- Group candles at different heights on a tray or mantel.
- Use amber glass to warm the light.
- If you have kids, pets or a rental with strict rules, swap in flameless tapers. The look carries over.

In a small living room, one cluster on a console or side table is enough. More than that starts to feel like a set.

## Build a Halloween Tablescape

The food and drink side of the list is the deepest, which makes it ideal for a dinner party or a simple weeknight upgrade. It includes:

- Linen napkins with an oak vine design
- Ghost-shaped pinch bowls
- A skull charcuterie board
- A skeleton serving collection with wine glasses
- Scorpion-topped cocktail picks
- Autumn leaf inlay coasters
- A lidded snack bowl and the gold-decaled "Hex Mix" snack bowl
- Pumpkin-shaped enameled cast iron cocottes

### A layered table in four steps
1. **Base:** Lay a plain neutral runner or leave the wood bare.
2. **Serving pieces:** Put the charcuterie board in the center with pinch bowls for olives, nuts or salt.
3. **Place settings:** Fold the linen napkins and set glasses at each seat.
4. **Finishing touches:** Add candles and a sculptural object, such as the golden snake, as a centerpiece.

You don't need all of it. Two or three statement pieces against plain white dishes looks more intentional than a full themed set.

**Budget option (conceptual):** Use your own white plates, thrifted linen napkins, and a wooden board you already own. Add a single novelty piece, like skull-topped picks, and let the rest stay neutral.

## Soft Touches: Pillows and Planters

The Gus the Ghost pillow with its bouclé pumpkin and the light blue ceramic pumpkin planter are the easiest pieces to blend into a living room. Put the pillow on a sofa or chair with solid-color cushions around it. Fill the planter with a small plant or dried stems.

Because both are everyday objects, the planter can stay out through fall, and the pillow can ride along until the weather turns.

## Smaller Accents for Shelves and Corners

A few more items from the list work well on a bookshelf, windowsill or side table:

- **Antique brushstroke birdcages:** A pair on a shelf adds height and a vintage feel.
- **Ghost incense smoker:** A small, giftable accent for a desk or entry table.
- **Sculptural golden snake:** Coil it around a stack of books or a candlestick.

In small spaces, choose one focal point per surface rather than filling every shelf.

## Make It Last Beyond October

The reason this approach is practical is that so many of these items have a second life. Brass, amber glass, linen and ceramic don't look out of place in November. Cast iron cocottes are useful all year, even if they're shaped like pumpkins.

When you shop, sort items into two groups: **keepers** (mirrors, planters, cookware, glassware) and **one-season** (themed picks, novelty bowls). Spend more on the first group and less on the second.

## A Quick Budget Plan

- **Under a small budget:** Thrift a brass mirror or candle holder, add flameless tapers, and swap in one themed accessory.
- **Mid-range:** Buy one or two keepers, such as a planter or a set of linen napkins, and pair them with items you own.
- **Splurge:** Choose the cast iron cocottes or a statement mirror, since they'll serve you for years.

## Your Next Step

Before you buy anything, walk through your home and pick one surface: an entry table, a mantel or your dining table. Style only that, using one mirror or candle, one textured piece and one spooky detail. If it still looks good in a week, you've found your Halloween look.
