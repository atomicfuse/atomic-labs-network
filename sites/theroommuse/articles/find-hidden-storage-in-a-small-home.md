BuzzFeed Shopping recently rounded up 31 organization products meant to help readers build a clutter-free home. Its pitch is that if you think your home "isn't big enough," some of those products can help you use space you didn't realize you had. The full product list isn't something I can walk you through here, so I'll do something more useful: show you how to find that unused space yourself, and what kind of storage to match to it at different budgets.

The examples below are conceptual setups, not tested makeovers. Measure your own spaces before you buy anything.

## Start by Hunting for Dead Space, Not Products

Most clutter problems aren't a shortage of square footage. They're a shortage of *usable* square footage. Walk through your home with a tape measure and a notes app, and look for these five spots:

- **The vertical gap:** the 12 to 24 inches between the top of your wardrobe or bookcase and the ceiling.
- **The back of every door:** usually 1 to 4 inches of clearance before it hits a wall or frame.
- **The underside of furniture:** beds, sofas, and consoles often hide 6 to 18 inches of height.
- **The wall beside the fridge or washer:** a gap of just 4 to 8 inches can hold a slim rolling unit.
- **The inside of cabinets:** tall cabinets with a single shelf waste most of their height.

Write down width, depth, and height for each. Those three numbers will save you from the most common organizing mistake: buying a lovely bin that doesn't fit.

## Room-by-Room Ideas for Each Dead Zone

### Entryway and Closets

A closet with one rod and one shelf is usually under-used. Add a second, lower rod for shirts and folded pants, which can nearly double hanging capacity in the section. Use matching hangers, since slim uniform ones take up less width and make the whole space look calmer.

- **Low budget:** over-the-door hooks and a set of matching slim hangers.
- **Mid budget:** stackable shelf dividers and fabric bins sized to your shelf depth.
- **Higher budget:** an adjustable closet system you can reconfigure as your needs change.

### Kitchen

Kitchens hide the most wasted height. Measure the space between shelves and the item you're storing, then add a riser or a second tier.

- **Low budget:** tension rods under the sink for spray bottles, plus a bin for sponges.
- **Mid budget:** shelf risers for plates and mugs, and turntables for corner cabinets.
- **Higher budget:** pull-out drawers installed in lower cabinets so you stop crawling to reach the back.

### Bedroom

The bed is your biggest storage opportunity. Check clearance under the frame first. If you have 6 inches or more, shallow rolling bins work well for off-season clothes and linens. If you have less, bed risers can add a few inches, as long as your frame is stable and rated for it.

For a more finished look, choose bins in one or two colors that echo your bedding, so stored items read as part of the design instead of as clutter in hiding.

### Bathroom

Small bathrooms benefit from going up. Mount shelves above the toilet, but keep the lowest shelf at least 8 to 10 inches above the tank so it doesn't feel cramped. Use clear or neutral containers for cotton swabs and spare items so the shelf looks styled instead of stuffed.

### Living Room

Living rooms collect cords, remotes, throws, and kids' items. Choose furniture that does two jobs: a lidded ottoman, a console with baskets, or a bench with a hinged seat. Woven baskets add texture and warmth while swallowing the mess, which is the point of storage that looks good.

## Match Material to the Job

When you shop, material matters as much as size.

- **Clear plastic:** best for pantry items, craft supplies, and anything you need to see at a glance.
- **Fabric bins:** soft, lightweight, and forgiving on shelves. Look for ones with a stiff structure so they don't sag.
- **Woven baskets (seagrass, rattan, or wire):** best where the storage is visible, since they double as decor.
- **Metal wire:** durable and breathable. Good for garages, under-sink areas, and laundry rooms.

## Keep It Looking Calm

Storage can add visual noise if every container is different. Pick a simple palette, such as white, natural wood, and one accent color, and repeat it across rooms. Good lighting helps too: a small plug-in or battery-powered light inside a dark closet makes the space feel organized and easier to maintain.

## A Realistic Way to Start

Don't try to do the whole house in a weekend. Pick one room, spend ten minutes measuring, remove what you no longer use, and only then buy storage for what's left. Buy the cheapest fix first. If it works, you've solved the problem for a few dollars. If it doesn't, you'll know exactly what to upgrade.

Measure the one spot that annoys you most today, and start there.
