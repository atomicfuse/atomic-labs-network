Designer Ken Fulk has released a jungle wallcovering called "Monkey Business" for Perennials' fall collection, per Elle Decor. It features spider monkeys, toucans, and butterflies printed on sisal grass cloth. Fulk envisions it as a statement piece for dining rooms and other bold design moments, according to the report.

You probably aren't ordering luxury grass cloth this week. You can still borrow the thinking behind it. Below is how to build a jungle-fantasy wall in your own home, with options from low-cost to splurge. The room plans here are conceptual suggestions, not tested makeovers.

## Start With the Room That Can Handle It

Fulk's pitch, as reported, points to the dining room, and that's smart. You spend limited, social time there, so a dramatic wall feels like an event rather than something you live with all day. Powder rooms, entryways, and a home office wall behind your desk work for the same reason.

If you're short on space, pick one wall only. In a small room, the wall you see first when you walk in is usually the best candidate. Leave the others in a calm color.

## Understand What Makes This Wallpaper Different

The material matters as much as the monkeys. "Monkey Business" is printed on sisal grass cloth, per Elle Decor. That means the wall has natural texture and a woven, slightly irregular look, not a flat, glossy finish.

You can copy that idea in a few ways:

- **Splurge:** natural grass cloth or sisal wallcovering from a design-trade brand.
- **Mid-range:** a textured vinyl or grass-cloth-look wallpaper with a printed animal or botanical pattern.
- **Budget:** smooth peel-and-stick paper, with texture added through baskets, rattan, and woven shades nearby.

Before buying any natural-fiber covering, ask the maker about installation and care. Natural textures can be fussier than vinyl, especially near cooking or splashes. That's a good reason to keep them out of kitchens and tight spaces with lots of moisture.

## Build a Palette Around the Print

A busy wall needs calm companions. Pull two or three colors from the pattern and repeat them, rather than adding new ones.

1. **Pick the dominant color** in the wallpaper (often a leafy green or deep tone) and match your largest piece to it, such as curtains or an upholstered chair.
2. **Pick one accent** from the animals or flowers, like a toucan-beak orange or butterfly yellow, and repeat it in three small items: a vase, a pillow, a candle.
3. **Add a neutral** like warm white, natural wood, or woven fiber to give the eye a rest.

If you've got any doubt, paint the adjoining walls and trim a quiet shade drawn from the paper's background. It keeps the print looking intentional.

## Keep Furniture Simple

With a wall this lively, choose furniture with clean lines. In a dining room, a plain wood table and simple chairs let the pattern lead. Rattan, cane, and wood suit the natural, tropical mood without competing.

Layout tips for small rooms:

- Place the table or sofa against or facing the papered wall, so the pattern is the backdrop to the room's main activity.
- Keep tall, bulky storage off that wall. Covering a pattern with a hutch hides your investment.
- Leave about 36 inches of walkway around a dining table so the room still feels open next to a busy wall.

## Light It Like a Feature

Pattern needs light to show. A few options by budget:

- **Low cost:** a plug-in wall sconce on each side of the wall, or a warm bulb (around 2700K) in the existing fixture.
- **Mid-range:** a picture light over a framed mirror hung on the wall, which also bounces light around.
- **Splurge:** a statement pendant over the table, hung roughly 30 to 36 inches above the tabletop.

Warm light makes natural textures and greens look rich rather than flat.

## Want the Look Without Luxury Wallpaper?

You can get the jungle effect for much less:

- **Peel-and-stick panels** in a tropical or animal print. Apply to one wall and you can remove them when you're done, good for renters.
- **Wallpaper just inside a bookcase or behind open shelves**, using a single roll for a big impact.
- **Framed wallpaper samples or fabric** hung as a grouped gallery, so you try the print before committing.
- **A painted deep-green wall** with a few botanical prints and plants. No monkeys, but the same cozy canopy feeling.

Measure your wall height and width, multiply, and add roughly 10 to 15 percent for pattern matching and waste. Check the roll coverage on the product you choose, since it varies by maker.

## Why a Bold Wall Works Right Now

Elle Decor's report frames the design as part of a wider maximalist, nature-inspired mood, with people wanting personality and escapism at home. You don't need to follow a trend to benefit. A single expressive wall tells guests something about you, which minimal rooms often don't.

## Your Next Step

Order a sample, tape it to the wall you're considering, and live with it for three days in morning and evening light. If you still smile when you walk in, you've found your jungle.
