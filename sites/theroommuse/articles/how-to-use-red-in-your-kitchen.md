If your kitchen is a sea of white or greige and you feel nothing when you walk in, red may be the fix. Per House Beautiful, designers are moving away from neutral kitchens and embracing red tones, from burgundy to brick, as a bold, warm, personality-driven choice. Below is how to try it, from a full commitment to a weekend experiment.

A note on what's here: House Beautiful's reporting gives the direction (which shades, what to pair them with). The budget tiers and sample steps below are my own conceptual suggestions, not a documented makeover, and I've left out specific paint names and measurements because I can't verify them from the reporting.

## Why red, and why now

The reasoning is simple. Homeowners are tiring of safe, all-white interiors, and richer, more expressive choices are gaining traction. A red kitchen fits a broader shift toward homes that feel personal, social, and emotionally energizing. The kitchen is where people gather, so a color with warmth and energy makes sense there.

## Think "red tones," not just one red

The trend isn't fire-engine red everywhere. House Beautiful describes a range from burgundy to brick. That range is your friend, because it lets you choose by mood:

- **Burgundy:** deeper and moodier, a good fit if you like a cozy, enclosed feel.
- **Brick:** earthier and warmer, easier to pair with wood and natural materials.

The shade you choose matters more than the decision to use red at all. Pick the one that matches how you want to feel while cooking, not just what looks good in a photo.

## Test the shade before you commit

This is my advice, not a reported rule, but it's the step that saves the most regret. Red shifts a lot with light.

1. Paint two or three large swatches (roughly 2 feet by 2 feet) on different walls, or on poster board you can move around.
2. Look at them in morning light, afternoon light, and at night under your actual kitchen bulbs.
3. Live with them for a few days. The one you still like on day four is the winner.

Warm bulbs will push red toward cozy; cooler bulbs can make it feel harsher. Check yours before you decide.

## Balance red with neutrals, texture, and natural materials

The practical guidance in the reporting is that red works best when it's balanced rather than allowed to take over. Three levers do that:

### Neutrals

Let walls, countertops, or backsplash stay quiet. If the cabinets are red, a calmer surface nearby gives the eye somewhere to rest.

### Texture

Mix finishes so the color doesn't feel flat: a matte cabinet next to a glossy tile, or a woven stool beside a smooth counter. Texture makes a bold color feel considered instead of loud.

### Natural materials

Wood, stone, and similar materials soften red and make it feel timeless. A wood butcher-block counter or open wood shelves against brick-toned cabinets is an easy pairing to picture.

## Three ways to try it, by budget

These tiers are conceptual ideas for applying the trend, not a documented project.

### Low commitment: under a small spend

- Add a red or burgundy runner, dish towels, or a ceramic bowl.
- Swap in a red-toned piece of art or a single stool.
- If it feels right after a month, you know you can go bigger.

### Medium commitment: a weekend project

- Paint one element: an island, a pantry door, or a lower run of cabinets.
- Keep everything around it neutral so the red reads as a deliberate accent.
- Use a durable paint made for cabinets and prep surfaces properly (clean, degloss, prime) so the finish lasts.

### Full commitment: a real revamp

- Go for red or brick cabinets, balanced with neutral walls and natural materials like wood or stone.
- Layer in texture through hardware, lighting, and textiles.
- Test the shade at scale first, as above.

## Small kitchens can do this too

Red can feel heavy in a tight space, so restraint helps. Try red on just the lower cabinets and keep uppers light, or paint a single wall. Keeping the rest neutral prevents the room from closing in, and natural wood elements keep it warm rather than boxed in.

## Is the trend timeless?

Hard to say for any color trend, but red tones like brick and burgundy have an advantage: they read as warm and classic rather than novelty. Choosing a shade you love, and pairing it with neutrals and natural materials, is your best protection if tastes shift.

## Your next step

Before you buy a gallon, paint one big swatch of burgundy and one of brick, and watch them for three days under your own kitchen lighting. Whichever you miss when you walk past, that's your color.
