Buzzfeed recently rounded up 36 products meant to make a home warmer, softer, and cozier before winter arrives. The full list isn't reproduced here, so this guide skips the product picks and gives you the method behind them: a handful of changes, in order of impact, that make a room feel warm without a redesign.

The measurements and material suggestions below are general decorating guidance, not tied to any specific product from that roundup. Pick the tier that fits your budget and stop whenever the room feels right.

## Start with what your feet and hands touch

Cozy is mostly a tactile feeling. Before you buy a single decorative object, deal with the surfaces you actually touch.

### Rugs

Bare floors read cold, even when the room is heated. A rug should be big enough that at least the front legs of your sofa and chairs sit on it. In a living room, 5x8 feet is the practical minimum for a small seating area, and 8x10 feet suits a larger one. In a bedroom, let a rug extend about 18 to 24 inches beyond the sides and foot of the bed so your first step out of bed lands on something soft.

- **Low budget:** Layer a small washable rug over what you already have, or put a runner at the bedside.
- **Mid budget:** Choose wool or a wool blend. Wool is dense, springy, and naturally warm underfoot.
- **Splurge:** Add a rug pad. A felt-and-rubber pad adds cushioning and keeps the rug from sliding.

### Throws and bedding

Keep two or three throws within arm's reach of your main seat. Heavier knits, fleece, flannel, and faux fur all work. On the bed, swap to flannel or brushed cotton sheets and add a quilt or duvet you can fold at the foot. The folded layer looks inviting and gives you an extra blanket in the night.

## Fix the drafts first

A cozy room that leaks cold air will never feel cozy. This is the cheapest improvement on the list.

- **Door draft stopper:** A fabric tube or adhesive door sweep, usually a few dollars, blocks the gap at the bottom of an exterior door.
- **Window film or weatherstripping:** Seal visible gaps around window frames. Adhesive foam tape is renter-friendly.
- **Curtains:** Lined or thermal curtains help buffer window chill. Hang the rod 4 to 6 inches above the window frame and let the panels reach the floor or just skim it. The extra height also makes ceilings feel taller.

If a window is noticeably cold when you hold your hand near it, start there.

## Rework your lighting

Harsh overhead light is the fastest way to make a room feel like an office. Winter, with its early dark, is when this matters most.

### Aim for three light sources

In each main room, try to have at least three sources at different heights: a floor lamp, a table lamp, and something small like a candle or string light. Turn the overhead off in the evening.

### Check the bulb

Look for bulbs labeled around 2700K, which is the warm white range. Cooler bulbs (4000K and up) look blue-white and feel clinical. Dimmable bulbs or a plug-in dimmer let you drop the brightness at night.

- **Low budget:** Swap the bulbs in lamps you already own.
- **Mid budget:** Add one plug-in dimmer and a fabric-shade lamp, since fabric diffuses the glow.
- **Splurge:** Smart bulbs that you can schedule to dim at sunset.

## Pick a warm color palette without repainting

You don't need paint. Shift the palette through textiles and accessories.

- **Anchor with warm neutrals:** Cream, oatmeal, camel, and soft taupe feel warmer than stark white or cool gray.
- **Add one deep accent:** Rust, forest green, burgundy, or navy in pillows or a throw gives depth.
- **Mix textures instead of colors:** Chunky knit, corduroy, velvet, linen, and wool in the same palette look rich and keep the room from feeling flat.

A simple rule: choose three colors and repeat each at least twice around the room.

## Make a small living room feel snug, not cramped

In a small space, coziness comes from grouping, not from adding more stuff.

1. **Pull the sofa off the wall** by a few inches if you can, or angle a chair toward it to form a conversation corner.
2. **Keep 30 to 36 inches** of walking path through the room.
3. **Put a side table within reach** of every seat, so a mug always has a place.
4. **Use a pouf or ottoman** as extra seating and a footrest. Choose one with storage to hide blankets.
5. **Go vertical** with wall shelves or a tall lamp, leaving the floor open.

## Build a night-in corner

You can make one chair into your winter spot with five things:

- A comfortable chair or the end of the sofa
- A throw and a pillow
- A lamp with a warm bulb
- A small table for a drink or book
- A basket for blankets and reading material

This works in a bedroom corner too. It's a small footprint with a big payoff.

## Add scent and greenery sparingly

A candle or diffuser adds atmosphere. Warm notes like vanilla, cedar, and cinnamon suit the season. Keep it to one or two scents per room so they don't compete. Branches, dried stems, or a few hardy houseplants soften hard edges and keep the room from feeling bare once the holiday clutter is gone.

## A simple weekend plan

- **Saturday morning:** Seal drafts and hang curtains.
- **Saturday afternoon:** Change bulbs, rearrange lamps, and set up your cozy corner.
- **Sunday:** Lay the rug, layer bedding, and add throws and pillows.

## Your next step

Walk through your home tonight with a cold hand and a critical eye. Find the one spot that feels coldest or harshest, whether it's a drafty door or a glaring ceiling light, and fix that first. Everything else is a bonus.
