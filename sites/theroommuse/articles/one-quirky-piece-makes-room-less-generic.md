BuzzFeed Shopping recently published a roundup of 33 eccentric home products for people who, in its words, "never pick the beige option." The full list isn't reproduced here, so this isn't a rundown of those items. What the roundup points to is a useful idea: you don't need a full redesign to make a room feel like yours. One well-chosen, slightly odd piece can do the job.

Below is a practical way to pick that piece, place it, and keep the rest of the room from fighting it. The setups are conceptual examples, not documented makeovers.

## Why one eccentric piece beats a room full of them

Maximalist and "dopamine" decor, meaning bright, expressive, personal styling, is still popular as people move away from sterile minimalism, per the trend context around BuzzFeed's roundup. The risk is that a pile of novelty items reads as clutter, not personality.

One standout piece gives the eye a place to land. Everything else in the room can stay calm and let it talk.

## Step 1: Choose a piece that earns its space

The most satisfying quirky buys are the ones that also do a job. Ask three questions before you buy:

- **Does it replace something you already use?** A bold lamp, tray, hook, or stool swaps in for a boring one, so you add personality without adding clutter.
- **Would you still like it in a year?** If the joke wears off after a week, skip it.
- **Does it start a conversation?** That's the point. A guest should be able to ask "where did you get that?"

## Step 2: Pick the right spot

Placement matters more than price. Good homes for a statement piece:

### The entryway
A quirky hook rail, mirror, or key bowl sets the tone the moment someone walks in. Aim to hang hooks and mirrors so the center sits around 57 to 60 inches from the floor, which is a standard gallery-style eye level.

### The living room sightline
Put it where you see it from the sofa: a console, a shelf, or a side table. Keep a few inches of empty space around it so it doesn't blur into neighboring objects.

### The small-space corner
In a studio or small living room, a bold stool or floor lamp can sit in a dead corner and add character without using up floor area. Keep walkways at least 30 inches wide so the piece doesn't become an obstacle.

## Step 3: Build a calm backdrop around it

The louder the piece, the quieter its surroundings should be.

- **Colors:** Choose two or three tones from the piece and repeat them in small doses, such as a pillow, a book spine, or a candle. Leave walls and large furniture in softer neutrals or muted shades.
- **Lighting:** Warm bulbs, around 2700K, flatter bold colors and make a room feel welcoming. A small accent light aimed at the piece makes it look intentional.
- **Surfaces:** Give a loud object a plain surface: a wood shelf, a white tray, a solid runner. Pattern on pattern is how a statement turns into noise.

## Budget options at every level

### Under $25
Swap small, visible things: drawer pulls, a ceramic dish, a set of unusual candle holders, or a fun shower curtain. Painting a thrifted frame or vase in a bold color costs only a little in paint.

### $25 to $100
This is the sweet spot for a lamp, a mirror, a statement stool, or a wall hanging. Compare materials: solid wood, metal, and glazed ceramic hold up better than thin plastic, so the piece still looks good after months of use.

### Over $100
Save your money for something you touch or see daily, like a chair, a rug, or a light fixture. A bold rug in a 5x8 foot size is large enough to anchor a seating area in most small living rooms.

## Step 4: Edit before you add

Before the new piece comes home, clear the spot. Take away at least one item that currently lives there. If the shelf already holds six things, the new one should replace two, not join them. A good rule is to keep about a third of any surface empty.

## Quick conceptual example: a plain rental living room

Imagine a beige sofa, white walls, and a small side table. Instead of replacing anything, swap the table lamp for a sculptural, brightly colored one. Add one pillow in a matching shade and set a wood tray under the lamp. You've changed the mood for the cost of one lamp, one pillow, and one tray, and nothing needed to be repainted or moved.

## When to skip the weird stuff

Some purchases are better left on the screen. Skip pieces that:

- need frequent cleaning or dusting in hard-to-reach crevices
- are too fragile for a household with kids or pets
- only work as a one-note joke
- don't fit your measurements (always measure the spot first, and check the product's listed dimensions)

## The takeaway

Pick one piece you'd be happy to explain to a guest, give it a clear spot with breathing room, and keep everything around it simple. That's enough to make a room stop looking like everyone else's.
