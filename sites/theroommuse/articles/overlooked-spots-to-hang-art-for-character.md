You have a sofa wall, and you've probably already filled it. The spots people forget are the ones that make a home feel collected rather than merely decorated. Below are seven places to hang or display art that most people skip, drawn from a roundup by Good Housekeeping. By the end, you'll have a list of corners to try this weekend, most of them for the price of a thrifted frame.

The idea is simple: think of it as hiding little treasures around your home. Guests spot them slowly, and you get a small lift every time you notice one yourself.

The specific tips and measurements below are my own practical suggestions, so treat them as starting points. The seven locations come from the Good Housekeeping list.

## 1. On a Kitchen Shelf

A kitchen shelf is a natural gallery that most people only use for dishes. Lean a small framed piece against the wall between your cookbooks or beside a stack of plates.

**Try this:** Leaning art needs no nails, which suits renters. Choose pieces roughly 5x7 to 8x10 inches so they sit comfortably without crowding the shelf. Keep them away from the stove, where grease and steam can damage paper and frames.

**Budget option:** A thrifted print in a mismatched frame works well here, since a kitchen shelf already looks collected.

## 2. Conceal Your TV

A big black rectangle can dominate a room. Art can soften it, either by hanging pieces around the screen or by using a cover or frame designed to hide it.

**Try this:** If you're building a gallery cluster around a TV, start with the screen as the center and work outward. Leave roughly 2 to 3 inches between frames so the group reads as one arrangement. Mixing frame finishes (wood, black, brass) keeps it from looking like a set.

**Note:** If you choose a product that actually covers the screen, check ventilation and the manufacturer's instructions before you commit.

## 3. Above a Door Frame

The strip of wall over a doorway is usually bare. A long, narrow piece fits well, and it draws the eye upward, which can make a ceiling feel taller.

**Try this:** Look for a horizontal piece no wider than the door frame itself, so it looks intentional. Because it sits high, use lightweight art and secure hanging hardware so nothing falls on someone walking through. Picture-hanging strips rated for the weight are a reasonable option for light frames, but check the packaging.

## 4. Tucked Into the Bathroom

Bathrooms tend to get towels and nothing else. A small piece of art turns a purely functional room into one that feels designed, and it's a space where guests have a quiet moment to look around.

**Try this:** Choose framed art under glass, or something printed on a moisture-tolerant material, since steam is hard on bare paper and canvas. Hang it away from the shower and above eye level when seated if you want it to feel balanced with the fixtures.

**Budget option:** A single small frame above the toilet or beside the mirror costs very little and changes the whole feel.

## 5. Beneath a Staircase

The wall under a staircase is awkward, and awkward spaces are where art does its best work. The angled space can hold a gallery arrangement that follows the line of the stairs.

**Try this:** Before you drill anything, lay your frames on the floor and arrange them in the shape of the slope. Trace each one on kraft paper, cut it out, and tape the templates to the wall. You can adjust the layout until it looks right, then hang with confidence.

## 6. In a Cupboard

This one is a delight. Open a cupboard and find a little surprise. It could be a small print taped inside a door, or a piece propped against the back wall of an open-fronted cabinet.

**Try this:** Inside a closed cupboard door, use removable adhesive hooks or tape so you don't damage the finish. If the cupboard is glass-fronted, a piece at the back becomes a backdrop for the dishes and glassware in front of it.

**Budget option:** Wrapping paper, a vintage map page, or a postcard in a cheap frame works just as well as a pricey print.

## 7. On the Face of a Bookshelf

Bookshelves don't have to hold only books. Hang or lean art on the front of the shelf, or let a piece sit in front of the books for layering.

**Try this:** Stagger heights so the art breaks up rows of spines. A small frame propped on a shelf edge, with a few objects beside it, makes a vignette. Keep a rhythm: a few books stacked horizontally, then a framed piece, then an object.

## Why this works now

Per Good Housekeeping's roundup, the appeal is the unexpected detail. As homes get more personalized and layered, small choices are a low-cost way to make rooms feel curated without a renovation. That fits well with thrifted art, collected objects, and lived-in styles, and none of these spots requires a big budget.

## Quick tips for any spot

- **Mix, don't match.** Thrifted pieces with different frames feel more personal than a coordinated set.
- **Go small.** Tiny art suits tight spaces and is easy to move around.
- **Test before you commit.** Prop things up for a few days before putting a hole in the wall.
- **Renters:** Leaning, shelf-standing, and removable-adhesive options keep your deposit safe.

## Your next step

Walk through your home today and pick the one spot you pass most often without looking at it. Put a single piece of art there before the weekend ends.
