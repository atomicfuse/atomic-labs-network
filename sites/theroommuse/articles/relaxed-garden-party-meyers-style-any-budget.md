Vogue's Eileen Kelly recently visited a garden party in the back garden of Nancy Meyers's Los Angeles home, where Meyers's daughter, Annie Meyers-Shyer, celebrated the launch of her renovation book, *This Oak House*. Per Vogue, the Meyers family approach to entertaining is polished, relaxed, detail-obsessed, and designed to look effortless.

You don't need a Los Angeles garden to borrow that. Below is a practical formula for a party that feels collected and easy, with options at three budget levels. One note up front: the details of the Meyers party beyond that general approach aren't something I can report here, so what follows is my own conceptual translation of the idea, not a recreation of their event.

## Start With the Idea: Effortless Is Planned

The word "effortless" is doing a lot of work. A party that looks unforced is usually one where the host did the hard parts days earlier. That's the real lesson to take from the Meyers approach: put your effort where guests can't see it, so you're relaxed when they arrive.

A simple timeline works for almost any gathering:

- **One week out:** Confirm guest count, check that you have enough seats, plates, and glasses, and plan the menu.
- **Two days out:** Shop, wash and press linens, and set up any furniture that will move outdoors.
- **Day before:** Prep food that holds well, chill drinks, and set the table.
- **Day of:** Arrange flowers, light candles, start the music, and get dressed an hour before guests arrive.

## Set the Table First

The table is the anchor of a garden party, so decide on it before anything else.

### Colors and materials

Keep the palette tight: two or three colors, mostly soft neutrals with one fresh accent such as sage green or a pale blue. Natural materials do most of the work. Linen, cotton, wood, and plain white or cream dishes look right outdoors and hide wrinkles and mismatches better than stiff fabrics.

### Measurements that matter

- Allow roughly 24 inches of table width per person so nobody is elbowing a neighbor.
- Keep centerpieces under about 12 inches tall, or very airy, so guests can see across the table.
- Leave at least 36 inches behind chairs for people to move around.

### Three budget routes

- **Under $50:** Use bedsheets or a thrifted tablecloth, mismatched plates you already own, and jam jars for vases.
- **Mid-range:** Buy a set of washed-linen napkins and a runner, and mix in a few inexpensive stoneware pieces.
- **Splurge:** Invest in a long linen tablecloth, quality flatware, and proper glassware you'll reuse for years.

## Keep the Flowers Loose

Formal arrangements can feel stiff outdoors. Looser, gathered-looking flowers suit the setting better. Cut a few stems from your own yard if you can, such as greenery, herbs, or whatever is blooming, and drop them into several small containers rather than one large one.

On a budget, a couple of grocery-store bunches split across five or six small vessels looks fuller than a single expensive arrangement. Mixing heights, from 4 to 10 inches, adds interest. Mint, rosemary, and lemon branches also smell good and cost little.

## Serve Easy Food That Still Feels Special

Choose a menu you can mostly make ahead, so you aren't stuck in the kitchen. A few principles:

- Pick dishes that are fine at room temperature: grain salads, roasted vegetables, tarts, sandwiches, or a big platter of cheese and fruit.
- Make one thing feel special, like a homemade dessert or a signature drink, and keep the rest simple.
- Serve drinks from a self-serve station with a pitcher, a bucket of ice, and a stack of glasses so guests help themselves.

Presentation does the elevating here. Put food on wooden boards, cake stands, and platters, and add a handwritten label or two.

## Light and Sound Set the Mood

Good lighting makes a party. If it runs into evening, plan for it before dusk:

- String lights hung 7 to 8 feet above the seating area give an even glow.
- Candles in hurricane glasses or lanterns keep flames steady in a breeze.
- Avoid harsh overhead or porch lights if you can; low, warm light is more flattering.

Music should sit in the background. Make a playlist ahead of time, keep it low enough that people can talk, and put the speaker near the edge of the space instead of the center.

## Let the House Look Lived In

The Meyers aesthetic is known for rooms that feel comfortable rather than staged, and that's a useful rule for hosting. Don't try to make your home look untouched. Tidy it, then leave signs of life: books on a side table, a throw over a chair arm, a bowl of fruit on the counter.

For small spaces or patios, a few moves help:

- Use a bench or bar cart as flexible seating or serving space.
- Add floor cushions or outdoor poufs, which store easily.
- Create two or three small clusters of seating instead of one big group, so conversation spreads out.
- Clear a path from the house to the garden so guests drift outside naturally.

## Your Pre-Party Checklist

1. Count seats, plates, and glasses.
2. Set the table the night before.
3. Prep food that holds well.
4. Arrange flowers in small containers.
5. Check lighting and charge the speaker.
6. Tidy, but leave a few personal touches out.

The most useful thing to copy is the timing, not the setting: finish the work before the first guest rings the bell, then spend the party actually enjoying it.
