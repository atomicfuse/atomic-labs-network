A Brazilian architect built a UFO-shaped house in 1974 and planned to live there temporarily, according to The Times of India. Nearly 50 years later, it appears to have become a long-term architectural curiosity. The report, as we have it, doesn't give us room dimensions, materials or interior details, so we won't invent any.

What the story does give us is a useful idea: a home built around one bold personal vision can stay compelling for decades. You don't need a flying-saucer footprint to borrow that. Below, you'll find ways to bring retro-futurist style into an ordinary room, sorted by budget. These are conceptual suggestions inspired by the story, not a record of what the architect did inside.

## Why 1970s Futurism Still Works

The appeal is a mix of curves, simple forms and a sense of optimism. Retro-futurist design is having a strong social-media moment, and audiences are gravitating toward quirky, personal architecture with a backstory. That's good news for renters and homeowners alike, because the look is built from a few repeatable moves rather than one expensive purchase.

The rule of thumb: pick one dramatic idea per room and keep everything else calm. A single curve, a single glossy surface, a single glowing light. More than that and the room starts to feel like a theme park.

## Under $50: Curves and Glow

These are quick swaps that change the mood in an afternoon.

- **Swap one lamp shade or bulb.** A white globe or opal glass shade, roughly 10 to 12 inches across, gives off the soft, round glow that reads as space-age. Choose a warm white bulb (around 2700K) so it feels homey, not clinical.
- **Add a round mirror.** A 24 to 30 inch circular mirror over a console or sofa breaks up the straight lines most rooms are full of.
- **Use circles in your styling.** Round trays, a circular rug layered over a larger one, and a porthole-style wall clock all repeat the shape.

## $50 to $300: Paint, Textiles and One Statement Piece

### Pick a restrained palette
A simple scheme keeps a bold shape from looking chaotic. Try warm white walls with one saturated accent such as burnt orange, or cool grey with a silver or chrome-look accent. Paint one wall or the inside of a bookcase rather than the whole room.

### Choose glossy, smooth materials
Laminate, lacquer-finish trays, acrylic and metal-look accents all suggest the era. Pair them with something soft, like a bouclé or velvet pillow, so the room doesn't feel cold.

### Buy one rounded furniture piece
A round coffee table, 32 to 36 inches across, or a curved-back chair does more than several small accessories. Leave about 18 inches between the table and the sofa so people can still walk and reach their drinks.

## $300 and Up: Change the Layout and the Lighting

### Float the furniture
Curved homes encourage movement around a center rather than along the walls. You can echo that by pulling the sofa 6 to 12 inches off the wall and angling two chairs toward each other. Conversation areas feel more intentional, and a small room often feels larger because you can see more floor.

### Layer the lighting
Put a ceiling or arc floor lamp on one dimmer, a table lamp on another, and a small accent light somewhere unexpected, like behind a plant or under a shelf. Dimmable LED strips are an affordable way to add the glow.

### Consider built-ins with soft corners
If you're already planning shelving or a niche, ask for rounded corners. It's a small detail that makes standard storage feel custom.

## Small Spaces: Use the Look Lightly

Compact rooms can handle futurism as long as you edit.

- Keep the large pieces light in color so the room doesn't close in.
- Choose furniture with legs, which shows more floor and feels airier.
- Use one oversized circular item, like a mirror or round pendant, as the focal point.
- Skip busy patterns. Let shape carry the style.

## Make It Yours, Not a Replica

The most interesting part of the UFO house story is the backstory: a home meant to be temporary that, per the report, outlasted that plan by decades. Personal spaces tend to do that. The room you set up "for now" can become the one you love.

So choose the elements that reflect something you actually like, whether that's a family object, a favorite color or a piece of salvaged hardware, and let them sit beside the futuristic touches. A glossy lamp next to a handmade frame feels lived in. A showroom of space-age pieces doesn't.

## Start Here

Pick one corner of one room. Add a round mirror, a warm globe light and one saturated accent. Live with it for a week, then decide what the room wants next.
