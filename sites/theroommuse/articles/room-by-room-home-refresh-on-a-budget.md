BuzzFeed Shopping recently rounded up 23 Walmart products it says can give your home a glow-up. The full list isn't reproduced here, so this article doesn't tell you what's on it. What it does is take the idea behind that roundup, affordable upgrades that make a home feel cleaner, cozier, and more stylish, and turn it into a plan you can use in any store or any budget.

By the end, you'll know which swaps in each room give the biggest visual payoff, what to look for in materials and sizing, and how to spend little or a bit more depending on your wallet.

*A note on labeling: the room plans below are conceptual makeover frameworks, not before-and-after projects I've completed. The measurements are common decorating guidelines you can adjust to your space.*

## Start With Lighting, Because It Changes Everything

Harsh overhead light flattens even a well-decorated room. Fixing it is often the cheapest big upgrade.

- **Aim for three light sources per room** at different heights: one overhead or ceiling-level, one at table height, one low (a floor lamp works).
- **Choose warm bulbs.** Look for 2700K on the package for a cozy glow. 5000K reads as cold and clinical.
- **Budget move:** swap bulbs and add one plug-in lamp.
- **Splurge move:** add a dimmer switch or a plug-in wall sconce with a fabric shade.

## Living Room: Make It Feel Bigger and More Pulled Together

### Anchor the seating with a rug
A rug that's too small makes a room look choppy. In a living room, the front legs of your sofa and chairs should sit on the rug. An 8x10 ft rug suits most seating areas, and a 5x7 ft works for a compact apartment if it sits under the coffee table and front sofa legs.

### Pick a three-color palette
Choose one dominant neutral (about 60 percent of the room), a secondary shade (30 percent), and an accent (10 percent). Warm white walls, a mid-tone sofa, and rust or deep green accents is an easy formula.

### Hang curtains higher
Mount the rod 4 to 6 inches above the window frame, and extend it 8 to 12 inches past each side. The ceiling looks taller and the window looks larger. Floor-length panels in linen-look fabric add softness for little money.

## Bedroom: Refresh Without Replacing the Furniture

- **Layer the bed.** Fitted sheet, flat sheet, duvet or comforter, a quilt folded at the foot, and two sleeping pillows plus two or three decorative ones.
- **Stick to natural-feeling textures.** Cotton, linen blends, and waffle weaves read as more expensive than shiny polyester.
- **Use a duvet cover.** It's cheaper than a new comforter, and washable.
- **Add a bedside lamp on each side** at roughly shoulder height when you're sitting up in bed.

## Small Spaces: Work Vertically and Hide the Clutter

### Go up, not out
Use tall bookshelves, wall hooks, and over-the-door organizers. Keep the lower third of a wall clear where you can so the floor reads as open.

### Use furniture with a job
Storage ottomans, nesting tables, and beds with underbed bins reduce visible clutter. Matching baskets or bins in one material (woven, fabric, or metal) look intentional on open shelves.

### Add a mirror
Place a large mirror across from or beside a window to bounce light. A leaning floor mirror, around 60 inches tall, is an easy renter-friendly choice that needs no drilling.

## Entry, Kitchen, and Bath: The Small Swaps

These are the spots where cheap upgrades look the most polished.

- **Entry:** a hook rail, a shoe tray, and a small mirror make the first spot guests see feel organized.
- **Kitchen:** swap mismatched containers for a matching set, add a dish towel in a pattern that fits your palette, and keep counters to three or four items.
- **Bathroom:** matching soap dispensers, fluffy towels in one color family, and a new shower curtain. Hang the curtain rod high and use a curtain longer than the standard length to add height.

## Renter-Friendly Options

If you can't paint or drill, lean on things that go away cleanly: removable hooks (check the weight rating on the package), plug-in sconces, peel-and-stick hardware covers, large leaning art, and textiles. Rugs, curtains, and lamps change a room more than people expect.

## How to Spend: Three Budget Tiers

- **Under $50:** bulbs, a throw pillow cover set, a hook rail, and a new shower curtain.
- **Around $100 to $200:** a rug, curtain panels with a rod, and a lamp.
- **Larger spend:** a quality duvet set, a statement mirror, or a storage piece you'll use daily.

For any store, including Walmart, check dimensions, material descriptions, and recent customer photos before buying. Roundups like BuzzFeed's are a useful starting point for discovery, but your room's measurements should decide what goes in the cart.

## Your Next Step

Pick one room and fix its lighting this weekend. Swap in warm bulbs, add a lamp at a different height, and see how much of the rest of the room already works.
