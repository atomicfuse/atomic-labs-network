It's a fun product, and fun products are exactly the ones that tend to end up forgotten in the back of a cabinet. So rather than just tell you to buy it, here's a practical way to decide whether this gadget, or any compact one, deserves a place in your home.

## What the pick actually offers

Per Buzzfeed's roundup, the appeal of this ice cream maker comes down to two things: it's built to store easily, and you eat from the same container you make it in. That second point matters in a small kitchen. Fewer separate bowls and serving dishes means less to wash and less to stash.

## Step 1: Measure the home before you buy

Before you add anything to your cart, decide exactly where it will live. Not "somewhere in the kitchen," but a specific shelf, drawer, or corner.

- Measure the width, depth, and height of that spot with a tape measure.
- Compare it to the product's listed dimensions, with a little breathing room so you can pull it out easily.
- If it needs to go in the freezer, measure that space too. Freezers in studios and dorms are often tiny, and a gadget that needs freezer room competes with your actual food.

If you can't name the spot, you're not ready to buy.

## Step 2: Ask whether it replaces something or adds something

The best small-space gadgets do double duty or cut down on other items. A container you make and eat from in one go reduces dishes, which is the kind of swap that helps a tiny kitchen. A single-purpose tool that needs its own bowls, lids, and accessories does the opposite.

A quick test: list everything the gadget needs to work, including anything you'd have to store alongside it. If that list is longer than two items, think twice.

## Step 3: Be honest about how often you'd use it

A gadget earns its space through repeat use. Ask yourself:

1. Would I use this at least a couple of times a month?
2. Is the process quick enough that I'll actually do it on a weeknight?
3. Do I already have a cheaper way to get the same result?

If you answered yes, yes, no, it's a keeper. If you're buying it for one dinner party, borrow or skip.

## Step 4: Give fun things a fair budget

Not every small-space purchase has to be pure utility. A treat-yourself item can make a tiny apartment feel more like yours, as long as it's small enough not to crowd you. Set a ceiling before you shop, and match the choice to your budget:

- **Lowest spend:** Skip the gadget. Mash frozen fruit in a bowl you already own, or buy a pint and use a nice small bowl and spoon to make it feel special.
- **Mid spend:** A compact, single-serve tool like this one, if the listing shows it fits your storage spot.
- **Higher spend:** A full-size appliance only makes sense if you have a dedicated cabinet and cook often. In most studios, it doesn't.

## Step 5: Give it a one-in, one-out rule

Small spaces stay livable when new things replace old ones. When the new gadget arrives, pick one item to donate or sell. A mug you never use, a duplicate spatula, a bowl set with too many pieces. This keeps the cabinet from slowly filling up.

## The takeaway

Before buying any compact gadget, find its home, measure it, and make sure it replaces something. If it passes, it's not clutter. It's a small pleasure with a place to live.
