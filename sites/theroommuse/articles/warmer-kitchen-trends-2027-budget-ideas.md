Veranda recently rounded up 12 kitchen trends designers expect to be everywhere in 2027, and the common thread is easy to spot: calmer, warmer kitchens that hide clutter and feel more like living spaces than workrooms. You don't need a luxury renovation to use that idea. Below, you'll find how to pull the shift toward warmth into your own kitchen at three budget levels, plus where a bigger spend makes sense.

The trends Veranda lists are: the rise of the scullery, invisible kitchens, kitchens that feel like living rooms, richer wood tones, natural materials and craftsmanship, statement stone, creative millwork, decorative range hoods, artistic tile backsplashes, tonal kitchens, islands as gathering tables, and smart kitchens that don't look high-tech. Per the report, the larger move is away from sterile minimalism and toward warmth, craftsmanship, and practical luxury.

This guide is a conceptual playbook, not a single finished makeover. Costs and measurements below are general rules of thumb of mine, not figures from Veranda.

## Start with the shift: hide the clutter

Two of the trends, the scullery and the invisible kitchen, are about the same goal: keeping the messy part of cooking out of sight. As open-concept layouts keep kitchens visible from the living area, that matters more.

### Low budget: fake a scullery

A true scullery is a secondary workroom, which most homes can't add. You can mimic the function instead:

- Clear one stretch of counter, ideally near an outlet, and designate it the "back kitchen." Keep the toaster, kettle, and coffee maker there.
- Add a tension rod or a simple curtain on a track to screen it. A fabric panel in a linen-like weight costs far less than cabinet doors.
- Use lidded baskets or bins on open shelves so dish racks and small appliances disappear.

### Mid budget: make the kitchen "invisible"

Invisible kitchens blend into the surrounding room. A practical version is to replace a few upper cabinet fronts or add panels so the fridge and dishwasher match the cabinetry. Paint is the cheapest way in: painting cabinets and trim one color keeps the eye from catching on appliances and hardware.

## Warm it up with wood and natural materials

Designers point to richer wood tones and natural materials with visible craftsmanship. The sterile, all-white look is giving way to materials with texture and depth.

### Low budget

- Swap cabinet pulls for solid wood or aged-metal ones. It's a one-afternoon job with a screwdriver.
- Add a thick wooden cutting board or butcher block tray you can leave on the counter.
- Bring in woven stools, ceramic bowls, and a linen runner to add handmade texture.

### Mid budget

- Stain or refinish an island or a run of base cabinets in a deeper wood tone. Test on an inside door first.
- Replace open shelving boards with solid wood planks, about 1.5 inches thick for a substantial look and a sturdy hold.

### Splurge

Statement stone is on the list, and it earns its cost where it's most visible: a waterfall island edge, a single slab backsplash, or a counter you'll see from the living room. Spend here rather than spreading money thin across the whole room.

## Add personality with color, tile, and trim

### Try a tonal palette

Tonal kitchens use variations of one color family rather than contrasting blocks. Pick one hue, such as a soft greige, clay, or sage, and vary it: a slightly darker shade on lower cabinets, a lighter one on walls, and the deepest on trim or the range hood. It reads calm and cohesive, and paint is the budget-friendly route.

### Make the backsplash the art

Artistic tile backsplashes are a listed trend. If a full tile job is too much, use it as a focal point only behind the range, roughly 30 to 36 inches wide, and keep the rest simple. Peel-and-stick tile is a renter-friendly stand-in, though it won't match the real thing.

### Dress up the hood and the walls

Decorative range hoods and creative millwork both add architecture. A budget version: apply simple wood trim or beadboard panels to a plain wall or the side of an island. For a hood, a wood or plaster-look wrap is a bigger project, but it turns a utilitarian object into a feature.

## Make the kitchen feel like a living room

This is the trend that ties the rest together. To get the feel without remodeling:

- **Lighting:** Add a table lamp on a counter or shelf, and swap harsh bulbs for warm ones (around 2700K is a cozy standard). Dimmers help the room shift from cooking to relaxing.
- **Textiles:** A washable rug runner in front of the sink adds softness. Choose a low pile that's easy to clean.
- **Art and objects:** Lean a framed print on a shelf or counter. Books and plants make it feel lived in.
- **Seating:** An island that doubles as a gathering table, another trend on the list, works best with stools that are comfortable and tuck in neatly. Leave roughly 10 to 12 inches of overhang so knees fit.

## Keep the tech, hide the gadgets

The last trend, smart kitchens that don't look high-tech, suits a calm room. Look for appliances and controls that blend in: panel-ready models, concealed outlets or charging drawers, and smart plugs that add control to the appliances you already own. A small hidden charging spot keeps cords and devices off the counter.

## Where to spend first

If you can only do one thing, choose what you see and touch every day. A rough order I'd follow:

1. Paint and hardware (lowest cost, big impact)
2. Lighting and textiles
3. Backsplash or millwork on one focal wall
4. Wood refinishing or new island
5. Stone and custom hood (the splurge)

Pick one warm material, one calm color family, and one place to hide the clutter. Do those three this month and your kitchen will already feel closer to where designers say 2027 is heading.
