A federal judge has dismissed a Trump administration lawsuit that tried to delay certifying Alaska's Senate race results because two candidates named Dan Sullivan appear on the ballot. ## What Happened

Per the BBC, the Department of Justice had sought to delay certifying the results of Alaska's Senate race if the Republican incumbent loses. The reasoning centered on voter confusion: two candidates on the ballot share the name Dan Sullivan.

A federal judge threw the case out. According to the BBC, the judge ruled that the DOJ lacks the authority to force ballot segregation and an investigation. The ruling also cited violations of voter secrecy and the impracticality of the request just days before the election.

The timing matters. Per the BBC, the ruling lands just before the midterm elections, where control of Congress hangs in the balance and Alaska's Senate race is among the tightest contests.

## The Angle That Matters: Why Voter Secrecy Limited the Case

The most useful takeaway for most readers is not the name coincidence. It's how the judge's reasoning shows the limits on investigating an election, even when someone argues the ballot is confusing.

### What the DOJ was asking for

Based on the BBC's summary, the request involved segregating ballots and investigating them. In plain terms, that would mean setting certain votes apart and examining them to figure out whether voters meant to choose one Dan Sullivan or the other.

### Why the judge said that doesn't work

The judge cited two broad problems, per the BBC:

- **Voter secrecy.** Ballots are cast privately. Pulling them aside and probing how people voted runs against that protection.
- **Impracticality.** Asking election officials to restructure how ballots are handled days before voting closes is a heavy lift.

The judge also found that the DOJ lacks the authority to compel this kind of segregation and investigation in the first place. That's a separate point from the practical concerns: even if the idea were workable, the court said the department can't force it.
