The first trailer for **"The Comeback King"** is out, and it has people talking. Glen Powell plays a disgraced country superstar trying to recover after a messy public breakup, and the trailer leans on a big lineup of country-music cameos. By the end of this piece, you'll know what the trailer sets up, what Powell did to prepare, and why online reaction is split, so you can decide which side you're on before the movie arrives.

The trailer and the reaction were covered by Fox News, which is the source for what follows.

## What the trailer sets up

Powell's character is a country star in free-fall. The setup, per Fox News, is a public breakup gone wrong and a career that now needs rescuing. It's a classic redemption-comedy engine: a famous person at rock bottom, with everyone watching.

The movie is a comedy, and Powell isn't only starring in it. Fox News reports he co-wrote the screenplay with Judd Apatow. That pairing matters, because it signals this is a project Powell has a personal stake in, not just a role he picked up.

## Powell did the homework

If you're wondering whether he's faking the music side, Fox News notes that Powell trained in singing and guitar for the part. Playing a believable country star is a high bar. Audiences tend to forgive a lot in a comedy, but a musician character who can't look comfortable with an instrument breaks the spell fast.

Whether the training pays off on screen is something a trailer can only hint at. The full film will be the real test.

## The cameo lineup is a big part of the pitch

The trailer packs in country star power. Fox News highlights the movie's large country-music cameo roster as one of its defining features. That does two things:

- It lends the fictional world credibility. A made-up country star feels more real when actual country names share the screen.
- It gives country fans a reason to show up beyond Powell himself.

The flip side is obvious. When a trailer leans heavily on famous faces, some viewers wonder whether the story can carry the film or whether the cameos are the story. That tension sits at the center of the debate.

## Why fans are divided

Per Fox News, reaction online has been mixed. Without putting words in anyone's mouth, here's how the split tends to look from the angles the coverage points to:

### The case for

Powell is coming off a run that includes "Top Gun: Maverick," "Anyone But You," "Hit Man" and "Twisters." Those span action, romance, crime comedy and disaster thriller. A country-star comedy co-written with Apatow looks like a natural next stretch, and supporters can point to that track record as a reason to give it the benefit of the doubt.

### The case against

Skeptics, as Fox News frames it, are weighing whether the trailer reads as a smart star vehicle or a pile of celebrity appearances. That's our read of the debate's shape, not a verdict: it's hard to judge tone and pacing from a couple of minutes of footage, and trailers for comedies often front-load cameos because they're easy to sell.

## What to watch for in the full movie

The trailer can't answer these, but they're worth keeping in mind:

1. **The music.** Does Powell look and sound at ease performing, given his singing and guitar training?
2. **The balance.** Do the cameos serve the story or crowd it?
3. **The heart.** Redemption comedies live or die on whether you root for the flawed lead. A disgraced star after a messy breakup has to earn sympathy.
4. **The writing.** With Powell and Apatow behind the script, the jokes and emotional beats should show their fingerprints.

## The bigger picture for Powell

It's reasonable to see this as a test of range. After a string of hits, Powell is now asking audiences to accept him as a comedy lead and a fictional country star at once. That's our analysis, not a reported claim, but the ambition is clear in the premise alone.

Watch the trailer yourself and decide what you're reacting to: Powell, the premise, or the cameos. Then hold your verdict until the full film. A trailer shows you what a movie wants you to think, and only the movie shows you whether it earned it.
