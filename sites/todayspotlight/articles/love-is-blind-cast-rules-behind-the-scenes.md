Love Is Blind looks like a spontaneous social experiment: people talk through a wall, fall in love, and decide whether to marry. But Cosmopolitan has rounded up nine rules cast members have to follow, and together they show how structured the experience is. Here they are, ordered the way you'd likely hit them if you signed up, from application to altar.

This list is built on Cosmopolitan's reporting. The details below are limited to what that report covers.

## Before the Cameras: What Applying Involves

### 1. They have to do psychological testing

Per Cosmopolitan, cast members go through psychological testing. It's a reminder that the show is screening for more than chemistry. Whatever the testing is used for, it means casting is a process, not a random draw of hopeful singles.

### 2. They have to do a 73-question survey

There's also a 73-question survey. Seventy-three questions is long enough to cover a lot of ground, and it suggests the production wants to know its participants well before anyone steps into the pods. That's our read, not a stated reason from the show.

### 3. They have to be willing to film for a long time

Cosmopolitan lists a willingness to film for a long time as a requirement. If you imagine the show as a quick romantic getaway, adjust that picture: this is a major time commitment, and it asks participants to put their regular lives on hold.

## Inside the Experiment: Daily Life in the Pods

### 4. They have to give up their phone and internet for 10 full days

This is the rule that gets the most attention, and it's easy to see why. No phone and no internet for 10 full days means no texting friends, no scrolling, and no looking anyone up. It also means the only people you interact with are the other participants and the production.

### 5. They have to date everyone, at least at first

Per the report, cast members must date everyone, at least at first. So the early conversations aren't purely driven by instinct. You may chat with people you'd never have picked, which can reshape who ends up connecting with whom.

### 6. They don't see each other unless they get engaged

The show's core premise is a rule too: contestants don't see each other unless they get engaged. That's the "blind" in the title, and it's the one rule that's openly part of the pitch.

### 7. They must use the gold cups

The last quirky rule on the list: cast members must use the gold cups. It's a small detail, but anyone who has watched the show knows how recognizable those cups are. Even the props are part of the format.

## Wedding Season: Who Is Really in Charge

### 8. They have to bring their own clothes and do their own styling

Cosmopolitan reports that cast members bring their own clothes and handle their own styling. It's a practical rule that may surprise you, given how polished everyone looks on screen. The look is theirs to put together.

### 9. Producers run the weddings

Per Cosmopolitan, producers run the weddings. That's a significant detail for a show built around a real life-changing decision. Planning and running the ceremony isn't left to the couples alone.

## What These Rules Tell You About the Show

Cosmopolitan's main takeaway is that the "experiment" is highly controlled behind the scenes to maximize romance, drama, and watchability. Taken together, the nine rules do point that way: screening before filming, isolation during, a required dating structure, and production-run weddings.

That doesn't mean the feelings are fake. Nothing in the report says that, and we can't say it either. It does mean the setting is carefully designed, which is worth keeping in mind as you watch. Love Is Blind remains one of Netflix's biggest reality franchises, and viewers are increasingly curious about how real these shows are.

## Which Rule Would Be Hardest for You?

For most people, it's probably the phone. Ten days without internet is a long stretch, and it's the rule that most changes how someone behaves. Next time you watch an episode, pay attention to what the cast members are doing with all that unplugged time. You'll see the format at work in a new way.
