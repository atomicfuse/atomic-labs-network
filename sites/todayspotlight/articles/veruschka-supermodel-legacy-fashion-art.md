Veruschka, the model and artist who became one of the first global fashion stars in the 1960s, has died at the age of 87, according to the BBC. If you know her only as a name from fashion history, this is a good moment to look at why she mattered: she treated modeling less as posing and more as performing, and that idea still shapes the images you scroll past today.

## Why she counts as a supermodel before the word was common

The BBC describes Veruschka as one of the first global fashion stars of the 1960s. Her influence reached across 1960s and 70s fashion, Vogue covers, film appearances, and body-based art. That range is what separates her from a model who is simply famous for a face.

The BBC frames her as more than a model: a shape-shifting performer who expanded ideas of beauty, fashion, and feminist performance. That phrase, shape-shifting, is the key to her career.

## Fashion as performance, not just pictures

Most fashion images ask you to look at the clothes. Veruschka's work, as the BBC portrays it, asked you to look at the transformation. She moved between the worlds of magazine glamour, film, and avant-garde art, and she used her own body as the material.

It's a useful way to look at any fashion image. Ask what the person in the frame is becoming, not only what they are wearing. That question is often more revealing than the credits.

## Surreal image-making before digital tools

Her work came decades before Photoshop and filters. Any transformation in her images had to happen in front of the camera, through the body, styling, and artistry. That's worth remembering the next time a heavily edited editorial feels like the peak of creativity. The BBC's coverage highlights her body-based art, a reminder that striking, surreal results were possible with physical presence and imagination.

## The feminist reading of her work

The BBC links her legacy to feminist performance. Here is my own take, not the BBC's: when a woman controls how she is transformed and presented, rather than simply being styled by others, the image shifts from being looked at to being authored. It's a lens that helps explain why her work still gets revisited.

## How to spot her influence today

This is analysis rather than reporting, but you can see her fingerprints in a few places:

- **Editorials that treat the model as a character.** When a shoot builds a persona instead of just showing outfits, it's following the path she helped open.
- **Beauty looks that function as art.** Face and body painting that crosses from makeup into costume has deep roots in the kind of work the BBC credits her with.
- **Personal branding as reinvention.** Creators who change their look and identity from project to project are, knowingly or not, borrowing the shape-shifting model.

## Where to start if you want to see her work

The BBC's piece is built around striking images of her career, so that is a natural starting point. From there, look for her Vogue covers, her film appearances, and her body-based art. Seeing all three side by side shows how one person moved between fashion, cinema, and the art world.

## The takeaway

Next time a fashion image stops you mid-scroll, ask who is being transformed, and by whom. Then go find a Veruschka photograph and see how an early supermodel answered that question with nothing but imagination and her own body.
