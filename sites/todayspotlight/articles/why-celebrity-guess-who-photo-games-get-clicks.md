## Why Guessing Games Perform

These formats have a few built-in advantages, and none of them depend on the star doing anything new.

### They create a small itch

A puzzle with a hidden answer is hard to scroll past. You want to know if your guess is right, and the only way to find out is to click through the gallery.

### They reward fandom

If you follow a star closely, you feel like you should be able to get the answer. That makes readers more likely to comment, share, or argue about the clues. Stars with active fanbases and viral moments are the safest bets for outlets, because someone is always ready to play.

### They're cheap to produce

A gallery, a teaser line, and a handful of clues require far less work than reporting. That's part of why the format keeps coming back.

## The Catch: Body-Focused Framing

Here's where our own take comes in. Many of these posts turn a person into a set of body parts and ask you to identify her from them. It's a longstanding tabloid habit, and it's worth noticing that the entertainment value comes from reducing a working actress to a crop of a photo.

Sweeney's career, by contrast, includes TV and film work that people talk about on its own merits. When a headline skips all of that for a body-part quiz, it tells you what the outlet thinks will drive traffic. Whether that's fair to the star is a question you can decide for yourself, but it's a fair one to ask.

## How to Read These Posts Critically

You don't have to swear off celebrity content to be a savvier reader. A few quick checks help.

1. **Look for the news.** If there's no new project, statement, or event, you're looking at engagement bait.
2. **Check who's credited.** Aggregators often repackage another outlet's gallery. Follow the credit back to the original reporter.
3. **Notice the framing words.** Teasers built around a mood boost ("perk you right up") are selling a feeling, not information.
4. **Ask what you'd gain.** If the answer is only the satisfaction of guessing right, decide whether it's worth the click.
