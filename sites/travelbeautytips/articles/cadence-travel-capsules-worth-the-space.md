Toiletries are the sneakiest space thief in a carry-on. A few half-empty bottles, a leaky cleanser, and a pill organizer can swallow the corner you wanted for a second pair of shoes. This guide walks you through one possible fix, the Cadence capsule set, and helps you decide whether it's worth the money for how you travel.

## What Buzzfeed Spotlighted

In a roundup of 22 travel products meant for people packing light, Buzzfeed featured the Cadence daily routine capsule set. Per the roundup, the capsules are leakproof, TSA-friendly, customizable, and made from recycled ocean-bound plastic. The set is priced at $138.

I'm only working from that description, so I can't tell you capacity per capsule, how many come in the set, or how they hold up after months of use. Treat what follows as a way to think through the purchase, not a verdict.

## Why a Capsule System Can Save Space

The appeal is simple: instead of carrying full-size bottles or a drawer's worth of travel minis, you decant only what you need into uniform containers. Uniform shapes pack tighter than a mix of tubes and bottles, and you carry only the amount you'll use. That's a general packing principle, and it's the logic behind the product.

Because Buzzfeed calls the set customizable, you can presumably build it around your own routine. Think skincare, hair products, or even medications, which is how the product is framed in the roundup's pitch.

## The Leakproof and TSA Question

Two claims matter most for a carry-on: leakproof and TSA-friendly. Leakproof means your silk blouse survives the flight. TSA-friendly means the containers are built for carry-on liquid rules.

Whatever you buy, do a home test before you trust it:

1. Fill each capsule with water, not your favorite serum.
2. Shake it upside down over a sink.
3. Leave it in a zip bag overnight, ideally on its side.

If it passes, then fill it with the real thing. Also check that you're staying within current liquid limits for your airline and airport, since rules can vary by location.

## Is $138 Worth It?

That price is a premium, and the honest answer depends on your habits.

**It probably makes sense if:**
- You fly often and repack the same routine every trip.
- You're tired of buying travel-size products that you toss after one use.
- You care about cutting single-use plastic. The recycled ocean-bound plastic material is a real selling point, per Buzzfeed.

**It's probably overkill if:**
- You take one or two trips a year.
- You already own decent refillable bottles.
- Your routine is three products or fewer.

A useful gut check: divide $138 by the number of trips you'll take in the next two years. If the per-trip cost looks smaller than what you spend on travel minis and replacement bottles, it's a fair buy.

## How to Pack Them Like a Stylish Minimalist

Style-minded travelers tend to pack toiletries as an afterthought, then wonder why the outfit math doesn't work. Try this order of operations:

- **Plan outfits first.** Know how much room your clothes need before you decide what your routine gets.
- **Build a core routine.** Cleanser, moisturizer, SPF, and one styling product cover most trips. Add extras only for specific events.
- **Group by moment.** Keep morning items together and evening items together so you can grab one set at the sink.
- **Label everything.** Identical capsules look alike at 6 a.m. in a hotel bathroom. A strip of tape fixes that.
- **Keep medications clearly identified.** If you use capsules for meds, label them well and keep them in your carry-on, not checked luggage.

## The Bigger Lesson

The broader point of Buzzfeed's roundup is that a good travel product earns its space. Whether it's Cadence or a cheaper refillable system, ask the same three questions before anything goes in your bag: does it replace several things, does it survive a flight, and will you use it again next trip?

Before you spend $138 on anything, pack your current toiletries into the smallest bag that holds them and see what you actually reach for. That list is your real shopping list.
