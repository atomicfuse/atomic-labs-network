Euronews recently published a roundup of the best food and drink tours worth travelling for, part of a wider shift toward trips built around what you eat and drink rather than what you tick off a sightseeing list. The outlet's full list isn't reproduced here, so this guide does something more durable: it gives you a way to judge any food or drink tour, and a practical plan for dressing and packing so you feel good through every course.

## Why food-led trips keep winning

Culinary tourism is a major driver of travel decisions, as people look for more immersive, experience-led holidays beyond sightseeing. Food and drink tours tap into the appetite for local culture, sustainability and authentic regional storytelling. That's the useful bit: a good tour isn't just a meal, it's a way into a place.

That also means quality varies. Here's how to sort the trip-worthy from the tourist filler.

## Four checks before you book

### 1. Does it tell a story about the place?

The best tours explain why a region eats or drinks the way it does. Read the tour description for specifics: producers, neighbourhoods, traditions. If it only lists "tastings" and "snacks," it's probably generic.

### 2. Is it rooted in the local scene?

Look for stops at family-run or regional producers and markets rather than a rotation of the same big venues. This is where the sustainability and authenticity angle shows up in practice.

### 3. Does it match your appetite?

Be honest about what you actually want to spend a day doing:

- **Wine regions:** best if you enjoy slow, scenic days and learning about a single craft.
- **Beer trips:** good for relaxed, social itineraries.
- **Street food cities:** suit people who want variety, movement and lower-key dining.

Pick the format that fits your pace, not the one that looks best on a map.

### 4. Would you travel for it on its own?

This is the Euronews test, and it's a good one. If the tour disappeared from your itinerary, would the trip still be worth taking? If yes, the destination carries you. If the tour is the whole reason, make sure it's strong enough to justify the journey.

## Planning the trip around the eating

Build the schedule so the food is the anchor, not an afterthought.

1. **Book the tour first.** Choose flights and hotels around it, not the other way round.
2. **Give it a clear runway.** Keep the morning light if you have an afternoon tasting, and avoid heavy meals beforehand.
3. **Leave a buffer day.** Food and drink days run long. Don't stack them back to back with early departures.
4. **Choose a base that's walkable.** Staying near your meeting point or the main dining area saves energy and money on transport.

## What to wear on a food and drink tour

This is where style meets practicality. You'll be standing, walking, sipping and sitting, often in a single outfit.

### Dress in layers with a polished base

A breathable top, a lightweight overshirt or cardigan, and easy trousers or a midi skirt move from market stalls to a table without looking rumpled. Dark or patterned fabrics hide the inevitable drip of sauce or wine.

### Pick shoes you can walk in all day

Cobbled streets and vineyard paths punish heels. Clean leather sneakers, loafers or flat sandals look put together and keep you comfortable.

### Bring a cross-body bag

Hands-free beats holding a bag between tastings. Choose one that zips, with room for a phone, wallet and a small pouch of essentials.

## The food-tour beauty kit

Keep it small enough to live in your bag all day:

- **Blotting papers or a mattifying powder** for warm kitchens and midday sun.
- **Lip balm with SPF,** since food and drink wear lips down fast.
- **Travel-size sunscreen** for outdoor vineyard or market stops.
- **Mints or a small toothbrush kit,** especially after garlic-heavy or wine-heavy stops.
- **Hand sanitiser and a few wipes** for street-food moments.
- **A stain-remover pen,** because it will happen eventually.

If you're tasting wine, go light on scented products. Heavy perfume can interfere with your own and other people's enjoyment of aroma.

## A quick pre-booking checklist

Before you pay, confirm:

- What's included, and how much food or drink you'll actually get
- Group size and duration
- Dietary accommodations, if you need them
- Cancellation terms
- The meeting point and how to reach it

If the answers are vague, treat that as information.

## The takeaway

Before you commit to any food or drink tour, ask whether you'd still take the trip if the tour were the only thing on the calendar. If the answer is yes, book it, pack a comfortable outfit that still looks sharp, and let the meal set the itinerary.
