A new Nomad eSIM study, reported by Fox News, ranks the U.S. airports most likely to deliver Thanksgiving travel chaos, and Chicago O'Hare lands at the top. If your trip runs through one of the ten hubs on the list, this guide shows you how to build a backup plan before you leave for the airport.

## What the Ranking Found

Per Fox News, the study scored airports on delays, cancellations and negative Google reviews. O'Hare took first place with a Chaos Score of 96.73. Delays hit nearly 28% of its flights and averaged 93 minutes over three years.

Here is the full top ten, in the study's order:

1. Chicago O'Hare International
2. John F. Kennedy International
3. Fort Lauderdale-Hollywood International
4. Detroit Metropolitan Wayne County
5. Dallas-Fort Worth International
6. Orlando International
7. Philadelphia International
8. Miami International
9. Newark Liberty International
10. Minneapolis-St. Paul International

The coverage I have doesn't include scores for anything below O'Hare, so treat the order as a ranking, not a measure of how far apart the airports are. It also blends in review sentiment, so it reflects the experience of being there as well as raw flight data.

The broader context: Thanksgiving is one of the busiest travel periods of the year, and holiday airfare is rising. Packed terminals leave less room for things to go wrong.

## Step 1: Check Whether You Actually Have a Hub Problem

Look at your itinerary and find every airport you touch, including connections. A layover counts. If one of the ten above appears, you have more exposure than a nonstop traveler flying between smaller airports.

If you have a choice, a nonstop flight removes the connection risk entirely. If you must connect, ask yourself whether a different connecting city, one not on this list, costs you only a little more. Sometimes a slightly pricier routing is cheaper than a night in an airport hotel.

## Step 2: Give Yourself Buffer Where It Counts

Delays on this list are not rare. At O'Hare, nearly three in ten flights were delayed on average over the study period. That makes tight connections the riskiest part of any itinerary.

- **Lengthen connections.** If you are choosing between a 45-minute and a two-hour layover at a listed hub, take the longer one.
- **Fly earlier in the day.** This is general travel wisdom rather than a finding of the study: an early flight gives you more same-day options if it gets scrapped.
- **Don't schedule anything unmovable on arrival day.** If Thanksgiving dinner is the goal, consider arriving a day early.

## Step 3: Set Up Your Real-Time Tools Before You Leave

Don't wait until you are standing in a gate-area crowd to figure out how you'll get information.

- Download your airline's app and turn on push notifications for gate changes, delays and cancellations.
- Save the airline's customer service number and any rebooking chat option in your phone.
- Keep a charger or battery pack in your carry-on. A dead phone in a delay is its own crisis.
- Make sure you have data access. The study came from an eSIM provider, so take its angle with a grain of salt, but the underlying point holds: if you are traveling and your phone can't get online, you can't rebook quickly.

## Step 4: Know Your Plan B Before You Need It

The fastest travelers during a cancellation wave are the ones who already know what they will do next. Before you leave home, write down answers to these:

1. **What is the next flight?** Look up the later departures on your route today, so you can ask for them by name.
2. **What is the alternate airport?** If you're flying into a metro area with more than one airport, know which one you'd accept.
3. **What is the ground option?** For shorter trips, know whether a rental car or train could get you there if flights collapse. Rental cars can sell out at peak times, so this is a fallback to check early.
4. **Who can you call?** Tell your host when you're traveling through a risky hub so no one is surprised by a late arrival.

## Step 5: If Your Flight Is Canceled, Move in Parallel

The instinct is to join the longest line at the gate. Do three things at once instead:

- Open the airline app and try to rebook there.
- Get in the service line anyway, as insurance.
- Call the airline's phone line while you wait.

Whichever channel answers first wins. Have your confirmation number and your Plan B flight options ready so the agent can act quickly.

## Step 6: Pack for a Long Day

Assume you could be stuck. Put these in your carry-on, not your checked bag:

- Medications and any essentials for a night away
- A change of clothes, especially if you're traveling with kids
- Snacks and an empty water bottle to fill past security
- Chargers and cables

Checked bags are the thing you can't control during a rebooking scramble, so carrying on keeps your options open when you need to switch flights quickly.

## The Bottom Line

A bad ranking doesn't mean you can't fly through these airports. It means the odds of a hiccup are higher, so the work shifts to you. Before you leave, check your route against the list, lengthen any tight connection, and write down your Plan B. Ten minutes of prep now is the cheapest travel insurance you'll get this Thanksgiving.
