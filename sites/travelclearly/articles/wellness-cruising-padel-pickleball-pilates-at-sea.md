The image of the cruise passenger sprawled on a deckchair, working through a buffet, is fading. Per a report from the Sydney Morning Herald, more guests now want to step off the ship feeling healthier and more refreshed than when they boarded, and cruise lines are responding with padel, pickleball and Pilates, along with bigger spas and more personalised wellness programs.

That's good news if you like to move on holiday. It also means more choice, and choice means you can book the wrong thing. This guide helps you decide what kind of wellness at sea you actually want, so you can pick a cruise that matches it.

## What's changing on board

According to the SMH report, the shift is from "flop-and-drop" relaxation toward active, personalised self-care, both at sea and on shore. The upgrades it describes include:

- Bigger spas and medi-spa treatments
- Pilates classes
- Racquet sports, namely pickleball and padel
- Thermal rooms
- Wellness-focused shore excursions

The report says this is happening across luxury, premium, budget and expedition brands, so you don't need a top-tier fare to find some of it. Exactly what each line offers varies, so check the specific ship before you commit.

## Step 1: Decide what "wellness" means for you

Before comparing ships, pin down your goal. Most people fall into one of three camps.

### You want to move
Look for ships that list fitness classes, Pilates and sports courts. Padel and pickleball are social, easy to pick up and good for meeting people, which suits solo travellers and groups alike. If a deck plan or ship page doesn't mention courts or a class schedule, ask the cruise line directly.

### You want to recover
If the aim is to switch off, the spa is your priority. Look at the treatment menu, whether the ship has thermal rooms, and whether medi-spa treatments are offered. These are the features the SMH piece highlights as growing.

### You want both
Most travellers do. Pick a ship with a mix, then plan your days so a morning class or game is followed by an afternoon in the spa, rather than trying to fit everything into one sea day.

## Step 2: Match the cruise style to your budget

The report notes that wellness upgrades are showing up from luxury to budget lines. The practical takeaway: you can choose by priorities rather than price alone.

- **Luxury and premium:** expect the push toward bigger spas and personalised programs to be most visible. Confirm what's included in the fare and what's charged per treatment.
- **Budget:** you may still find fitness classes and sports facilities, but check which activities are free and which cost extra.
- **Expedition:** the report includes this category among those expanding wellness, so if you prefer small-ship, destination-heavy travel, ask what's on offer rather than assuming there's nothing.

I can't give you a price list here, because the source doesn't provide one. Treat spa treatments and some classes as likely add-ons until the cruise line tells you otherwise.

## Step 3: Look beyond the ship

The trend isn't confined to the deck. The SMH report points to wellness excursions and destination-based healing experiences as part of the growth. That means a port day can be a restorative one rather than a rush around sights.

When you review the excursion list, look for activities that tie into local wellness traditions or gentle outdoor movement, and weigh them against standard sightseeing tours. Book popular ones early, since small-group experiences tend to fill first.

## Step 4: Questions to ask before you book

A short checklist saves disappointment:

1. Does this ship have padel or pickleball courts, and are they free to use?
2. Is Pilates included, or is it a paid class that needs booking?
3. What does the spa offer, and are thermal rooms part of it?
4. Are medi-spa treatments available if I want them?
5. Which shore excursions focus on wellness?
6. Is there a way to reserve classes and treatments before boarding?

That last question matters. Popular sessions on a full ship can go quickly, so reserving early is a sensible habit even where the source doesn't confirm booking rules.

## Step 5: Build a realistic sea-day plan

A wellness cruise works best when you plan lightly. A simple template for a sea day:

- **Morning:** a class such as Pilates
- **Midday:** a game of pickleball or padel, or just a walk around the deck
- **Afternoon:** spa time or a thermal room
- **Evening:** dinner without the guilt-or-gorge dynamic

On port days, swap the middle of that for a wellness excursion. You don't need to fill every hour. The point of the trend, as the SMH describes it, is to get off the ship refreshed.

## The bottom line

Cruising is no longer only about doing nothing. Before you book, decide whether you want to move, recover or both, then check the specific ship for courts, classes, spa facilities and wellness excursions. Ask those six questions first, and you'll choose a cruise that leaves you better off than when you boarded.
