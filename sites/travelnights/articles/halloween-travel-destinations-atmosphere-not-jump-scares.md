Halloween travel doesn't have to mean haunted-house screams. The best versions are about atmosphere: old cities, local legends, candlelit hotels and fall scenery that looks like a film set. Vogue's roundup of Halloween destinations points to seven places that deliver exactly that, and this guide sorts them by the kind of trip you want, so you can pick one for this year or save it for the next.

The common thread, per Vogue's list, is eerie history paired with autumn mood, local folklore, seasonal events and atmospheric places to stay. Late October also sits in a travel sweet spot before the holiday rush, which makes it a good time to plan. One honest caveat: the details below stay at the level the roundup supports, so check current event schedules, tour operators and hotel availability before you book anything.

## If You Want Ghost Tours and Cemetery Strolls

### New Orleans, Louisiana

New Orleans leads the list, and it's the obvious pick for a reason. Vogue's framing highlights ghost tours and cemetery strolls as the way in, taking travelers into the city's spookiest corners. Build your trip around evening walks and one daytime cemetery visit, then let the city's food and music do the rest. It's a spooky trip that never feels like a theme park.

### London, England

London brings centuries of layered history, which is the raw material of any good ghost story. If your idea of Halloween is moody streets and a story behind every old building, this is the city-break version of the holiday. Pair a guided evening walk with a daytime museum or landmark visit so the trip isn't all after-dark.

## If You Want Classic American Fall

### Sleepy Hollow, New York

Sleepy Hollow is the pick for cinematic fall scenery. Its legend-driven identity makes it a natural fit for travelers who want folklore and autumn color more than gore. It works well as a short trip, since the appeal is the setting and the story rather than a long checklist of sights.

### Salem, Massachusetts

Salem is the other great American option, known for its Halloween-season energy. The upside is that the whole town leans into the holiday. The trade-off, which is my own read rather than anything in the roundup, is that a place famous for Halloween is likely to be busy in late October, so book early and plan your quieter moments deliberately.

### A quick note on choosing between them

New Orleans suits you if you want food, nightlife and ghost tours in one trip. Sleepy Hollow and Salem suit you if the goal is crisp-air, small-town fall atmosphere. That's analysis, not a ranking from Vogue, but it's a useful way to narrow seven options fast.

## If You Want Folklore and Gothic Romance

### Kilkenny, Ireland

Kilkenny represents the Irish side of the list, a good fit if you like your Halloween rooted in local legend and old-world character. It's a smart choice for couples who would rather wander historic streets and settle into a cozy pub than join a crowd.

### Venice, Italy

Venice is the most romantic entry. Its mist-and-canal mood suits travelers who want spooky-but-stylish rather than scary. If you're planning a getaway for two, this is the one that blends gothic atmosphere with a proper fall city break.

### Transylvanian Alps, Romania

The Transylvanian Alps are the most dramatic bucket-list pick, tied to some of the world's best-known gothic folklore. This is the choice for travelers who want the mountains, the legends and a sense of real distance from home. Expect it to take more planning than a weekend in a US city.

## How to Pick Your Spooky Trip

Use these quick filters:

- **Short on time:** Sleepy Hollow or Salem work as compact fall trips.
- **Want food and culture alongside the ghosts:** New Orleans or London.
- **Traveling as a couple:** Venice or Kilkenny.
- **Planning a bigger adventure:** the Transylvanian Alps.

These groupings are my own suggestions based on each destination's character as described in the roundup, so treat them as starting points.

## What to Book First

Vogue's piece stresses atmospheric hotels as part of the experience, so start there. A historic property can make an ordinary city break feel like part of the story. Then add one guided ghost or folklore experience, and leave the rest of your days loose.

If this Halloween has already slipped by, don't write the idea off. Pick one destination from the list, check next October's dates, and book your stay first. The best spooky trips are the ones planned early enough to get the good room.
