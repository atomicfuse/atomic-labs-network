The new *Sense and Sensibility* adaptation was shot at real houses, castles and beaches across England, and you can build a Jane Austen road trip from them. Per Cosmopolitan, which mapped out the filming locations, the list runs from stately homes to a seaside beach. Here is what each stop is, what it stood in for on screen, and how to think about stitching them together.

One caveat up front: the details below are limited to what Cosmopolitan reported. Opening times, ticketing and access vary by property, so check each site's own page before you set a route.

## Start in the West Country: Norland, Barton Cottage and the beach

The film's early, emotional chapters are set in the south west, and that is where a handful of the locations cluster, according to the list. Dorset gets particular attention, and it is the county the article's author says they are heading to.

### Norland Park: Kingston Russell House and Parnham Park

Norland is the family home the Dashwood women lose at the start of the story. Two properties share the job: Kingston Russell House and Parnham Park. If you like comparing screen and reality, this is a good one, because you are effectively visiting two versions of one fictional house.

### Barton Cottage: Ditsworthy Warren House

Barton Cottage, the modest country home where the Dashwoods land, was filmed at Ditsworthy Warren House. It is the cottagecore stop on the list: a humble house rather than a grand one, and a reminder that the story is about a family's reduced circumstances.

### The Beach at Barton: Mothecombe Beach

The seaside scenes at Barton were shot at Mothecombe Beach. This is your breather stop. After a day of historic interiors, a walk along the sand is the easiest way to see the film's coastal landscape for yourself.

## Add the big estates

The rest of the list is about scale: castles, palaces of dancing and country houses with serious presence.

### Barton Park: Broughton Castle

Barton Park, the grander estate in the story, was filmed at Broughton Castle. A castle standing in for a country house is a fun detail to notice when you arrive.

### The Dance Scene: Penshurst Place

Penshurst Place is where the film's dance scene was shot. If you are picking stops based on how romantic they feel, a location tied to a dance is a strong candidate. Rewatch the scene before you go so you can match the rooms to the choreography.

### The London Ball: West Wycombe Park

The London ball was filmed at West Wycombe Park. Note the split: the film's London society scenes were not shot in central London, but at a country property, which makes this one a practical day trip rather than a city stop.

## Don't skip the London-set stop

### Mrs. Jennings's London Home: Rainham Hall

Mrs. Jennings's London home was filmed at Rainham Hall. It is the entry on the list that most directly represents the city on screen, so it is the natural pick if you are based in London and want a Austen-themed outing without a long drive.

## How to turn seven stops into a trip

The source gives locations, not distances or driving times, so treat this as a planning approach rather than a fixed route. My own suggestions:

- **Group by region.** Norland (Kingston Russell House or Parnham Park), Barton Cottage (Ditsworthy Warren House) and Mothecombe Beach belong together as a south west leg, and Dorset is a sensible base for it.
- **Treat the others as separate outings.** Broughton Castle, Penshurst Place, West Wycombe Park and Rainham Hall are not part of the same cluster as far as the reporting indicates, so look up their locations before assuming you can do several in one day.
- **Pick by mood.** Want romance? Penshurst Place and West Wycombe Park carry the dance and ball scenes. Want cottagecore? Ditsworthy Warren House and Mothecombe Beach.
- **Rewatch first.** Matching a doorway or a view to a scene is half the fun of a filming-location trip.

## Why this trip works now

Per Cosmopolitan, Jane Austen adaptations keep driving screen tourism, as viewers look for atmospheric places tied to period dramas they love, and many of these locations are open to visitors. That makes this a rare film-inspired itinerary where you can actually go inside.

Your first move: pick the one location that matches your favorite scene, check its visiting details, and build the rest of the trip around it.
