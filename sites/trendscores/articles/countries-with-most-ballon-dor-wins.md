Argentina and France are tied at the top of the men's Ballon d'Or table, with eight awards each since the prize began in 1956. That's the headline from an Al Jazeera breakdown published as this year's announcement approaches. Below you'll find the full ranking, the story behind the leaders, and a clear sense of which nations could move up when the next winner is named.

## The full ranking by country

Here is the nations leaderboard as Al Jazeera reports it:

1. Argentina: 8
2. France: 8
3. Portugal: 7
4. Germany: 7
5. Netherlands: 7
6. Brazil: 5
7. England: 5
8. Italy: 5
9. Spain: 4

The top five are separated by just one award. Three of those nations (Portugal, Germany and the Netherlands) sit level on seven. Then comes a gap down to Brazil, England and Italy on five, with Spain on four.

## Argentina's total comes from one player

The most striking detail in the table is how Argentina got there. Al Jazeera notes that Lionel Messi has won all eight of his country's prizes. In other words, a nation tied for first place owes its entire total to a single career.

That makes Argentina's position unusual. Other countries on the list built their totals across different players and eras. Argentina's rests on one man, which also means it is unlikely to grow unless a new Argentine challenger emerges.

## Portugal's seven, and Ronaldo's part in them

Portugal sits third with seven. Al Jazeera's description credits Cristiano Ronaldo with five Ballon d'Or awards. The report breakdown doesn't list who won Portugal's other two in the material we have, so we won't guess. What the numbers do show is that Ronaldo accounts for most of Portugal's total, a pattern that echoes Argentina's reliance on Messi.

## Why the table could shift this year

Al Jazeera frames this year's contenders as a test of whether the award's national power map changes. The names it highlights are:

- **Lionel Messi** (Argentina)
- **Ousmane Dembele** (France)
- **Harry Kane** (England)
- **Lamine Yamal** (Spain)
- **Rodri** (Spain)

Here is what each could mean for the standings, based on the table above. This part is our analysis, not a prediction.

### France could take sole possession of first

If Dembele wins, France would reach nine and move ahead of Argentina on its own. Messi winning again would do the same for Argentina, so the top spot is genuinely in play.

### England's long wait

England has five awards. A win for Kane would make it six and lift it clear of Italy and Brazil, though neither Italy nor Brazil would be affected in any other way. For England, it would be the first step toward closing the gap on the leaders.

### Spain has two routes up

Spain is on four, and both Yamal and Rodri are named as contenders. Either could take Spain to five and level it with Brazil, England and Italy. Because two Spaniards are in the conversation, the country is well placed to improve its standing.

## The passing-of-the-torch question

Al Jazeera points out that Messi and Ronaldo's dominance is fading. That matters for the table because those two players account for the bulk of the totals for Argentina and Portugal. As their era winds down, the award's balance of power has more room to move than it has in years.

The tight spread helps. With five countries within one award of each other at the top, a single result changes who leads. That's rare in a prize with a history stretching back to 1956.
