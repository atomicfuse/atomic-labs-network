Seven weeks into the 2026-27 club season, Fox Sports has ranked the top 20 teams in world soccer, and Manchester City sits at No. 1, just ahead of Barcelona. If you want a quick read on who is strong right now, and which names might surprise you, this breakdown gives you the order and the logic behind it.

Per Fox Sports, the ranking weighs domestic form, Champions League outlook, squad strength, and momentum. The title in the original piece carries a hedge worth keeping: City is ahead of Barcelona "for now."

## The Top Five: City, Barcelona, and Three Heavyweights

Here is how Fox Sports orders the top of the list:

1. Manchester City
2. FC Barcelona
3. Arsenal
4. Bayern Munich
5. Paris Saint-Germain

The City-Barcelona call is the headline. Barcelona is unbeaten this season, which makes the case for No. 1 easy to argue. City gets the nod anyway, which suggests the ranking leans on overall strength and standing as an elite side rather than on the unbeaten record alone. That is my read of the list, not a reason Fox Sports spelled out in the material I have.

Arsenal, Bayern, and PSG round out the top five, a reminder of how crowded the top of the European game is.

## The Next Tier: Inter, Atlético, Dortmund, and Real Madrid

Positions six through nine are:

- 6. Inter Milan
- 7. Atlético Madrid
- 8. Borussia Dortmund
- 9. Real Madrid

Real Madrid at No. 9 is the placement most fans will argue about. Real Madrid and Liverpool have both been uneven this season, and both have been ranked despite those slow starts. Liverpool lands lower still, at No. 16.

If you judge clubs on reputation, those spots look harsh. If you judge on the first seven weeks, they are easier to defend. This is exactly the kind of list where the eye test and the table disagree.

Several clubs outside the traditional giants earn spots:

Brighton at No. 10 sits above Chelsea (13), Roma (14), Monaco (15), and Liverpool (16). Freiburg and Real Betis also rank ahead of Chelsea. Whether Brighton, Brentford, and Freiburg are real contenders or early mirages is a fair question, and the next few weeks will start to answer it.

Club soccer is back after the international break, and the coming Champions League matchdays will test whether the early-season form holds. With Barcelona unbeaten, City still elite, and Real Madrid and Liverpool inconsistent, the balance of power looks unusually fluid.
