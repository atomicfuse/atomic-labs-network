Rewatching Lionel Messi in Argentina's blue and white can feel like standing in front of a library with no catalogue. Where do you start? The New York Times, in a piece on its writers' favourite Argentina moments, framed the problem this way: Copa America wondergoals, World Cup final brilliance, or all the way back to the Under-20s?

The outlet's description gives us the map, and that's what this guide uses. It doesn't give a full ranked list, and I won't invent one. Instead, here is a practical way to watch Messi's international career in three stages, so you finish knowing what each era shows you.

## Why watch it in order

Per the Times, the story runs from his Under-20 days through to his World Cup triumph, with Copa America goals in between. Watching chronologically beats chasing a highlights reel, because the real pleasure is seeing one player change shape over years.

The Times' headline captures the arc in two phrases: "floppy-haired brilliance" at one end and "World Cup heroics" at the other. Treat those as your bookends.

## Stage one: the floppy-haired era

Start with the Under-20s. This is the prodigy phase, and the Times points to it as part of the conversation about his best moments in blue and white.

**What to look for:**

- How young he looks next to everyone else, and how little that seems to matter.
- The way he receives the ball and decides what to do with it.
- The raw material of everything that came later.

This is my read, not the Times': early clips work best when you don't judge them against the finished product. Watch them as a preview.

## Stage two: the Copa America wondergoals

The Times specifically flags Copa America wondergoals as one of the places to start. That makes the continental tournaments a natural middle chapter. Here you're watching Messi as the established senior star, carrying the weight of a national team.

**A simple way to do it:**

1. Pick one Copa America tournament.
2. Search for its goals only.
3. Watch each twice: once for the finish, once for the 10 seconds before it.

That second viewing is where the craft shows. A wondergoal is usually the end of a decision made earlier.

## Stage three: the World Cup final

The Times description names World Cup final brilliance and, in its summary, treats his World Cup victory as the point that cemented his place as Argentina's greatest player. Save this for last.

By now you'll have seen the prodigy and the tournament star. The final lands differently when you know the road behind it. Watch it in full if you can, not just the goals. The key question to ask yourself: what does a player who has done everything look like when the biggest game of all arrives?

## Why this matters for 2026 viewers

With the 2026 FIFA World Cup on the horizon, the Times' tags tie Messi's story directly to the tournament. Whatever role he plays there, Argentina's past is the context for every match they play. Knowing the three stages above means you'll recognise what you're seeing, and what's being referenced, when commentators reach for history.

- **Evening two:** One Copa America tournament's goals, around 30 minutes.
