Val Chmerkovskiy has been suspended from *Dancing With the Stars* after a since-deleted TikTok appeared to reference Will Smith's Oscars slap, per Fox News. The post came in the wake of judge Carrie Ann Inaba's criticism of his wife Jenna Johnson's routine. ABC says it is the first known suspension of a professional dancer in the show's history.

That last detail is the real headline. Ballroom drama is nothing new, but this is a line the show had apparently never needed to draw for a pro until now.

## What We Know So Far

Here is what the reporting supports. Chmerkovskiy posted a TikTok that appeared to nod to the Will Smith Oscars incident, according to Fox News. The post was a reaction to Inaba's critique of Johnson's routine. It was later deleted. The report also details the backlash, an apology from Chmerkovskiy, and how the show plans to handle his absence.

Note the word "appeared." The coverage describes the post as appearing to reference the slap, so that is how it should be read: as an apparent reference, not a confirmed statement of intent. I'm not going to guess at what he meant beyond that.

## The Angle: A Deleted Post, a Very Undeleted Precedent

The question everyone will argue about is whether the punishment fits the offense. My take is that the suspension matters less as a verdict on one TikTok than as a marker of where reality TV now sets its limits.

Consider the sequence. A spouse feels a judge was unfair to his partner. That is the oldest emotion in competition television, and *DWTS* has built a franchise on pros bristling at the judges' table. Defending your partner or your wife is practically a genre convention. What changed here, it seems, was the choice of reference. The 2022 Oscars slap is one of those cultural moments that still carries weight years later, and invoking it in connection with a real judge criticizing a real person's work puts the joke somewhere very different from a pointed eyebrow raise.

It's hard not to wonder whether the deletion made things better or worse. Pulling a post is a nod to second thoughts, but screenshots outlive deletions, and a since-deleted post is usually the one everyone is still discussing a week later.

## Why a First Is a Big Deal

ABC's statement that this is the first known suspension of a pro in *DWTS* history reframes the story. The show has run for many seasons, with plenty of feuds, tense critiques, and pro-versus-judge sniping, and apparently none of it triggered a suspension before. That suggests either that this post crossed a line previous ones didn't, or that the show's tolerance has shifted. The timing suggests some of both, though that is my read and not something the reporting establishes.

There is also the broader climate. Social posts and off-camera conduct now turn into storylines fast, and productions seem increasingly willing to act on them. A pro dancer is both an employee and a public face of a network brand, and a TikTok blurs that line in a way a heated rehearsal-room moment never did.

## Fair or Too Harsh?

Both sides have a case.

**The case it's fair:** The post appeared to invoke an incident of real violence at a live televised event, aimed in context at a colleague on the same show. A network protecting its judges from that kind of implied comparison is not a wild position. Chmerkovskiy's apology, per Fox News, also suggests he saw the problem.

**The case it's harsh:** The post was deleted and apologized for. Fans of the show may feel that a suspension, a first-ever one at that, is a heavy response for a social media misstep from someone defending his wife in the moment. Precedent cuts both ways: a first suspension looks like either a necessary line or an overcorrection, depending on your loyalties.

I lean toward thinking the novelty is doing a lot of the work here. When a punishment has no precedent, it inevitably gets read as a statement, whether or not the network intended one.

## What Happens Next

Fox News reports on how the show will handle Chmerkovskiy's absence, and that will be the practical story to watch as the season continues. Johnson's own run in the competition now sits in the middle of a very public conversation, which is an awkward place to be for a dancer whose routine started it all.

For viewers, the takeaway is simple. The *DWTS* ballroom has always had its share of drama, but the rules of engagement for pros online are clearly being tightened. The judges' table has survived harsh scores for years. What it, and the network behind it, apparently won't tolerate is a punchline built on the most infamous slap in recent awards history.

Maybe the real lesson is the oldest one in showbiz: if you're going to clap back, make sure the reference isn't the headline.
