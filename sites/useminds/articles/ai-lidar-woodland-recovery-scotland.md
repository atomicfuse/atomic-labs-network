Scotland's Cairngorms are witnessing a remarkable natural woodland recovery thanks to cutting-edge technology and smarter deer management. A Cambridge-led research team has harnessed AI-supported airborne LiDAR mapping to document this success, revealing over 6 million trees across an area of 569 km². This study not only highlights the ecological benefits of such recoveries but also provides a scalable monitoring model for nature recovery projects across the UK and beyond.

## Unlocking the Secrets of LiDAR Mapping
LiDAR, which stands for Light Detection and Ranging, is an astonishing technology that uses laser pulses to create high-resolution maps of the Earth's surface. This powerful tool can penetrate forest canopies, enabling researchers to identify and quantify trees hidden beneath a dense layer of foliage. In the Cairngorms, LiDAR mapping has proven invaluable, allowing scientists to unveil more than 5,235 hectares of previously open land that now support vigorous young woodlands.

## The Role of Deer Management
One key factor contributing to this successful woodland expansion is effective deer management. Overabundant deer populations can hinder forest recovery by grazing young trees and saplings. By implementing sensible deer management strategies, land managers have allowed native trees to regenerate naturally. This approach not only aids in ecological restoration but also contributes to important outcomes such as increased carbon capture, enhanced biodiversity, and improved climate resilience.
