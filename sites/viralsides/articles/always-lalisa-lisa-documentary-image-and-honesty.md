## How to watch it with a critical eye

Celebrity documentaries can be honest, marketing, or both at once. A few questions help you tell where *Always Lalisa* lands:

### Who gets to be seen at their worst?

If a film only shows struggle that ends in a neat redemption, be skeptical. Real accountability is usually slower and less flattering than a third-act apology.

### Does the apology come with specifics?

The BBC frames the scandal as a moment of accountability. When you watch, notice whether Lisa addresses what actually happened and why it landed badly, or whether it stays at general regret. That difference is what separates a statement from learning.

### Who benefits from the release timing?

The film arrives as Lisa moves into a new solo phase. It's fair to wonder how much a candid portrait also serves a career relaunch. That's my take, not a claim about anyone's intent, and both things can be true: a film can be genuinely revealing and still help the person in it.
