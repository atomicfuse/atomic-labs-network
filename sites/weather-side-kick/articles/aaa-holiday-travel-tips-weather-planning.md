## Why Weather Belongs in Your Holiday Plan

Crowds are predictable. Weather isn't. A busy travel day with clear skies is an annoyance. A busy travel day with rain, snow, fog or high wind can mean long delays, closed lanes or canceled flights.

That's why a good holiday plan has two layers: the crowd plan (when to go, what to book) and the weather plan (what could change it).

## A Simple Weather Check for Road Trips

If you're driving, check conditions along your whole route, not just where you start and finish. A clear morning at home doesn't tell you much about a mountain pass or a city three states away.

- **Check the forecast for each stretch.** Look up a few cities along your route and the time you'll likely pass through them.
- **Use official sources.** The National Weather Service (weather.gov) is the place for forecasts and alerts in the U.S. If a warning or advisory is active along your route, read it in full.
- **Check again before you leave.** Forecasts shift. A look the night before and another right before you walk out the door takes two minutes.
- **Know the alert wording.** A "watch" means conditions could develop. A "warning" means they're happening or about to. Treat a warning as a reason to change plans.
- **Pack for delays.** Water, snacks, phone charger, blanket and a full tank or charge cost almost nothing and help a lot if you're stuck.

## A Simple Weather Check for Flying

Airports are only part of the picture. Weather at your departure city, your arrival city and any connection can all cause trouble.

- Check the forecast for every airport on your itinerary.
- Watch your airline's app for delay and rebooking notices, especially if storms are in the forecast.
- If you have a connection, a longer layover gives you more cushion when weather slows things down.
- Pack medications, chargers and a change of clothes in your carry-on in case your plans stretch out.

## Think About Timing Before You Think About Price

One of the angles in AAA's coverage is when to drive or fly during holiday week. We don't have AAA's specific recommended windows, so look those up on AAA's site. What you can do is layer weather on top of them: if the best travel window lines up with a storm, a slightly less ideal day with calm weather may be the smarter pick.

Flexibility is the most valuable thing you can carry. If you can leave a few hours earlier or a day later, you have options when the forecast turns.

## Why Cruises Are on the Rise, and Where Weather Fits

CBS News reports that AAA expects cruises to be a major travel choice in 2027. The report ties that to a broader shift toward bundled, easier-to-plan trips, where lodging, transportation and meals come in one package.

Weather still matters on the water, though in a different way. Seasonal patterns and storm activity in the regions you'd sail can affect itineraries. If a cruise is on your list for next year, check typical weather for your destination and travel dates before you book, and ask about the cruise line's policy if conditions force a change.

## Mistakes That Make Holiday Trips Harder

These aren't from AAA's report; they're common-sense weather-minded habits that fit any holiday trip.

- **Checking the forecast once.** Check it at booking, the day before and the day of.
- **Ignoring the destination.** Your route can be clear while your arrival city is not.
- **Skipping a buffer.** Build extra time in on both ends.
- **Leaving with a low tank or battery.** Delays and detours happen.
- **Assuming an alert is exaggerated.** If officials issue a warning, take it seriously.

## Your Next Step

Before you pack the car or head to the airport, read AAA's full holiday travel guidance, then open weather.gov and check every stop on your route. Do it again the morning you leave. That's the cheapest way to avoid a bad travel day.
