## Why small homes can carry a big kitchen

Mobile homes and compact houses are easy to overlook. Modernluxury's story shows the opposite case: an overlooked home can become a showpiece when the design starts with the people living in it.

If you're working with limited square footage, ask these questions first:

1. Who cooks, and how many at once? A kitchen for three generations needs room for several people to move without colliding.
2. Where do people naturally gather? In many homes, that's the kitchen whether you planned it or not.
3. What do you actually use daily? Design around those items and let the rest go.

## Think about weather before you pick anything

This is where a coastal kitchen differs from an inland one. Living near the ocean means salt in the air, strong sun and humidity that changes through the day. We can't say which choices the Malibu designers made, but these are practical weather questions for any beachside remodel.

### Salt air and humidity

Moist, salty air can be hard on metal finishes and hardware. When you shop, ask the manufacturer whether a faucet, handle or fixture is rated for coastal or high-humidity use. It's a simple question that can save you a replacement later.

### Sun and glare

Strong afternoon sun can fade surfaces and turn a bright kitchen into a hot one. Note which direction your windows face and when the sun hits them. That tells you where shade, window coverings or sun-tolerant materials matter most.

### Ventilation

Cooking adds moisture and heat. In a small coastal home, being able to open windows and move air is part of the design, not an afterthought. A good range hood and cross-breezes help keep things comfortable.

### Wind and open doors

If your kitchen opens to a patio or deck, think about a breezy day. Lightweight items, paper and napkins go airborne fast. Plan some closed storage near the door.

## Designing for three generations

A kitchen for grandparents, parents and kids has to work for very different people. A few general principles apply, though they are our suggestions, not details from the Malibu project:

- Keep walkways clear so people of different ages and speeds can move easily.
- Put everyday dishes and snacks within reach of both kids and older adults.
- Leave a spot where someone can sit and chat while another person cooks.
- Choose surfaces that forgive spills and sandy feet.

## Make a small space feel generous

The appeal of this project, per Modernluxury, is the sense of a space that's warm, personal and connected to its setting. You can chase that feeling at any budget:

- Let the view in. If your home is near water, keep sightlines open so the outdoors feels like part of the room.
- Pick one or two things to splurge on and keep the rest simple.
- Use light colors and natural light to make a compact room feel larger.
- Make the kitchen the heart of the home, not a corridor.

## Before you start your own remodel

Check your local National Weather Service forecast before scheduling any work that opens up walls, roofs or windows. Rain, high wind or a heat wave can delay a job or damage exposed materials. In coastal areas, ask your contractor how they protect the site during sudden weather changes.

The takeaway: start with who uses the space and what the local climate will do to it, then choose finishes and layout to match. Do that, and even a small, overlooked home can become the place everyone wants to be.
