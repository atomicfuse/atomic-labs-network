A living room in Southern California's Mandeville Canyon just earned a Design 9 Award, and the idea behind it is easy to borrow: a polished room can still be built for everyday family life. Per Modern Luxury, the architecture and design firm Marmol Radziner used softer silhouettes and left plenty of room for family time. Below are the takeaways you can use in your own living room, whatever your budget or climate.

A quick note on scope. Modern Luxury's write-up, as summarized here, gives us the big picture of the project rather than a room-by-room inventory. So this article sticks to the design direction it describes and offers our own practical analysis on how to apply it.

## What the award-winning room is going for

The project is a Mandeville Canyon residence designed by Marmol Radziner. According to Modern Luxury, the living room favors softer silhouettes and is built around family time. The coverage frames it as part of a broader shift in high-end residential design: warmer, more livable, and less rigid, with comfort and gathering taking priority over a room made for show.

That's the core lesson. Elegance and everyday use aren't opposites.

## Why soft silhouettes work for real life

"Silhouette" simply means the outline of a piece of furniture or architectural feature. Soft silhouettes have gentler, more rounded edges rather than sharp corners and hard lines.

The following are our observations on why that approach suits a busy household, not details reported from the project:

- **Fewer sharp corners.** Rounded edges are friendlier to kids, pets and guests moving through the room.
- **A calmer look.** Curved forms tend to read as relaxed, which suits a room meant for lingering.
- **Easier flow.** Rounded pieces can make walking paths feel less boxed in.

## How to apply it in your own living room

You don't need a canyon-view house to use the approach. Start with small, doable changes.

### Swap one hard-edged piece for a softer one

If your coffee table has square corners, consider a round or oval version. Of all the changes here, this one tends to have the biggest effect for the least effort.

### Arrange seating for conversation

A room meant for family time works best when people can face each other. Pull seating closer together, and make sure every seat has a spot to set down a drink or a book.

### Keep the polish, lose the "don't touch" feeling

Choose durable, comfortable pieces you won't worry about using. A room feels untouchable when people are afraid to sit in it, so pick things you're happy to see used.

### Leave open floor space

Family time often happens on the floor: games, puzzles, a pile of pillows. Resist filling every square foot with furniture.

## What Southern California gets right about relaxed elegance

The takeaway from Modern Luxury's framing is that sophistication doesn't require stiffness. That idea is often associated with California design, and it works anywhere. A room can look composed and still be the place where everyone ends up on a Tuesday night.

## A weather-minded footnote

Since you're reading this on a weather site, one practical tie-in: living rooms get more use when the weather keeps everyone indoors. Rainy weekends, cold snaps and hot afternoons all send families to the same room. If you're planning a refresh, think about how the space will work on those long indoor days, not just how it looks in photos.

## The takeaway

This week, pick one hard-edged or off-limits piece in your living room and replace it with something softer and more comfortable. Then see how much longer everyone stays in the room.
