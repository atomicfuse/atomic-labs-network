Stuck inside on a rainy weekend with nothing to do but stare at your living room? Good time for a quick styling audit. Per a HomeDecor roundup picked up by Yahoo, interior designers tend to notice five common mistakes almost immediately, and each one is cheap and quick to fix. The main message: a home looks better when its choices feel intentional, personal, and properly scaled.

This is a styling piece, not a forecast. But a gray, wet day is a natural moment to rearrange a shelf or rehang a wall, so here is the list and what to do about each item.

## 1. Hanging too many small pieces of art

A wall of tiny frames scattered at random can read as clutter instead of a collection. The issue is scale: small pieces get lost on a big wall.

**Try this:**
- Swap several small pieces for one larger piece that fits the wall.
- If you love your small pieces, group them tightly so they read as one unit.
- Hold the art against the wall first, or mark the spot with painter's tape, before you hammer anything in.

## 2. Relying on so-so pillows

Limp, forgettable throw pillows are one of the quickest ways a sofa looks unfinished. The HomeDecor piece flags "so-so" pillows as a problem designers spot fast.

**Try this:**
- Replace the flattest, saggiest pillows first.
- Choose pillows you actually like, not whatever matched the set.
- Fewer good pillows beat a pile of mediocre ones.

This is also the cheapest fix on the list, which makes it a good place to start.

## 3. Choosing mass-market art only

The article's point is not that all store-bought art is bad. It's that a home filled only with mass-market pieces can feel generic. As homes get styled for social media, many risk looking alike instead of lived-in.

**Try this:**
- Mix in something with a story: a vintage find, a piece from a trip, or something meaningful to you.
- Keep a store-bought piece if you love it, but let it share the wall with something more personal.

This fits the broader shift the piece describes toward personality-driven interiors, vintage finds, and meaningful decor over copy-paste trends.

## 4. Turning books backwards

Books turned spine-in have become a familiar styling move, but the roundup lists it as a mistake designers notice. Our read: when the spines face out, a shelf looks like it belongs to a real reader. Backwards books look like props.

**Try this:**
- Turn the spines back out.
- Mix in objects you care about, so the shelf feels lived-in rather than staged.

## 5. Treating every shelf the same with trendy items

The fifth mistake is styling every shelf with the same trendy items. Repetition makes a bookcase feel like a catalog page instead of your home.

**Try this:**
- Give each shelf its own character instead of repeating one formula.
- Use personal objects, not just whatever is trending.
- Remove one trendy item per shelf and replace it with something that means something to you.

## How to run your own five-minute audit

Walk into your main room and ask five questions:

1. Does the art fit the size of the wall?
2. Would I buy these pillows again?
3. Is anything here personal, not just popular?
4. Are my books facing the right way?
5. Do my shelves all look identical?

Any "no" is your first project.

## The takeaway

Pick one mistake from the list, fix it today, and let the rest wait for the next gray afternoon. One intentional change does more than a full redo of everything at once.
