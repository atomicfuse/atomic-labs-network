## How to set up a guest-room coffee station

These are our own setup suggestions, built around the compact-machine idea.

### 1. Pick the right surface

Choose a sturdy, flat spot near an outlet. A dresser top, a narrow console, or a desk corner works well. Keep it away from the edge so a sleepy guest doesn't knock anything over.

### 2. Keep it away from the bed and any water-sensitive surfaces

Place a small tray or mat under the machine to catch drips. This protects wood finishes and makes cleanup easy after the weekend.

### 3. Fill the water before guests arrive

Top off the reservoir so the first cup is effortless. Add a short note about how to run it, since not everyone has used a pod machine.

### 4. Stock a small, tidy selection

A handful of pods, a few mugs, spoons, sugar, and creamer in a small basket or on a tray looks intentional. Offering a decaf option is a thoughtful touch for evening drinkers.

### 5. Add a few finishing touches

A folded napkin, a small dish for used pods, and a card with the Wi-Fi password turn a corner of the room into a mini hotel amenity.

Homes & Gardens' angles also point to bedrooms, offices, and small kitchens as good homes for a compact Keurig. If you buy one for guests, it doesn't have to sit idle between visits. Moving it to a home office or a tight kitchen counter keeps it useful year-round.

A $45 discount is a real saving on a small appliance, and the timing lines up with holiday hosting. Before your next overnight guest arrives, check the forecast, pick a clear dresser corner, and set out a filled machine with a few pods and mugs. That one small station does more for a cold-morning welcome than most decor upgrades.
