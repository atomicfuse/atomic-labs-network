Holiday travel is crowded and prone to disruption, and a bad weather day can turn a tight itinerary into a mess. Time Out's editors put together 11 tried-and-true tips for making the trip less stressful, cheaper and calmer. The weather side is our addition: several of their tips double as protection against storms, so we've flagged where. Pick the three or four that fit your trip and build your plan around them.

One caveat up front. Time Out's list came with headlines only in the material we had, so we're not going to invent the editors' reasoning. Where we explain why a tip helps, that's our own analysis, and we'll say so.

## The tips that protect you when weather goes sideways

These are the ones we'd put first, because they build slack into your trip. Slack is what you want when a storm, fog or ice slows things down.

### 1. Plan a buffer day (or two) around travel

Time Out's first tip is to build a day or two of cushion around your trip. Our take: this is the best single defense against a weather delay. If a storm cancels your flight, a buffer day means you miss nothing important, and a rebooked flight doesn't wreck the holiday.

### 2. Leave at least two hours between connections

Time Out recommends at least two hours between connecting flights. Our take: a short connection leaves no room for a late inbound plane, and weather is a common reason flights run behind. Two hours gives you a chance to make the next leg even when the first one slips.

### 3. Travel on the actual holiday day if you can

Another Time Out tip: travel on the holiday itself if your schedule allows. Our take: when you're not moving with everyone else, you have more flexibility. Check the forecast for that specific day before you commit, though, because flexibility only helps if conditions cooperate.

### 4. Schedule your ride

Time Out suggests scheduling your ride rather than leaving it to chance. If you're heading to the airport in rain, snow or cold, a booked ride removes one variable. Our suggestion: confirm it the night before, and glance at the forecast so you know whether to leave earlier.

## The tips that make delays easier to live with

Even with good planning, you may end up waiting. These tips are about being comfortable and fed when that happens.

### 5. Bring your own airport food

Time Out lists bringing your own food as a tip, and it pairs with another entry on the list: "Airport charcuterie." We only have the headline for that one, so we can't tell you exactly what the editors recommend. The common thread is obvious, though: don't rely on an overpriced terminal when your flight is delayed and you're hungry.

### 6. BYO…W.

The list includes a tip titled "BYO…W." We don't have the editors' explanation, so we won't guess at what the W stands for. Check Time Out's original piece for that one.

### 7. Moisturize

Time Out's list includes a reminder to moisturize. Cold winter air and cabin air both tend to be dry, so tossing a small tube of lotion or lip balm in your bag is an easy move. That reasoning is ours, not the editors'.

### 8. Don't pack what you can get delivered instead

Time Out suggests having items delivered rather than packing them. Our take: a lighter bag is easier to carry through a snowy parking lot or a long gate change, and it leaves room for the winter layers you actually need.

## The big-picture options

The last two tips on the list step back from logistics entirely.

### 9. Celebrate the holiday on another day

Time Out suggests shifting your celebration to a different date. If the forecast looks rough around the traditional day, or flights and fares are painful, moving the gathering a few days can take most of the pressure off.

### 10. Just don't travel

The editors' final tip is a blunt one: skip the trip. Our take: when a major storm is forecast along your route, staying home is a legitimate plan, not a failure. Check your area's official forecast and any active alerts from the National Weather Service before you decide.

## How to pick your tips by trip type

You don't need all of them. Here's a quick way to choose.

- **Flying with a connection:** buffer day, two-hour layover, bring your own food.
- **Driving:** schedule your timing around the forecast, travel on the holiday itself if you can, and pack light enough to keep the trunk organized.
- **Traveling with family:** consider celebrating on another day, and carry snacks and moisturizer for everyone.
- **Facing a major storm:** weigh the last two options seriously.

Time Out's list counts 11 tips; we've covered the ones the editors' headlines let us speak to, and a couple remain best read in the original.

## Check the forecast before you lock anything in

Holiday plans are easier to change early than late. Look at the National Weather Service forecast for both your departure city and your destination several days out, and again the night before. If a storm shows up, use your buffer day, rebook sooner rather than later, or move the celebration. The best travel tip is the one you act on before the delay happens.
