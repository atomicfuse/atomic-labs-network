## What a king tide actually is

A king tide is a especially high tide. It's a normal, predictable part of the tide cycle that happens when the alignment of the sun, moon, and Earth pushes water levels higher than usual. On a calm, dry day, a king tide may mean water creeping up over shoreline edges.

The problem is stacking. When a storm arrives on top of a very high tide, rain has a harder time draining, and wind and waves can push water farther inland. That combination is why this week's setup draws more concern than either factor alone. That explanation is general background, not a detail from HuffPost's report.

## How to plan around flood risk

### Before you leave

- Check the National Weather Service forecast and any active coastal flood advisories for your exact route and destination.
- Look up the high tide times for the area. Flooding tends to be worst around the peak, so timing your trip away from it can help.
- Check your airline or transit agency for delays before heading to the airport or station.
- Ask yourself whether the trip is necessary. The mayor's request is to reconsider travel, so moving a gathering or leaving a day earlier or later may be reasonable.

### On the road

- Don't drive through standing water. You can't tell how deep it is, and even shallow water can stall a car.
- Choose routes away from the waterfront and low spots when you have the option.
- Leave extra time, and keep your gas tank or battery well charged.
- Follow local detour and closure signs rather than navigation apps alone.

### What to keep in the car

- Phone charger and a portable battery
- Water and snacks
- A flashlight
- Warm layers and a rain jacket
- Any medications you'd need if you were delayed

## If you live near the water

Residents are being urged to prepare, per the report. Sensible steps include moving valuables and electronics off the floor in ground-level rooms, clearing nearby storm drains of leaves, and knowing where you'd go if you needed to leave. Keep your phone charged and enable local emergency alerts.
