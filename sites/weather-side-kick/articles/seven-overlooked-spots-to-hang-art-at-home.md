When the forecast says rain all weekend, you have a built-in excuse to finally do that small home project. Here's an easy one: hang art in places you've never thought to put it. Per Good Housekeeping, unconventional spots can add surprise, warmth, and personality beyond the standard framed print over the sofa. Think of it as hiding little treasures around your home.

Below are the seven spots Good Housekeeping highlights, plus some practical ways to try each one. You don't need a renovation, just a free afternoon.

## Why a Rainy Day Suits This Project

Small design choices are a low-cost way to make a home feel curated, and they fit well with thrifted art, collected objects, and a more lived-in style. They also take little time and few supplies. If you're stuck indoors, you can dig through a closet, rearrange a few things, and see the result right away.

## 1. On a Kitchen Shelf

Instead of hanging art on the wall, lean it on a kitchen shelf among your dishes and everyday items. It turns a purely functional spot into something that looks designed.

- Choose a small piece so it doesn't crowd the shelf.
- Lean it against the back wall, and let a bowl or jar overlap the edge slightly.
- Keep it away from the stove and sink, where steam and splashes could damage it.

That last tip is my own common-sense advice, not from the source, but kitchens are humid, so it's worth thinking about.

## 2. Conceal Your TV

A television is often the biggest dark rectangle in a room. The idea here is to use art to cover it up. Some people do this with a frame that sits over the screen, but check the product details and your setup before buying anything, since this is a hardware question the source doesn't detail.

Even a simpler approach helps: place art near the TV so the screen stops being the only focal point in the room.

## 3. Above a Door Frame

The space over a doorway is usually empty. Hanging art there draws the eye upward and makes a passage feel intentional.

- Pick lightweight pieces, since you'll be reaching overhead.
- Use a secure hanger and check that it's level from a few steps back.
- Slim, wide pieces tend to suit the shape of the space, which is my suggestion rather than part of the source.

## 4. Tucked Into the Bathroom

Bathrooms are often the plainest room in the house, and that's exactly why art can make a difference. A small piece adds personality in a place guests do notice.

Because showers create moisture, keep art out of the direct path of steam, and consider a frame with glass. Rainy, humid weather can add to indoor dampness, so a spot farther from the shower is the safer choice.

## 5. Beneath a Staircase

The awkward wall under a staircase is easy to ignore. Art here makes a forgotten corner feel considered, rather than leftover space.

- Measure the wall before you shop, since the angled ceiling limits height.
- Try a few small frames grouped together instead of one large piece.
- Lay everything on the floor first to plan the arrangement before making holes.

## 6. In a Cupboard

This is the most "hidden treasure" idea on the list. Place art inside a cupboard so it appears when you open the door. It's a private little surprise for you, and a fun discovery for anyone who looks in.

It's also a good option if you rent and don't want to put holes in walls. Propping a piece inside a cupboard or taping lightly (check what your finishes can handle) keeps things low-commitment.

## 7. On the Face of a Bookshelf

Rather than only filling shelves with books, place art on the front of the bookshelf. It layers the look and breaks up rows of spines.

- Lean a small frame in front of a stack of books.
- Mix in a collected object or two for a lived-in feel.
- Vary heights so the display doesn't look stiff.

## Make It Work With Thrifted Art

The "unexpected detail" approach pairs well with thrifted finds. Small, inexpensive pieces are ideal for these tucked-away spots because you're not committing to a large statement piece. If you're hunting for art, look for sizes that suit shelves, cupboards, and tight corners.

## Protect Your Art From Weather and Moisture

Since this is a weather site, one practical note: humidity can affect paper and wood over time. Keep pieces away from steamy bathrooms and stove areas, and avoid spots near leaky windows or damp walls, especially during rainy stretches. Glass-fronted frames offer extra protection.

## Start With One Spot

Pick the one place on this list that you walk past every day, grab a small piece of art, and put it there before the rain stops.
