## What Storm Watch Mode Is, in Plain English

Per BASENOR's coverage, Storm Watch Mode is part of how Powerwall prepares for storm-related outages. The idea behind features like this is simple: a backup battery is only useful if it has charge when the lights go out. Getting ready ahead of a storm is the whole point.

## Why Battery Charge Matters Before a Storm

A home battery can't help much at 10% charge. Many owners run their batteries in modes that use stored power to save money or cut grid use. That's fine on a sunny day, but it can leave less in reserve when a storm knocks out power.

That's the problem a storm-focused feature is designed to address: making sure the battery is ready before the weather arrives, not after.

## Check Your Forecast the Official Way

No app replaces the official forecast. In the US, the National Weather Service at weather.gov issues the watches and warnings that matter. Learn the difference:

- **Watch:** Conditions are favorable for the hazard. Prepare now.
- **Warning:** The hazard is happening or imminent. Act now.

Enter your ZIP code at weather.gov to see alerts for your exact location, and note the time zone and the "last updated" time on any alert you read.

## A Pre-Storm Checklist for Homes With Backup Power

These steps apply to a Powerwall or any home battery system. Do them a day or two ahead when a storm is in the forecast.

1. **Open your battery app and check the charge level.** Know the number before the storm, not during it.
2. **Review your backup settings.** Look for any storm-related or backup-reserve option and confirm it's set the way you want.
3. **Confirm app notifications are on.** You want outage and status alerts on your phone.
4. **Charge your devices.** Phones, power banks, and laptops should be full.
5. **Decide what matters most.** Fridge, internet, lights, medical devices. If your battery has limits, you'll want to know your priorities.
6. **Keep non-electric backups handy.** Flashlights, batteries, and a battery-powered or hand-crank radio.

## Don't Forget the Rest of the House

A battery keeps the lights on, but it doesn't protect your yard or roof from the storm itself. Before heavy wind or rain:

- Bring in or tie down patio furniture, planters, and trash bins.
- Clear gutters and downspouts so rain can drain away from the foundation.
- Check for dead branches near the house or power lines, and call a professional for anything near lines.
- Know where your main water shutoff and breaker panel are.
