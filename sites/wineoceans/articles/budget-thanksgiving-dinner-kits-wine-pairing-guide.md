This guide shows you how to do that: how to think about the wine budget, which styles suit a classic Thanksgiving plate, and how to pour with confidence even if you didn't cook a thing from scratch.

## What the under-$70 kits give you

Exact contents and prices vary by store and I won't guess at them here, so check each retailer's current listing for what is included and how many people it feeds.

What matters for wine is the shape of the meal. A traditional Thanksgiving plate is built from the same pieces almost everywhere: roast turkey, stuffing, gravy, cranberry sauce, and sides. Kits tend to follow that template, so the pairing advice below works whichever store you choose.

## Set a wine budget before you shop

Here is a simple way to think about it. If you save money on the meal, you have room to buy a better bottle or a second one. A practical split:

- Plan roughly one bottle for every two to three guests if wine is the main drink at the table.
- Buy two styles, one red and one white, so people can choose.
- Keep one sparkling bottle for the toast or for pouring with appetizers.

You don't need expensive bottles. Thanksgiving food is busy, with sweet, salty, and savory flavors on one plate, and a delicate, costly wine gets lost in it. Fruity, balanced, easygoing wines do better.

## Wines that work with a classic Thanksgiving plate

### Pinot Noir

The go-to red. It's light to medium in body, with red fruit and enough acidity to handle gravy and cranberry sauce without overpowering the turkey. If you only buy one red, make it this.

### Riesling

A off-dry Riesling handles sweet and savory sides well, and its acidity keeps rich stuffing and gravy from feeling heavy. Look for a label that mentions off-dry or a lower alcohol level if you want something gentle.

### Chardonnay

Choose a lightly oaked or unoaked style. It suits the buttery side dishes and roast turkey, and it is a crowd-pleaser for guests who don't like sweeter wines.

### Sparkling wine

Bubbles cut through richness and cleanse your palate between bites. They also work before dinner while you're heating the kit, and a sparkling bottle is a cheap way to make the day feel special.

### Beaujolais

If you want a second red, a Beaujolais is juicy, light, and served slightly cool. It is forgiving with nearly everything on the table.

## Smart hosting shortcuts that don't feel like shortcuts

A prepared meal can still feel personal. A few small touches go a long way:

1. **Heat and plate on your own dishes.** Moving food out of its packaging changes how the meal looks immediately.
2. **Add one thing you make yourself.** A simple salad or a warm bread basket gives the table a homemade note.
3. **Chill whites and sparkling early.** Put them in the fridge the night before. Pull reds out for about 15 minutes before pouring if you like them a little cool.
4. **Open the wine before guests sit.** It removes a task from the busiest moment.
5. **Set out water glasses next to wine glasses.** It helps guests pace themselves over a long meal.

## Small gathering or big table?

With a small group, one bottle of each style may be plenty, and you can afford to spend a bit more per bottle. With a larger group, lean on versatile, fairly priced bottles and pick up an extra sparkling or white, since those tend to disappear fast. Whichever you choose, check how many servings a kit covers before you plan your pours, since the coverage describes the kits generally and serving sizes differ.

## A simple shopping plan

If you're buying the meal and the wine in one trip, use this order: choose your kit first, count your guests, then add one Pinot Noir, one off-dry Riesling or Chardonnay, and one sparkling bottle. Adjust up or down based on headcount. That keeps the wine decision quick and leaves you free to enjoy the day.

The takeaway: let the kit handle the cooking, and spend your attention on two or three well-chosen bottles. That is where a budget Thanksgiving starts to taste like a celebration.
