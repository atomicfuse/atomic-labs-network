In-N-Out CEO Lynsi Snyder has named Arkansas, Mississippi and Oklahoma as possible future states for the California burger chain, per Fox News, while stressing a cautious approach to growth. No dates are attached, and nothing is confirmed. But if a location does land near you, you will want to know what to pour with the meal. This guide gives you a simple way to pair wine with a burger, fries and a shake-adjacent appetite.

## The news in brief

Fox News reported that Snyder described the company's growth as quality-first rather than rapid. The chain, per the report, reached Tennessee last year and Washington state in August 2025, so it has been moving east. Arkansas, Mississippi and Oklahoma are named as possibilities for the coming years, not promises.

That's all the expansion detail we have. What follows is our own pairing advice, not anything In-N-Out or Snyder has said.

## Start with the rule: match weight, then fat

A cheeseburger is rich, salty and a little tangy from sauce and pickles. Good wine for it does two jobs: it has enough body to stand next to the beef, and enough acidity or tannin to cut through the fat.

- **Fat wants acidity or tannin.** Both scrub your palate between bites.
- **Salt softens tannin.** Fries make a firm red feel smoother.
- **Sweet and tangy toppings dislike heavy oak.** Big buttery wines can taste flat against ketchup-style sauce.

## Best reds for a classic cheeseburger

### Zinfandel
Jammy, peppery and generous, Zinfandel is the natural California match. Its ripe fruit plays well with a sweet-savory sauce. Look for bottles in the moderate price range and serve slightly cool.

### Merlot or Cabernet Sauvignon
If your burger is a double with extra cheese, the structure of a Cabernet or a plush Merlot holds up. The fat in the meat softens the tannins, so even a firm bottle drinks easily.

### Pinot Noir
If you prefer a lighter touch, Pinot Noir brings red fruit and bright acidity. It works best with a single patty and plenty of lettuce and tomato.

## Whites and bubbles that work harder than you'd expect

### Sparkling wine with fries
Bubbles and salty fried food are a reliable combination. The fizz cleans the oil off your tongue. Any dry sparkling wine, such as a brut cava or crémant, does the job without a Champagne budget.

### Chardonnay for the cheese
Choose an unoaked or lightly oaked style. Its apple and citrus notes cut through melted cheese without fighting the toppings.

### Dry rosé for warm days
A crisp rosé handles a burger eaten outdoors, and it is forgiving with sauce and onions.

## Match the wine to how you order

1. **Single patty, everything on it:** Pinot Noir or dry rosé.
2. **Double with cheese:** Zinfandel or Merlot.
3. **Grilled onions and extra spread:** Zinfandel or a fruity Syrah.
4. **Lots of fries:** brut sparkling wine.

## Practical tips for burger-night wine

- **Chill your reds for 15 minutes** in the fridge. Takeout burgers tend to be eaten in warm rooms, and cooler reds taste fresher.
- **Skip the expensive bottle.** The food is simple, so a $12 to $20 wine is the better fit.
- **Bring a few glasses.** Even a parking-lot or picnic meal feels better in real glass than a paper cup.
- **Open one bottle, not three.** Pick the style that suits the table and commit.

## Why a slow rollout helps you

Snyder's emphasis on cautious growth, as reported by Fox News, means any new location in these states is likely a way off. That gives you time to find a house burger wine. Try two or three of the bottles above with whatever burger you can get now, and note which one you reach for again.

When the first drive-thru does open near you, you will already know what to bring. Start with a Zinfandel.
