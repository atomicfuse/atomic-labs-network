Las Vegas brunch no longer means eggs and a mimosa. Per a roundup from Las Vegas Weekly, four resorts and restaurants now pitch weekend brunch as a full outing: Pilates by the pool, a live DJ, a room full of games, or a prime rib and Champagne buffet. This guide helps you pick the one that fits your group, based on what that roundup reports.

One note up front: the original piece is a sponsored roundup, and it doesn't include prices, hours or reservation details. Check each venue's own listing before you plan around them.

## If You Want a Wellness Reset: Resorts World

Resorts World offers the most unusual pairing on the list. Per Las Vegas Weekly, you can take Poolside Pulse Pilates at AYU Dayclub and follow it with brunch at FUHU. It's a workout first, then a meal, which makes the food feel earned.

Resorts World also has a second option: a Latin-inspired brunch at ¡VIVA!. If Pilates isn't your thing, that's the route for a flavor-forward meal without the class.

**Best for:** Travelers who want to start Saturday active and still end up at the table.

## If You Want a Party Brunch: STK Las Vegas

STK Las Vegas runs an All You Can Brunch, which the roundup describes as chef-driven bites, cocktails and live DJs. Expect brunch with a nightlife soundtrack rather than a quiet, linen-napkin affair.

**Best for:** Groups celebrating something, or anyone who treats brunch as the opening act of the night.

## If You Want Games With Your Mimosa: Play Playground Las Vegas

Play Playground Las Vegas combines a brunch menu, bottomless mimosas and Bloody Marys, and more than 20 interactive games, per the roundup. The games give the group something to do besides wait on the next round, which helps if your friends aren't all the sit-and-chat type.

**Best for:** Mixed groups who want entertainment built in.

The source doesn't say whether Play Playground is family-friendly or what age rules apply, so confirm that before bringing kids.

## If You Want a Classic Feast: South Point Garden Buffet

South Point's Garden Buffet serves a prime rib and Champagne brunch with global buffet stations, per Las Vegas Weekly. It's the old-school Vegas move: pile a plate, go back, repeat.

**Best for:** Big appetites and anyone who wants to sample many things instead of committing to one entree.

## How to Choose Between Bottomless and Buffet

The two formats reward different habits. These are my take, not the roundup's:

- **Bottomless drinks** (Play Playground's mimosas and Bloody Marys) suit people who plan to sip over a long, social stretch.
- **A buffet with Champagne** (South Point) suits people who care more about variety on the plate than the pace of the pour.
- **All You Can Brunch** (STK) sits in between, with the food and the energy both part of the deal.

Whichever you choose, pace yourself. Alternate each mimosa with water, and eat something substantial early. A brunch that stretches into the afternoon is much better with a clear head.

## A Simple Way to Match the Spot to Your Group

1. **Want to feel good first?** Resorts World.
2. **Want music and a crowd?** STK.
3. **Want something to do between rounds?** Play Playground.
4. **Want to eat until you're happy?** South Point.

If you're deciding between two, the question is simple: do you want to be entertained, or do you want to be fed? Answer that, book that one, and look up the current menu and hours before you go.
