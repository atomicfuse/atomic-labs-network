With just over a week to go until the written PSLE papers, anxiety levels may be peaking for both students and parents. You might be wondering how to support your child effectively without adding to their stress. This article offers you a practical calming checklist to help your child navigate this crucial final week calmly and confidently.

## 1. Create a Consistent Routine  
Establishing a clear daily schedule can provide your child with a sense of security. Set aside specific times for study sessions, breaks, meals, and relaxation. A predictable routine helps eliminate uncertainty and allows students to manage their time efficiently, reducing last-minute panic.

## 2. Encourage Emotional Expression  
Normalize the feelings your child may be experiencing. Spend some time each day checking in: ask open-ended questions about how they are feeling about the exams. Let them express their worries or excitement free of judgment. This will foster a supportive environment, making them feel understood rather than pressured.

## 3. Foster a Calming Environment  
Create a dedicated study space that is free from distractions. Ensure it's well-lit, comfortable, and stocked with all necessary supplies. Consider incorporating calming elements such as plants, soft lighting, or quiet background music. A peaceful atmosphere can significantly enhance focus and reduce anxiety.

## 4. Focus on Positive Reinforcement  
Remind your child of their strengths and past successes. Use affirmations to reinforce their abilities and dedication. Statements like "You've studied hard, and I'm proud of you" can instill a sense of competence and confidence. Positive reinforcement can go a long way in counteracting self-doubt.

## 5. Teach Mindfulness Techniques  
Introduce short mindfulness practices that can help reduce stress. Simple techniques, such as deep breathing exercises or short meditative breaks, can help your child remain centered. Encourage them to take five deep breaths whenever they feel overwhelmed — it's a quick way to reset their mental state.

## 6. Limit Last-Minute Reviews  
While it's natural to want to cram information in the final days, this can lead to increased anxiety. Instead, focus on quality over quantity in the review sessions. Encourage your child to revisit key concepts and practice releasing pressure by embracing mistakes as learning opportunities.

## 7. Regulate Your Own Emotions  
Your emotional state can significantly influence your child’s experience. Maintain serenity and express confidence in your child's preparation. If you feel anxious, take steps to manage your emotions — this might mean practicing your own mindfulness strategies or speaking to other parents for support.

## 8. Build Time for Relaxation  
Incorporate slots in the daily schedule for relaxation and fun. Whether it’s a short walk, watching a favorite show, or playing a game, allowing time for leisure is vital. It helps recharge their mental batteries and reminds them that there’s life outside of exams.

## 9. Prioritize Healthy Habits  
Encourage nutritious meals and sufficient sleep leading up to the exams. Proper nutrition powers the brain, while quality sleep enhances focus and cognitive function. Avoid high-sugar snacks that could lead to energy crashes and irritability.

## 10. Plan for After the Exams  
Discuss what comes next after the PSLE as a way to look forward to the future. Planning a fun outing or celebration after the exams can serve as a motivational booster and provide something exciting to anticipate.

By actively engaging in these calming strategies, you can create a nurturing environment that empowers your child in the last week leading up to the PSLE. This approach will not only help manage their stress but will also enhance their overall confidence. Remember, the goal is to support them in feeling grounded and capable as they work through this crucial phase. Your calm and encouragement are the most valuable tools in their preparation.
