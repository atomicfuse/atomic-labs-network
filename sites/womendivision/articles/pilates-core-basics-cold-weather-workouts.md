When the weather turns and workouts move indoors, you don't need a gym full of equipment or a high-impact class to train your core. You need a plan that is simple, low-impact, and easy to repeat. This guide shows you how to build that kind of routine, and how to choose a Pilates class that fits it.

The idea comes from a recent Fitness Friday segment on CBS News Chicago, where Michael Samuels of JETSET Pilates demonstrated core-strengthening Pilates moves and spotlighted the studio's approach to low-impact fitness. I only have the broad outline of that segment, not a move-by-move breakdown, so I won't pretend to reproduce his routine. What follows is general guidance in the same spirit, and my own framing, not his instructions.

## Why Pilates Fits Colder Months

Colder weather pushes more people indoors, per the segment's framing, and that makes quick, accessible core work especially useful. Pilates suits that moment for a few practical reasons:

- **It's low-impact.** No jumping or pounding, so it's easier on joints.
- **It needs little space.** A mat-sized patch of floor is enough for most core work.
- **It's scalable.** You can keep a move small as a beginner and make it harder as you get stronger.

## Set Up a Short Core Session

Consistency beats length. A short session you actually do will serve you better than an ambitious one you skip. Try this structure as a starting point:

1. **Pick a spot.** Clear a mat-sized area and wear something you can move in comfortably.
2. **Choose three to five core moves.** Stick with beginner-friendly versions. Classic Pilates mat work such as the hundred, single-leg stretch, and dead bug-style leg lowers are common starting points, though these are my suggestions rather than moves confirmed from the segment.
3. **Work slowly.** Controlled movement is the point. If you're rushing, you're probably leaning on momentum instead of your core.
4. **Keep it to about five minutes** on busy days, and extend it when you have time.

## Form Cues That Make Beginner Moves Count

Core training works when you feel it in the right place. A few general cues help:

### Breathe on purpose
Exhale through the hardest part of a move and inhale as you reset. Holding your breath tends to make your neck and shoulders do the work.

### Keep your movements smaller than you think
A small, controlled range with steady abdominal engagement beats a big, sloppy one. Reduce the range if your lower back starts to take over.

### Stop before form breaks
When you can no longer keep control, stop or modify. Muscle fatigue is fine; pain is a signal to back off. If you have a back issue, injury, or are pregnant, check with a healthcare provider before starting a new routine.

## What to Expect in a Studio Class

If you'd rather learn in person, a studio class gives you something a video can't: someone watching your form. I can't describe JETSET Pilates' specific class format beyond its low-impact focus, so ask any studio you're considering these questions:

- Is there a beginner or intro class?
- How large are the classes, and does the instructor offer individual corrections?
- Is the class mat-based, equipment-based, or both?
- Can you modify moves for injuries or limited experience?

Going in with those answers makes it much easier to choose a class you'll stick with.

## Pair Pilates With the Rest of Your Routine

Core work plays well with other healthy habits. A few minutes of Pilates can follow a walk, warm you up before yoga, or act as a mindful reset in the middle of a workday. Because the pace is slow and focused, it also doubles as a moment of mindfulness, which is a welcome break when schedules fill up.

## Your Next Step

Tomorrow, set a five-minute timer, pick three beginner core moves, and do them slowly with steady breathing. Repeat on a few days this week. Once that feels easy, book an intro class to get a trained eye on your form.
