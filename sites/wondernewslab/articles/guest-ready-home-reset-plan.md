If your pre-hosting routine involves shoving random items into the oven, you are in good company. BuzzFeed Shopping recently rounded up 29 Wayfair products for exactly this crowd: people who scramble to make a home look cleaner, calmer, and guest-ready before visitors arrive. The roundup's product details aren't something we can reproduce here, so this guide takes its core idea and turns it into a plan you can run tonight: a reset that targets what guests actually notice, with the kinds of storage that make it stick.

## Why a messy room feels worse than it is

The categories in the BuzzFeed roundup tell the story: hiding clutter, reducing visual clutter, containing loose items, keeping essentials out of sight. The common thread is that a room looks messy mostly because of things in the open, not because it is dirty. That's our read, not a finding from the roundup, but it explains why the fastest reset is about moving objects, not scrubbing.

So the goal is simple: get loose stuff into a closed container, clear flat surfaces, and let a few clean spots do the talking.

## Step 1: Start at the door (3 minutes)

Guests form their first impression in the entryway, and the roundup includes products for organizing entryways for that reason.

- Put shoes in one spot, whether a rack, a bench, or a single lined-up row.
- Clear the nearest surface of mail, keys, and chargers into one bin or tray.
- Hang coats you don't need on display, or move them to a bedroom closet.

A bin you can slide under a bench or into a closet is the key tool here. It beats a drawer you have to organize first.

## Step 2: Clear the living room surfaces (5 minutes)

This is where most panic cleaning happens, and where tidying living rooms and making surfaces look neater earn their place on the roundup.

1. Grab a laundry basket and walk the room once, tossing in everything that doesn't belong.
2. Stack remote controls, books, and magazines in a single tidy pile or tray.
3. Fold or drape throw blankets, and straighten cushions.
4. Wipe the coffee table and any other surface people will put a drink on.

The basket is your emergency container. Whatever is in it goes to another room, not the oven.

## Step 3: Give clutter a real home (5 minutes)

Hiding clutter works best when the hiding place is built for it. Wayfair-style storage in this category includes closed options that keep essentials out of sight, which is the point: a lidded basket, a cabinet, or an ottoman with storage swallows mess without looking like a mess.

If you're shopping, a few things to check before you buy:

- **Closed beats open.** If you can see the contents, you haven't reduced visual clutter.
- **Measure first.** Small spaces need storage that fits the footprint you actually have.
- **Pick pieces that double up.** A bench, ottoman, or table that stores things does two jobs.

## Step 4: Quick polish for the bathroom and kitchen (4 minutes)

Guests will see these rooms up close, so aim for the fast wins.

- Bathroom: wipe the sink and mirror, swap the hand towel, empty the trash, and put out clean soap.
- Kitchen: clear the counters, run or empty the dishwasher, and wipe the surfaces you can see.

This is also where everyday mess control matters. A single caddy or tray that corrals bottles and sponges makes counters look cleaner in seconds.

## Step 5: Do the last-minute tricks (3 minutes)

The roundup highlights quick home refreshes and decor improvements, and the cheapest ones don't need a purchase.

- Open a window or two for a few minutes to freshen the air.
- Turn on warm lamps instead of the harsh overhead light.
- Add something simple, like a plant, a candle, or a bowl on the table, to make the room feel intentional.

Lighting does a lot of quiet work. Soft light hides what a quick wipe-down missed.

## Small spaces: the same plan, with tighter rules

Several items in the roundup are aimed at small-space organization, and the principle holds: with less room, every object has to earn its spot. Keep fewer things on display, use vertical storage where you can, and hold one rule firmly. If it doesn't fit in a closed container, it leaves the room.

## Make it easier next time

The real fix for panic cleaning is a setup where tidying takes minutes. Put a lidded basket in the living room, a tray at the door, and a caddy in the bathroom. Then a reset becomes a five-minute sweep instead of a frenzy.

The takeaway: before the next guest list goes out, buy or designate one closed container for each room, and your future self won't have to open the oven.
