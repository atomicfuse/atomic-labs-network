## A quick test for any "boring but works" product

Use these questions before you click buy on something that promises to make daily life easier.

### Does it fix one specific annoyance?

Products that try to do everything usually do nothing especially well. Name the exact irritation first. If you can't, skip it.

### Can you tell it worked?

The best everyday upgrades give you proof within a day or two. If you can't tell whether it's helping, you'll never know whether it was worth the money.

### Is the material simple and durable?

BuzzFeed highlights stainless steel for the scraper. As a general rule, a solid single-material tool tends to be easier to clean and keep than a gadget with parts that wear out. Check the product's own care instructions for how to clean it.

### Would you be fine if it cost twice as much?

If a small tool still feels worthwhile at double the price, it's solving a real problem. If it only makes sense at a bargain price, it's probably a impulse buy.

## Try it like a small experiment

Since this is a site about curiosity, treat a new cheap tool like a mini lab test:

1. Write down the annoyance you want to fix.
2. Use the product consistently for one to two weeks.
3. Note what changed, even if it's just "I stopped thinking about it."
4. Keep it if it earned its place. Pass it on if it didn't.

For a tongue scraper in particular, check the maker's directions for use and cleaning, and talk to a dentist if you have a concern about your mouth rather than relying on a shopping list.
