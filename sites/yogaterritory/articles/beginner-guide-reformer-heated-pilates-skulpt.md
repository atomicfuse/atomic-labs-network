Opening its doors in downtown Indianapolis, Skulpt Pilates offers a fresh approach to fitness with both reformer and heated Pilates classes under one roof. If you’re new to Pilates or simply looking to diversify your workout routine, this guide will walk you through what to expect at Skulpt, the benefits of each class type, and how to navigate their credit-based membership system.

## Understanding the Class Types

### Reformer Pilates
Reformer Pilates utilizes a specialized machine known as the reformer, which uses springs, pulleys, and a sliding carriage to provide resistance. This method helps improve your strength, flexibility, and alignment. It's particularly beneficial for beginners as it allows for controlled movements that enhance your understanding of body mechanics. 

At Skulpt, reformer classes are designed to accommodate various skill levels, making it a perfect starting point for those who have never tried Pilates before. You don’t need any prior experience; just come with a willingness to learn.

### Heated Pilates
Heated Pilates takes the principles of traditional Pilates and adds warmth – usually between 85 to 95 degrees Fahrenheit. The heat allows for an increased heart rate, aiding in flexibility and detoxification through sweat. This is ideal for individuals looking to elevate their workout and challenge their endurance.

For beginners, heated classes can be more intense, so it’s essential to listen to your body and hydrate well before and after classes. At Skulpt, the heated environment enhances both your fitness and mental focus, making it a unique experience.

## Why Choose Skulpt?

Skulpt's flexible, credit-based membership system is a standout feature. Rather than committing to a fixed monthly fee or specific class times, members can purchase credits that can be used across both reformer and heated classes. This flexibility allows you to tailor your Pilates experience according to your schedule and fitness goals.

### How to Get Started
To kick off your Pilates journey at Skulpt:
1. **Visit the studio**: Experience the welcoming atmosphere and speak with instructors about your fitness level and goals.
2. **Choose your classes**: Decide whether you want to try reformer, heated, or both. Don’t hesitate to attend introductory classes to understand the basics.
3. **Purchase credits**: Select a membership package that aligns with how often you plan to attend classes. The staff can guide you through the best options based on your interests.
4. **Prepare for your first class**: Wear comfortable clothing that allows for movement. Bring water to stay hydrated, particularly for heated classes, and show up 10-15 minutes early to set up your reformer and settle in.
