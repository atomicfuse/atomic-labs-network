## Why Low-Impact Core Work Makes Sense Right Now

Low-impact means one foot (or your back) stays in contact with the floor, and your joints aren't absorbing repeated landings. That makes it approachable if you're new to exercise, returning after a break, or just tired of cold-weather runs.

Core training is also a natural fit for the format. You can do it on a mat in a living room, with no equipment, in a few minutes.

## Before You Start: Three Form Cues

These matter more than how many reps you do.

- **Breathe on purpose.** Inhale through your nose to prepare, exhale through your mouth as you work. Holding your breath tightens your neck and shoulders instead of your core.
- **Keep your ribs knitted down.** Imagine your lower ribs softening toward your hips so your back doesn't arch off the floor.
- **Go small and slow.** A smaller, controlled movement beats a big, swingy one every time.

## A 5-Move Core Routine (About 5-8 Minutes)

Do each move for the suggested count, rest briefly, then repeat the whole circuit once if you feel good.

### 1. Pelvic Tilts (8 reps)
Lie on your back, knees bent, feet hip-width apart. Exhale and gently flatten your lower back toward the mat by tipping your pelvis. Inhale to release. This wakes up the deep abdominals without strain.

### 2. Toe Taps (6 per side)
From tabletop position (knees bent over hips, shins parallel to the floor), lower one foot to tap the mat, then return. Keep your lower back steady. If it lifts, shrink the range.

### 3. Dead Bug (6 per side)
Start in tabletop with arms reaching to the ceiling. Exhale as you extend your right arm overhead and left leg out, then switch. Move only as far as your back stays settled.

### 4. Modified Chest Lift (8 reps)
With knees bent and hands behind your head or thighs, exhale and curl your head and shoulders up, then lower with control. Keep your chin lifted slightly, as if holding a small peach under it.

### 5. Forearm Plank on Knees (3 holds of 15-20 seconds)
Place your forearms down, elbows under shoulders, knees on the mat. Make a long line from head to knees and breathe steadily. Progress to full plank when 30 seconds feels easy.

## Make It Stick

- **Pick a anchor time.** Right after coffee or before a shower keeps it from becoming a "someday" workout.
- **Aim for consistency over intensity.** Three short sessions a week beats one heroic effort.
- **Stop if something pinches.** Mild muscle effort is normal. Sharp or joint pain isn't. Check in with a qualified instructor or healthcare professional if you're unsure.
