A recent review from Arab News of Relive Studio makes a simple case: when a studio offers a range of fitness options, you lose most of your excuses for skipping a workout. The review's reporting is brief on specifics, so I won't invent details about classes or schedules. But the idea behind it is worth borrowing, and you can put it to work this week: build a small menu of workouts matched to your energy, so there's always something you'll actually do.

## The Idea Behind the Review

Per Arab News, Relive Studio is presented as a versatile fitness destination with enough choices to take away the usual reasons people bail on exercise. That framing matters more than any single class. Most missed workouts aren't about laziness. They happen because the one planned workout doesn't fit how you feel that day.

That's an observation of mine, not a finding from the review, but it matches how many people experience routines: a rigid plan breaks the first time you're tired, sore, or stressed.

## Why Variety Makes Consistency Easier

A single workout asks you to show up in the same condition every time. A menu asks only that you show up. Variety helps in a few practical ways:

- **It lowers the bar on hard days.** If a gentle option exists, "I'm too tired" stops being a dealbreaker.
- **It fights boredom.** Switching formats keeps the routine from feeling like a chore.
- **It reduces intimidation.** Newcomers can start with something approachable instead of facing the most intense option first.

## Build Your Own Energy-Based Menu

You don't need a studio membership to apply this. Set up three tiers at home and choose by how you feel, not by what you think you "should" do.

### Low energy: 10 minutes of gentle movement

Think slow, floor-based yoga. Try cat-cow for a minute, child's pose for a minute, a low lunge on each side for about a minute each, and a supine twist on each side. Finish with a few slow breaths. The goal is simply to move and keep the habit alive.

### Medium energy: 20 to 30 minutes of a steady flow

Link a few familiar poses: downward dog, low lunge, warrior II, and a standing forward fold. Repeat the sequence two or three times at a pace where you can still breathe comfortably through your nose.

### High energy: a harder session

Save your strongest workout for days you feel good: a longer yoga flow with holds, a strength circuit, or a brisk walk or run. Because you have easier options elsewhere, you won't feel pressure to make every session intense.

## A Beginner's Rule of Thumb

If you're new, start at the bottom of the menu. A short, easy session you complete beats an ambitious one you skip. Once the low tier feels automatic, add the next one. Variety works best when it widens your choices, not when it overwhelms you with them.

## Create Your "No Excuses" Rule

Here's a simple way to borrow the spirit of the review: for every excuse you tend to use, pre-assign a workout that answers it.

- "I'm exhausted" gets the 10-minute gentle sequence.
- "I'm short on time" gets one round of the flow, about 10 minutes.
- "I'm bored" gets a format you haven't tried recently.
- "I'm nervous about being a beginner" gets the easiest tier, done at home.

Write these on a note near your mat or workout shoes. When an excuse shows up, you already have an answer.

## If You Want to Try a Studio

The Arab News review points to Relive Studio as one example of a place built around choice. If you're evaluating any studio, look for the same qualities: more than one style of class, options at different intensities, and an atmosphere where a beginner won't feel out of place. Check the studio's own listings for what's currently offered, since the review's summary doesn't spell out details.

## Your Next Step

Pick your three workouts today, one for low energy, one for medium, one for high. Then tomorrow, do whichever matches how you feel. Showing up in a smaller way still counts.
