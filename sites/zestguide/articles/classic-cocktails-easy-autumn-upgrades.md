You don't need 17 new recipes to make a fall cocktail night work. You need a few classics you can make without looking anything up, plus one or two autumn flavors to swap in. Forbes recently rounded up 17 fall cocktails, from whiskey sours and hot toddies to martinis and margaritas, with seasonal flavors like apple and pumpkin. This guide takes that idea and makes it practical: start with the classic, then upgrade it.

A note on sourcing: Forbes' list names whiskey sours, hot toddies, martinis, margarita and apple and pumpkin cocktails. The ratios and steps below are standard home-bartending starting points, not the Forbes recipes. Adjust to your taste, and pour responsibly.

## Start With the Whiskey Sour

The whiskey sour is the easiest classic to move into fall, because whiskey already pairs well with apple, warm spices, and maple.

**Basic ratio (one drink, about 5 minutes):**

- 2 oz whiskey (bourbon is a friendly choice)
- 3/4 oz fresh lemon juice
- 3/4 oz simple syrup
- Ice

Shake everything with ice for about 15 seconds, then strain over fresh ice.

**Fall upgrades:**

- Swap the simple syrup for maple syrup, using the same amount.
- Replace 1 oz of the whiskey with 1 oz of apple cider for a softer, fruitier drink.
- Garnish with a thin apple slice.

## Make a Hot Toddy When the Weather Turns

A hot toddy is a warm drink you can build in a mug in about 5 minutes. It also works well for guests who want something cozy rather than chilled.

**Basic build (one drink):**

- 1 1/2 oz whiskey
- 1 tablespoon honey
- 2 teaspoons fresh lemon juice
- 4 to 6 oz hot water (hot, not boiling)

Stir the honey and lemon into the whiskey first, then add the hot water. Taste and add more honey if it's too sharp.

**Fall upgrades:** Add a cinnamon stick and a few cloves, or use hot apple cider in place of the water for a sweeter version.

## Give Your Martini an Apple Twist

Martinis showed up on the Forbes list, and they're a good option if your guests prefer something crisp over something sweet.

**A simple apple martini (one drink, about 5 minutes):**

- 2 oz vodka
- 1 oz apple cider
- 1/2 oz fresh lemon juice
- 1/2 teaspoon maple syrup, optional

Shake with ice for 15 seconds and strain into a chilled glass. Chill the glass in the freezer for 10 minutes ahead of time if you can. It makes a noticeable difference.

## Turn the Margarita Into a Fall Drink

Margaritas are usually a summer drink, but they adapt well to fall flavors.

**Basic ratio:**

- 2 oz tequila
- 1 oz fresh lime juice
- 1 oz apple cider (in place of orange liqueur, or alongside a splash of it)

Shake with ice, strain over fresh ice, and rim the glass with cinnamon sugar. To make the rim, mix 2 tablespoons sugar with 1/2 teaspoon ground cinnamon, rub a lime wedge around the rim, and dip.

## Apple or Pumpkin: How to Choose

Both flavors appear in Forbes' roundup, and they behave differently in a glass.

- **Apple** is bright and easy. Cider mixes into almost anything, hot or cold, and it's forgiving if your measurements are a little off.
- **Pumpkin** is richer and more dessert-like. It tends to work best in creamy or spiced drinks, so use it where you want something heavier.

If you're serving a mixed group, default to apple. Save pumpkin for a single signature drink.

## Set Up a Easy Fall Cocktail Night

You can host without spending the evening behind the counter.

1. **Pick two drinks.** One chilled (a whiskey sour or apple margarita) and one warm (a hot toddy).
2. **Prep ahead.** Squeeze your lemon and lime juice the same day and refrigerate it. Make a cinnamon-sugar rim mix and set out garnishes.
3. **Batch the base.** For a sour, multiply the ratio by the number of guests and stir the whiskey, juice, and syrup together in a pitcher. Shake or pour over ice per glass. Add about a splash of water per serving if you're not shaking, since shaking dilutes the drink.
4. **Keep a nonalcoholic option.** Warm cider with a cinnamon stick works for anyone skipping alcohol, and uses the same ingredients.

## Your Next Step

Pick one classic from this list and make it once this week, with one fall swap. Once you know how it tastes, scaling it up for a party is just multiplication.
