Americans aren't drinking much more coffee than they used to, but they're getting nearly 40% more calories from it. That's the finding of a new study reported by Healthline, and the main driver is the growing use of non-dairy creamer. Here's how to spot the creep in your own cup and build a version that still tastes good.

## What the Study Found

Per Healthline's report, coffee consumption among U.S. adults stayed stable between 2003 and 2018, but calories from coffee beverages rose nearly 40%. Non-dairy creamer was the biggest contributor. Its caloric contribution more than doubled, from 7.7 to 18.5 calories per day on average.

Those numbers are averages across many people, so your own cup may look quite different. Still, the pattern is useful: the coffee itself didn't change. What went into it did.

Healthline also notes that more than 60% of Americans drink coffee daily. A small add-in, repeated every morning, adds up quietly.

## Step 1: Measure What You Actually Pour

Most of us pour creamer by eye. For one week, measure it instead.

1. Grab a tablespoon measure.
2. Pour your usual amount into it over your mug, or count how many tablespoons it takes to get the color you like.
3. Compare that number to the serving size on the label.

Many creamer labels list a serving as one tablespoon. If your usual pour is three or four, you're getting three or four times the calories and sugar printed on the front of the bottle. This takes about two minutes and is the most useful thing you can do before changing anything else.

## Step 2: Read the Label in 30 Seconds

Flip the bottle and check three things:

- **Serving size:** Is it realistic for how you pour?
- **Added sugars:** Look for the "Added Sugars" line, and for sugar, corn syrup, or similar words near the top of the ingredient list. Ingredients are listed by amount, so the earlier they appear, the more of them there is.
- **Calories per serving:** Multiply by the number of tablespoons you measured in step 1.

Flavored creamers often work more like a dessert syrup than a splash of cream, so the flavor name on the front tells you little. The back of the bottle tells you what you're drinking.

## Step 3: Try Lighter Swaps

You don't have to go fully black. Change one thing at a time so your taste buds adjust.

### Cut back gradually
Reduce your creamer by half a tablespoon every few days. Going from three tablespoons to one over two weeks is far easier than quitting overnight.

### Switch to plain milk
A splash of plain milk gives you the creamy color and a softer bitterness without the added flavorings. Warm it for 30 seconds in the microwave so it doesn't cool your coffee.

### Add flavor without sugar
Stir a small pinch of ground cinnamon, about 1/4 teaspoon, into your grounds before brewing. It gives a sweet, warm impression with no sugar. The same trick works with a drop of vanilla extract, about 1/4 teaspoon per mug.

### Try chicory root
Chicory root is a traditional coffee companion with a roasted, slightly sweet flavor. Replacing a spoonful or two of your grounds with it can round out the cup. Start small, since the taste is distinct.

## Step 4: Make a Quick Homemade Creamer

If you like a flavored, creamy cup, make your own so you control what goes in. This takes about 5 minutes and keeps about a week in the fridge.

1. Combine 1 cup plain milk with 1/2 teaspoon vanilla extract and a pinch of cinnamon in a jar.
2. Add sweetener only if you want it. Start with 1 teaspoon and taste.
3. Shake for 15 seconds.
4. Use 1 to 2 tablespoons per mug, and shake before each use.

It won't be as thick as a store-bought product, and that's fine. You're aiming for a cup you enjoy with fewer extras, not an exact copy.

## Why Bother With Black Coffee at All

Healthline's coverage points to the documented health benefits of black coffee, including links to cardiovascular health, diabetes, and cancer prevention. The takeaway isn't that creamer ruins those benefits overnight. It's that a plain cup is the baseline, and each add-in moves you away from it. You get to decide how far.

## A Sample Two-Week Plan

- **Days 1 to 3:** Measure your pour and read the label.
- **Days 4 to 7:** Cut your creamer by about a third.
- **Days 8 to 10:** Swap to plain milk with cinnamon.
- **Days 11 to 14:** Try the homemade creamer or a smaller milk splash.

By the end, you'll know exactly what's in your mug and which version you actually prefer.

## The One Thing to Do Tomorrow

Tomorrow morning, pour your creamer into a tablespoon first. Whatever number you land on is the starting point for every change that follows.
