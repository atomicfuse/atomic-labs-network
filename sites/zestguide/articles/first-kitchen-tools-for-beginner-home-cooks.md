BuzzFeed Shopping recently rounded up 26 products it pitches as starter gear for anyone entering their "home cook era." The listing as we have it doesn't spell out which 26 products those are, so we won't pretend to review them. What the roundup does show is how many people want to cook more, and how quickly a shopping cart can balloon when they start.

Here's a more useful approach: buy a handful of tools first, learn what you actually reach for, and add from there. By the end of this article you'll have a short starter list, a sense of what can wait, and a first-week plan that gets dinner on the table.

## Start With the Tools You'll Use Every Single Time

Most weeknight cooking comes down to cutting, heating, stirring, and draining. A beginner kit should cover those four jobs and little else.

- **One chef's knife (8 inch).** It handles nearly everything: onions, chicken, herbs, squash. A sharp knife is safer than a dull one because it doesn't slip.
- **One cutting board.** Pick one large enough that food doesn't fall off the edges. Put a damp towel underneath so it doesn't slide.
- **One 10 to 12 inch skillet.** This is your workhorse for eggs, stir-fries, seared chicken, and quesadillas.
- **One medium pot with a lid (3 to 4 quarts).** Pasta, rice, soup, and boiled eggs all live here.
- **A sheet pan.** Roasting vegetables and sausages on one pan means one dish to wash.
- **A wooden spoon and a spatula.** Cheap, and you'll use them daily.

## Add a Few Small Tools That Make Cooking Easier

Once you have the basics, a few inexpensive extras remove common frustrations.

- **Measuring cups and spoons.** Cooking is forgiving, but rice-to-water ratios and baking are not. A standard stovetop rice ratio is 1 cup of long-grain white rice to 1½ to 2 cups of water, simmered covered about 15 minutes.
- **A colander.** Draining pasta from a pot without one is how dinner ends up in the sink.
- **An instant-read thermometer.** It takes guessing out of meat. Chicken is done at 165°F (74°C).
- **Tongs.** They flip, toss, and lift without burning your fingers.

## What Can Wait

A long "must-have" list is where budgets disappear. Skip these until you've cooked for a few weeks and know you want them:

- Single-purpose gadgets (avocado slicers, egg separators, banana hangers)
- Large appliance bundles
- Full knife block sets, since you'll use one or two blades
- Specialty pans for one dish

A useful rule: if you can't name three meals you'd make with it this month, don't buy it yet.

## A First Week of Cooking at Home

You don't need to cook seven nights. Aim for three or four, and let leftovers cover the rest. Here's a gentle plan that uses only the tools above.

### Night 1: Sheet Pan Sausage and Vegetables (about 35 minutes)

Heat the oven to 425°F. Toss 1 pound of chopped broccoli or potatoes with 2 tablespoons of oil, ½ teaspoon of salt, and pepper on a sheet pan. Add 4 sausages. Roast 25 to 30 minutes, flipping everything halfway, until browned and the sausages reach 160°F or are cooked through per the package.

### Night 2: Fried Rice From Leftovers (about 15 minutes)

Cold, day-old rice crisps better than fresh. Heat 1 tablespoon of oil in the skillet over medium-high heat. Scramble 2 eggs, push them aside, then add 2 cups of cooked rice and a cup of frozen peas and carrots. Cook 5 to 6 minutes, finish with 1 to 2 tablespoons of soy sauce.

### Night 3: Simple Tomato Pasta (about 25 minutes)

Boil 8 ounces of pasta in well-salted water, following the package time. Meanwhile, warm 2 tablespoons of olive oil in the skillet, cook 3 sliced garlic cloves for about 1 minute, then add a 14-ounce can of crushed tomatoes. Simmer 10 minutes, toss with the drained pasta, and add a splash of pasta water if it looks dry.

### Night 4: Pan-Seared Chicken Thighs (about 30 minutes)

Pat 4 boneless thighs dry and season with salt and pepper. Cook in a hot, lightly oiled skillet 5 to 7 minutes per side until they reach 165°F. Rest 5 minutes before slicing. Serve over the rice or with a quick salad.

## Build Confidence Before You Build a Kitchen

The appeal of the "home cook era," as BuzzFeed's roundup frames it, is that cooking feels easier and more approachable. That's achievable with very little gear. Pick the six or seven tools that cover your first week, cook three meals, and write down what you wished you had. Buy only that.
