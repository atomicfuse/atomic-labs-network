The yogurt aisle is long, and most of it is a guessing game. Three dietitians who spoke to Health landed on a clear answer: plain Greek yogurt is the healthiest yogurt you can buy. Icelandic yogurt and flavored kefir follow close behind. Below, you'll find what they have in common, how to read a label, and how to make plain yogurt something you'll actually want to eat.

## What the Dietitians Looked For

According to Health, the picks come down to two things:

- **Protein.** Greek and Icelandic yogurts are known for delivering it, which makes them more filling than a snack that's mostly sugar.
- **Live, active cultures.** These support gut health, and they're the reason yogurt and kefir show up in conversations about digestion.

That's a simple filter. If a container has both, it's worth a look.

## 1. Plain Greek Yogurt: The Top Pick

Plain Greek yogurt took first place in Health's roundup. It's easy to find, works in sweet and savory cooking, and gives you protein and live cultures in one scoop.

The word to focus on is *plain*. Flavored versions can carry added sugar, and a plain tub gives you control over what goes in your bowl.

### Easy ways to use it

This is where plain yogurt earns its spot in your fridge. Each of these takes under five minutes:

- **Breakfast bowl:** Spoon about 3/4 cup into a bowl and top with sliced fruit and a small handful of nuts.
- **Sweetened lightly:** Stir in a teaspoon of honey or maple syrup and a pinch of cinnamon. You get sweetness, but you decide how much.
- **Savory swap:** Use it in place of sour cream on tacos, baked potatoes, or chili.
- **Quick sauce:** Mix 1/2 cup yogurt with 1 tablespoon lemon juice, a small grated garlic clove, and a pinch of salt. Drizzle it over roasted vegetables or grilled chicken.

## 2. Icelandic Yogurt: A Strong Runner-Up

Icelandic yogurt, often sold under the name skyr, was the second choice. It's another high-protein option with live cultures, so if you like a thick, spoonable texture, it's a good alternative to Greek.

It works the same way in the kitchen. Use it in bowls, smoothies, or as a base for dips. If you're deciding between the two, the practical move is to compare labels on the shelf and pick the plain one with the protein number you like.

## 3. Flavored Kefir: The Drinkable Option

Kefir rounded out the list, and Health notes it as flavored kefir. It's a drinkable cultured dairy product, which makes it handy when you don't want to sit down with a spoon.

Because this pick is flavored, it's the one where label reading matters most. Check the added sugar line before it goes in your cart, and compare brands if you have options.

## How to Read a Yogurt Label in 30 Seconds

The dietitians' criteria translate neatly into a quick shelf check:

1. **Look for "plain" first.** It's the simplest way to avoid added sugar.
2. **Check the protein.** Greek and Icelandic styles are the ones to lean toward.
3. **Look for live and active cultures** on the container.
4. **For flavored products like kefir,** scan the added sugar line and compare it against another brand.

That's it. You don't need to memorize anything beyond those four steps.

## Make Plain Yogurt Taste Better Without a Sugar Bomb

The most common reason people drift back to flavored yogurt is that plain tastes tart. A few fixes help:

- **Use ripe fruit.** Mashed banana or berries stirred in bring natural sweetness.
- **Add crunch.** Toasted nuts, seeds, or a spoonful of oats make it feel like a real meal.
- **Start with a little sweetener.** A teaspoon of honey in a single serving is a modest amount, and you can reduce it over time.
- **Add warmth.** Cinnamon or vanilla extract makes it taste sweeter without adding sugar.

## Greek, Icelandic, or Kefir: Which Should You Buy?

If you want one answer, follow the dietitians and buy plain Greek yogurt. If you want variety, keep an Icelandic yogurt around for thicker bowls and a kefir for grab-and-go mornings. All three meet the same standard: protein and live, active cultures.

Next time you're in the dairy aisle, pick up a tub of plain Greek yogurt, flip it over, and check for protein and live cultures. Then take it home and top it yourself.
