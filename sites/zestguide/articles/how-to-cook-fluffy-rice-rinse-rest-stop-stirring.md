Rice is one of the simplest foods in the kitchen, and one of the easiest to get wrong. If yours comes out gummy, clumpy, or somehow both mushy and undercooked, the cause is probably a small habit rather than bad luck. Chefs interviewed by Fox News point to a handful of common mistakes: skipping the rinse, using one method for every rice type, stirring during cooking, and fluffing too aggressively.

Below, you'll find how to fix each one, so your next pot has separate, tender grains.

## Start with the step most people skip: rinsing

The chefs' main advice is to rinse basmati and jasmine rice before cooking. Per Fox News, this is the key to fluffy, distinct grains that don't clump together in the pot.

A simple way to do it:

1. Put the rice in a bowl or fine-mesh strainer.
2. Cover with cold water and swish with your hand.
3. Drain, then repeat until the water looks noticeably clearer.

This takes a couple of minutes, and it is the easiest upgrade on the list.

## Don't treat every rice the same

The chefs stress that rinsing, soaking, water ratios, resting, and gentle fluffing all depend on the variety of rice and the texture you want. A method that suits basmati won't necessarily suit another rice, and the texture you're after changes the plan too.

The practical takeaway: before you cook, decide what you want. Separate, airy grains call for rinsing. A softer, stickier result may call for a different approach. Check the package directions for the specific variety, and treat them as your starting point for water ratio.

### A quick decision guide

- **Basmati or jasmine, and you want fluffy grains:** rinse first.
- **Using a different variety:** don't assume the same steps apply. Look at what that rice is meant to do.
- **Unsure about soaking:** it's a variety-dependent step, so follow the instructions for your specific rice rather than soaking everything by default.

## Stop stirring

Stirring rice while it cooks is another habit the chefs warn against, because it can ruin the texture. Once the rice is simmering, leave it alone. Resist the urge to lift the lid and poke at it, too.

## Let it rest

Resting is part of the cooking. When the heat goes off, give the rice time to sit before you serve it. This is one of the steps the chefs say depends on the variety, so follow the guidance for your rice, but don't skip the pause entirely and go straight from stove to plate.

## Fluff gently

The final mistake is fluffing too aggressively. Rough handling can break the grains and turn a good pot into a clumpy one. Use a fork or a rice paddle and lift the rice rather than mashing or stirring it, working with light motions.

## Troubleshooting your last batch

The chefs' advice suggests where to look when rice goes wrong. These pairings are my own reading of their tips, so treat them as a starting point:

- **Gummy or clumpy:** you may have skipped the rinse, stirred while it cooked, or fluffed too roughly.
- **Undercooked or uneven:** the water ratio or resting time may not suit that variety. Recheck the package for your specific rice.
- **Broken or mushy grains:** try gentler fluffing and leave the pot undisturbed while it cooks.

Change one thing at a time so you can tell what made the difference.

## The takeaway

Next time you make basmati or jasmine, rinse until the water runs clearer, leave the pot alone while it cooks, let it rest, and fluff with a light hand. That one routine is the biggest step toward rice that actually fluffs.
