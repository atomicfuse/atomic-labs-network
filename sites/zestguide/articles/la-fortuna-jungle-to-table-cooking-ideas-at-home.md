You don't need a plane ticket to cook the way the best restaurants in La Fortuna, Costa Rica, are reportedly cooking. You need a shift in approach: start with what's local, respect traditional dishes, and let both do the work. Here's how to put that mindset into your own weeknight cooking.

## What's happening in La Fortuna

Forbes reports that around La Fortuna, local ingredients and traditional Costa Rican cooking are increasingly becoming the kind of dining luxury diners prefer. In other words, fine dining there is heading back to the jungle, treating regional food as the premium draw rather than something to dress up or replace.

That's the whole idea worth borrowing. Based on what Forbes describes, the appeal comes from place: the landscape, the sourcing, and the culinary heritage. I can't speak to specific menus or dishes from that reporting, so what follows is my own kitchen advice inspired by the approach, not a description of any particular restaurant.

## Start with one local ingredient, not a recipe

The easiest way to cook in a place-based way is to let the ingredient pick the dinner. Before you decide what to make:

1. Visit a farmers market, farm stand, or the local produce section and note what looks best and costs least.
2. Choose one star ingredient, such as tomatoes, greens, squash, or whatever is in season near you.
3. Build the plate around it with pantry staples you already own.

This takes about 10 minutes of planning and usually produces better food than following a recipe that calls for out-of-season produce.

## Treat traditional dishes with respect

A luxury version of a traditional dish isn't about piling on fancy ingredients. It's about doing the familiar thing carefully. A few ways to apply that at home:

- **Pick one dish from a cuisine you like** and make the classic version first, before you change anything.
- **Upgrade the technique, not the identity.** Toast spices before using them, salt in layers, rest meat before slicing, and let rice or beans cook until properly tender.
- **Change one thing at a time.** If you swap an ingredient, keep everything else the same so you can tell whether it helped.

## Upgrade simple plates with a few details

High-end cooking often comes down to small finishing moves. Any of these takes under five minutes:

- A squeeze of fresh citrus over cooked vegetables or fish right before serving.
- A handful of chopped fresh herbs added at the end, not the beginning.
- A pinch of flaky salt on the finished dish.
- A spoonful of something acidic or pickled beside rich food to balance it.

None of these require special equipment, and together they make a basic dinner feel considered.

## Cook sustainably without making it a project

Forbes frames the broader trend as part of a shift toward sustainable, local, and culturally grounded travel. At home, that translates into easy habits:

- Buy produce you'll use within a few days so less goes to waste.
- Use stems, peels, and trimmings in stocks or sautés where they make sense.
- Choose one or two locally sourced items per week rather than trying to overhaul your whole grocery list.

## A simple template for a jungle-to-table-style dinner

Use this as a flexible weeknight formula (about 30 minutes):

1. **Base:** Cook rice or another grain you like, following the package directions.
2. **Star:** Roast or sauté your best local vegetable in a hot pan with oil and salt until browned at the edges, about 10 to 15 minutes.
3. **Protein:** Eggs, beans, fish, or chicken, cooked simply.
4. **Finish:** Fresh herbs, citrus, and something pickled or tangy.

It's not a recreation of any La Fortuna menu. It's the same philosophy at weeknight scale: good ingredients, familiar technique, and a sense of where the food comes from.

## The takeaway

Next time you shop, buy the one ingredient that looks best and cheapest, then cook it the simplest way you can. That's where place-based cooking starts.
