Dark chocolate gets a lot of credit as a magnesium source, and it deserves some. But per Health, it isn't the richest one. Six everyday foods contain more: pumpkin seeds, spinach, amaranth, Brazil nuts, chia, and soybeans. Below, you'll find simple ways to work each into meals you already make, with no special equipment and very little extra time.

Magnesium is a popular wellness topic, often linked to sleep, stress, and muscle recovery. This guide sticks to the cooking side: how to get these foods onto your plate. If you have a health condition or take medication, ask your doctor or dietitian before changing your diet in a big way.

## 1. Pumpkin Seeds: The Easiest Swap

Pumpkin seeds (pepitas) need no cooking, which makes them the lowest-effort item on the list.

- **Toast them:** Heat a dry skillet over medium heat and stir 1/4 cup of seeds for 3 to 4 minutes, until they puff and start to pop. Salt lightly.
- **Use them as a topper:** Sprinkle over soups, grain bowls, roasted vegetables, or yogurt.
- **Make a quick crunch:** Toss with a pinch of chili powder and a squeeze of lime.

Watch the pan closely. They go from toasted to scorched fast.

## 2. Spinach: Fast Greens for Any Dinner

Spinach cooks down dramatically, so a big bag becomes a modest serving. That works in your favor.

- **Wilt it into hot dishes:** Stir 4 to 5 packed cups into a pot of soup, pasta, or curry during the last 1 to 2 minutes of cooking.
- **Sauté it:** Heat 1 tablespoon olive oil with a sliced garlic clove, add spinach, and stir for about 2 minutes. Finish with lemon juice.
- **Blend it:** A handful disappears into a fruit smoothie without much flavor change.

## 3. Amaranth: A Tiny Grain with Big Payoff

Amaranth is less familiar than rice or quinoa, but it cooks simply as a porridge-style grain.

- **Basic method:** Combine 1 cup amaranth with 2 1/2 cups water and a pinch of salt. Bring to a boil, then cover and simmer about 20 minutes, until tender. Stir occasionally, since the texture turns soft and a little sticky.
- **Breakfast:** Stir in cinnamon, fruit, and a spoonful of nut butter.
- **Dinner:** Use it as a base under stews or roasted vegetables, or stir it into soup to thicken it.

If you want a fluffier texture, try mixing it half and half with another grain you cook regularly.

## 4. Brazil Nuts: Keep It to a Few

Brazil nuts are rich and large, so a small handful goes a long way.

- **Snack smart:** Pair a few with fruit for an afternoon bite.
- **Chop them:** Scatter over oatmeal, salads, or roasted squash.
- **Blend them:** Grind finely into a crumb topping for baked fish or vegetables.

Store them in a sealed container in a cool place, or the fridge if you keep them for weeks. Their high fat content means they can turn rancid.

## 5. Chia: The Pantry Stretcher

Chia seeds are the simplest way to sneak magnesium into breakfast and snacks.

- **Overnight chia pudding:** Stir 3 tablespoons chia into 1 cup milk (dairy or plant-based) with a little sweetener or vanilla. Refrigerate at least 2 hours, or overnight, stirring once after 10 minutes to prevent clumps.
- **Add to smoothies or oatmeal:** Start with 1 tablespoon.
- **Make a quick jam:** Mash 1 1/2 cups berries, warm briefly, and stir in 2 tablespoons chia. It thickens as it cools.

## 6. Soybeans: Edamame and Beyond

Soybeans are the most dinner-friendly of the six.

- **Edamame:** Boil shelled or in-pod edamame for about 5 minutes, drain, and salt. Frozen is convenient and cooks fast.
- **Stir-fries and bowls:** Toss shelled edamame in during the last 2 to 3 minutes.
- **Salads:** Mix cooled edamame with cucumber, scallions, and a sesame-soy dressing.

## A Simple Way to Put It All Together

You don't need a new meal plan. Try this one-bowl dinner, which uses four of the six foods in about 25 minutes:

1. Start 1 cup amaranth simmering (20 minutes).
2. Meanwhile, sauté 5 cups spinach with garlic and olive oil (2 minutes).
3. Steam or boil 1 cup shelled edamame (about 5 minutes).
4. Toast 1/4 cup pumpkin seeds (3 to 4 minutes).
5. Pile it all into bowls and finish with lemon juice and salt.

For breakfast, chia pudding takes about 5 minutes of work the night before.

## Dark Chocolate Can Stay

This isn't a reason to give up dark chocolate. It remains a good source; it just isn't the top one. Treat it as a bonus and let seeds, greens, grains, nuts, and beans handle the heavy lifting.

Pick one food from this list and use it in tonight's dinner. Small, repeatable habits beat a perfect plan you never start.
