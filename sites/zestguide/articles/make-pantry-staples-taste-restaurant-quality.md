NYC private chef Julia Chebotar appeared on "Fox & Friends Weekend" to share budget-friendly ideas for home-cooked meals, per Fox News. Her focus, as reported, was simple ingredients that still deliver flavor. The segment's specific recipes aren't detailed in the coverage, so what follows isn't her menu. It's a set of tested techniques built on the same idea: cheap staples can taste like a restaurant made them if you handle them well.

By the end, you'll have five habits and one flexible 25-minute dinner that turns rice, beans, eggs, or pasta into something worth sitting down for.

## Start With Salt, Fat, and Heat

Most bland home cooking comes down to timid technique, not cheap ingredients.

- **Salt in layers.** Season the onions as they cook, then the sauce, then taste at the end. A pound of pasta needs about 2 tablespoons of kosher salt in 4 quarts of water.
- **Use enough fat.** Roughly 1 to 2 tablespoons of oil for a pan of vegetables lets them brown instead of steam.
- **Preheat the pan.** Give a skillet 2 to 3 minutes over medium-high heat before anything goes in. Browning is flavor, and it costs nothing.

## Build a Flavor Base in 8 Minutes

A base turns plain starches into a meal. Here's the simplest version:

1. Heat 2 tablespoons oil in a wide pan over medium heat.
2. Add 1 diced onion and a pinch of salt. Cook 6 to 8 minutes, stirring now and then, until soft and golden at the edges.
3. Add 3 chopped garlic cloves and 1 tablespoon tomato paste. Cook 1 minute until the paste darkens slightly.
4. Add 1 teaspoon of a spice you like (cumin, smoked paprika, or chili flakes all work).

That base works under beans, lentils, rice, eggs, or pasta.

## The 25-Minute Pantry Skillet

This is a template, not a strict recipe. It serves 4.

**You need:** the flavor base above, one 15-ounce can of beans (drained and rinsed), one 14-ounce can of diced tomatoes, 1 cup cooked rice or 4 slices of toast, and 4 eggs.

1. Make the base (8 minutes).
2. Stir in the beans and tomatoes with 1/4 cup water. Simmer 8 minutes, until slightly thickened.
3. Taste, then add salt and a splash of vinegar or lemon juice if it tastes flat.
4. Make four small wells, crack in the eggs, cover, and cook 4 to 5 minutes for set whites and soft yolks.
5. Serve over rice or with toast.

Total time is about 25 minutes, using ingredients that keep for weeks.

## Finish With Acid and Something Fresh

This step is what makes home food taste restaurant-quality. Right before serving, add a teaspoon or two of lemon juice, lime juice, or vinegar. Then add a handful of chopped herbs, a spoonful of yogurt, or a few sliced scallions. One bunch of parsley or cilantro can finish several meals across the week.

## Stretch Ingredients Across the Week

Cooking once and using twice saves money and effort.

- **Cook a double batch of rice or beans.** On day one, serve them as a side. On day two, fry the rice with an egg and leftover vegetables, or mash the beans into a quick spread for toast.
- **Roast one tray of vegetables.** Use half at dinner. Fold the rest into pasta, eggs, or a grain bowl the next day.
- **Save the odds and ends.** Onion ends, carrot peels, and herb stems can go in a freezer bag. Simmer them with water for 45 minutes for a basic stock.

## Swap, Don't Skip

When prices climb, you don't have to give up comfort food. Swap the pricier ingredient and keep the technique.

- Use bone-in chicken thighs instead of boneless breasts. They're more forgiving and brown well.
- Stretch ground meat by mixing in cooked lentils or finely chopped mushrooms, about half and half.
- Use canned or frozen vegetables when fresh ones are out of season. Frozen spinach, peas, and corn all work in soups and skillets.

## The Takeaway

Budget cooking doesn't need fancy ingredients. Next time you cook, brown the onions properly, season in layers, and finish with acid. Those three moves will change what a can of beans can do.
