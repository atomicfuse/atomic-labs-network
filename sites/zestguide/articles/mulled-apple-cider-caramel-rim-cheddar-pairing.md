Bill Michaels of Fly Creek Cider Mill & Orchard recently appeared on *Fox & Friends Weekend*, per Fox News, to demonstrate autumn apple cider recipes. The centerpiece was hot mulled cider in a caramel-rimmed glass, topped with whipped cream and mulling spices, served alongside extra-sharp cheddar cheese.

Here's how to build that same idea at home: a dessert-style mulled cider and a simple cheese pairing that turns a pot of cider into a fall hosting plan. The segment as reported didn't include exact quantities, so the measurements below are my own starting points, scaled to a typical batch. Adjust to taste.

## The Mulled Cider Base

The demo featured mulling spices, and that's the part that makes cider taste like a café drink instead of just warm juice. Use fresh, unfiltered cider if you can find it, since it has more apple flavor to build on.

**Makes about 6 servings. Prep 5 minutes, simmer 15 to 20 minutes.**

- 6 cups apple cider
- 3 cinnamon sticks
- 6 whole cloves
- 3 whole allspice berries
- 2 strips orange peel (optional)

### Steps

1. Combine everything in a medium pot.
2. Bring to a gentle simmer over medium heat. Don't let it boil hard, which can dull the flavor.
3. Reduce heat to low and simmer 15 to 20 minutes, until fragrant.
4. Strain out the spices, or ladle carefully around them.

Keep the pot on the lowest heat while guests arrive. Whole spices are better than ground here: ground spices turn cider cloudy and gritty.

## The Caramel-Rimmed Glass

This is the detail that makes the drink feel special. Prepare the glasses before you ladle anything.

1. Warm about 1/4 cup caramel sauce in the microwave for 10 to 15 seconds, so it's pourable but not hot.
2. Spread it on a small plate.
3. Dip the rim of each mug or heatproof glass about 1/4 inch deep, turning to coat.
4. Let it set for a few minutes.

Use sturdy mugs or heatproof glasses, since you're pouring in hot liquid. If the caramel is too thin, it will drip down the outside; chilling the glasses for 10 minutes first helps it grab.

## Top It With Whipped Cream

Ladle in the hot cider, leaving about an inch of room, then add a generous spoonful of whipped cream. Store-bought works fine. If you whip your own, beat 1 cup cold heavy cream with 1 tablespoon sugar until soft peaks form, about 2 to 3 minutes with a mixer.

The cream melts slowly into the cider, so the first sips are rich and the last ones are sweet and spiced. A pinch of cinnamon on top finishes it.

## Pair It With Extra-Sharp Cheddar

The other half of the demo was extra-sharp cheddar served with the cider. It's a classic fall combination, and it's easy to see why. That's my take, not Michaels': the cider brings sweetness and a little acidity, while aged cheddar brings salt, fat, and sharpness. Each one resets your palate for the other.

To serve it simply:

- Cut about 2 ounces of cheddar per person into thin slices or small cubes.
- Pull the cheese out of the fridge 20 to 30 minutes ahead so the flavor opens up.
- Add sliced fresh apples if you like, since they bridge the two.

You don't need crackers, but a handful of walnuts or plain crackers wouldn't hurt.

## A Realistic Hosting Plan

This whole setup is low-stress, which is its real appeal.

- **Day before:** Buy the cider and cheese. Make sure you have cinnamon sticks, cloves, and allspice.
- **One hour before:** Cut the cheese and let it come to room temperature. Rim the glasses with caramel.
- **30 minutes before:** Start the cider simmering. Your kitchen will smell like autumn by the time people arrive.
- **At serving:** Ladle, top with whipped cream, and set out the cheese.

Total hands-on time is around 20 minutes.

## Tips That Save the Batch

- **Too sweet?** Add a squeeze of lemon or a splash more cider to a pot that's reduced too far.
- **Spices too strong?** Strain earlier. Spices left in for hours can turn bitter.
- **Making it ahead?** Simmer the cider with spices, strain, cool, and refrigerate. Reheat gently before serving.
- **Serving kids and adults?** Keep the base non-alcoholic, as in the demo, and let guests add their own extras.

The takeaway: simmer whole spices into your cider, rim the glass with caramel, and put a plate of sharp cheddar next to it. That's a cozy fall gathering without much work.
