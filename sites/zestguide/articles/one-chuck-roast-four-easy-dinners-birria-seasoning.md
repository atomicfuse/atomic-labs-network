BuzzFeed's latest cooking-gadget roundup is mostly a shopping list, but one item stands out for anyone who wants restaurant-style flavor without a cabinet full of spices: EZ Bombs birria cooking bombs. Per BuzzFeed, they're pitched as a quick way to make flavorful tacos, quesadillas, ramen, nachos, and more.

I can't vouch for the product itself. But the idea is sound, and it points to a smart weeknight strategy: cook one big chuck roast once, then build a different dinner from it each night. Here's how to plan that, whichever seasoning shortcut you use.

## Why one roast makes sense

Birria is traditionally a long-simmered, deeply seasoned beef dish, and the usual obstacle is the shopping list: dried chiles, multiple spices, and time spent blending and balancing them. A seasoning bomb is meant to collapse that into one step. The meat does the rest of the work while you're doing something else.

Chuck roast is a good fit because it's a tough, well-marbled cut that turns tender and shreddable with long, moist cooking. A 3 to 4 pound roast will typically cover several meals for a small household.

## Step 1: Cook the roast

Follow the package directions for how much seasoning to use with your roast. I don't have the product's specifics, so don't guess at quantities. Then pick your method.

### Slow cooker

- Cut the roast into 3 or 4 large chunks so it cooks evenly and takes the seasoning better.
- Add the seasoning and enough liquid to come about halfway up the meat (check the package for what it calls for).
- Cook on low for 8 to 10 hours, or on high for 4 to 6, until the meat shreds with a fork with no resistance.
- Hands-on time: about 10 minutes.

### Pressure cooker

- Same setup: chunks of meat, seasoning, and liquid.
- Cook at high pressure for roughly 60 to 75 minutes, then let the pressure release naturally for 10 to 15 minutes.
- Hands-on time: about 10 minutes, plus the time the pot takes to come to pressure.

The slow cooker is better if you're leaving for work. The pressure cooker wins when you decide at 5 p.m. that tonight is a birria night. Either way, keep the cooking liquid. It's the best part.

## Step 2: Shred and store smart

Shred the meat with two forks right in the pot so it soaks up the juices. Then divide it:

- Portion the meat into containers, covering each with some of the cooking liquid so it doesn't dry out.
- Refrigerate what you'll use within 3 to 4 days.
- Freeze the rest in flat zip-top bags for up to 3 months.
- Skim the fat from the top of the liquid and save a cup or two for dipping and for ramen.

## Four dinners from one pot

These match the uses BuzzFeed called out for the bombs. Each takes about 15 minutes once the meat is cooked.

### Night one: tacos

Warm corn tortillas in a dry skillet for 30 seconds per side. Fill with shredded beef, chopped white onion, cilantro, and a squeeze of lime. Serve with a small bowl of the cooking liquid for dipping.

### Night two: quesadillas

Layer shredded beef and a melty cheese (Oaxaca or Monterey Jack both work) on a flour tortilla, fold, and cook in a skillet over medium heat for 2 to 3 minutes per side until crisp and the cheese is melted. Dip in the liquid.

### Night three: birria ramen

This is the one that makes leftovers feel like a new dish. Heat 2 cups of the cooking liquid with 1 cup of water or broth per serving, cook instant or fresh ramen noodles in it for about 3 minutes, and top with a handful of beef, scallions, and a soft-boiled egg if you have one. Taste before adding salt, since the liquid is already seasoned.

### Night four: loaded nachos

Spread tortilla chips on a sheet pan, scatter beef and shredded cheese over them, and bake at 400°F for 5 to 7 minutes until the cheese bubbles. Finish with pickled onions, sliced jalapeños, sour cream, or whatever's in the fridge.

## Making it work for a real week

- **Cook on Sunday, eat Monday through Thursday.** Beef reheated in its liquid stays juicy. Reheat gently in a covered skillet over medium-low heat, or in the microwave with a splash of liquid.
- **Don't skip the toppings.** Fresh onion, cilantro, lime, and something pickled keep four beef dinners from feeling repetitive.
- **Vary the carb.** Corn tortillas, flour tortillas, noodles, chips: the same meat tastes different in each.
- **Taste and adjust at the end.** Salt, acid, and heat are easy to tweak after cooking. A squeeze of lime often does more than extra salt.

## Is a seasoning bomb worth stocking?

If you cook birria-style dishes rarely, a single-purpose seasoning shortcut can be more practical than buying a dozen spices and chiles you'll use once. If you cook them often, making your own blend will likely be cheaper and more customizable. Check the ingredient list and sodium level on whatever you buy, and don't be afraid to taste the finished liquid before you add anything else.

The real takeaway doesn't depend on the brand: the next time you're at the store, buy the biggest chuck roast that fits your pot and plan four different dinners around it.
