## What's actually happening

Per Fox Business, the century-old cafe is preparing to reopen under a nonprofit model. All profits will go to Hope the Mission's homelessness programs. The diner is also set to keep its classic recipes and bring back longtime union employees.

That's the full picture available so far. Details like hours, menu prices, and reservation policies weren't part of the reporting, so check the restaurant's official channels before you plan a trip.

## Why the model is worth understanding

A social enterprise is a business whose main purpose is a mission, not owner profit. In this case, the reported structure is simple: the restaurant sells food, and the profits fund homelessness services through Hope the Mission.

For you as a diner, that means the meal works like any other: you order, you eat, you pay. The difference is where the profit goes.

The reopening also follows a high-profile closure tied to labor disputes, per the reporting. Rehiring longtime union employees makes the comeback a story about people as much as pancakes. Staff who know the grill, the regulars, and the rhythms of the place are a big part of what makes a legacy diner feel like itself.

## Bring the diner home: a basic pancake method

Can't make it to Los Angeles? A good diner-style pancake is a technique worth knowing. This is a standard home method for about 8 four-inch pancakes, with roughly 10 minutes of prep and 15 minutes of cooking. It is not the cafe's recipe, which hasn't been shared.

### Ingredients

- 1 1/2 cups (190 g) all-purpose flour
- 2 tablespoons sugar
- 2 teaspoons baking powder
- 1/2 teaspoon salt
- 1 1/4 cups (300 ml) milk
- 1 large egg
- 3 tablespoons melted butter, plus more for the pan

### Steps

1. Whisk the flour, sugar, baking powder, and salt in a large bowl.
2. In a second bowl, whisk the milk, egg, and melted butter.
3. Pour the wet mixture into the dry and stir just until combined. Lumps are fine. Let the batter rest 5 minutes.
4. Heat a griddle or skillet over medium heat and lightly butter it.
5. Pour about 1/4 cup of batter per pancake. Cook 2 to 3 minutes, until bubbles form and the edges look set.
6. Flip and cook another 1 to 2 minutes, until golden.
7. Keep finished pancakes in a 200°F oven while you cook the rest.

The biggest mistake is overmixing, which makes pancakes tough. Stop stirring when you can't see dry flour.
