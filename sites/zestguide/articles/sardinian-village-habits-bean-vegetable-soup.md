Researchers at National Geographic are studying Bolotana, a small village in Sardinia, as a potential new "blue zone." Per Fox News, the village has 10 centenarians among 2,262 residents, and the habits behind that number are surprisingly ordinary: natural movement all day, a mostly plant-based diet, and strong social connection.

You can't move to Sardinia on a Tuesday. You can make a pot of bean-and-vegetable soup, invite someone to eat it with you, and walk to get the groceries. This article shows you how, with a pantry-friendly minestrone-style recipe at the center.

A quick caveat: Bolotana is a *potential* blue zone still under study, and nothing here is a medical promise. Think of it as a template for a daily rhythm, not a cure.

## The Three Habits, in Plain Terms

Per the report, the village's everyday longevity habits come down to three things:

- **Constant, natural movement.** Not scheduled workouts, just daily life that keeps people on their feet.
- **A mostly plant-based diet.** Vegetables, legumes, and similar staples carry most meals.
- **Strong social connection.** People are woven into a community.

The common thread is that none of these require willpower. They're built into the day. That's the useful lesson: design your routine so the healthy choice is the easy one.

## The Food Habit: A Pantry Minestrone

Beans, olive oil, and vegetable soup are a natural way to eat in this spirit. This version uses mostly pantry and freezer staples. It serves 4 and takes about 35 minutes: 10 minutes of prep, 25 minutes of simmering.

### Ingredients

- 3 tablespoons olive oil, plus more for serving
- 1 medium onion, diced
- 2 carrots, diced
- 2 celery stalks, diced
- 3 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 can (14 oz) diced tomatoes
- 4 cups vegetable broth
- 2 cups water
- 2 cans (15 oz each) white beans or chickpeas, drained and rinsed
- 2 cups chopped greens (kale, chard, or spinach)
- 1 cup small pasta or 1 diced zucchini (optional)
- 1 teaspoon dried oregano or Italian seasoning
- Salt and black pepper

### Steps

1. **Build the base (8 minutes).** Heat the olive oil in a large pot over medium heat. Add the onion, carrots, and celery with a pinch of salt. Cook, stirring now and then, until softened, about 8 minutes.
2. **Add aromatics (1 minute).** Stir in the garlic and oregano until fragrant.
3. **Simmer (15 minutes).** Add the tomatoes, broth, water, and beans. Bring to a boil, then reduce to a gentle simmer for 15 minutes.
4. **Finish (5 minutes).** Add the greens and the pasta or zucchini if using. Cook until the pasta is tender (check the package, usually 7 to 10 minutes) or the greens are wilted.
5. **Season.** Taste, then adjust with salt and pepper. Finish each bowl with a drizzle of olive oil.

### Easy swaps

- No fresh vegetables? Use 2 cups of frozen mixed vegetables in step 3.
- Want it heartier? Mash about a cup of the beans against the side of the pot to thicken the broth.
- Leftovers keep in the fridge for up to 4 days and freeze well for up to 3 months. Leave the pasta out if freezing, since it turns soft.

## The Movement Habit: Let Errands Do the Work

The takeaway from Bolotana isn't "exercise more." It's "move without making it a project." A few doable ways to copy that:

- Walk to pick up the fresh ingredients for your soup instead of driving, if it's practical.
- Take a 10-minute walk after dinner, with a friend if you can.
- Stand or pace during phone calls.
- Park farther away and carry your own bags.

These small moments add up without a gym membership or a calendar slot. Choose one and attach it to something you already do.

## The Social Habit: Make the Pot Bigger

Soup is one of the easiest foods to share. This recipe stretches to 6 bowls with an extra can of beans and a cup of water. Some low-effort ways to put connection on the menu:

- Invite a neighbor or coworker for a weekly soup night. Ask them to bring bread.
- Cook a double batch and drop off a container with someone who lives alone.
- Call a relative while you chop. It makes prep time pass faster, and it counts.

Connection is the easiest of the three habits to skip, because nothing forces it. Put it on the calendar the way you would a grocery run.

## A Simple One-Week Starter Plan

- **Monday:** Make the soup. Walk to the store for the greens.
- **Wednesday:** Eat the leftovers and take a post-dinner walk.
- **Friday or Sunday:** Share a bowl with someone, at your table or theirs.

That's three small changes, each one tied to the habits researchers are noticing in Sardinia.

This week, put a pot of beans and vegetables on the stove, then ask one person to come eat it with you.
