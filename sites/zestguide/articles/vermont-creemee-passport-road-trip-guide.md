Vermont's signature soft serve now comes with a passport. Per Afar, the Vermont Creemee passport launched in April 2026 as a 20-page booklet with a digital directory covering 189 places to try the state's creemee. The booklet has pages for collecting stamps, and proceeds support Vermont nonprofits.

If you're planning a trip, here's how to turn that into a route that makes sense, plus what to know about the treat itself.

## What a creemee is (and why it's not just soft serve)

Afar describes the creemee as Vermont's richer, higher-butterfat soft serve. That's the key difference: more butterfat generally means a creamier, denser texture than the airy swirl you may be used to. If you've only had standard soft serve, expect something fuller.

The passport frames creemees as a way to explore local farms, maple culture, and small-town Vermont. Maple is the flavor most closely tied to the state, so it's a smart first order. Once you've had that, try whatever unusual flavor a shop is proud of.

## How the passport works

The basics, based on what has been reported:

- **The booklet:** 20 pages, with room to collect stamps as you visit.
- **The directory:** 189 shops are listed, and a digital version is available alongside the booklet.
- **The purpose:** Proceeds go to Vermont nonprofits, so buying the passport is part of the point, not an add-on.

I can't tell you where to buy the booklet, what it costs, or how stamping works at each shop from the reporting available, so check the official passport listing before you go.

## Plan your route like a trail, not a checklist

With 189 shops across the state, you won't hit them all in one trip. A few ways to keep it manageable:

### Pick a region, not the whole state

Use the digital directory to cluster stops by town. Three or four shops in neighboring towns beats a long drive for one stamp. A creemee is a treat, not a meal, so spacing matters more than volume.

### Split orders

If you're traveling with someone, share one creemee per stop. You get the stamp and the taste without feeling overfull by stop three. Order a small when sharing isn't an option.

### Mix classic and curious

At each stop, ask what the shop is known for. Alternate between a straightforward maple and a flavor you wouldn't normally pick. That gives you a real comparison between shops instead of five similar cones.

### Build in small-business stops

The passport is designed to send travelers toward small, independent businesses. Plan time to walk around each town, since the creemee stand is often next to a farm stand, general store, or local shop worth a look.

## Practical tips for eating your way across the state

- **Bring napkins and wipes.** Higher-butterfat soft serve softens in warm weather, and you'll be eating in the car or on a curb.
- **Carry your passport in a zip bag.** Sticky fingers and paper booklets don't mix.
- **Check hours before you drive.** Seasonal stands often keep limited schedules, so confirm each stop ahead of time.
- **Keep a note of each flavor.** A quick phone note after every stop makes ranking them at the end easy.

## Make it a game

The passport gives you built-in rules for a trip. Try one of these:

- **Maple showdown:** Order a maple creemee at every stop and rank them.
- **One-weekend challenge:** See how many stamps you can realistically collect without rushing. Set a target based on drive times, not ambition.
- **First-timer crawl:** If you're visiting a single town, such as Burlington, focus on shops within walking distance of each other.

## The takeaway

Open the digital directory, choose one region, and plan three to five stops with time to explore between them. A passport with 189 shops is an invitation to come back, so don't try to finish it in one trip.
